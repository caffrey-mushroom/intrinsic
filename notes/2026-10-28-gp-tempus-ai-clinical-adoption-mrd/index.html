SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined clinical use of molecular profiling, circulating tumor DNA, and minimal residual disease testing across solid and hematologic cancers. The expert practices in a community-based oncology setting and uses several vendors based on clinical utility, turnaround time, tissue availability, payer support, and workflow integration.

MRD testing is most established for colorectal cancer treatment decisions and hematologic malignancies where results can guide therapy. Its value in advanced solid tumors remains uncertain, particularly when a positive result does not lead to an actionable intervention.

Key Insights

Caris currently benefits from easy electronic medical record ordering, institutional familiarity, vendor education, and effective handling of insurance denials. Tempus has historically differentiated itself through RNA capabilities. Its emerging artificial intelligence tools could accelerate biomarker identification in lung and head-and-neck cancers. The expert viewed AI-based biomarker prescreening as potentially important because treatment decisions in lung cancer are often time-sensitive. Clinical actionability should govern MRD use. Detecting progression earlier may only increase monitoring anxiety or start therapy sooner without improving survival. A combined Tempus and Personalis offering could strengthen pan-cancer MRD and serial monitoring capabilities, although the expert had no direct experience with Personalis. A single vendor offering both profiling and MRD could simplify care, but evidence, competition, and institutional workflows will continue to influence selection. EMR integration is a meaningful competitive advantage because clinicians in busy practices favor tests that require minimal ordering effort.

Clinical Data

MRD is considered most actionable in colorectal, bladder, leukemia, lymphoma, and myeloma settings. In advanced solid tumors, positive MRD with negative imaging may justify closer surveillance but does not clearly establish when treatment should begin. The expert cited prior ovarian cancer evidence in which initiating therapy from an earlier blood-marker signal did not improve survival compared with waiting for clinical progression.
