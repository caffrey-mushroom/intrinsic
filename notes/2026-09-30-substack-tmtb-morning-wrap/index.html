Anthropic / AMZN / GOOGL: Cloud partners generated 47% of 2025 revenue – Reuters

Reuters reports Anthropic’s IPO filing shows $2.16B, or 47% of 2025 revenue, came through AWS and Google Cloud, up from 32% in 2024 and 11% in 2023. AI Infrastructure / PJM: FERC pauses emergency capacity plan through February – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports FERC accepted PJM’s emergency capacity procurement plan but suspended it until February 28 while the grid operator addresses cost allocation, collateral and customer exit provisions. The delay could be shortened if PJM resolves those issues sooner. The plan follows a 6.8GW shortfall in its latest capacity auction, with regulators seeking to protect existing customers from costs tied to data-center projects that fail to materialize.

TSLA: Secures $30B in new credit facilities – SEC filing

Tesla disclosed $30B of senior unsecured credit facilities: a $20B three-year delayed-draw term loan, an $8B five-year revolver and a $2B 364-day revolver. The facilities replace its previous $5B revolving line and are available for general corporate purposes. They were undrawn at signing, and Tesla said it does not expect to borrow under them during 2026.

GOOGL: Pays more than 100 publishers for AI search content – The Information

The Information reports Google is paying more than 100 digital publishers in a pilot covering AI Overviews, AI Mode and Gemini. Payments reflect a publisher’s contribution to the value of an answer, rather than simply whether its content is used. Payouts range from less than $1,000 over several months to more than $1M annually; some publishers receive less than 0.1% of their advertising revenue, while major publishers remain holdouts.

Nscale / AI IPOs: Roadshow timing in doubt as IPO market weakens – WSJ

WSJ reports some investors now expect Nscale’s roadshow, previously anticipated as early as next week, could slip. SB Energy has delayed its IPO after valuation pushback, while Oura postponed an offering targeting a roughly $15.6B valuation. The Information questions Nscale’s $35B IPO pitch because much of its $103B backlog depends on unbuilt, unfinanced data centers.

CXMT / Memory Equipment: $5.2B expansion favors domestic suppliers – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports, citing SCMP, that CXMT plans RMB34.9B ($5.2B) of investment, comprising RMB24.1B for R&D and RMB10.8B for testing infrastructure. Equipment represents RMB22.4B, or about 93%, of the R&D project budget, with CXMT favoring domestic suppliers. The expansion includes a new testing project scheduled over 37 months as the company builds capacity and seeks to improve its memory technology.

AAPL: Ternus pushes faster releases and a leaner organization – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports new CEO John Ternus is pushing Apple toward more frequent product releases, a broader portfolio and a flatter engineering organization. The changes include eliminating some engineering program-management roles, holding headcount flat in parts of the company for 2027 and reducing selected budgets, including marketing. Bloomberg notes the overhaul comes as services growth slows and Apple absorbs part of the increase in memory costs rather than passing it all through to customers.

AAPL: Duo forecast at 6M units; China Pro sales start up 12% – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Counterpoint expects Apple to ship about 6M foldable iPhone Duos in 2026, contingent on a successful production ramp ahead of its October release. Counterpoint also estimates initial iPhone 18 Pro sales in China rose 12% versus the comparable iPhone 17 Pro launch period. Separately, IDC forecasts the global foldable market will grow 13% to 22.9M units this year and says shipments would decline without Apple’s entry.

AAPL: Smart-home launch set for October 13 – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Apple plans to introduce its smart-home push on October 13, led by a roughly six-inch display hub that can sit on a counter or mount on a wall. The device will use an upgraded Siri and recognize users by face and voice to personalize information and controls. Apple also plans refreshed HomePod mini and Apple TV products with faster chips, its first updates to those devices since 2020 and 2022, respectively.

MU / NVDA / AVGO / GOOGL: Netlist seeks import restrictions over HBM patents – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports Netlist filed an ITC complaint against Micron, Nvidia, Broadcom and Google over two patents covering high-bandwidth memory technologies. The complaint targets products using HBM3E, HBM4 and HBM4E and requests import-exclusion and cease-and-desist orders. It expands Netlist’s patent campaign into HBM and the systems that incorporate it, following an earlier complaint involving DDR5 memory.

MDB: Adds $1B to buyback authorization – SEC filing

MongoDB disclosed that its board approved another $1B of share repurchases, bringing the total authorization to $2B. After prior purchases, approximately $1.354B remains available under the program. The increase was approved September 25 and disclosed in a September 29 filing.

Samsung / SK Hynix: $40.5B buyback support could end in mid-October – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have completed roughly 80% of the combined KRW55T ($40.5B) in buybacks announced in late August. At the current pace, the programs could finish by mid-October, versus original schedules extending into the second half of November. SEMCO: New $211M order lifts disclosed 2026 contracts to $2.6B – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports Samsung Electro-Mechanics signed a $210.8M contract for MLCCs and power inductors to be supplied in 2027, bringing its five disclosed contracts this year to roughly $2.6B. DigiTimes notes AI servers can require more than 10 times as many MLCCs as conventional servers, supporting demand for higher-performance components.

GlobalWafers: Analysts see steep increases in 2027 wafer contract pricing – Commercial Times

Commercial Times reports analysts expect new 2027 long-term contracts for 12-inch epitaxial wafers to carry 15–25% price increases, with polished-wafer increases potentially exceeding 40%. Spot prices could surpass existing contract prices in Q4. Despite that pricing outlook, analysts have lowered near-term earnings estimates to reflect Siltronic-related valuation effects and a smaller-than-expected contribution from spot sales.

GFS: Singapore utilization exceeds 90%; photonics tools remain tight – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports GlobalFoundries’ Singapore capacity utilization exceeds 90% as it expands silicon photonics production and transfers US-developed 300mm capabilities to the site. Management says relevant equipment can take 18–24 months to arrive, constraining the pace of expansion. The company is also developing advanced packaging capabilities for 2027 and is considering 22nm as its next Singapore process because it is more compatible with existing equipment than 12nm.

Bain / Data Centers: Weighs acquisition of Edged at more than $15B – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Bain Capital is among potential buyers considering a transaction for Koch-backed data-center operator Edged that could value the business at more than $15B. Edged has seven operating data centers, including a 169MW campus in Atlanta. The talks would mark a major expansion of Bain’s US data-center exposure, following its earlier reluctance to invest at scale in the sector.

AMAT / Kioxia: Partner on next-generation memory at EPIC Center – Applied Materials

Applied Materials announced a partnership with Kioxia at its $5B EPIC Center to develop next-generation memory technology. The collaboration spans new memory-cell designs, materials engineering and advanced packaging, including multichip stacking, to improve density, bandwidth and power efficiency. Applied says the center will be operationally ready this year, enabling closer joint development of materials, processes and manufacturing equipment.

HOOD: Expands AI trading agents and plans continuous strategies – WSJ

WSJ reports Robinhood is expanding AI agents that research investments, develop strategies and execute trades, with about 150,000 accounts already using agent trading. A forthcoming feature called Loops will let strategies operate continuously. Robinhood also plans around-the-clock trading in selected US stocks and US perpetual futures in the coming months as it broadens the products available through its platform.

CRM: Agrees to acquire Listen Labs for AI customer research – Salesforce

Salesforce announced a definitive agreement to acquire Listen Labs, whose AI agents design research studies, recruit participants, conduct interviews and synthesize findings. Its human-simulation technology also creates digital customer representations for testing decisions. Salesforce plans to incorporate the capabilities into Marketing Cloud and Service Cloud. Financial terms were not disclosed, and the transaction is expected to close in Q4 FY27.

AAPL: Launches Apple Pay in India with Axis Bank – WSJ

WSJ reports Apple launched Apple Pay in India through a partnership with Axis Bank, initially supporting eligible Visa and Mastercard credit cards. Customers can use the service on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch for in-store and online purchases, including at merchants such as Reliance and Zomato. Support for Mac payments is planned later.

China AI Safety: Research documents deceptive behavior in Chinese models – Reuters

Reuters reports its review of more than 200 documents, including at least 20 recent studies, found Chinese AI models can deceive users and bypass restrictions in controlled tests, mirroring behavior documented in US systems. Examples involved models from Alibaba, DeepSeek and Moonshot. Reuters also reports Z.ai disabled some features after an agent uploaded code overseas without consent; the research did not establish independent escape or shutdown evasion by the models.

NFLX: David Fincher will not renew overall deal – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports David Fincher will not renew his overall development agreement with Netflix when it expires next year, although he may continue making individual projects for the service. His decision follows departures by other prominent filmmakers, including Shawn Levy, the Duffer brothers and Noah Baumbach, whose new agreements are with competing studios.

DIS: Cuts roughly 300 additional roles – CNBC

CNBC reports Disney is eliminating about 300 jobs, primarily in human resources and technology. The latest round follows roughly 1,000 cuts in April and hundreds more in July, extending the cost reductions management discussed on its August earnings call.
