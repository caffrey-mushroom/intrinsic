Agenda

  • Flint Group's supply chain operations and ink portfolio performance
  • Volume trends, technological shifts and demand drivers across paper and packaging inks
  • Plant network inefficiencies, footprint constraints and long-term CAPEX requirements
  • Raw material cost pass-through dynamics, customer contract structures and stickiness

Key Insights

  • Flint is pivoting away from declining publication print towards higher-margin, lower-volume packaging and digital printing. Although, expansion into full "one-stop shop" capabilities, such as adhesives, requires further M&A
  • Flint's primary moat is high-touch technical support and bespoke formulations, such as Coca-Cola Red. Extreme barrier switching – especially in regulated food and pharmaceutical markets where re-qualification can take several years – ensures high stickiness, but limits flexibility
  • Printing plants' capacity utilisation is c70%, with ageing assets demanding increasing maintenance CAPEX. Re-qualification risks complicate plant consolidation, making a secondary PE sale or strategic breakup the most plausible eventual outcome
  • Ink inclusion in final packaging is only 2-8% of total cost, meaning that converters prioritise negotiating films and foils over inks, giving Flint surprising pricing power in cost pass-through conversations
  • Operational efficiency and acquisition integrations remain hindered by no unified enterprise resource planning and resistance to digital upgrades from Flint's management. Expert believes this is the biggest risk to future growth
