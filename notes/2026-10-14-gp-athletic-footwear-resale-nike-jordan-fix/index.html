SUMMARY

Overview

A UK footwear reseller described a severe contraction in sneaker resale, forcing the business to pivot toward conventional retail accounts and apparel. Nike, Jordan and Adidas have lost resale relevance because of oversupply, repeated reissues, discounting and weak product innovation. Consumers increasingly prioritize apparel, comfort, versatility and value rather than collectible footwear.

Key Insights

The expert believes UK mass-market sneaker culture has effectively disappeared and may remain weak for three to five years. Jordan’s problems are viewed as structural rather than temporary. Older collectors feel alienated by repeated releases that reduced collection values, while younger consumers lack nostalgia for the brand. Restoring scarcity alone is unlikely to revive Jordan. The expert cited weak quality, high prices and limited versatility as additional disadvantages. Product innovation, rather than marketing or archive-driven releases, was identified as the only credible recovery path for major brands. Apparel now anchors purchasing decisions, while footwear has become a secondary consideration. Better apparel margins are also prompting retailers to allocate less space to shoes. Saucony is the only brand consistently generating meaningful resale activity for the expert. Kobe footwear was considered the most dependable product for full-price sell-through.
