Agenda

  • Gatwick Airport's GBP 2.2bn multi-year capital expenditure programme, spanning runway realignment, taxiway alterations and terminal capacity upgrades
  • Potential challenges around disruption runway operations and airline resistance over increased landing charges
  • Risks of dilution in short-term shareholder returns through CAA (Civil Aviation Authority) allowances and environmental caps

Key Insights

  • Gatwick's H1 FY26 revenues and EBITDA grew despite falling passenger numbers, proving fewer passengers spending more effectively validates a "value over volume" strategy
  • GBP 2.2bn Northern Runway project shifts existing emergency runway just 12 metres north, entirely within airport perimeter, making it far simpler than comparable UK infrastructure projects
  • On PE buyer-EasyJet negotiation, "EasyJet will also be losing out of disposing some airport slots that are highly valuable […] Those will be filled very quickly. In a way, EasyJet and [PE buyer] have much more to lose than Gatwick Airport"
  • Seven new airlines joined Gatwick in H1 2026, including Air France returning after 20 years away – a significant commercial win likely facilitated by owner Vinci's French corporate relationships
  • On UK macro risks, deteriorating British fiscal conditions and political instability are single biggest threats to Gatwick's credit spread widening vs sovereign bonds over next five years
