Data Centers: San Francisco adopts immediate 45-day development moratorium – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports San Francisco unanimously approved an immediate 45-day ban on new data centers while considering longer-term restrictions. The moratorium can be extended for roughly two years. Officials cited electricity, water and environmental concerns, with the ordinance’s sponsors specifically targeting a proposed development in Bayview-Hunters Point.

CRWD / Cybersecurity: CrowdStrike links Korean bank breaches to AI-assisted attacker – Reuters

Reuters reports CrowdStrike suspects a China-based attacker used the ARTEX penetration-testing agent and Anthropic’s Claude Code in breaches targeting South Korean banks. At least nine banks have disclosed or been reported as targets since late September; Shinhan exposed data on about 25,000 customers. CrowdStrike says the case shows how one attacker can use AI agents to target many organizations quickly.

MU / Samsung / SK Hynix: Counterpoint pushes back on reports of lower HBM content – EBN

EBN reports Counterpoint’s checks indicate most Nvidia Rubin products are converging on 12-layer HBM, countering speculation that the platform would move to eight layers. The research firm also found no evidence of the four-layer HBM configurations rumored for custom AI chips. Counterpoint says HBM requires more than three times the production capacity of conventional DRAM, keeping pressure on broader memory supply.

BESI / HBM: Hybrid-bonding delays weigh on shares as memory shortages persist – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Besi shares fell 14% this week after UBS cut its rating to sell and BofA moved to neutral. Investors worry memory shortages and the cost of hybrid bonding will delay adoption. SemiAnalysis says Nvidia’s Rubin Ultra will use fewer memory layers, reducing the immediate need for Besi’s tools; Bernstein still expects adoption longer term as memory supply catches up.

NVDA / MSFT: RTX Spark Windows PCs open for orders; shipments start October 16 – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports Nvidia-powered Windows laptops are available for preorder across Microsoft, Dell, HP, Lenovo, ASUS and MSI, with shipments beginning October 16. Microsoft’s Surface Laptop Ultra starts at $2,599, while its $5,999 RTX Spark Dev Box ships in November. The platform combines Nvidia’s Grace CPU and Blackwell GPU with up to 128GB of unified memory; MediaTek helped design the CPU

TSM / ASX / AMKR: Analysts see CoWoS capacity rising 57% in 2027 – China Times

China Times reports analysts expect TSMC’s monthly CoWoS capacity to increase from 140,000 wafers at end-2026 to 220,000 at end-2027. They estimate related outsourced packaging capacity at ASE, SPIL and Amkor will rise from 40,000 to 65,000 wafers. The report also cites forecasts for TSMC capex of $80B in 2027 and $100B in 2028; these are analyst estimates.

SK Hynix / Solidigm: Banks selected for potential $10B US IPO in 2027 – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Solidigm has selected Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley to lead a US IPO next year that could raise roughly $10B. SK Hynix’s SSD subsidiary is also pursuing a pre-IPO funding round and has discussed a valuation of as much as $100B. The planned listing comes as AI infrastructure spending boosts demand for enterprise storage.

TCEHY: Considers up to $5B offshore bond offering – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Tencent is considering raising as much as $5B through dollar and offshore-yuan bonds, potentially as soon as October, after a $4.7B sale in June. The financing discussions come as the company accelerates AI investment: capex in the June quarter rose 176% to RMB52.8B ($7.9B).
