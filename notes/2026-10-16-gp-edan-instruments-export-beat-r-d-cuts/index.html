SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed Edan Instruments across patient monitoring, ECG, maternal-fetal monitoring, ultrasound, and in-vitro diagnostics. Overseas expansion remains central as centralized procurement and pricing pressure constrain China’s medical-device market.

Edan is primarily positioned in developing markets, particularly Southeast Asia, Russia, Latin America, and Africa. Its strongest potential growth products are portable ultrasound and blood-gas analyzers, while expansion in developed markets is limited by its lack of large direct-sales teams.

Key Insights

The expert expects international IVD and ultrasound to be Edan’s main growth engines over the next three to five years. Indonesia is a distinctive competitive advantage because local manufacturing gives Edan access to public tenders with domestic-production requirements. Blood-gas analyzers have gained share rapidly in Russia as Western sanctions created opportunities for Chinese suppliers. Entry-level portable ultrasound offers strong value relative to competing Chinese products, combining comparable functionality with lower pricing. Western Europe and North America are unlikely to become major growth drivers under the current distributor-oriented model. Meaningful expansion would require costly direct-sales infrastructure. Domestic tender activity has not improved materially, while winning prices continue to decline. Price has become the principal procurement criterion. Organizational restructuring improved compliance and lowered costs but weakened some domestic distributor relationships and reduced local sales capacity in developed markets. Currency volatility in emerging markets affects order timing and collections, although US-dollar settlement and industry-wide exposure make the impact manageable.

CS: Hospital procurement today is very different from what it used to be. Before centralized procurement, major hospitals such as Tier II and Tier III hospitals generally used their own budgets to conduct procurement, whereas

township health centers relied on budgets allocated through health authorities. For large hospitals, China began promoting import substitution in medical devices quite some time ago. Before making a purchase, department heads would first prepare a procurement proposal and submit it to the hospital's equipment department.
