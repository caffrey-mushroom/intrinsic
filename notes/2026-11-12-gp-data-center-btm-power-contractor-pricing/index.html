SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, an experienced industrial construction and commissioning professional, discussed how rising data center electricity demand is changing power development. Grid constraints and uncertainty around utility interconnections are prompting developers to consider behind-the-meter generation. The discussion covered Texas and alternative markets, generation technologies, equipment lead times, construction costs, contractors, labor, and natural gas infrastructure.

Key Insights

The expert expects data center development to continue despite potential permitting and policy delays. Texas may see a temporary slowdown, but developers can adapt by building on-site generation or shifting projects to other states. Behind-the-meter power is increasingly practical for large campuses. The expert cited several active campuses, each exceeding 250 MW, planned around this approach. Equipment availability is a major constraint: demand is growing faster than manufacturers can supply specialized turbines and related systems. The expert warned that experienced construction teams are difficult to scale. Splitting strong teams across more projects can dilute expertise and increase schedule and execution risks. Contractors are gaining leverage in negotiations, including on schedules and other contract terms. Developers are increasingly working with contractors to set achievable plans rather than imposing aggressive timelines. The expert favored assigning one lead contractor responsibility for the overall power package to reduce coordination problems and gaps between scopes.

Turbine lead times

Large gas turbines have extended from about 24–30 months to 36–48 months. Some aeroderivative options were described as having roughly 36-month lead times.

Generation overbuild

Typically 10% above anticipated peak load at minimum; often around 20–25%. Actual consumption generally remains below the facility’s full capacity.

Power plant cost

Approximately $1.5–$2 million per MW.

Turbine output

The discussed LM2500 range was approximately 34–37 MW per unit.

Pipeline examples

One project budgeted about $7–8 million for a gas connection; another was budgeted at about $7.5 million. Distances varied by site.

Workforce development

The expert estimated that specialized power-generation workers may need four to five years of experience before working independently.

The expert also noted that battery storage adds equipment and procurement requirements. New turbine concepts and small modular reactors could expand options, but their viability, approvals, and availability remain uncertain.
