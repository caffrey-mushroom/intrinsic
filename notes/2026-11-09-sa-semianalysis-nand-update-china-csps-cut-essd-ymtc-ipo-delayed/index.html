NAND Model Update: China CSP Cut NAND Demand Into 2027 + YMTC IPO Delayed

Author: Sagar Kumathe, Jiwon Jeong, Minjae Kang

Date: Monday, November 9, 2026 (published 2026/11/09 21:32:41 Taipei)

Executive Summary

Alibaba, ByteDance, and Tencent are taking fewer eSSD bits from 4Q26 into 2027, although this

does not change our broader view on NAND. The main driver of this reduction is an inability to

secure enough GPUs to deploy, while spot pricing has dropped to the point that buyers are holding

back on purchasing contracted volumes. DRAM is not affected and is still in severe shortage.

We see this as short-term pain, not a turn in the cycle. China is only ~6% of server NAND

demand. Even after the cut, Chinese hyperscalers have secured far less supply than they need. Our

2027 shortfall only narrows to 5.8% from 6.2%.

US hyperscalers continue to carry NAND demand, with buyers still asking for 2027 bits beyond

what their LTAs cover.

On the supply side, the YMTC IPO delay also slows its next fab ramps, as our model pushes out

Fab 4 and Fab 5 by two quarters. This cuts YMTC’s 2030-bit share to 17.1% from 18.1%, leaving

end-of-decade supply-demand tighter than in our previous model.

China CSPs Reduce Server NAND Demand Into 2027

Recent industry chatter suggests that Chinese hyperscalers, specifically Alibaba, ByteDance, and

Tencent, have lowered their eSSD volumes by up to 10% from 4Q26 into 2027. These are not

cancellations, however, as orders stay in place and only shipments are lowered. One of the CSPs

indicated it wanted to be “more responsible” with its eSSD spend, while Baidu is believed to no longer

entertain NAND LTAs with suppliers. We note that this is a NAND-specific issue, while DRAM remains

in severe shortage and is not seeing a pullback from China to date.

Chinese hyperscalers cite short GPU supply as the direct cause, while market speculation suggests this

demand cut may be a ploy to temper further price increases. Ultimately, storage demand is tied to actual

server deployments, and China’s limited accelerator supply into 2027 remains a key constraint on their

buildout.

Spot price pressure is a secondary factor. As we flagged in our NAND model update last week, spot

pricing has come under pressure, with eSSD spot prices trading below LTA pricing. This marks a

reversal, as China spot prices had been running at over 2x the contract price since 4Q25. Chinese

hyperscalers have historically sourced much of their supply through brokers rather than direct long-term

contracts, and they now appear to be going back to the channel.

Industry chatter suggests some customers are even looking to walk away from their LTAs with YMTC in

favor of buying on the spot market. We believe YMTC’s LTAs carry far looser commitments than those

of Samsung and SK hynix, which makes easier it for customers to exit once spot price dip.

Taking all this into account, we do not read these changes as a sign that Chinese CSPs are oversupplied.

As recently as October, they indicated that LTA coverage for 2026 sat below 80% of end-customer

demand, with 2027 below that, so a cut of up to 10% does not get them near equilibrium. With HDD

largely sold out into 2028, there is little room for HDD to absorb their storage capacity needs once

accelerator capacity becomes available. Our estimates put Chinese hyperscalers at ~6% of server NAND

demand, limiting the impact on the broader market. This cut takes less than 0.5% off total NAND

demand each year, narrowing the 2027 supply shortfall to 5.8% from 6.2% in our previous model. For

4Q26 and 2027, we now expect NAND ASP growth of 10.1% QoQ and 31.0% YoY, respectively, down

from 11.6% and 34.9% in our previous model.

US hyperscalers remain the main pillar of NAND demand, and they seem confident in keeping it that

way. Industry chatter suggests US hyperscalers continue to ask for additional bits in 2027 beyond

what their LTAs cover.

YMTC IPO Delay Impacts Supply Bring-Up

YMTC will likely push out its STAR Market IPO after last week’s CSRC guidance instructing

investment banks to slow AI and semiconductor IPOs. The listing application was filed in late August

with an expected one-to-two-quarter timeline, but the poor market conditions mentioned above make the

timing unfavorable for a mega-cap listing like YMTC.

Per the draft prospectus, YMTC aims to raise 33 billion RMB, essentially equal to its 33.38 billion RMB

of net profit in 1Q26 alone. NAND contract prices rose through 2Q26 and 3Q26, giving YMTC enough

cash on hand for near-term spending. With domestic CSP NAND purchases dropping, there is little

urgency to raise equity to rush capacity additions.

YMTC will likely push its IPO into 2027. Still, building out Fab 4 and Fab 5 to reach 100k wafers per

month each requires north of $20B in capex, which would be hard to fund from operating cash flow

alone. We expect a roughly six-month IPO delay and have pushed Fab 4 and Fab 5 deployment back two

quarters, to 3Q29 and 4Q30, respectively.

We have built these changes into our model. The delay hits 2030 harder than 2029, since that is when

both fabs would ramp together. The push-out takes 7.0% off YMTC’s 2030-bit shipments and a point off

its share, to 17.1%. Revenue falls about 5% in both 2029 and 2030. The market impact is larger than that

of the China CSP demand cut, widening the 2030 shortfall to 1.3% from 0.4%, and we revise our 2030

NAND ASP assumptions up slightly as a result.
