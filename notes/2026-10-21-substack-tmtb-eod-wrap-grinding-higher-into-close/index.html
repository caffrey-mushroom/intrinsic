This is roughly in line with his Oct 15th framing of $100B 2026 exit run rate doubling would be “like $200B ARR exit next year.” Some context on what that implies and what we’re hearing buyside is modeling:
  • The $140/$150 # is 2027 GAAP revenue, not ARR, and 2.6-2.8x ~$54B of 2026 revenue. To put these numbers into

context: From an assumed~$90B of end-2026 ARR (what we model right now), getting there takes ~$7.8-9.4B of new ARR a month (equal monthly adds), for ~$180-198B of ARR by Dec-27. That’s back to 1H26’s ~$8B of new ARR a month, vs. ~$2-3B a month in Aug/Sep (~$5.4B ex one-offs). Right now, we model $132B of 2027 revenue ($167B of Dec-27 ARR), so he’s ~6-15% above us. Something closer to a $100B exit ARR would make the math a lot easier.

  • Still asking around where buyside settling in for 2027 numbers, getting wide ranges anywhere from $115B to $200B

(high end of Anthropic’s Reuters reported plan from August — hopefully they do a better job at setting expectations once public). So let’s call it ~$140B. SOFTWARE

  • Cyber ripped on critical vulnerabilities identified in PHP: ZS +7.8%; OKTA +7.5%; CRWD +5.3%; NET +5% S +3.5%; PANW +2%; FTNT +1.8%. Two critical PHP flaws let attackers run code remotely without logging in; they were found with AI, OpenAI called it one of the most impactful vulnerabilities in recent memory, and the WSJ said AI had found a bug putting up to 70% of the world’s websites at risk. The ELI5 for all us non-technical folks via Claude:

PHP is the behind-the-scenes software that builds a web page each time you load it (your login screen, your shopping cart). It runs about 70% of websites, and the flaw is in PHP itself, not in any one company’s code, so even a well-built site is exposed. A stranger can send a specially crafted request, with no password, and get the website’s computer to run their commands, letting them take it over, steal data, or deface the site.
