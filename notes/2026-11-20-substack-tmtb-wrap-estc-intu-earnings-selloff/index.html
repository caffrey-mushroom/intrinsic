EARNINGS

ESTC -5%: Cloud-Led Beat Overshadowed by an F3Q Air Pocket and an F4Q-Loaded 2H With No Underlying Raise

Positioning negative here going in with LOs underweight and HFs short which is why initial -20%+ reaction only -5% now. Overall, a headline guide-down mgmt tried to brush off as fx and renewal timing — better than feared vs. the bigger miss shorts were playing for but still not good.

F2Q beat (sales-led subscription +18.9% vs. the ~18% bogey, cloud +23% vs. ~20%, record 22.2% OM, EPS $0.94 vs. $0.81), but the Jan-qtr guide missed on every line even ex a $4.5M FX hit (revenue $508M vs. Street $517M), and FY only passed the beat through net of FX. The +1.9% beat on the guide mid trailed the ~2.7% eight-quarter average.

On 2H acceleration, the worry going in, the CFO blamed FX, a self-managed renewal base ~20% heavier in Q4 (implied Q4 sales-led cc growth 19.2% vs. 16.2% in Q3) and caution on Europe and the Middle East: “we just did not want to lean in anymore.” Asked where momentum sits, the CEO said “there is stability even in our Observability growth rates,” but declined solution breakouts, and quarterly cloud disclosure ends in FY28.

On the callback, mgmt cautioned against extrapolating the F4Q exit rate (~19% cc SLS) into FY28. That undercuts the math bulls want to use ahead of the FY28 guide, where the exit rate becomes next year’s run-rate. Tone was otherwise confident. Pipeline continues to progress and renewals are improving across all geos, and sales productivity is strong in the Americas and global public sector. Europe prudence is mainly FX, but also geopolitical uncertainty (Germany and France were cited) and softer European sales productivity, though deal flow remains healthy. cRPO should keep tracking ~20% with some quarterly variability. Hiring is stable, with scope to accelerate in the Americas and public sector given stronger productivity. Mgmt sees HoneyHive’s traces, evals, guardrails and agent memory as differentiated when paired with the observability and security portfolio. Post-call, most read the renewal weighting as a messaging miss rather than a fundamental change.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

The bull thesis is that ESTC owns one of the few scaled, independent search databases just as enterprise AI shifts from chatbots to agents that take action. Agents need accurate, permission-aware retrieval over messy enterprise data, which makes that the critical layer of the stack. Elasticsearch now spans documents, vectors and a columnar store for time series under one query language. With Jina embedding and reranking models, hybrid retrieval and the new Context Engine, ESTC can monetize the shift first through compute consumption and over time through tokens. Bulls see three durable advantages. The first is an open-source footprint that makes Elasticsearch a developer default and keeps acquisition costs low. The second is a single datastore with resource-based pricing: customers spend the same ECUs/ERUs on search, security or observability, so cross-sell is nearly frictionless and one AI workload pulls in others. The third is deployment flexibility across cloud, self-managed, sovereign and air-gapped environments. That grows more valuable as AI raises data-sovereignty and regulatory requirements for governments, banks and European telcos. Security is the near-term engine. AI-enabled attackers are shortening the time between finding a vulnerability and exploiting it, which pushes large enterprises to consolidate onto next-gen SIEMs that can ingest any telemetry at scale, and ESTC is winning those displacements.

This quarter gave bulls their best demand evidence in some time. Cloud accelerated three points to 23% with annual Cloud at ~30%, and consumption was broad across every region and customer group. SLS reached 19.1% cc, within a point of the FY29 target. AI use in the >$100K base climbed to 40% from 21% at FY25, and Security landed an 8-figure SIEM consolidation. Profitability also inflected, with a record 22.2% OM, the first sustainable GAAP operating profit and a fresh $500M buyback. Bulls read the guide as deliberate conservatism rather than a demand signal. Mgmt “couldn’t have been happier” with consumption and openly risk-adjusts F3Q and F4Q. The F3Q gap to Street is only ~1% ex-FX, and implied F4Q revenue matches where Street sat before the print.

The bear thesis is that ESTC is a structurally mid-teens grower whose reacceleration keeps getting pushed out, and that AI is as much a threat to the core as a tailwind. Vector search and retrieval are commoditizing. Postgres (pgvector), MongoDB, Snowflake, Databricks and every hyperscaler now bundle them, and agent frameworks and larger context windows reduce the need for a dedicated search tier. AWS-backed OpenSearch also caps pricing on the core engine. In Observability, ESTC remains a logs specialist competing against Datadog’s breadth, Grafana/Prometheus and cheaper ClickHouse-based stacks, and metrics, APM and RUM are catch-up efforts where incumbents are entrenched. Security is strong today, but part of that strength reflects a migration wave triggered by incumbent disruption (Cisco’s purchase of Splunk, IBM’s exit from QRadar SaaS). CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Microsoft and Google are all chasing the same next-gen SIEM consolidation as comps toughen. Financially, bears argue the “cheap on FCF” framing ignores SBC of ~16% of revenue. SBC-burdened FCF margin is only ~5% and GAAP OM is ~4%. The cumulative $435M of buybacks has mostly offset dilution (basic shares roughly flat y/y) rather than shrinking the count. Execution has also been uneven, with past sales reorganizations and an F1Q27 restructuring charge. The pivot to tuck-in M&A (Deductive AI, HoneyHive) adds integration risk to a historically organic, product-led model.

This quarter supports the bear case mostly on visibility and credibility. F3Q revenue was guided ~2% below Street (~1% ex-FX), and the FY guide only passed through the beat. The F4Q ramp (a ~300bp cc swing) now depends on a larger pool of self-managed renewals closing in a shorter quarter, in a year mgmt had framed as a steady acceleration. The renewal weighting surfaced three weeks into F3Q rather than with the FY guide. Leading indicators did not confirm the revenue acceleration. cRPO slipped to 19.6% cc and missed Street, RPO growth fell to 24% cc from 26.5%, and >$100K net adds dropped to an eight-quarter low. NER stayed at 111% even though in August mgmt expected it to improve. Mgmt is incrementally cautious on Europe (including softer sales productivity), told callers not to extrapolate F4Q into FY28, and still says Observability has “more work to do.” The FY margin guide implies a ~140bp 2H step-down vs. the prior plan, and FCF missed. Dropping quarterly Cloud disclosure right after Cloud’s best quarter also reduces transparency on the most volatile, consumption-driven line.

INTU -10%: Another miss as Credit Karma AI discovery drives FY rev cut and soft Q2 guide; quickbooks and margin beat only bright spots.

Set up going in was washed out, but not much for bulls to like in this print. Wait, are there any bulls here? We don’t hear of many but this was worse than expected and will continue to feed the AI disintermediation bear thesis.

FQ1 beat on revenue, GBS and EPS, but a CK miss drove a $173M cut to the FY27 revenue midpoint slowing to 9% (from 17%) on rates and AI-led discovery, cutting FY CK to 3-7% (from 11-13%); the FQ2 guide landed ~3%/14% below Street on revenue/EPS, and INTU agreed to sell Mailchimp for $3.68B (entirety of proceeds used for buybacks). Asked cyclical vs. structural, the CEO said “the impact was faster than we anticipated, and we expect to be in a better place in the coming months” without sizing it.

Bulls will point to F1Q OI of $1.046B (24.0% OM) beating Street by $138M, FY OI and EPS held so the Jan-qtr profit miss reads as phasing (2H OI implied ~$56M above Street at the mid), Quickbooks beat and IES Online Ecosystem revenue +41%, GBS’s 13-14% FY guide untouched, and $3.31B of Mailchimp cash (~4.5% of the market cap at $279) going to buybacks at ~12x FY27 EPS.

Bears will lean on Credit Karma’s FY guide cut to +3-7% on rates and discovery moving to AI, a Jan-qtr guide of +6-7% (Street +9.9%) with OI ~15% under Street, Online Ecosystem’s 5th straight decel to its first sub-20% quarter, and a ~$3.7B Mailchimp exit on a ~$12B 2021 purchase.

Key Takeways

  • Callback. The 3–7% CK range was built mechanically: the low end assumes today’s environment persists with no GEO investment and no change in rates, while the high end assumes only that GEO and content work, as already done for TurboTax and QuickBooks, improves placement over a matter of months, again with no rate relief. Mgmt sees placement as fixable but the behavior shift as harder, since members see and respond to fewer notifications as AI tools filter inboxes and go straight to LLMs when they need a card or loan; the response is repositioning CK as a personal finance assistant, driving more frequent engagement and leaning on differentiators like security and lender-driven approval odds. Whether AEO pressure could spread beyond CK was a central investor question; mgmt’s on-call position is that the impact is “very limited to Credit Karma.”
  • AI. Mgmt drew a sharp line between CK and its other franchises: TurboTax is the most-cited tax brand in LLM answers (ranked first ~4x as often as the next brand) and QuickBooks the most-cited SMB finance brand. QuickBooks now runs inside Claude, ChatGPT and Perplexity, with new launches in Meta’s Muse for Small Business and Microsoft Copilot for finance; >30% of LLM-connected QuickBooks customers engage weekly, calls exceed 2M per week, connected customers show higher payroll, payments and bill pay adoption and more upgrades, and invoicing through the connector reached a >$1B annualized TPV run-rate since June (Investor Day cited only 80% m/m growth from June to July). Inside the platform, AI-native capabilities drive 75% of IES engagement, customers save ~30% of manual time, and Agentic Books Close and ProPartner (early 2027) extend automation to the 430K accountants on IAS. The bear counter is that CK is the first reported case of AI disintermediation at INTU, and mgmt conceded it came faster than anticipated.
  • Credit Karma. CK grew 9% to $708M (last q +16.5%) vs. Street $720M (+10.6%), with personal loans and insurance partly offset by credit cards, which fell ~$16M y/y after growing ~$14M in Q4. Mgmt blamed two forces that hit late in the quarter: higher-for-longer rates slowing demand and partner spend (cyclical), and a faster-than-expected shift of card and loan discovery into LLMs, where CK barely appears because it never relied on search (<1% of traffic) and its email/push engagement engine is increasingly filtered by AI tools. The FY27 CK guide was cut to +3–7% ($2.72–2.83B) from +11–13%, ~6% below Street $2.94B, implying only ~4% growth over the remaining nine months (~1% at the low end), and Q2 assumes current demand persists. This is incremental vs. Investor Day two months ago, when mgmt pitched CK as the consumer “front door” and presented its in-app, “behind a firewall” model as a brand-building opportunity rather than a near-term revenue risk; mgmt now plans to port the TurboTax/QuickBooks GEO playbook to CK and expects to be “in a better place in the coming months,” backed by a brand relaunch as an AI-powered daily financial platform and a favorable partner rate adjustment in personal loans.
  • Guide. The FQ2 revenue guide of $4.931–4.954B (+6.3% y/y at the midpoint) is ~3% below Street $5.11B (+9.9%), and EPS of $3.17–3.22 (+17% y/y) is ~14% below Street $3.73, with OI of $1.13–1.15B (23.1% OM) vs. Street $1.34B (26.1%). Mgmt cited four drivers, three it called “on strategy”: a heavier early-season mix of price-sensitive filers on free offerings (lower tax ARPC), in-house refund-transfer processing that shifts ~$30M of revenue from Q2 to Q3, and desktop going ~flat against +10% in the year-ago Q2; the fourth is CK. FY27 revenue was cut to $23.081–23.365B (+8.3% at the midpoint, $173M lower) from +9–10%, vs. Street $23.41B (+9.1%), entirely on CK, while GBS (+13–14%), TurboTax (+2–3%), ProTax and Mailchimp were held, as were non-GAAP OI ($8.063–8.145B, +17–18%) and EPS ($22.88–23.12, +23–24% vs. Street $22.90–23.01). The guide implies 2H revenue accelerates to +7.9% from +6.3% in Q2, largely on TurboTax. It is INTU’s first Q1 revenue cut since FY23 (also CK-driven) and the second top-line reset since August’s below-Street FY27 guide.
  • Pricing. Price was the largest GBS growth driver (ahead of mix and customer growth), with an effective uplift north of 20% on the QBO SKUs that took increases in August, and retention is 83%, flat-to-up y/y (in the 90s excluding business failures), with churn “within control limits.” Mgmt sees further pricing room as AI deepens usage but says its three-year targets assume pricing conservatively. Customer growth remains the soft spot: mgmt acknowledged more work to do on QBO customer adds, with Free/Lite helping only gradually (of July–August Free/Lite sign-ups that upgraded, ~80% did so within 30 days, and connecting a bank or sending an invoice makes an upgrade 9x/4x likelier). In consumer, INTU is pricing the other way, with free federal and $15 state filing in CK Tax and a “disruptive” price point for the new Sign and File.

Bull vs Bear Debate

Bulls argue the market is pricing INTU as an AI loser when its core asset, QuickBooks, is exactly the kind of system of record and action that AI agents need to call into. QuickBooks sits on the ledger, payroll, payments, bill pay and working-capital data of millions of SMBs and is reinforced by an accountant network (430K accountants on IAS; 65% of core and 70%+ of mid-market customers use an accountant) that drives switching costs, referrals and new IES deals. The long-term engines are the “big bets,” roughly 30% of revenue and each compounding at 30%+ since FY23: mid-market (QBO Advanced and IES in a ~$90B TAM, now +41%), money (payments, bill pay, BNPL and capital, with TPV +33%) and assisted tax (~88% of the tax TAM, with TT Live +37% in FY26). Bulls see durable advantages in proprietary financial data, compliance-grade accuracy and human accountability that a general-purpose chatbot cannot absorb (Sign and File is expert accountability at AI cost), proven pricing power in QuickBooks, and a cost base that AI and the May restructuring are flattening, all against a >$300B TAM that is only ~7% penetrated.

This quarter gave bulls real evidence on the QuickBooks debate: GBS beat the Street by ~2 points and the buyside bogey by 3–4 points, mid-market accelerated for a second straight quarter, retention held at 83% through August’s price increases, and the first LLM connector data (2M+ weekly calls, higher attach and upgrade rates, a >$1B TPV run-rate) suggests AI is a distribution channel for QuickBooks rather than a substitute. Bulls frame CK as ~12% of revenue with a partly cyclical rate problem and a placement problem INTU has already solved for TurboTax and QuickBooks, and CK is already available inside ChatGPT and Claude through connectors; because the 3% low end of the CK guide assumes no fix and no rate relief, they see room for 2H upside if GEO work lands or rates ease. They also point to the resilience of the EPS algorithm: opex fell y/y, FY27 OI and EPS held through a $173M revenue cut, and INTU is retiring ~5% of its shares a year. Estimates and the multiple could move higher on a strong tax season (CK Tax pulling in new-to-franchise filers, Sign and File and a ~30% larger local footprint lifting assisted share, with IRS Direct File shelved since the 2026 season), CK reacceleration, SBC trending toward 8% of revenue, and the Mailchimp exit, which improves the growth mix, simplifies the GBS story and funds $3.3B of extra buybacks at ~12x earnings.

Bears argue INTU’s moats were built on distribution and guided help, and that AI is attacking both at once. TurboTax DIY depended on brand, search-led acquisition and a step-by-step interview that LLMs can increasingly replicate for simple filers; INTU already lost price-sensitive filers last season, conceded it “lost on price,” and is now cutting DIY ARPC with free CK Tax. CK is a lead-generation marketplace whose value comes from being present at the moment of intent, and if that moment moves into assistants like ChatGPT, which launched an account-linked personal finance experience in May and says 200M+ people ask it financial questions each month, CK risks becoming a supplier of offers into someone else’s interface, with less traffic and weaker take rates. In SMB, bears worry that AI-native bookkeeping and agentic accounting tools commoditize the software layer over time, and they note QuickBooks growth is increasingly price-led while customer growth lags (online paying customers grew only 3% in FY26). Layer on a reset year with many moving parts (DIY price cuts, CK Tax, assisted expansion, a 17% workforce reduction, a Mailchimp sale at a ~$8B loss, a new non-GAAP definition and a securities class action after May’s drop), and bears see a franchise whose terminal value is less certain than a 12x multiple implies, with the stock ~65% below its mid-2025 peak near $800 for a reason.

This quarter handed bears their first reported evidence of AI disintermediation: CK slowed from 16.5% to 9% in a single quarter, mgmt admitted the shift came faster than it anticipated, and FY27 revenue was cut for the second time in three months, the first Q1 cut since FY23. Bears think the bull case is too optimistic in assuming a quick GEO fix, because the bigger problem is behavioral (AI filtering the notifications that drove CK engagement) and because even good placement inside an LLM may not restore CK’s economics. The consumer algorithm also looks more fragile: FY27 Consumer at +2–4% sits below the 4–8% three-year floor set in September, the CAGR now rests on assisted tax just as TT Live slows from +37% to mid-teens, and the acquire-then-monetize logic behind the DIY price cuts relied on CK to monetize free filers. Bears also question earnings quality, since FY27 EPS was held mainly by lower SBC, part of the Q1 margin beat looks like marketing timing that reverses in Q2, gross margin fell ~150 bps on money mix, and Mailchimp will dilute EPS once guidance is reset. Estimates and the multiple could compress further if tax season disappoints on share or ARPC, CK keeps decelerating, AEO pressure shows up in TurboTax DIY, or QuickBooks price-led growth fades without a pickup in customer adds.
