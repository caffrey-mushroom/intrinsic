SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed preliminary RAINFALL-02 results for RENA-S, an investigational folate receptor-targeting antibody-drug conjugate in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. The expert viewed the reported survival benefit as unusually strong, while emphasizing that full data, biomarker subgroups, safety tables, and prior mirvetuximab outcomes remain unavailable.

The call also covered treatment sequencing, folate receptor testing, emerging ADCs, and a mutation-specific p53 modulator.

Key Insights

The expert characterized RENA-S as potentially transformative and expects it to become the preferred initial treatment after platinum resistance if the full results confirm the press release. Survival efficacy, rather than biomarker independence alone, was considered the main reason for likely early adoption. Broad activity across folate-expression levels would expand access and potentially eliminate a companion diagnostic. High folate expression may produce deeper and more durable benefit, but low or negative expressors could still outperform conventional chemotherapy. RENA-S could precede mirvetuximab, with mirvetuximab used after progression in eligible high expressors. Prior mirvetuximab exposure within RAINFALL-02 will provide important evidence about sequential ADC use. If RENA-S becomes standard care, it may create a difficult new control-arm benchmark for future trials. The expert estimated that 80%–85% of patients in the expert’s practice receive another platinum-resistant treatment line, supporting continued demand for post-RENA-S options.
