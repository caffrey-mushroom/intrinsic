SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, who works in enterprise success at an AI company, discussed how forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) help businesses implement AI, and compared Mistral’s platform with Palantir’s Foundry. The conversation also covered data sovereignty, partnerships, pricing, and how AI may change enterprise software and implementation work.

Key Insights

FDEs are especially valuable to traditional businesses that lack in-house AI expertise. They help with infrastructure, tailored applications, and specialized scientific work. AI-native firms are less likely to need this support. FDEs can accelerate early deployments and train customer teams to take over routine work. Expert-led applied science projects may still require vendor specialists. AI can substantially reduce documentation, project coordination, and data-migration effort. However, understanding a customer’s needs and navigating internal decision-making remain difficult to automate. The expert sees Palantir’s structured data ontology as a strength when governance and reliable outputs matter, particularly in regulated settings. Mistral’s more flexible approach may be easier to adapt, but can require more initial work and produce weaker early results. European data sovereignty concerns may create opportunities for Mistral and challenges for Palantir, although national relationships and perceptions vary. Palantir’s established deployments and switching costs remain meaningful advantages.
