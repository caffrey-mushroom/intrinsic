Agenda

  • Devoteam's market positioning and competitive differentiation across European IT consulting
  • Commercial execution and client adoption momentum across primary cloud hyperscaler partnerships
  • Customer demand dynamics across enterprise and mid-market clients
  • AI impacts on delivery model, offerings and potential revenue cannibalisation risks

Key Insights

  • Devoteam's 2025 decline was driven by Broadcom's massive VMware product price increases, which forced enterprises to redirect budgets from T&M consulting towards licensing, disproportionately hurting Devoteam vs fixed-price competitors such as Capgemini. Switching from T&M to fixed-price contracts will be difficult for Devoteam, as it requires maintaining bench capacity and restructuring how talent is deployed and presented to clients
  • Most large French enterprises lack the data infrastructure and budget foresight to execute meaningful AI transformations, meaning current AI projects remain small quick-wins rather than large-scale deployments
  • Devoteam's recent AI expert additions are unlikely to increase daily rates, as customers are actively pushing for lower pricing, arguing AI makes consultants faster. Moreover, expert considers it to be "very difficult to save cost in Devoteam"
  • Data sovereignty is considered an underappreciated upcoming growth driver, where Devoteam already holds strong credentials across sovereign cloud providers such as Scaleway and OVH alongside hyperscalers
