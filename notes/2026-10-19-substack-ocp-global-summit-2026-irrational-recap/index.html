1] NPO // Open CPX

Last OCP (2025) the hottest topic was CPO. This year it was NPO, in the Open CPX format.

https://www.opencpxmsa.org/wp-content/uploads/OpenCPX-Specification-v1.0.pdf

Official spec is 78 pages and quite boring. Will cover it here for the section intro. Much more useful info was in OCP talks and expo show floor.

I like to think of this in terms of OSFP (8-lane) transceiver consolidation. So Open CPX is equivalent to 4 or 4.5 legacy transceivers. Not sure why the 4.5 option exists but whatever.

Type 1 is true NPO (connectors on package substrate)

Type 2 is lame NPO (connectors on PCB)

There is an option for integrated and external laser. I have a lot of opinions on this… but must contain myself a little.

For now just note the integrated laser version is at least 9 mm longer and at most 34 mm longer than the external laser version.

These connectors are so fucking expensive. I just know it from experience.

Standard coolant temp worst case is 50 C so makes sense for worst case to be 52 C.

40W worst case is pretty trash. Implies pj/bit of 5.6 to 6.3.

Personally I care about die-die (aka bump-bump) loss but I understand why standards orgs include die to socket spec. Margin of Open CPX is reduced by 4 dB compared to standard C2M style specs for regular/legacy transceivers.

You know how that 4 dB gap is closed? Semtech linear equalizers inside the CPX module. :)

My view is the most common split ration will be 1:8 (3x) for both ILS and ELS based systems.

One fiber/laser split into 8 lanes of 200G on the PIC using a 3-stage athermal MZI splitter with 3.5 dB insertion loss per stage.

These supply noise specs are kinda terrible. Places a large burden on the CPX module internal power tree to clean up the crap it receives from the host. Makes PCB real-estate inside the CPX module precious.

1.b] INTEGRATED LASER NPO IS A TERRIBLE IDEA

It is well established by public data that both CPO (external laser) and traditional transceiver failures (link flap + device dead combined) come from lasers.

In the Meta+Broadcom large scale CPO trial, the most common failure (link flap) mode was in the ELS electrical driver. Lasers are extrodinarily sensitive to current ripple and will easily mode hop if something polutes the driver.

Last year OCP had excellent data on this exact topic for traditional tranceivers.

In my modeling, I see only the need for 4x discrete DFB in the ILS CPX, each of which are split x8 (3 stage splitter) on the PIC to get to the 32 lane density.

Four extra lasers means one cold spare for each active laser. These guys know the lasers are going to die and literally admitted this to me inadvertently.

There is a trick behind this. You are probably familiar with MZI modulators. The thing is, MZI is just a basic photonic structure. You can make lots of things with them. Filters and switches for example.

An MZI always has two inputs and two outputs. It’s just that in most cases, these are tied together. For a modulator, both inputs and both outputs are tied together respectivly.

For a switch or de-mux filter, you tie only one end together.

This failover MZI switch has maybe an insertion loss of 0.5 dB and trivial amount of heater/control tuning power draw. Its a clever trick to help with FIT but does nothing to help with link-flaps due to mode hop risk.

Also, this strategy makes the PIC much taller than it needs to be and requires double the active alignments, lenses, and isolators. Drives up cost a lot beyond the obvious “we need double the lasers now”.

Overall I remain extremely bearish against integrated laser NPO/CPO.

OCI-MSA does not mandate a particular electrical channel or config. Spec is designed with ring modulators in mind but if you want to be stupid you can try to use MZI lol.

Guys… OCI is slow and wide. You are never going to run 400G direct-drive CPO. Literally opposite of your own roadmap…?

Electrically, I think 400G PAM4 is a non-starter. PAM6 is possible but my view is the cowards in standards bodies should just suck it up and goto PAM8. Trade the requirement for magic materials and connectors for a much heavier FEC to compensate for SNR penalty.

OCI gen2 doubles modulation speed while keeping wavelength the same. Gen3 almost certainly doubles wavelengths and moves to a 200GHz grid. This is my guess nothing is official.

4] Slow-and-Wide

Slow-and-wide is defined as anything slower than 112G PAM4.

From an electrical perspective, I am heavily biased towards 32-64Gbps per lane.
