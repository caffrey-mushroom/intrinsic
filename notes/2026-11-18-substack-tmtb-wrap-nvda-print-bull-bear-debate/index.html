NVDA +3%: Clean beat and a Jan-qtr guide at the top of the buyside bogeys with biggest beat since 3Q’25, FY28 Guide raised to 75%, but open questions remain

Overall, investor reaction was mixed after last night. On the positive: Colette did a solid enough job with her commentary especially around Neos (expansive to “rigorously evaluating”), financing, Rubin and pricing power (“we got this”); the quarter and guide raise were good and helps de-risk CY27; all excess FCF will given back to shareholders; and buyside numbers are going up. On FY27’s ~$424B (F1Q-F3Q plus the $130B Jan-qtr mid), that’s ~$741B vs. ~$712-717B implied by the bogeys. The FY28 guide implies close to $17 of EPS at 72-73% FY28 GM, which is better than street at $16 but a tad light of where buyside is at $18, but assuming average cadence of beats, buyside likely going up to $18+.

On the flip side, the open CEO position is still a big question mark, along with the ramifications of Jensen leaving. More importantly, the real question on NVDA (and the broader AI semi trade) is CY28 not CY27 so the better guide doesn’t do much to answer that. In addition. still the open question of what the actual “indirect” China SEA exposure is.

Overall, we thought the print was better than expected and a lot is priced in at 15x a 50% haircut to CY28 #s that doesn’t price in any buybacks (5-6% of mkt cap a year), but the open questions still remain which are keeping LOs on the sidelines. What to do with the stock here and now is an open question we don’t have a great answer to.

Key Takeaways

  • Demand. Cloud industry backlog rose to $2.5T from $2.0T in three months, NVDA compute is fully utilized at every hyperscaler and neocloud it serves, and Kress said customers spent the past week asking for more supply. Rubin did nearly $20B in its first production quarter (~19% of DC vs. the ~20% flagged in August), NVDA’s fastest ramp ever, with hyperscalers reaching production in about a month, half Grace Blackwell’s time. The AI datapoints were unusually specific: frontier labs ~15% of DC revenue this year (roughly flat vs. summer disclosures), Anthropic at $81B of annualized revenue, OpenRouter weekly tokens +35x y/y with agentic workloads ~80% of tokens, and token prices down ~60% since May pushing GPU rental rates up across generations.
  • Callback. Post-call, mgmt pointed to an accelerating Blackwell-to-Rubin transition, with Rubin growing in JanQ in both dollar and percentage terms, which is pulling some B300 unit estimates lower. Mgmt also framed NVDA’s neocloud support as becoming more marginal over time as larger players fund themselves through investment-grade-backed contracts and prepays, said talks with all six partners on the ~$500B third-party financing platform are ongoing, and characterized the risk from guarantees to the buyback as low.
  • Pricing. Asked whether NVDA would accept margin compression as frontier labs lean into ASICs, Kress gave no long-term commitment beyond “our pricing is well thought through, but that is something for the future,” and stressed that every customer gets the same price regardless of allocation. The supportive evidence sits in the rental market (Hopper recontracting at up to 2x its original price, B300 rates up 60%+) and in blended ASPs rising as memory costs are passed through. Mgmt did not address AMD or specific ASIC programs; the one relevant move was extending NVLink Fusion to custom XPUs through a deeper MediaTek partnership.
  • Mix. Hyperscale ($60.1B, +23% q/q, +71% y/y; 56% of DC) beat Street ~$52.6B by ~$7.5B, while ACIE ($47.5B, +18% q/q, +195% y/y) missed Street ~$49.1B by ~$1.6B and slowed from +25% q/q. That inverts August’s guide (ACIE to drive OctQ, Hyperscale to re-accelerate in JanQ) and appears helped by one large customer classified in Hyperscale (likely SPCX/xAI) growing faster than expected; ACIE’s share of DC slipped to 44% after mgmt said in August it would be roughly half. Concentration is drifting back toward the buyers with the most ASIC options, even as NVDA’s take of their spend sits near lows: ~24% of hyperscaler capex converted to NVDA revenue in the Sept quarter, flat q/q but a two-year low vs. ~32% at the Dec-25 peak.
  • Supply. Supply commitments jumped to $390B from $279B, mostly multi-year memory procurement; with leases, cloud, equity and capex, total commitments are $518B, or ~$668B including neocloud commitments and guarantees (vs. ~$530B last quarter). New substrate (Kinsus, SEMCO) and packaging (SPIL) agreements add to Amkor, IBIDEN, KYEC and Unimicron, supplier prepayments rank as the top use of capital after buybacks, and Kress said memory and advanced packaging are “secured,” with TSMC leadership in NVDA’s offices this week. Inventory rose 26% q/q to $39.7B for Rubin. This underwrites the FY28 raise, but it is also a much larger fixed claim on memory struck near peak pricing.
  • Export controls. Licensed H200 sales to China were $2.4B (~2% of revenue, up from <1% of DC last quarter) and are now zero; the JanQ guide assumes no China DC compute. On BIS’s Nov 16 interim rule targeting Chinese remote access to advanced compute (new licensing for shipments to, and DC operators in, Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil), mgmt is “still going to spend some time studying” it but expects performance to remain strong because demand far exceeds supply, i.e., any displaced units get reallocated. Exposure to those countries was not quantified. The board also formed a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee of independent directors, outside advisors and counsel.
  • Leadership. The board will not reinstate Jensen and has retained Heidrick & Struggles for an internal/external search; Chairman Jones said the next CEO must navigate “national security dynamics” and won’t be rushed, and no questions were taken on the resignation or the board’s ongoing investigation. Kress described operations as “business as usual,” with customers and suppliers “100% up behind us,” but the 10-Q is delayed to “the coming weeks” (a supplemental document was posted instead), and the government overhang, from reported DOJ/SEC probes to Treasury pressure on export compliance, is unresolved.
  • Capital return. A record $28.1B was returned ($22B buybacks, $6B dividends) on $43.8B of FCF, and mgmt now intends to return “all excess free cash flow, net of strategic uses,” a step up from “50% or more” earlier this year. The $227B remaining authorization (after a record $150B add in September) is to be used by FY28-end, implying ~$45B/qtr, roughly double the OctQ pace and ~6% of market cap. Buybacks are the “super top priority,” followed by supplier prepayments and selective customer guarantees.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bulls see NVDA as the toll collector on the largest infrastructure build in tech history, with a lead that compounds rather than erodes: an annual cadence (Rubin now, Rubin Ultra in 2027, Feynman in 2028) co-designed across GPU, CPU, NVLink and Spectrum-X networking, systems and CUDA software, sold increasingly as full racks and now as pre-integrated clusters. The demand engine is shifting from training to agentic inference, which is far more token-hungry (agents are ~80% of OpenRouter tokens, which grew 35x y/y) and is turning frontier labs into very large businesses (Anthropic at $81B of annualized revenue), while hyperscaler capex is on track to roughly double to ~$875B in CY26 and reach ~$1.3T in CY27. Bulls argue the moats are widening: NVDA has locked up memory, substrates and packaging through decade-end with $390B of supply commitments, which also caps how fast ASIC and AMD programs can scale; networking is now an ~$84B annualized business growing ~158% y/y; Vera adds a server-CPU leg where revenue should more than double in FY28; and NVDA owns the ~44% of DC revenue (neoclouds, sovereigns, enterprise) where custom silicon barely exists.

This quarter was the bull case’s stress test, and it passed. With the founder gone, government probes reported and a new BIS rule landing the same week, interim mgmt raised FY28 to ~$741B (~6% above Street), beat OctQ by ~$7B and guided JanQ ~$5B above Street, which is hard to square with a demand air pocket or customer defections; customers and suppliers are “100% up behind us,” TSMC’s leadership was in the building, and memory and packaging are secured. The GM bridge held (74.9% vs. a 74% guide, a 71–72% trough as old-price POs ship, 72–73% in FY28 on new pricing), and the rental data undercut the useful-life bear case, since Hopper capacity recontracting at up to 2x its original price and B300 rates up 60%+ suggest installed GPUs are earning more over time, not less. Bulls see further upside from supply (the 75% is supply-gated against demand mgmt framed near 100% in August), operating leverage (opex runs ~7% of revenue) and a share count shrinking ~4–5% a year as ~$45B/qtr of buybacks run at ~9x earnings. Add a re-rating once a permanent CEO is named and the export and investigation headlines fade, and bulls argue a stock at ~9x guide-implied FY28 EPS and an ~8–10% FY28 FCF yield is pricing a CY28 cliff that nothing in this guide shows.

Bears see an earnings base that is both peaking and increasingly propped up. NVDA is ~93% data center, and the FY28 guide leans on hyperscaler capex rising another ~50% in CY27 after roughly doubling in CY26, just as the marginal dollar shifts to custom silicon: NVDA’s share of hyperscaler capex has slid from ~32% at the Dec-25 peak to ~24%, and ASICs are expected to overtake merchant GPUs in units by CY28. The labs NVDA has funded (~$50B invested, plus $105B of PORTS-Pike credit support for OpenAI) are the same customers designing competing chips, and growth outside hyperscale leans on neoclouds and labs that, in NVDA’s own disclosure, are “growing faster than their balance sheets and long-term credit profiles can support.” Layered on top is a governance and geopolitical overhang with no near-term resolution: the founder’s exit is entangled with export-control scrutiny (a DOJ criminal probe of Megaspeed, an alleged chip-diversion conduit that NVDA put on its no-sell list in June 2025; no charges have been announced and the agencies have not said whether NVDA is a subject), the board’s own investigation is ongoing, the 10-Q is late, a securities class action has been filed, and BIS has just extended licensing to DC operators across SE Asia and Brazil. Bears also worry that a successor chosen partly to navigate Washington runs a more conservative NVDA on ecosystem financing and non-U.S. exposure, and won’t attract the same talent as Jensen did.

The quarter gave bears more than the headline suggests. ACIE, the segment mgmt said would drive OctQ growth, missed Street by ~$1.6B and slowed to +18% q/q, while the beat came from Hyperscale, the group with the most ASIC alternatives. Financing language turned from expansive to “rigorously evaluating,” neocloud commitments are now explicitly contingent on customers raising their own money, and “customers’ access to capital” became a named FY28 swing factor; meanwhile DSO rose to 64 days on extended terms for large customers, NVDA took a $1.8B impairment on private investments, and commitments plus guarantees rose to ~$668B from ~$530B. JanQ is softer than it looks (~+4% q/q on a 13-week basis), GM falls 340bp on memory that NVDA has now locked in at scale, and Kress declined to commit to long-term pricing when pressed on ASICs. The bull assumptions most at risk are that CY28 grows ~30% off a +75% CY27 (mgmt didn’t reaffirm the ~100% demand framing), that rental rates keep rising as Rubin and Rubin Ultra supply lands. Estimates and the multiple compress if CY28 becomes a digestion year (tighter neocloud financing, land and power bottlenecks, post-midterm policy risk to U.S. data centers), if ASIC share gains force price concessions that push GM into the high-60s, or if export rules or legal outcomes widen.
