Deep LLM: RSI Is the Most Important Variable, and Compute Is the Deepest Moat

Aug 6

Executive Summary Read full story

Competition: The Field Expands from Two Leaders to 4+1

  • When we published our August RSI report, OpenAI and Anthropic dominated frontier model development: at the end of July, they accounted for four of the top five models on the Artificial Analysis index. Three months later, Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon leads version 5 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, introduced on October 27, with Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra close behind. Meta has released Muse Wonder, and SpaceX has acquired xAI and Cursor. In terms of capital, compute and investment in frontier model development, we see the competitive landscape taking shape as a 4+1 field: OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta, plus SpaceX.

OpenAI Is Strong in Research, but Safety Issues Affect Releases

Subscribed

  • OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra on September 3, followed by GPT-6 Sol and Luna, then GPT-6.1 Sol and Terra. More capable models remain unreleased. In September, OpenAI said a model significantly stronger than Astra had produced a proof of finite-time blow-up for the forced Navier–Stokes equations. On September 21, it said the model had also solved more than 100 other long-standing open problems in mathematics (see the section on physical AI, video and science). These results are understood to have come from a model codenamed Bel.- Safety issues have already affected releases. According to The Wall Street Journal, the planned October release of GPT-6.1 Astra was canceled after the model became less reliable at reporting its actions truthfully and respecting limits on authorized tool use. The Journal also reported that a research model contacted an external chatbot via DNS during a September training run, after which OpenAI paused tool-enabled training, evaluation and inference for its most capable models.- The Information and Fortune report that parts of Astra’s architecture use loops, repeating computations within the model without exposing them in a visible chain of thought. This architecture could make Astra harder for outsiders to distill and its reasoning more difficult to monitor. Astra’s system card documents a decline in chain-of-thought monitorability, consistent with this view.

Anthropic’s Opus 5.5 Leads Agent Tasks, but Revenue Growth Slows

  • Anthropic released Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 in late September and Haiku 5.5 in October; access to the more powerful Mythos series remains restricted (see the cybersecurity section). In our hands-on use, Opus 5.5 is far ahead of its peers on multi-step agent tasks and knowledge work. It leads LMArena’s Agent Arena, which uses blind user comparisons, and Anthropic’s launch data put its GDPval-AA score about 300 Elo points above Astra’s. Revenue growth has slowed, however. Anthropic’s S-1 reports first-half revenue of US$16.29 billion, while the Financial Times puts Q3 revenue at about US$16.8 billion, up 45% quarter over quarter.- Price cuts and customers switching models explain part of the Q3 slowdown. Sonnet 5 launched at a price one-third below Sonnet 4.6, while Opus 5 cost about half as much as Fable 5. Enterprises quickly switched to the newer models, reducing API revenue from the same tasks. Opus 5.5 subsequently launched at a list price a further 20% lower, and the S-1 reports a roughly 40% reduction in cost per task. With prices still falling, slower revenue growth does not imply a proportionate slowdown in demand.

Google Returns to the Top Tier; Ad Growth Slows While Cloud Accelerates

  • Google released Gemini 4 Argon on September 30 and Carbon 28 days later. On version 5 of the Artificial Analysis index, Carbon scores 9 points above Argon. The two models appear to share the same pretrained base, with most of the improvement coming from post-training. Google is reported to use model soups to average the weights of multiple post-training branches, accelerating iteration and combining improvements without increasing inference costs. Carbon performs well on long-running tasks, long-context workloads and cybersecurity. In our hands-on use, it still trails Opus 5.5 slightly, ranking between Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5.- Google Search & other revenue grew 12% in Q3, with growth slowing for a second consecutive quarter. Management said AI Mode monetizes at a lower rate. Google Cloud revenue rose 103% to US$30.7 billion, its second consecutive quarter of accelerating growth. Backlog reached US$561 billion, of which management expects to recognize slightly more than 55% as revenue over the next 24 months. Gemini and external TPU sales drove most of the growth. Google’s first-party model APIs process about 32 billion tokens per minute, up roughly 40% quarter over quarter. TPU system sales also contributed significantly, with around 100 customers, including Anthropic. The CFO expects sales to ramp further in Q4 and grow substantially in 2027. TPU demand is understood to exceed supply, with supply-chain constraints limiting availability. Google also said it began using third-party compute toward the end of Q3 because of supply constraints.

Meta’s Muse Wonder Shows Gains Consistent with Scaling Laws

  • After launching its Muse personal agent and Muse Spark 1.3, Meta released Muse Wonder on October 12 at API list prices of $4/$20, calling it the company’s first frontier model. Muse Wonder reportedly has about 3 trillion total parameters; its gains from a larger base model are consistent with scaling laws. The Full version, available only to Muse Code subscribers, reportedly adds a loop to the same base model. On the new Artificial Analysis index, the Full version matches Argon, while the API version scores 7 points lower. On October 15, Meta also released the weights of the 816-billion-parameter Muse Spark Open. Compute requirements are substantial: the Muse agent has more than 10 million users in the US and Canada, but its compute demands have delayed a global launch. Mark Zuckerberg expects cost efficiency to improve more than tenfold within a year and says Meta is already developing models larger than Muse Wonder. Q3 capex was US$34.9 billion, and Meta raised full-year guidance to US$135–145 billion.

SpaceX: Model Capabilities Trail, but Compute Resources Are Strong

  • SpaceX is the “+1.” It acquired xAI in February and completed its acquisition of Cursor in August. Grok 4.7 was trained jointly with Cursor but still trails the other four labs in independent evaluations. Its company-reported Terminal-Bench 4.0 score is 38%, versus about 26% in independent testing. Test configurations can affect results, but the gap suggests that the model may have been heavily optimized for public benchmarks (“bench maxxing”). So far, Cursor’s coding data has not delivered the improvement we expected.- SpaceX has substantial compute capacity. Nameplate compute capacity was 1.7 GW at the end of Q3 and is expected to reach 2.5–3 GW by year-end. SpaceX rents compute to Anthropic and Google; its AI business generated US$7.56 billion in Q3 revenue, exceeding its connectivity business, which consists mainly of Starlink. On October 20, Elon Musk announced that the boards of SpaceX and Tesla had formed special committees to explore a merger; no terms have been announced. Tesla has real-world vehicle and robot data that OpenAI and Anthropic would struggle to obtain at scale. Grok could eventually provide cloud-based remote assistance for Robotaxi and serve as Optimus’s brain. Combined with Tesla’s real-world data, Grok has a chance to build a strong position in physical AI.

Open-Weight Models Fall Further Behind, and Distilling Astra Yields Limited Gains

  • Among models from developers known for open-weight releases, Kimi K3.1 Pro scores highest on the new Artificial Analysis index, 13 points behind Carbon, although its weights have not yet been released. DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, whose weights are public, trails by more. Kimi’s shortfall is particularly clear on knowledge and science tests: it scores 37% on HLE-Diamond, versus 68% for Opus 5.5. It also consumes about 1.7 times the median number of output tokens across the full evaluation suite.- The Information reported on October 28 that leading open-weight developers relied heavily on GPT-6 Astra outputs to train their latest models. Astra’s implicit reasoning may not be fully recoverable from those outputs, making distillation increasingly difficult. Models trained this way also tend to be less token-efficient and perform less well outside benchmark settings. Distillation is becoming a less viable route to catching up at low cost.- The top three labs are closely matched, pushing each to release its next generation faster and driving up compute demand for training and post-training.

Competition and Pricing: Enterprises Shift from Token Maxxing to Token Optimizing

  • Over the past three months, frontier labs have cut prices or launched at low prices to win share. Prices at the top tier remain at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens. The next tier is converging on $4/$20, while Google’s introductory price is half that, at $2/$10. We use this input/output pricing convention throughout. Haiku 5.5 is 90% cheaper than Haiku 4.5. Lower token prices do not always mean lower task costs: across the Artificial Analysis evaluation suite, Opus 5.5 (max) averaged about $10 per task, versus about $6 for Astra (max), despite Astra’s higher list price. The difference reflects token consumption.

Price changes for major frontier models, July–October 2026

Source: company websites and media reports; compiled by FUNDA.

  • Enterprises are shifting from encouraging AI usage to controlling spending. According to The New York Times, Meta and Shopify had used token consumption in employee evaluations, and since June Uber has capped spending on each AI coding tool at $1,500 per employee per month. KPMG’s September survey found that 43% of large US companies had introduced usage or token budgets.- Usage is growing much faster than spending. The effective average price per million tokens in Ramp’s business sample is about 40% below its March peak. In a week in late October, usage was 37% higher than a month earlier, while spending was up only about 9%.- Anthropic’s revenue slowdown therefore does not simply reflect a weaker competitive position. Price cuts, customers switching models, caching, routing, customer budgets and compute availability all affect revenue. Its share of token spending in Ramp’s sample nevertheless rose from 51.3% in the week of September 25 to 60.5% in the week of October 23.- Enterprise adoption also depends on implementation and ongoing support. Colin Jarvis, who leads OpenAI’s forward-deployed engineering team, says about 80% of stalled enterprise AI projects are held up by deployment issues. OpenAI has established DeployCo and announced its acquisition of Tomoro, which has about 150 forward-deployed engineers (FDEs). Companies that combine customer relationships, data platforms and implementation capabilities, such as Microsoft and Palantir, can connect enterprise data, redesign workflows and maintain systems over time, and their position in the value chain is becoming more important. We expect enterprise deployments supported by FDE teams to reach scale in 2027 at the earliest.

RSI Accelerates as AI Takes on More of Its Own R&D

  • The indicators in our August report remain broadly unchanged: release cycles, the interval from pretraining to release, the pace of price cuts, the share of code written by AI and the doubling time of METR’s task-completion time horizon. New evidence comes mainly from the labs’ own research and development, where AI is moving beyond coding assistance to engineering training systems and informing research decisions.

RSI tracking indicators, August report vs. October 2026

Source: FUNDA reports; company releases and public statements; Artificial Analysis; AGI Tracker; compiled by FUNDA.

  • Media reports citing Anthropic’s S-1 describe “closing the loop” as each generation of Claude accelerating the development of its successor. Valued at list prices, Anthropic’s own use of Claude grew more than 250-fold from January to September. This usage is understood to consume more tokens than all external customers combined, making Anthropic its own largest user; research-related inference demand is also rising rapidly. These tokens generate no revenue and are additional to the compute used directly for training.- Anthropic’s published tests show AI contributing to research beyond routine coding. In a training-code optimization task, average speedups rose from about 3x with Opus 4 to about 52x with Mythos Preview; human experts achieved about 4x after 4–8 hours of work. Across 129 research decisions where researchers had taken a wrong turn, the proportion of model suggestions that beat the original decision rose from 51% for Opus 4.5 to 64% for Mythos Preview.- AI is beginning to optimize the software and chips it runs on. In results presented at Hot Chips in August, OpenAI reported that AI-assisted optimization reduced the area of Jalapeño’s matrix multiplication unit by a further 10% relative to an engineer-optimized design. For selected GPT-OSS attention and MoE blocks, AI-optimized implementations ran 1.5–1.8 times as fast as expert-written versions.- In September, Anthropic reported that two biomolecular modeling researchers with no prior kernel-engineering experience used Claude to optimize more than 30 open-source biomolecular models in under four weeks. Their triangle attention kernels ran 2.7–2.9 times as fast as Nvidia’s cuEquivariance implementation on average. Anthropic is also hiring Chip Design RL researchers to train Claude in RTL generation, design verification and physical design optimization. Better computational efficiency allows the same compute to produce more tokens and could also lower inference costs in model development.- Sundar Pichai expects RSI to accelerate Google’s research further over the next 12 months, while OpenAI aims to build an automated AI researcher by March 2028. METR considers Opus 5.5 unlikely to fully automate AI research. Human researchers remain important for choosing research directions and evaluating results. In the four-stage framework of our August report, AI is moving from deep involvement in its own development toward leading experiments.

Foundation Models Underpin Advances in Physical AI, Video and Science

  • Over the past three months, physical AI, video production and science have begun to draw on frontier models’ coding and reasoning capabilities. Better base models, combined with task-specific training, can advance several fields at once. Frontier labs are beginning to apply their advantages in language models to physical-world applications.

Physical AI: Better Models Turn Robot Understanding and Planning into Coding Problems

  • GPT-6 Astra’s launch materials did not mention robotics, yet within days third parties were using it to control robotic arms and perform simple pick-and-place tasks without specialized training on robot actions. Astra ranked first in Artificial Analysis’s October evaluation using real robotic arms. According to people familiar with the work, OpenAI’s pretraining team had not anticipated this capability. We also understand that Astra was trained on about 1 million hours of egocentric, or first-person, video, and that OpenAI has since merged its robotics team into its foundation model team and plans to increase this dataset to more than 10 million hours.- Anthropic is understood to have formed a robotics team, while Moonshot AI and DeepSeek have begun purchasing embodied AI training data. Anthropic has publicly tested Claude on robot dogs, humanoids and robotic arms. According to The Information, it also held acquisition talks with Physical Intelligence this spring, but no deal was reached.- In our view, advances in physical AI are linked in part to models’ emerging 3D spatial understanding. Astra’s ability to perform pick-and-place tasks without robot-specific training suggests that coding, 3D and video capabilities can transfer to physical manipulation. Models can already reconstruct a scene from a photograph using Blender code, then correct errors by rendering, comparing and revising it. First-person video provides experience of hand–object interactions. Anthropic likewise attributes most of Claude’s progress on robot dogs to general model scaling.- With a sufficiently capable model, high-level robot understanding and planning become coding problems. To put a cup in a bowl, for example, the model identifies each object’s position and orientation, writes a sequence of actions, calls control interfaces and corrects errors using camera feedback. Robot systems can be programmed through high-level instructions: large models handle understanding and planning, while dedicated controllers and smaller models manage low-level, high-frequency control.- A large model may still need half an hour to complete a tabletop task, and reliability in fine manipulation and changing environments remains unproven. Continued improvements in LLM infrastructure should lower costs and latency, while further model advances could help address both constraints. Epoch AI estimates that the inference cost of achieving a given score on math and reasoning benchmarks falls by a factor of about 13 per year on average. Distilling these capabilities into smaller models running on the robot can further reduce the overhead of calling large models during operation. Frontier models available by the end of 2027, combined with task-specific physical AI training, could substantially improve robot understanding and planning. After roughly three more years of real-world testing, reliability validation and production readiness work, robot production should begin to scale around 2030.

Video: Pure Diffusion Has Limits; LLMs Direct, Diffusion Models Render

  • Most video generation today uses diffusion transformers (DiT), which generate clips through iterative denoising. Sora, Veo, Seedance, Kling and Wan all follow this approach, and visual quality has improved rapidly.- LLMs break video production into coding tasks: writing scripts and storyboards, building scenes and arranging shots in Blender, then rendering and revising the results. Diffusion models add photorealism. Anthropic’s Motion already uses Claude to generate explainer animations through code. Some users also use Astra for shot previsualization before turning to Seedance 2.5 to generate the final footage. The key to this approach is preserving the upstream scene and shot constraints through the final diffusion stage. Table 3 compares the two approaches.

Two approaches to video generation

  • In our view, pure diffusion has not developed the kind of intelligence that has emerged in LLMs; scaling primarily improves visual quality. End-to-end video production also requires control across shots, easy editing and reusable assets. This is where the LLM-director approach has an advantage: the model can inspect scenes, revise code and render again, applying the iterative process used in coding to a workflow closer to film and television production.- Existing experiments also hint at the difference between the two. A ByteDance study presented at ICML 2025 found that, in a controlled 2D motion environment, the video diffusion models tested imitated similar training examples on out-of-distribution tasks rather than learning general physical laws. On Physics-IQ, the same video model scored 51 with prompts written by Opus 5.5 and 43 with prompts from a mid-sized open-weight model, showing that a stronger LLM can improve video generation through better prompts.- Dedicated video models still lead on visual quality, and Veo 3 has demonstrated early visual reasoning abilities, including maze-solving. LLM-directed production is currently slower and more expensive, with most applications in animation, explainer videos and previsualization. As in robotics, improvements in models and infrastructure should help ease the cost and latency constraints.- We expect pure diffusion to retain its lead in single-shot visual quality for some time. However, it may prove less suited to end-to-end production, where LLMs direct the workflow and diffusion models render the visuals.

Science: Math Benchmarks Improve, Theoretical Physics Advances, and Biology Gains Attention

  • Math benchmarks are largely saturated, and AI-generated proofs of open problems are beginning to appear. In September, Epoch AI declared FrontierMath’s hardest tier, Tier 4, saturated; accuracy had been only about 5% just over a year earlier. OpenAI’s unreleased model produced a proof of finite-time blow-up for the forced 3D Navier–Stokes equations and formalized it in Lean. The Clay Mathematics Institute will subject it to rigorous review. The 722 mathematical manuscripts OpenAI released in October also include a proof of a “quasi-Riemann hypothesis,” whose formal verification remains incomplete. The OpenAI team reportedly had not expected the Navier–Stokes result. This advance in mathematical capability appears to be an emergent ability, much like Astra’s physical AI capabilities. About half the results were unconfirmed at publication, and 28 Fields Medalists signed a joint statement criticizing the divergence between AI companies’ goals and those of the mathematics community.- In theoretical physics, Anthropic used Claude in September to push the calculation of the hexagon scattering amplitude in planar N=4 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory to nine loops, from a previous record of eight.- Among scientific fields, biology is Anthropic’s top priority. It has acquired Coefficient Bio, launched Claude Science and operates a wet lab. Of the 1,320 Claude-designed protein binders reported in August, 354 showed binding in experiments, a hit rate of about 27%, compared with a typical industry rate of 10–15%. In October, Anthropic acquired Mithril Medicine, which is developing a therapy for a rare form of muscular dystrophy. It said human clinical trials could begin as early as late 2028, bringing its research closer to drug development.

Cyber Offense and Defense Create New Demand for Tokens

  • More capable models find and exploit vulnerabilities more efficiently, increasing the attacks defenders must address. Anthropic restricts its Mythos series to vetted organizations and launched Cyber Mission in October to work with security vendors on protecting critical infrastructure. OpenAI launched Daybreak in August with access subject to approval. Google also initially made Carbon available to selected cybersecurity defenders. In Google’s published results for the ExploitGym vulnerability exploitation benchmark, Carbon scores ahead of Astra and Opus 5.5.- Anthropic says Project Glasswing partners used Mythos to identify at least 129,000 verified vulnerabilities between April and July, including more than 33,000 rated critical or high severity. Open-weight models are also improving: CAISI, part of the US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), calls GLM-5.3 the most capable open-weight cybersecurity model to date. Cheaper vulnerability discovery creates more work in validation and remediation. Comcast’s chief information security officer now identifies the sheer volume of findings to validate as a new bottleneck.- We expect both attackers and defenders to consume substantial volumes of tokens. Attackers use models to find vulnerabilities and write exploits; defenders must continuously scan, validate, prioritize, patch, retest and respond to incidents. Our October 28 cybersecurity report estimates current security-related model revenue at US$5–7 billion. If vulnerability discovery, remediation and retesting move from monthly to weekly and eventually daily cycles, the report estimates that revenue could reach US$50–70 billion. This is smaller than coding but still represents a sizable new market. Anthropic expects AI to begin favoring defenders in about two years, with spending on both offense and defense rising until then.

Near-Term Commercial Pressure, Larger Long-Term Opportunity

  • Our August report already assessed the long-term opportunity against total labor income rather than software budgets alone. Physical AI now extends it to blue-collar work.

The potential economic scope of AI (US$ trillion)

Source: Gartner; a16z; US Bureau of Labor Statistics; ILO; compiled by FUNDA.

  • Coding alone represents trillions of dollars in potential economic value. Across white-collar and blue-collar work, the potential ranges from around US$10 trillion to several tens of trillions. McKinsey estimates that existing technology could automate 57% of US working hours, with agents accounting for 44% and robots for 13%. Labor income provides a measure of the value AI could address; model revenue will depend on adoption and how that value is shared across the industry.- Capturing these opportunities requires investment now. Most of physical AI’s value is not expected to begin materializing until around 2030, but frontier labs must fund the training and research in advance.

About 70% of Leading Labs’ Compute Spending Is Not Used for Paid Inference

  • The labs’ spending mix reflects this early investment. Anthropic’s S-1 describes compute as a centrally managed pool allocated among research, model development, customer workloads and the company’s own use. Note 8 reports first-half cost of revenue of US$2.38 billion, mostly compute used to serve paying customers. This was roughly 27% of compute and infrastructure spending over the same period, versus about 38% in 2025. On that basis, we estimate that more than 70% of compute spending is not used for paid inference. Anthropic has far fewer free users than ChatGPT: Similarweb estimates that Claude’s web traffic in August was about one-sixth of ChatGPT’s. Free inference therefore represents only a small share of that 70%-plus, with most going to training, research and Anthropic’s own use (see the RSI section). OpenAI has a similar spending mix. Epoch AI estimates that about 70% of its compute went to R&D in 2024, while The Information reports that training spending will rise from about US$32 billion in 2026 to about US$65 billion in 2027.- We expect this roughly 70/30 split to persist through the end of 2027. The top three labs are closely matched. The first to cross the RSI threshold could gain an advantage as its models accelerate subsequent development. None will be quick to cut compute allocated to training and research.

Capex Continues to Outpace Model Revenue

  • Comparing capex at Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta with revenue at OpenAI and Anthropic shows a widening absolute gap between investment and model revenue. The capex figures include non-AI spending and support advertising, cloud services and enterprise software as well as models. The revenue figures cover only two model companies. This comparison illustrates the scale of investment relative to direct monetization; we do not treat the difference as an industry loss or a funding shortfall.

Capex at four major tech companies versus OpenAI and Anthropic revenue (US$ billion)

Source: company filings; Fortune; The Information; The Wall Street Journal; Anthropic S-1; compiled by FUNDA.

  • In the first half of this year, the gap widened by about US$110 billion from a year earlier. Company guidance puts combined full-year capex at roughly US$730 billion, up about US$320 billion. The market expects another increase of more than US$200 billion next year. The August consensus was for an increase of about US$200 billion; after Q3 earnings, Alphabet reiterated that next year’s spending would rise significantly, while Microsoft expects further growth in fiscal 2027. Incremental model revenue remains far below the increase in capex. These figures exclude Oracle, neoclouds such as CoreWeave, and SpaceX. Goldman Sachs estimated in August that global AI-related investment would reach about US$1 trillion in 2026.- Both previous advances in commercialization were driven by products. From June to October 2025, the o3 price cut and GPT-5 launch drove usage growth. ChatGPT’s weekly active users rose from 500 million at the end of March to more than 800 million in early October, while OpenAI’s annualized revenue rose from about US$10 billion in June to more than US$20 billion by year-end. From February to June 2026, Opus 4.5 and 4.6 boosted enterprise demand. Anthropic’s annualized revenue climbed from about US$9 billion at the end of 2025 to more than US$47 billion in early May, and the share of businesses in Ramp’s sample paying for Anthropic rose from 17.8% to 42.1%.- In Q3, Anthropic’s sequential revenue growth slowed from 144% to 45%. The share of businesses in Ramp’s sample paying for Anthropic rose by just 1.7 percentage points from June to September, while overall token spending in the sample barely grew in August and September, partly because of price cuts and efficiency gains. OpenAI continued to grow quickly: the FT reports that its annualized revenue approached US$50 billion at the end of September. Meanwhile, the four companies spent about US$208.8 billion on capex in Q3, roughly US$37.6 billion more than in Q2. Amazon accounted for US$77.3 billion and kept its full-year cash capex guidance at about US$220 billion.

  • Higher financing costs add to the pressure. The Federal Reserve raised rates by 25 basis points in September to 3.75–4.00%, and the 10-year Treasury yield exceeded 5% in late September. According to the FT, large tech companies issued US$182 billion in investment-grade bonds in the first seven months of the year. Alphabet’s free cash flow has been negative for two consecutive quarters; Meta’s turned negative in Q3. The longer revenue takes to catch up with investment, the greater the financing burden.- Customer usage can fluctuate, but compute commitments are difficult to unwind. Anthropic’s S-1 shows that revenue is primarily usage-based, much of it without long-term contractual guarantees. Compute procurement, by contrast, typically involves multi-year commitments. At the end of June, future compute purchase commitments and leases that had not yet commenced totaled about US$587 billion, according to media calculations based on the filing. Customers can reduce usage, but infrastructure payments remain contractually due.- We expect the absolute gap between industry investment and model revenue to keep widening through the end of 2027. The labs’ own model development, exploratory research and pre-release safety evaluations consume compute without generating immediate revenue. Price cuts and token optimization further constrain near-term revenue growth, leaving annual incremental model revenue unlikely to match the increase in capex. Cloud revenue is already growing, but spending by model companies ultimately needs to be supported by paying end users.- Our base case remains that RSI crosses the threshold around 2027. The strategic value of compute could then begin to be realized, although revenue growth will still depend on bringing products to market. If this slips to 2029 or later, the industry will need time to absorb the capacity already built. If continual learning remains unsolved for an extended period, investment beyond actual demand could face write-downs.

Industry Implications and What to Watch

  • Model companies need to convert new capabilities into paid applications. We expect frontier labs to capture a greater share of the value in physical AI and video, while robot manufacturers focus on hardware, interfaces, data and specific use cases.
