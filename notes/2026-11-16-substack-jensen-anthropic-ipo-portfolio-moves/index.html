Jensen

We may still see him come back???

There’s a lot of support for it, including more than 1,600 comments on an internal post, most supporting his return. A public petition had over 115,000 signatures as of November 14 and Jensen has reportedly been discussing a comeback with allies.

But the board also has an investigation to work through. The independent directors announced outside investigators on November 10, and the findings could affect both who leads the company and how much oversight they have.

Gavin Baker and other big players also asked the board on November 13 to take its time with succession and keep a return on the table. They supported Colette Kress running things in the meantime.

Then on Sunday, Scott Bessent called for an outside CEO.

So there are people pushing for very different outcomes here. Lot’s going on here. Hard to think there’s not a lot going on under the surface.

But when we think about the AI trade, think about the customer trying to fund its next site. We spent time on this in the CoreWeave article.

Jensen is amazing at what he does. NVIDIA has helped parts of the buildout through investments, capacity purchases and guarantees. A customer can have all this demand and still need help paying for the expansion. If NVIDIA becomes more selective with that help, or takes longer to approve it, some of the spending could get pushed out. And the optics, power and cooling companies are waiting on that spending too.

How much appetite will there be for the next round of commitments?

Even with Jensen back, the board could put tighter limits around those decisions. A return could help customers feel more comfortable about leadership and still come with a different approach to financing them.

If he’s gone permanently, the next CEO has an incredible business to work with. But they also have to get customers, suppliers and lenders comfortable making years of commitments together. Jensen has been central to that. I think it takes time for someone else to build that confidence, especially when everyone is committing so much money.

On Tuesday’s call I’d like to hear how they plan to keep customers building through this. The delivery plans and the willingness to support future projects matter a lot for the rest of the trade.

Anthropic

Anthropic is expected to start trading Thursday, November 19, under ANT. At the proposed $70–78 range, the deal implies roughly $1.26–1.41 trillion of fully diluted equity value.

We worked through the growth and compute costs last Sunday.

Now I’m thinking about how much money this could pull from the names we already own. The proposed public offering is roughly $58–64 billion before fees, mostly newly issued shares.

For scale, SpaceX raised about $86.25 billion including its additional-share option. Remember back then Space stocks tanked after the IPO.

So I think a capital drain is a real possibility here. A manager who wants direct exposure to Anthropic could fund it by trimming some of the AI stocks they’ve been using to get that exposure. We don’t know how much will come from existing cash or sales, but it’s a sizeable new allocation for the market to absorb.

But the money going into Anthropic also helps fund the customer spending we want to see. At the midpoint, the company estimates $84.6 billion of net proceeds including a separate $26 billion strategic placement.

NVIDIA, Google and Microsoft are among those partners, with some of Google’s portion fulfilling earlier commitments.

The public offering would broaden that funding beyond the partners themselves, and Anthropic plans to use the money across compute, infrastructure and the rest of the business.

We could get some pressure on existing AI stocks as investors make room, while the raise improves a major customer’s ability to pay for expansion. Both can happen.

Rates

It’s happening with inflation still pretty uncomfortable. Friday’s producer-price report showed a 6.5% increase over the past year and 0.3% for October. Excluding food, energy and trade services, prices rose 0.5% for the month.

And Fed Governor Waller said a half-point December hike should be considered if core CPI came in around 0.3% or higher. Core CPI did come in at 0.3%.

That was his view before the release and not a Fed decision but it tells you why I’m reluctant to assume cheaper money will come along and help these projects out.

Higher financing costs can make a site harder to fund and put pressure on what investors will pay for the future earnings, even when demand looks good.

Permits

With all the money going into AI, getting enough power built and connected is becoming a bigger part of the story obviously. The bipartisan bill moving through the Senate is meant to speed up federal reviews for energy projects and make it easier to build transmission lines.

It also changes who pays when a new data center needs more power. If a utility has to build generation or upgrade the grid to serve that site, the bill would put those extra costs on the data-center customer instead of other customers. It even calls for those costs to keep being recovered if the data center leaves.

We maybe can see why a well-funded hyperscaler would accept that trade. Paying more to get power sooner could be worth it when customers are waiting for capacity. But for a developer already stretching to finance a project, those costs could change whether the site makes sense at all.

Orders

Then we have Applied Materials saying its largest customers are giving it rolling eight-quarter forecasts and ordering equipment earlier. Management expects wafer-fab-equipment demand to grow more than 30% in 2027.

It’s nice to see that with so much doubt around how long AI spending can continue. Customers are trying to get ahead of the equipment they’ll need, while cleanroom space is still limiting installations. That gives us something concrete to put next to all the uncertainty this week.

Cisco

Cisco raised its fiscal 2027 hyperscaler AI revenue outlook from $7.5 billion to $9 billion.

Again, in a market where there is concern about the durability of these companies’ earnings, we are seeing time and time again that the actual upcoming quarters are going to see continued growth.

The first connection I’d make is FN. Cisco was 19.9% of Fabrinet’s revenue last fiscal year, and this increase came after FN had already reported. How much of it reaches FN depends on what Cisco sells and where it manufactures those products, but we have a sizeable existing customer raising its outlook.

With CIEN and MRVL, you also have competition for some of the same optical business. More spending gives them more to go after.

Portfolio

My view the last few weeks has been pretty consistent.

Overtime I want fewer names that I know really well, with a core in each that I can trade around. This week I sold a broad basket of stocks. I have been saying that if we get a multi day rally I will aim to reduce exposure.

I am at a place right now where I just want to be in a heavy cash position (currently at 50%). There is something about this market that is just concerning to me and I don’t feel like having lots of exposure and seeing my portfolio drop

10% in a day. I am ok sitting on the sidelines and missing gains for the sake of my overall mental capacity. There will

be people that will trade this volatility really well and make lots of money. But most people that try will be chopped up. I just don’t feel like being that.

Where things stand today I would be happy redploying more capital if the Q’s can test that $630 level and find support. Even better if we can visit ~$600. Today that feels a long way off. But with how willing (and pulled forward) the market was to punish AI stocks with a dem win, I am concerned with any other meaningful developments like the when the market with start caring about hyperscaler capex peak like what Oracle is projecting.

If we do get a really nice sell, there are companies at the front of my list that I want. I will present them below. Like I said, I want to progressively trim furhter and further, given what I am seeing today. Not doing too much at once as I still want exposure if we do go up.

Here’s a TLDR in how I view what an ideal port would look like for me when I deploy most of my cash. These would be my heaviest concentrated positions.

Hyperscalers

  • AMZN. I want AWS and Trainium, but also the margin opportunity from robotics and fulfillment, where a small reduction in cost per order can move earnings. Amazon can fulfill orders for other merchants too, so it can still earn from purchases when somebody else’s AI assistant handles the shopping.
  • MSFT. I like Microsoft getting paid as agents do more work through Azure, Microsoft 365 and GitHub, with usage growing beyond the number of employees buying software seats. It already has the data, workflows and permissions around that work, even when customers choose somebody else’s model.
  • GOOGL. TPUs can lower Google’s own AI costs and bring in revenue from outside customers, including companies building competing models. Add Cloud, AI shopping and the distribution it already has through Search, YouTube and Android, and there are several ways for it to monetize the shift.
  • META. Better ad targeting and recommendations can get advertisers more out of their spending, while WhatsApp agents could help turn Instagram discovery into completed sales. Advertising is the foundation of the thesis, with glasses and consumer agents adding more upside.
  • ORCL. I want OCI growth and the database business expanding inside AWS, Azure and Google Cloud, where Oracle can keep earning even when customers choose another cloud. Customer-funded chips and prepayments could improve the economics, though the remaining funding burden keeps me more cautious here.

AI Infrastructure

  • COHR. I want this as a staple because they have such broad exposure. Pluggables, NPO, CPO, OCS. Massive capacity ramp. Hopefully significant margin improvements. Domestic manufacturing.
  • VRT. Liquid cooling, power distribution and prefabricated systems give Vertiv more to sell as racks get denser and customers try to get sites running faster. I like that it can win across NVIDIA and custom-chip architectures, then keep earning from servicing the equipment.
  • MRVL / LITE. Custom silicon, optical DSPs, silicon photonics and scale-across networking give Marvell several ways to grow with hyperscalers, and Lumentum adds lasers, optical transceivers and OCS. I like the breadth the combination could create across AI connectivity, alongside Marvell’s multigeneration AWS relationship.
  • BE. Bloom can help customers get power to a site sooner when waiting on the grid means delaying revenue. Oracle’s contracted deployment gives that thesis a real commercial foundation, and I think there’s a lot of opportunity in solving the power bottleneck.

Physical AI

I still want lots of Physical AI exposure, especially for stocks that are not directly impacted by hyperscaler AI infra capex. But since they hre high growht tech, they will still move with them (for now).

I need to do some tax-loss harvesting and plan to buy back a handful once I can do that without creating a wash sale.

These are the four main names I want here to be the centerpeice of this sleeve.

  • CGNX. I like that Cognex can grow by helping customers upgrade existing warehouses and factories. Easier-to-deploy AI vision expands the jobs it can handle, and the proposed RealSense acquisition would add 3D perception for robotics.
  • NXPI. NXP has a chance to sell more processing, connectivity and control chips into each increasingly capable machine. Its reported humanoid robotics design win shows how technology built for automotive can open up another market, with the existing automotive business underneath it.
  • AMBA. I want the low-power AI vision exposure as more cameras, vehicles and robots process what they see locally. There’s also potential acquisition upside. NXP was reported to be in talks to buy Ambarella, though no deal has been announced.
  • TDY. FLIR thermal imaging, machine-vision cameras and unmanned systems give Teledyne several ways to benefit as machines see and operate more independently. Defense, aerospace, marine, medical and industrial customers support the business while those newer applications develop.

I’m also looking at software, healthcare and consumer companies. ARGX and Lilly are among them but im not a buyer today.

Honestly, I don’t see many industries that look attractive at current levels, even outside AI.

I’m fine being patient and just researching for now.

But my end goal is to have these 5 different sleeves or whatever you want to refer to them as. And I will just turn up/down the dial on them individually as they see rotations.

So when consumer/healthcare goes on a rip like they have recently, I would aim to reduce exposure and rotate into tech. Then visa versa.
