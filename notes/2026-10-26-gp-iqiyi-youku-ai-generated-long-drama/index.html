SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined how AI could reshape long-form video production as traditional streaming platforms face intense pressure from short-form drama services. AI may increase content output, reduce reliance on some actors and production roles, and improve scheduling flexibility. However, audience willingness to pay and finished-product quality remain unproven.

iQIYI is pursuing an integrated, technology-led strategy spanning scripts, storyboards, production, post-production, scheduling, and distribution. Youku is positioned mainly as a distributor using external AI tools, revenue-sharing incentives, marketing support, and production assistance.

Key Insights

The expert viewed iQIYI as the platform most likely to deliver meaningful AI long-drama results because of its sustained investment and systematic production pipeline. AI is currently strongest in script development, character planning, visual design, subtitles, sound, effects, and production scheduling. It remains weak in nuanced expressions and complex emotional performances. The first commercial breakthrough is expected from production volume rather than premium quality. AI could replace lower-tier projects and fill programming gaps caused by delays or regulatory reviews. No AI long-form drama has yet become a genuine breakout success. Storytelling, character development, visual direction, and audience acceptance remain major barriers. Commercialization may rely more on advertising, lower-priced membership tiers, international licensing, localization, and separately licensable digital assets. The expert argued that AI will widen the gap between leading and weaker platforms rather than improve the entire market evenly. The moderator repeatedly explored whether AI long-form drama could achieve premium quality and sustainable commercialization, reflecting uncertainty about audience conversion.
