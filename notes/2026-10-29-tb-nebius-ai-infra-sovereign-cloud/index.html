Agenda

  • Nebius's (NASDAQ: NBIS) market positioning as a specialised AI cloud infrastructure provider
  • Customer demand velocity, workload allocation and reservation dynamics across AI start-ups, enterprise labs and foundation model builders
  • Supply chain execution, Nvidia's (NASDAQ: NVDA) GPU cluster allocation and power and data centre capacity expansion across European and international sites
  • Go-to-market execution and software stack differentiation
  • CAPEX requirements, unit economics and path towards sustained free cash flow generation

Key Insights

  • Nebius uses an auction-based pricing system where customers bid against each other for cluster access, allowing it to act as a market maker and raise prices by roughly 20%
  • Demand isn't disappearing but concentrating around GB300 and Vera-level GPUs, leaving providers with older fleets increasingly uncompetitive and marginalised. Expert thinks the biggest risk to Nebius is a cascade of neocloud defaults flooding the market with GPUs, overwhelming demand and crushing pricing industry-wide
  • Meta and SpaceX each possess hundreds of thousands of immediately deployable GPUs, a capability no neocloud can match, directly displacing contracts that would otherwise flow to Nebius or CoreWeave
  • Nebius requires 60-65% upfront payment on contracts, gaining advantage over the contrasting CoreWeave strategy, which is a more debt-dependent model
  • Anthropic and OpenAI are losing enterprise customers to open-source models, which deliver similar results at 70-80% lower cost, undermining the closed-source demand narrative underpinning massive infrastructure contracts
