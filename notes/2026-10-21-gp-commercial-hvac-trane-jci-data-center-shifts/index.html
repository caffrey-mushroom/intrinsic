SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined commercial HVAC demand, OEM backlogs, data center permitting, equipment quality, pricing, and labor availability. The expert has experience across HVAC manufacturing, distribution, contracting, and sales.

Market activity remained stronger than expected, supported by data centers, education, pharmaceuticals, healthcare, and industrial projects. Data center cooling is the principal growth engine, but power access, permitting, and community opposition are increasingly delaying or relocating projects.

Key Insights

Inquiry volumes rose by high single digits to low double digits year over year, while bookings and pipelines remained strong. Major OEM production lines face exceptionally long lead times. Constraints are also spreading from data center equipment into conventional commercial products. Data center buyers increasingly prioritize equipment availability over optimal efficiency or preferred system design. The expert expressed serious concern about equipment quality, citing cost reductions, supplier limitations, plant utilization, and manufacturing complexity. Daikin was viewed as having the greatest quality risk among the largest suppliers. Public opposition and grid limitations have become measurable barriers. Some canceled projects may relocate to more receptive states rather than disappear. Replacement cooling for aging data centers was described as a particularly attractive opportunity because uptime requirements encourage comprehensive system upgrades. Trane received the strongest outlook due to its applied-equipment focus and service momentum. Johnson Controls also appears well positioned. Skilled labor scarcity is a major structural risk as data centers recruit technicians at compensation levels traditional service organizations struggle to match.
