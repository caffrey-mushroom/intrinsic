STX / WDC / TDK: Seagate and Toshiba vie for key HDD head supplier – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Seagate and Toshiba are competing for TDK’s HDD magnetic-head business in a deal potentially worth several billion dollars, with Seagate submitting a higher offer after Toshiba began talks. TDK is the only independent head supplier; Toshiba relies on it entirely, including for its HAMR roadmap, while Seagate and WDC use it to supplement in-house output. Toshiba disputed the report and denied a reported meeting.

STX/WDC: Evercore says HDD bottleneck shifts upstream, making vertical integration the key advantage in HDD Deepdive

Evercore argues higher areal density shifts the HDD bottleneck from final assembly to heads, HAMR photonics, media/glass and test, favoring vertically integrated STX/WDC over Toshiba’s merchant-supplier model. It expects 2027–28 shortages to be driven by components rather than drive assembly, while 32TB 10-disk HAMR drives carry ~$190–195 of BOM and 40TB drives should improve cost/TB by ~20%.

AMD: Su plans substantial supply increase in 2027 – Reuters

Reuters reports Lisa Su said AMD plans to substantially increase chip supply in 2027 after raising output this year, citing very high demand over the next several years. During her Taiwan visit, Su said AMD is working with partners to expand both CPU and GPU production and secure memory supply. She said the industry needs more advanced wafer capacity and AMD is now planning three to five years ahead.

AMD: Citi raises PT to $800 as agentic AI expands CPU TAM to ~$300B by 2030

Citi says always-on agents like Muse could drive orders-of-magnitude more compute than chatbots, expanding CPU TAM from ~$29B in 2025 to ~$300B by 2030 (~60% CAGR), with AMD the primary beneficiary and Intel secondary. Citi estimates 100M Muse DAUs could also require ~200–390K B200-class GPUs / 2.8–5.5K NVL72 racks, worth ~$7–19B to NVDA, while rising CPU density creates incremental DRAM/SSD and networking demand; reiterates NVDA Buy/$315.

SHOP: JPM sees agentic commerce driving share gains, outlines bull case to ~30% ’27 revenue growth

JPM says SHOP is well positioned across all three layers of agentic commerce, with Shop Pay preserving payment economics while AI traffic converts better and drives larger merchant sales. Its bull case gets to ~30% ’27 growth via core business, Europe/international, enterprise and offline expansion, with pricing adding ~$90M of annualized subscription revenue; JPM raises 2H26/’27 estimates ~1–3% and sees ~20% FCF margins sustained. Reiterates OW, raises PT to $200 from $185 and keeps SHOP on the Analyst Focus List.

We wrote a little about SHOP in our EOD Wrap yesterday in case you missed it

GOOGL / CEG: Google signs 3.6GW power deal with Constellation – Reuters

Reuters reports Google contracted 3,590MW of power from Constellation in the PJM market. A 20-year agreement covers 890MW of new nuclear output from upgrades to 11 existing units, enabling more than $4.3B of investment, with initial electricity expected in 2028. The remaining 2,700MW is long-term supply not tied to a specific generation source. Reuters notes the deal comes as PJM considers requiring large new users to bring their own power supply or accept shutdowns during periods of peak demand.

Anthropic/OpenAI: SemiAnalysis says subscription subsidies are a major compute/margin drag, with OpenAI sharply cutting limits

SemiAnalysis estimates subscriptions can consume >40% of inference compute despite being only ~10% of revenue, materially depressing revenue/MW; for Anthropic the hit is ~$36M/MW. OpenAI just halved token limits on its $200 plan and standardized tokens/$ across its $100/$200/$500 tiers, while Anthropic now offers ~4–5x better API-equivalent value on Opus-class usage. The broader implication is that subscription limits are becoming an important lever for labs to improve inference margins, with OpenAI taking the more aggressive path versus Anthropic’s gradual reduction in subsidies.

AVGO / Anthropic: Banks launch $60B chip financing; Broadcom backs $42B – FT

FT reports BofA, Citi and Morgan Stanley began syndicating $42B of senior loans backed by Broadcom as part of a record $60B financing for Anthropic to lease Google TPUs. Another $18B of junior debt carries no Broadcom guarantee; Blackstone has committed roughly $9B and is syndicating the balance. The financing covers Anthropic’s 2027 chip orders, with lease payments starting after delivery. FT also reports Anthropic could issue up to $42B of convertible notes to Broadcom to fund those payments. Junior lenders would bear Anthropic’s credit risk directly

GOOGL: Wells sees TPU sales at $99B/$169B in ’27/’28, but only ~13% GM

Wells raises external TPU deployments to 4GW/7GW in ’27/’28, implying ~$99B/$169B revenue, ~40% of GCP, but just ~6%/~8% EPS contribution given a ~15% licensing markup/~13% GM. The framework has GOOGL buying TPU systems from AVGO at ~$21B/GW and selling at ~$24B/GW, while AVGO ultimately sells to the financing SPV at ~$35B/GW and provides the financial backstop, helping reconcile its higher content/revenue per GW.

Cybersecurity: Korea and Japan breaches spur security-stock rally – FT / Xinhua

FT reports South Korea’s president flagged possible AI use in attacks exposing customer data at seven financial firms. Attackers targeted third-party systems, including a loan-broker portal that exposed about 25,000 Shinhan Bank customers. Xinhua reports separate breaches across Japan potentially affecting millions, including at least 130,000 students and teachers at Osaka Metropolitan University, where a suspected ransomware attack disabled about 500 servers.

SPCX: GS raises CY26–28 numbers, says AI estimates still conservative despite massive capex ramp

GS raises CY26/27/28 revenue to $51.6B/$127.5B/$205.6B from $47.6B/$107.4B/$180.6B and EPS to $1.30/$3.96/$7.01, citing new hosting deals, model improvements and tight compute supply. Terrestrial compute capacity ramps from 2.4GW in 2026 to 7.0GW/10.6GW in 2027/28, but capex of ~$72B/$222B/$193B drives deeply negative FCF, making funding and monetization the key debate. GS maintains Buy/$230 and views AI estimates as more floor than ceiling.

AI SEMIS: Mizuho sees agentic AI driving ~30% of 2030 server CPUs, pushing CPU TAM above $200B

Mizuho says always-on agents like Muse and Dots could drive a major CPU demand inflection as orchestration, tool calls and sandbox workloads increasingly run 24/7, with agentic CPU penetration rising from ~4% today to ~30% by 2030. It models total server CPU TAM growing from ~$68B in 2026 to ~$209B by 2030, including ~$80B of agentic CPUs; AMD is the preferred beneficiary, while NVDA standalone CPU revenue could grow >100% y/y in 2027 and ARM benefits from AGI CPU plus royalties across Vera/Graviton/Axion/Cobalt. Memory also benefits, with agentic AI potentially driving ~18% of NAND demand by 2030. Mizuho raises PTs on AMD to $705 from $580, DELL to $650 from $600, SNDK to $2,050 from $1,875, INTC to $114 from $92 and SMCI to $43 from $35.

The report says TSMC’s five 2nm fabs are operating at full capacity and production targets have risen 15–20%, yet customers including Google, Amazon, Qualcomm and AMD still face shortages. EBN says Samsung is seeking alternative-foundry business with its second-generation 2nm process, targeting yields above 70%.

TSM: GS raises PT to $660/NT$3,300, sees strong 2027 growth + higher CapEx on broader AI demand

GS expects 2026/27 revenue growth of +42%/+36.9% y/y, with 3Q/4Q revenue +15.3%/+11.0% q/q as AI demand broadens across GPUs, ASICs, networking and increasingly server CPUs. GS raises 2027/28 CapEx to $85B/$98B from $78B/$82B, arguing supply remains insufficient despite expansion; near-term GM edges down to ~67.5%/67.3% on N2 ramp dilution. Reiterates Buy.

NOK: CEO says supply constraints hold back twice the buildout pace – CNBC

CNBC reports Nokia CEO Justin Hotard said customers could build data centers twice as quickly without supply constraints, identifying memory chips and energy as major bottlenecks.
