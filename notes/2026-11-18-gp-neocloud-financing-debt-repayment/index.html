核心摘要

  1. 前Nebius集团CFO表示，AI数据中心融资正从债务转向客户预付款和供应商融资，部分客户甚至预付100%的资本开支或5年合同额的50%，债务融资往往需要5-8个月，时间成本过高。
  2. 部分无担保企业债收益率已超过15%，CoreWeave的融资成本约16%-17%，因而被迫将客户预付比例从约20%提高到30%以上。
  3. NVIDIA更加挑剔芯片销售对象，会派专家核查数据中心是否满足Rubin要求，GPU残值担保比例从过去最高约90%降至约30%甚至更低，新兴neocloud融资难度明显加大；而Google、AMD等则更积极提供担保以争夺份额。
  4. Nebius净债务约70亿美元，已接近净盈利，计划资本开支400-450亿美元，其中约170-200亿美元来自客户预付款，并持有Toloka、ClickHouse、Avride等资产可变现，在建设节奏上有较大弹性。
  5. CoreWeave净债务超过600亿美元（含大量托管租赁），季度亏损约7亿美元，但随首批Hopper合同进入第5年后续约，折旧已提完，有望在1-1.5年内实现净盈利。
  6. Nebius拥有约300家客户并提供更广泛软硬件服务，坚持约90%自有或定制数据中心；CoreWeave客户不到40家、以5年长约为主，正在效仿Nebius拓展服务和短期合同，以将EBITDA利润率从约40%推向60%。
  7. 专家预计全球150-200家neocloud中约50-80家将面临严峻生存挑战，2027年或有20-30家倒闭，Crusoe和Nscale已出现融资和项目问题，若其一破产可能引发连锁反应。
  8. AI基础设施支出并未终结但增速可能放缓，美国政局变化导致许可审批变慢、美欧日利率上升以及社区反对都可能拖延项目。

SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed how AI infrastructure providers are funding data-center and GPU expansion as borrowing becomes slower and more costly. The discussion focused on customer prepayments, supplier financing and guarantees, competition among established and newer neoclouds, and the risks of rapid industry growth.

Key Insights

Funding is shifting away from debt. Providers are seeking larger customer prepayments and asking equipment suppliers to shoulder more of the upfront cost. Strong demand gives established providers greater leverage to charge more and secure advance payments. Newer providers face a tougher path. The leading GPU supplier is becoming more selective about which companies and facilities receive its chips, and its residual-value guarantees are reportedly much smaller than before. The expert expects this to make financing harder for younger operators. Established providers have different strengths. Provider A was described as financially less exposed, with a broader service offering and a more cautious approach to profitability. Provider B has more capacity and long-term contracts, but higher debt and lease obligations. The expert expects industry shakeout. They anticipate that many newer operators could struggle to survive as permitting delays, financing constraints, and weaker supplier support converge. They also identified the possibility of failures among larger providers creating further disruption. Spending is not necessarily over, but growth may slow. The expert expects continued investment, while warning that regulatory changes, higher interest rates, and community opposition could delay projects.
