SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed peptide contract manufacturing capacity, demand for GLP-1 and other peptide drugs, and the competitive positions of Polypeptide, Bachem, Corden Pharma, and WuXi. The discussion also covered large pharmaceutical companies’ manufacturing strategies and Samsung’s acquisition of Polypeptide.

The expert’s central view is that announced capacity additions do not yet mean the market is oversupplied: downstream processing, rather than peptide synthesis, remains the main constraint. Capacity expansion should be targeted because only a limited number of programs require very large-scale production.

Key Insights

Oral GLP-1 products may require more API, but the expert questioned whether they offer a clear patient-compliance advantage over improved injection devices. They emphasized the need for more sustainable, intensified downstream processes to support large-scale peptide production. CDMO revenue growth is expected to rely heavily on metabolic peptides, particularly GLP-1s. The expert also sees a strong longer-term outlook for non-metabolic peptide applications, including oncology, rare diseases, and agriculture. The expert attributed Polypeptide’s operational and margin challenges to site-level execution, staffing, and scale-up issues. They viewed Samsung’s financial resources as helpful, but warned that imposing a rigid operating model could weaken Polypeptide’s customer flexibility and early-stage business. The expert considers Bachem technically strong and relatively well positioned on high-volume costs, while flagging delays and execution risks in its expansion plans.
