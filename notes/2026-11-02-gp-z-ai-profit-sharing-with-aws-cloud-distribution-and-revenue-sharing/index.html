SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examines how Z.AI’s GLM models are distributed through major cloud platforms, including AWS and domestic providers. The expert sees cloud partnerships as a practical route to customers and revenue for independent model developers, but warns that long-term success depends on model differentiation, customer integration, and access to applications and user data.

Key Insights

Cloud distribution is important but not enough: The expert believes independent model companies generally need cloud partners because building their own infrastructure, sales, and support operations is costly, while customers can switch models relatively easily. Usage-based model revenue may be less durable than SaaS: Unlike enterprise software embedded in business workflows, model services can be replaced with comparatively little friction. Applications and user data are increasingly strategic: The expert argues that model providers need to move into applications or risk losing influence, bargaining power, and feedback useful for improving their models. Z.AI may be better positioned in targeted sectors: Its work with public-sector, research, and large enterprise customers could support specialized deployments, though the expert sees weaker prospects in broad consumer and general-purpose use. The expert expects a small number of major model providers to remain, and suggested that access to mainstream cloud distribution could be essential for survival.
