SUMMARY

Overview

The expert described a weak residential HVAC replacement market alongside strong repair demand and unexpectedly robust light- commercial activity. Despite hot summer weather, residential equipment sales did not accelerate as they typically would. Household budget pressure, higher system costs and cautious consumer behavior encouraged repairs, lower-efficiency purchases and extensive comparison shopping.

Watsco and Ferguson were viewed as formidable competitors on large projects because their scale supports aggressive pricing. Inventory management has largely normalized, although coils experienced shortages following the refrigerant transition.

Key Insights

Residential replacement unit sales were flat to slightly lower year over year. The expert considered the muted response to hot weather highly unusual. Consumers increasingly repaired aging systems, including expensive components in equipment that historically would have been replaced. Demand shifted toward entry-level air conditioners and heat pumps, while premium efficiency and indoor-air-quality upgrades weakened. Lower-cost, side-discharge heat pumps from Asian and emerging manufacturers gained acceptance. Improving cold-weather performance should support further adoption. Distributor margins remained pressured as homeowners sought more bids and contractors demanded wholesale discounts. Light-commercial demand was notably stronger than expected, supported by data-center construction, related regional development and replacement of very old equipment. Private-equity-owned contractors appeared to lose sales after raising homeowner prices and weakening their local-business positioning. AI adoption remains early but could improve call handling, sales coaching, purchasing, cost control and profitability.
