SPCX / NVDA: SpaceX seeks $40B to buy chips led by Apollo – FT

SpaceX’s BBB rating opens the deal to insurance and pension investors, although its existing 2056 bonds trade around 85 cents on the dollar amid concerns over debt and capital spending.

TD Cowen says MRVL’s risk profile has materially improved as growth shifts toward its connectivity franchise and away from concentrated custom XPU exposure; only ~$15B of the ~$80B CY30 revenue midpoint is now expected from custom accelerators, with XPU just ~19% of revenue vs. >40% previously feared. Management targets ~$38B interconnect, ~$10B switching/storage and ~$30B custom by CY30, with FY28 revenue already expected near ~$20B, ~$18B from datacenter. Cowen sees >$30 EPS within three years, upgrades to Buy from Hold and raises PT to $350 from $245.

NTAP: Evercore upgrades to OP/$300, sees conservative FY27 guide + AI/storage driving durable growth

Longer term, AI/storage expansion, Novus/AIDE and operating leverage support a path toward ~$15+ EPS, while high-60s GMs and >32% FY27 EBIT margins look sustainable despite NAND pressure. Upgrades from In Line and raises PT to $300 from $210.

Evercore ISI models MSFT datacenter capacity rising from ~12GW exiting FY26 to ~30.5GW by FY30, with ~10.3GW of revenue-generating GPU capacity and Azure AI revenue/GW rising from ~$20B today to ~$27.5B. That supports ~$389B of FY30 Azure revenue (~40% CAGR, ~66% AI mix); the bull case reaches ~34GW, ~$447B Azure revenue (+14% vs. Street), ~$40.42 EPS (+16%) and ~$880/share. ISI sees upside from faster dock-to-live, OpenAI/always-on agent demand and better monetization, while its base case supports the new $635 PT

AI: Ray Dalio Warns AI Bubble Is Approaching Burst Point - Bloomberg

Getting passed around this AM…

He called artificial intelligence a “classic bubble” nearing a bursting point because of rising interest rates and the need to turn wealth into cash. A large amount of debt is being taken out to fund AI, and “as rates continue to climb, that is a point at which the bubble begins to pop.”

“There’s a lot of pressure from all the supply for much more rises in interest rates, and then you have the dynamic of when it starts to have an effect, and that is the point at which the bubble begins to burst.”

“We’re in the part of the cycle that is before that, but approaching that. I think we’re close to that….“What creates the bursting of a bubble is the fact that you need cash. And you start to convert the wealth that you have to do that. Something like a wealth tax would have that effect, or having to pay back loans. So there’s that dynamic that we have to keep an eye on.”

Commercial Times reports substrate suppliers are seeing average product-mix prices increase 7–8% quarterly, with some quotes rising double digits. Kinsus plans 25% more ABF capacity in 2027; major new Unimicron and Nan Ya PCB capacity arrives later.

MU: Settles Netlist patent dispute for $600M over five years – Reuters

Reuters reports Micron will pay Netlist $30M quarterly for five years for a patent-portfolio license that includes HBM technology. The $600M agreement follows Netlist’s $445M infringement verdict in 2024 and a September ITC complaint seeking to block imports of Micron chips used in Google, Nvidia and Broadcom AI products.

INTC / TSM: Musk rules out TSMC operating Terafab; Intel remains involved – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Musk clarified that his companies will build and operate Terafab themselves, with TSMC potentially subleasing part of the site but having no operating role. Intel remains involved in developing the project’s chipmaking technology. The clarification follows speculation that TSMC might run the facility after Musk disclosed discussions with the Taiwanese foundry.

TSLA: Germany backs FSD approval, but EU vote slips to December – Reuters

Reuters reports Germany’s transport minister is pushing for EU approval of Tesla’s driver-assistance system and supports allowing speeds up to 10% above posted limits. Tesla has offered to rename it “Tesla Assisted Driving.” However, an EU vote expected in October has been pushed to at least December as countries including France and Sweden seek additional testing and raise safety concerns.

Anthropic / Cyber: Expands advanced-model access for security testing – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Anthropic is expanding access to Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Mythos 5.1 for verified organizations working with the US government. Approved participants can conduct offensive testing of critical infrastructure, while other security teams receive access for tasks including malware analysis and incident response. New organizations require joint review, and the program extends the earlier Project Glasswing rollout.

Big Tech / EU: Brussels explores broader corporate levy to capture tech revenue – FT

FT reports the European Commission is considering changes to its proposed corporate levy to collect more from large companies such as Apple, Meta and Google without singling out digital services. Officials are discussing higher thresholds and contributions that would exclude more midsize businesses. The levy would apply across industries, but details remain unsettled and adoption requires agreement from all 27 EU countries.

OpenAI: Publishes hundreds of new mathematical results – WSJ

WSJ reports OpenAI released 722 papers organized into 372 groups of mathematical results after testing an internal model on roughly 4,000 problems. The company reported advances related to three remaining Millennium Prize Problems, including the Riemann hypothesis, and said the average result used a fraction of the computing power consumed by its earlier flagship proof.

PENG: Q4 beat; FY2027 revenue growth raised to 40% – Penguin Solutions
