COHR

A roughly $20B PhotonLink agreement over five years. Another major AI customer, on top of the previously announced NVIDIA relationship. That is a huge deal for Coherent.

The unnamed customer’s ramp is expected to start in early calendar 2028. No annual revenue split, minimum purchases, cancellation terms or funding amounts were disclosed.

The business today is already growing quickly. Revenue of $2.37B, up 16% sequentially and 50% year over year.

Adjusted EPS of $2.09, versus $1.74 last quarter and $1.16 a year ago. Adjusted gross margin at 40.6% and adjusted operating margin at 23.2%.

Revenue was near the guide’s top, and EPS beat.

Next quarter they are guiding to $2.55B-$2.75B and $2.28-$2.48 of adjusted EPS. Another roughly 12% sequential revenue increase at the midpoint.

Think about that in dollars. Around $280m of additional revenue in one quarter. On top of the $2.37B they just did!

Datacenter and Communications did $1.95B, up 79%. Now 82% of Coherent. The optical business has become large enough to drive the overall result.

This goes back to what I wrote about PhotonLink in August. Coherent has all these different photonics assets. Bringing them together lets it sell more around the optical engine, instead of fighting for just one component.

The 400mW continuous-wave laser anchors the new agreement. Now look at everything else they can put around it.

Lasers, isolators, coolers, fiber assemblies, connectors. Supplying more of the assembly means more content for Coherent.

Their $15,000 content example around a 100Tbps chip assumes the customer buys the full portfolio. It shows how far the opportunity extends beyond the laser.

PhotonLink also includes VCSEL and multicore-fiber approaches. Several architectures to sell into, but the InP and gallium-arsenide lines aren’t interchangeable. Capacity still has to match what customers buy.

And there is a PhotonLink ramp before the new 2028 agreement. A small amount of revenue is expected in the December quarter, more than $100m in the March 2027 quarter, and more than $250m a quarter before calendar 2027 ends.

DCI is already above $200m a quarter. Up 53% sequentially and almost 4x year over year. Insane.

Management expects that quarterly revenue to roughly double again by the end of calendar 2027. Multi-Rail starts ramping in March, so the current result came before that product contributed.

800G is still growing while 1.6T ramps. This is the overlap we keep talking about. The older generation doesn’t just vanish because the newer one starts shipping. Coherent expects pluggables to remain the dominant scale-out architecture for the next several years.

For scale-up, management puts CPO in late 2027 and NPO in late 2027 or early 2028. Chip-to-chip comes around 2029-2030. More product ramps after the current ones.

Now, six-inch InP was one of the things I wanted to see translate into margins back in August. That, the CPO and NPO schedule, and the fiscal 2027 acceleration.

By the October review and this earnings preview, I had become more cautious. Substrate supply, yields and the pace of margin improvement were the issues. The financial improvement was taking longer than I wanted.

Internal InP capacity doubled year over year, a quarter early. They plan to more than double it again by the end of 2027 and say the substrates and critical inputs are secured.

They also gave a bit more of a direct answer on yields. Management says six-inch yields in Texas and Sweden are already above the three-inch lines, with Zurich due to start in the first half of 2027. The site-by-site detail helps show where the next production comes from.

But the mix of internal and external supply barely changed. They doubled internal output, and transceiver demand grew enough to eat up the extra supply.

So they are making much more themselves AND still need outside suppliers in roughly the same proportion. That is how fast the demand is moving. Getting a larger share made in-house could help margins as that expansion continues.

Adjusted gross margin only moved from 40.2% to 40.6%. Small improvement relative to the revenue growth. Operating margin improved more, to 23.2%, as sales spread the cost base.

The first quarter above 45% adjusted gross margin is targeted before the end of 2028, mainly from new products and the full six-inch benefit. Not that amazing of progress honestly, but at least the revenue is growing significantly.

They are also shipping 320-by-320 OCS systems. Management claims power and reliability advantages from liquid-crystal switching without moving mechanical parts. No separate OCS revenue number yet.

The outlook is now more than 60% pro forma revenue growth in fiscal 2027. First $3B quarter by June 2027. First $4B quarter in fiscal 2028.

Operating cash flow was negative $92.7m, with $775.5m of capex. Put those together and free cash flow was negative $868.2m. Capex is expected to go up again next quarter.

Inventory rose about $760m sequentially. Gross debt went from $3.22B to about $4B. A lot to buy and build before the larger ramps.

Customers help fund it. Almost every recent LTA includes upfront funding, management says, but the amount attached to this $20B deal wasn’t disclosed.

Another PhotonLink customer, rapid DCI growth and capacity ahead of schedule. The commercial progress is substantial. Margins are moving more slowly, and the cash is going out now.

VIAV

Revenue of $469.3m, up 56.9% year over year and above the $450m-$460m guide.

Adjusted EPS of $0.47 against $0.40-$0.42 guided.

Adjusted operating margin of 29%, up from 16.3% a year ago.

Nearly 13% operating-margin expansion. EPS more than tripled. Really strong.

NSE, the Network and Service Enablement business, did $376.1m, up 74.1%. Spirent contributed $52.7m of that.

Legacy NSE still grew about 50% without Spirent. Plenty happening beyond the acquisition.

OSP grew 12.2% to $93.2m. That includes 3D sensing and anti-counterfeiting. The AI networking-test business is mainly in NSE.

The tariff refund and cost of an extra fiscal week netted to about $0.02 of adjusted EPS and 110 basis points of operating margin. Already in the guide, so they beat with that benefit anticipated.

Old tax-loss carryforwards helped the low adjusted tax rate. The 29% operating margin also shows how much more profit the sales are producing.

For the next Q we are lookign at $505m-$515m of revenue and $0.50-$0.52 adjusted EPS. The pre-release consensus was around $468m and $0.39-$0.40.

OSP is expected to fall seasonally to roughly $80m. NSE goes to about $430m, up another 14% sequentially and more than covering that decline.

That NSE increase isn’t all optics. Roughly 40% comes from data center, a third from aerospace and defense, and the rest from service providers. Timing systems, anti-jamming and field-equipment refreshes contribute too.

The first $500m quarter keeps arriving sooner. Management expects sequential revenue growth through the rest of fiscal 2027 too.

An analyst suggested 70% incremental operating margins. Management pointed to low-to-mid-50%, with expenses rising in March. Still a lot of additional sales reaching profit.

This is mainly scale, the CFO said. Spirent integration finished two to three quarters ago. New combined products are still ahead.

One question I raised in the September ECOC piece and November preview was tester productivity. If a faster tester can handle more lower-speed devices, the customer gets more work done with the same box.

But they also have more channels to test, more customers building optical products, and more steps in the manufacturing process. That expands the work available to VIAVI. The current NSE growth is showing it.

Semiconductor optical production test has started shipping, with more coming in December and March. Small dollars initially.

CPO can need as many as five test insertions. Wafer probe, known-good die, optical engine, assembled substrate and engine, then final test.

You really don’t want to find the bad optical part after you have put the entire expensive assembly around it. Catch it earlier and save the good components. That is why adding testing can make economic sense for the customer as the systems get more complicated.

Management sees a potential $100m-plus semiconductor-test opportunity in a couple of years, not a revenue guide. OCS testing is separate, with multicore fiber and dense connectors adding work too.

Cash was good too. $104.7m of operating cash flow, $12m of capex, $92.7m of free cash flow.

Compare that with Coherent’s capacity spending. VIAVI gets exposure to the same buildout through testing, without needing to fund the same huge optical manufacturing footprint.

I had wanted more earnings visibility into 2028. We still don’t have a numerical fiscal 2028 EPS target, but the $500m quarter is arriving much sooner and management expects growth through the rest of this fiscal year.

More existing test sales, new production-test work and earnings turning into cash. A good update on the thesis.

MTSI

MACOM’s adjusted operating margin went from 31.5% to 38.2% in one quarter.

Revenue grew 24% sequentially to $424.8m, and adjusted EPS went from $1.40 to $2.09. A lot more of those additional sales is reaching profit.

Revenue was near the top of the $415m-$425m guide. Profitability beat. Adjusted EPS above $1.97-$2.03 and gross margin of 61.3% above 60%-61%.

Data center did $188.1m, up 37% sequentially. Industrial and Defense grew 23% to $164.6m. Telecom was steadier at $72.1m. Orders also outpaced shipments, with a 1.4 book-to-bill and record backlog.

In the October review, I liked the optical AND copper exposure, with a broader business supporting it. This quarter shows why that broader business belongs in the discussion. Industrial and Defense is growing quickly alongside data center.

For the optical side, MACOM supplies drivers on the transmit side and transimpedance amplifiers, or TIAs, that turn the tiny current from a receiving photodiode into a usable electrical signal. Faster links need faster versions of those parts.

The nearer-term growth is coming from more 200G-per-lane drivers and TIAs for 1.6T, InP photodiodes, coherent-lite and linear pluggable optics. The refreshed 200G copper equalizers could bring new business in the second half of fiscal 2027 too.

Management’s fiscal 2027 base case is roughly 80% data-center growth and a goal above 30% for Industrial and Defense, working toward roughly $2B overall.

The actual fiscal Q1 guide, for the quarter ending January 1, 2027, is $450m-$460m of revenue, 62%-63% adjusted gross margin and $2.21-$2.29 of adjusted EPS.

NPO products are being sampled and developed with major hyperscalers. Early revenue could fall in fiscal 2027 or 2028. No named production wins or firm starting date yet.

The CW laser discussion now points to 2028. Management is encouraged by improvements to the laser facet, which is important to reliability, but that work is still being completed. The initial discussion is around 75mW and 100mW devices, with higher power further out.

Cash generation was strong. Q4 operating cash flow of $155.5m, against $17.5m of capex. Full-year operating cash flow reached $357.1m, up from $235.4m.

Capex is expected to rise from $64.4m to $100m-$110m in fiscal 2027, largely inside existing fabs. Outsourced SiGe foundries also want firm commitments, and assembly and test capacity needs to expand.

The CFO sees 25-50 basis points of quarterly gross-margin progress and adjusted operating margin above 40% during fiscal

  1. A more measured pace after this quarter’s big jump.

QCOM

Nearly $2B of automotive revenue in one quarter. Qualcomm’s auto business is getting big.

The overall result is still down. Adjusted revenue of $10.44B, down 7%, and adjusted EPS of $2.17, down 28%. GAAP revenue of $10.32B includes a $120m customer settlement.

Handsets fell 26% to $5.14B.

Automotive grew 89% to $1.99B and IoT grew 4% to $1.89B.

Industrial, networking and robotics grew 20% inside IoT. Memory constraints held back consumer devices. The 4% headline hides a stronger Physical AI business.

Auto and IoT together grew about 36%, yet QCT chip revenue fell 8%. That is how much the handset decline is taking out.

Auto is now at roughly an $8B annualized revenue rate. Last quarter management was talking about around $7B. Full-year automotive revenue was $6B, up 52%.

This is more compute going into vehicles and Qualcomm winning more of it. They are moving beyond individual chips into integrated compute modules, so there is more revenue attached to each vehicle program. Management said the acceleration came from those platform ramps, rather than customers buying ahead of price increases.

That was the content argument in in my recent article on QCOM. It is showing up in shipments now.

Then data center. More than $650m expected in the December quarter. $5B for fiscal 2027. Roughly $7B annualized as they exit the year. This could change the revenue mix quickly.

Two hyperscalers, including Amazon, drive most of the $5B. Custom AI accelerators first, then connectivity. Merchant CPUs and accelerators matter more in the fiscal 2029 plan.

AWS custom accelerators began shipping in October and connectivity ramps in November. A second optical-connectivity customer is secured.

Three custom-silicon and AI-accelerator customers overall. Two drive most of fiscal 2027.

Supply supports the $5B target, management says, but could limit upside beyond it.

The Amazon warrant arrangement covers up to $60B of potential purchases over ten years. $60B! Purchases still have to happen, and warrants are part of the economics.

Non-handset revenue should exceed half of QCT in fiscal 2027. The mix could look very different a year from now.

The earnings take longer. Next-quarter guidance is $10.4B-$11.2B of revenue and $2.15-$2.35 adjusted EPS. The EPS midpoint is only a little above this quarter’s $2.17.

Input costs are up. Price increases start helping in fiscal Q2, with the full benefit expected in the second half of fiscal 2027. The custom-silicon business can also have lower gross margins while still producing attractive operating profit. Development spending and the scale of each program affect how much gets through to earnings.

Data Center moves into QCT in fiscal 2027. Its existing development costs take about three points off QCT’s margin, without changing consolidated earnings. That helps explain the 20%-22% QCT EBT margin guide versus 24% this quarter.

Apple’s chip and licensing relationships are separate. Apple is replacing Qualcomm modems, but QTL renewed LTA’s with Apple, Huawei and Transsion. The licensing outlook is unchanged.

Qualcomm also supplies Starlink Mobile’s terrestrial U.S. 5G network. Ground equipment, with revenue late in fiscal 2027 and more in 2028.

Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow was $11.15B, capex $2.49B and free cash flow about $8.65B, down a third. Dividends and buybacks totaled $11.67B, exceeding free cash flow.

The auto ramp is already large. The data-center ramp has specific programs and a schedule behind it. Now they have to grow enough to get past that handset decline. The revenue mix is changing faster than the total earnings right now.

AEVA

First optical revenue!

Ultra-high-power chipsets shipped to the existing joint-development partner for integration and validation. Hardware with the customer, and initial optical revenue booked.

Back in October, I wanted more progress before putting more money into Aeva. I also found it hard to put a defensible price target on it. Getting chipsets into the optical partner’s hands is a more concrete step than another product announcement.

That partner makes optical engines and is working on NPO for a major hyperscaler. Integration and tape-outs are expected in early 2027.

Hyperscaler qualification is targeted to start by mid-2027 and finish late in the year. Production could start in late 2027, then ramp in early 2028.

A second optical customer is negotiating for a different hyperscaler, with potential annual volumes in the millions from

  1. Not an award yet. The existing partner is exploring another hyperscaler too.

The products include the new UHP laser, the earlier semiconductor optical amplifier, SPECTRA multiwavelength arrays and MESA external-source assemblies. Several pieces of the optical system.

Aeva quotes over 350mW at 50°C for the laser and over 600mW per channel for MESA. Performance and reliability still need customer qualification ahead of volume manufacturing.

Micro-Epsilon signed a multi-year agreement with an initial order for several thousand sensors. Actual industrial units ordered.

Micro-Epsilon already ships more than 100,000 precision sensors a year across its business. Aeva’s order is several thousand, but it is getting into an established channel with customers who already buy precision measurement equipment. Much easier to understand the route to customers here.

Daimler Truck’s production-intent Atlas sensors are shipping from the automated line. Aeva passed the European passenger OEM’s VDA audit. Bendix development is complete, with the production agreement still in negotiation.

Aeva is NVIDIA DRIVE Hyperion’s reference lidar supplier outside China and delivered its first unit for validation. Management expects OEM deployments around 2028-2029.

Also development work with the Air Force and Navy on airborne autonomy. No production volume or revenue amount given for that yet.

Revenue was $6.67m, up 86% year over year and 9% sequentially. Product revenue of $3.50m more than doubled year over year and rose 38% sequentially. Product shipment volume grew over 50% from last quarter.

Products reached 53% of revenue, up from 42%. But product gross margin is only about 4%, versus 66% for services. That mix pulled overall margin down to 33.6%. More shipments now need better unit economics.

Adjusted operating loss was $27.7m, above last quarter and slightly worse than a year ago.

Cash and securities were $147.5m, plus a $125m undrawn facility, giving $272.5m of available liquidity. The facility was extended by two years in November.

Management also said its existing foundry and manufacturing agreements provide two to three years of supply visibility. CoreVision module capacity is being installed, with additional optical capacity being secured as more customers enter the picture.

So all in all, good progress. Still pre-inflection and high risk.

OUST

Ouster’s shipment mix shows how much they have changed.

More than 27,000 sensors shipped. Over 7,000 lidar units and over 20,000 cameras. Lidar was only about 27% of the total units.

Last year’s roughly 7,200 shipments were lidar. Different products and selling prices in this year’s total.

This was the idea in my June deep dive. Ouster could supply more of the perception system, with lidar, cameras and software working together. It didn’t need lidar to be the answer to every sensing job.

Camera production more than doubled sequentially as they added capacity. A robot might use a lot of cameras. Mapping or surveying can need more lidar. With Stereolabs in the business, Ouster can sell into both.

Rev8 combines native color with lidar depth data without the same separate camera-alignment process. Hundreds of customers have chosen it, with thousands of sensors in backlog.

Forterra and John Deere’s GUSS plan to use it, alongside mapping and wildfire-response applications. The same sensing capability across different jobs.

ZED Box 2 adds compute and sensor fusion. Gemini combines large lidar deployments. BlueCity won a multimillion-dollar expansion for another 100 intersections.

Gemini supports up to 100 lidar sensors on one server. Some customers operate across 500-plus sites. Native color helps the system read signs and traffic lights.

They still don’t give separate software revenue or attach rates. Those would help show how much more revenue comes with each deployment as customers add the other pieces.

Revenue was $59.3m, up 50% year over year and about 9% from last quarter. Product revenue was $56m, up 42% year over year and 6% sequentially. Royalties made up the other $3.3m.

Fifteen consecutive quarters of product-revenue growth.

On the filing’s combined figures, total revenue grew about 36% and product revenue roughly 29%. Still healthy with both businesses in last year’s base.

Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to $3.4m from $8.4m. GAAP operating loss was $19.3m, including $11.5m of stock compensation.

They have about $421m of cash, restricted cash and short-term investments after the equity raises. The July offering alone brought in roughly $191m net. That gives them room to fund these ramps.

The CFO says that funding is sufficient for current operating objectives, without expecting another capital raise. Management is also keeping its 5%-8% full-year operating-expense growth framework, adjusted for the specified tax refund and acquisition costs, despite including roughly eleven months of Stereolabs. They are trying to grow the product business much faster than the expense base.

Q4 revenue is guided to $61m-$64m, including roughly $3m of royalties.

Management expects Rev8 to go from hundreds of units in Q3 to thousands in Q4, then tens of thousands by the end of fiscal 2027.

Planned annual lidar capacity reaches 100,000 units by the end of 2027.

Cameras ramping, Rev8 getting selected, BlueCity expanding. The different parts are starting to find their way into customer systems.

GFS

GFS is getting more of the optical buildout.

Silicon photonics, silicon germanium and advanced packaging. Look at the mix change.

Revenue of $1.899B, up 13% year over year and 6% sequentially. Record adjusted gross margin of 31.4%, up 540 basis points. Adjusted EPS of $0.53 versus $0.41.

Communications Infrastructure and Data Center grew 81% to $316m. Now 17% of revenue, versus 10% a year ago. Automotive grew 20% to $368m, and Home and Industrial IoT grew 49% to $384m.

Mobile went the other way, down 19% to $611m. Its share of revenue fell from 45% to 32%.

In the earlier Marvell discussion, this was the interesting part for GFS. More SiGe demand means more manufacturing work, and more of that work can improve the revenue mix. The numbers are starting to reflect it.

Silicon photonics is expected to do roughly $500m in 2026. Now they are targeting a $1B annualized rate exiting 2027. A full year earlier than before.

Silicon-photonics capacity should roughly triple in 2027. More than 50 customers, including seven of the ten largest transceiver suppliers, with designs ramping in 2027 and 2028.

SiGe demand is also above available capacity. The expanded Marvell relationship supports more production in Burlington for pluggables, NPO and CPO.

Optical capacity is booked through 2027 and increasingly into 2028. Customers want assurance into 2029 and beyond and are willing to help fund expansion. Tools need installing and qualifying now.

Silicon photonics handles the optical functions. SiGe helps with the fast electrical interfaces around them. Bring the optics closer to the processor and you can need more work in both, plus packaging and fiber attachment. Several ways for GFS to participate.

There is also a separate Physical AI business developing around MIPS, ARC processor IP and custom design. Management puts the potential lifetime revenue from Q3’s new custom-silicon design wins at nearly $1B, including radar, real-time control and a data-center board-management chip.

TSMC’s interposer agreement starts at $2B over five years, with volume ramping in the first half of 2028. TSMC co-invests. Management expects gross margins above GFS’s long-term targets.

Interposers connect compute and memory inside the package. Another piece of the AI system for GFS.

The U.S.-Japan fab arrangement is interesting for a different reason. Up to $21B is planned for two new fabs in Malta, with first capacity expected in 2029. The government-owned project vehicle funds and owns the infrastructure and equipment. GFS supplies the technology, construction management and operating work, earning fees, reimbursements and a share of EBITDA.

Think about the capital involved in owning those fabs yourself. Here, the project vehicle pays for them. Management thinks profit per wafer could be comparable to owning the fabs, without GFS putting up the construction money. That would be a very useful way to expand.

GFS still plans roughly $4B of its own net capex in 2027, including interposers, optics and other expansion.

Customer prepayments should fund about a third.

This quarter, operating cash flow was $341m against $301m spent on property, equipment and intangibles. Grants of $96m reduced net capex and brought adjusted free cash flow to $136m.

Q4 guidance is $2B of revenue, plus or minus $25m.

Adjusted gross margin of 31.5%, plus or minus a point, and adjusted EPS of $0.59, plus or minus $0.05.

Gross-margin goals remain 35% exiting 2027 and 40% exiting 2028. Utilization stayed in the high 80s while capacity expanded.

The CFO sees roughly 10% uplift in blended selling prices and mix for 2027.

CGNX

Record revenue of $313m, up 13%. Adjusted EPS of $0.53, up 61%. GAAP operating margin at 32.2%, versus 20.9% a year ago.

More than eleven points of operating-margin improvement in one year. From 13% revenue growth. That is a lot more profit coming through.

Revenue was inside the $300m-$320m guide and EPS was inside $0.50-$0.54. The 35.5% adjusted EBITDA margin beat the 32%-35% range.

Last year included a one-time commercial-partnership benefit. Excluding it from the comparison, revenue grew 19% and adjusted EPS 89%.

Adjusted gross margin went from 68.4% to 71.3%. Better mix, more volume and faster pricing execution, enough to offset higher memory costs.

And operating expenses came down. Adjusted opex fell 7% to around $116m. So they sold more, kept more gross profit from those sales, and spent less running the business. The pieces all helped earnings this quarter.

Adjusted EBITDA reached $111m at a 35.5% margin. Its increase exceeded the added revenue because costs were falling too.

The next stage is getting more growth from the resources already in place. The CFO described roughly 60% incremental revenue-to-EBITDA conversion for 2027, with operating expenses growing around inflation.

The business is now generating much more profit from the sales already coming through.

Logistics, packaging, consumer electronics and semiconductor grew double digits and automotive mid-single digits.

Logistics has grown double digits for eleven straight quarters. Really consistent.

And management says much of it is customers improving existing warehouses. It has not seen an acceleration in new warehouse construction. Better identification and routing can pay off inside the buildings already operating. Easier installation and higher-value vision systems also give Cognex ways to improve the economics of those deployments.

The full-year logistics view rises from high-single-digit to double-digit growth. Automotive remains flat to low-single-digit.

Operating cash flow was $106m, capex under $3m and free cash flow $104m. Free cash flow exceeded adjusted net income for both the quarter and trailing twelve months.

Then RealSense. The deal was announced in September and is expected to close in Q4. Roughly $500m, paid from existing cash and investments.

RealSense gives robots depth information about the space around them. That takes Cognex further into robotic perception, alongside the identification and inspection work it already does. You can see the connection with Ouster here. Different sensors and software helping a machine understand where it is and what is around it.

The case starts with fixed-arm robots and mobile warehouse and factory robots already in use. Humanoids could add demand later. Integrating RealSense comes before another acquisition.

RealSense expects $80m-$90m of 2026 revenue, up over 50%.

Cognex had $846m of cash and investments and no debt before the deal.

The Q4 guide excludes RealSense. Revenue of $270m-$290m, adjusted EBITDA margin of 29%-32%, and adjusted EPS of $0.37-$0.41.

Down from the record Q3, but still about 11% revenue growth and 44% EPS growth year over year at the midpoints.

Electronics seasonality, slightly lower logistics revenue and fewer selling days explain the sequential decline

The full-year revenue midpoint goes up $13m to $1.153B. Adjusted EPS goes from $1.66 to $1.70 at the midpoint.

BKSY

In the October review, I was still uncertain about how meaningful a Physical AI business BlackSky could become. This quarter gives us something much more concrete to work with.

Revenue of $42.8m, up 118% year over year. Adjusted EBITDA of $12.8m, or about a 30% margin. Intelligence and AI services did $32.7m, compared with $15.8m a year ago.

Now look at the cost of delivering those services. Revenue increased by roughly $17m. The corresponding cost of sales increased by only about $0.6m.

More customers using the satellites and software already built. That is where the economics get interesting.

The HEO agreement is a good practical example of getting more out of those assets. Its annual seven-figure extension uses BlackSky satellites to inspect other objects in orbit, including at night when they would otherwise have idle capacity. Another paying use for the same constellation.

Management said operating cash flow turned positive in Q3 without an unusual customer prepayment driving it.

The intelligence-services annual run rate is now above $100m. It includes pilots as well as subscriptions. Some pilots are sizeable, and revenue can pause between an evaluation ending and a larger contract starting.

The new SkyFi and Robinhood work is also a several-month, nonexclusive pilot. It is testing whether imagery and services can reach more customers through an app and subscription offering. A different route to market, with the paid demand still being tested.

Then the $1B space-AI initiative.

BlackSky is the exclusive very-high-resolution optical provider for an international initiative initially covering 50 satellites, including other sensor types.

Actual backlog is $355m, with about $45m expected to convert in Q4.

Q4 plans include two more commercial Gen-3 launches and three sovereign satellite deliveries. A delivery can mean hardware leaving production or reaching a contractual milestone, before it is operating in orbit.

And this explains an important part of the guide. More lower-margin sovereign and satellite deliveries can lift revenue while pulling EBITDA down from Q3. Pilot timing can move the service mix around as well. The quarter they just reported had particularly favorable service economics.

Full-year guidance is $140m-$150m revenue and $18m-$24m adjusted EBITDA, with $50m-$60m capex. Revenue and EBITDA floors rose and ceilings stayed unchanged.

More monitoring, pilots and nighttime inspection from the same assets, alongside sovereign deliveries with different margins. Much more tangible than the project headlines alone.
