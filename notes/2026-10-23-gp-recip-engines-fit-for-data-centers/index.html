SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed gas reciprocating engines for primary, bridge, backup, and grid-connected data center power. The expert emphasized their rapid starting, strong load-following ability, modularity, and compatibility with battery storage. However, large installations require substantial maintenance, staffing, spare-parts support, and careful redundancy planning.

Key Insights

Bridge and islanded power are the most operationally sensitive configurations because fuel or equipment failures can interrupt the entire data center. Large engines generally provide smoother load response and reduce equipment count, while smaller engines offer greater redundancy and operating flexibility. Battery systems can absorb sudden demand, smooth load patterns, and provide temporary power while engines restart. Isolated plants should have at least one redundant unit, with two additional units potentially preferable for bridge applications. The expert strongly favored OEM service agreements for new, mission-critical projects because remote monitoring, engineering support, and parts guarantees reduce operational risk. Maintenance intensity is the main disadvantage versus gas turbines. Very large engine fleets will experience frequent individual failures and require skilled personnel. There is no strict technical limit on fleet size, but land, emissions equipment, service capacity, parts logistics, and organizational complexity become practical constraints.
