  7. 尽管供应较2-3年前明显改善，专家强调当前GPU产能依然是真实瓶颈，云端预留实例（RI）与按需实例（On-demand）依然难抢、Spot实例虽有供给但不可预测；整个AI技术栈中硬基础设施层（CPU/GPU）仍是确定性赢家，而应用层价值归属仍高度不明。

SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, who previously worked on GPU partnerships and products at NVIDIA, compared Snowflake and Databricks as enterprise data platforms and discussed their roles in AI. Snowflake is a proprietary cloud database that separates storage from compute, while Databricks builds on open-source Spark and offers more deployment flexibility.

The discussion covered AI adoption, model routing, GPU availability, platform competition, and where value may accrue across the AI technology stack.

Key Insights

Databricks appears better positioned for AI workloads. The expert attributed this to its earlier GPU support and stronger handling of unstructured and multimodal data. AI is not yet the main consumption driver for Snowflake. Its core warehouse business remains central, and the expert was unconvinced that its AI products are generating comparable usage. Databricks may have more AI-related activity, but revenue quality is uncertain. Some reported revenue may be passed through to model providers, and the expert could not establish how much benefits Databricks directly. Production use of enterprise data with large language models remains unresolved. The expert sees privacy, data movement, cost, and technical challenges as barriers to connecting vast data stores to AI agents. GPU access remains a constraint. Organizations may secure capacity before they have workloads ready, creating utilization and return-on-investment risks. The infrastructure layer is currently capturing substantial AI value. The expert views future value distribution between AI platforms and applications as uncertain. Customers often use both platforms rather than replace one with the other. Competition is more evident in notebooks and some machine-learning workloads; Databricks has an advantage for multimodal and unstructured data.
