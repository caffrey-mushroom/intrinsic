Key line from the release: “More than $3.5 trillion of AI infrastructure spending could be exposed to impairment by the end of 2030, if AI providers continue their current pace of capital spending.” The math: ~$1T of spend this year and ~$6T more in 2027-30 needs ~$8.5T of cumulative revenue to cover capex, financing and a 12% return, vs. Gartner’s ~$3.6T of AI-attributable revenue.

The backdrop is worse than one name however as all this financing focus has put circular financing back in focus: a snippet passed along to us from a sell-side credit call said that data-center financing has “effectively stopped,” with a META-backed DC deal pricing at 7.5% vs. 6.5% expected, QTS settling for a 5-year non-call-2 because a 10-year wouldn’t clear, and no DC issuance for one to two months despite a healthy pipeline, as it’s become impossible for DC developers to issue debt at 8-8.5% and keep the equity economics attractive enough to build.

Some were flagging CDS among hyperscalers blowing out:

Part of this is hedging rather than anything to read-through on credit: lenders and bond buyers taking down the wave of AI financing (GPU- and TPU-backed deals, data-center debt, etc.) hedge by buying CDS on the names standing behind it, which pushes spreads wider across the complex regardless of the fundamentals. AVGO’s deal is the cleanest example: it backstops the bonds that finance its customers’ TPU buys. Buyers of those bonds hedge the backstop by buying AVGO CDS, which gaps wider on an otherwise high-quality name; the move hits the WSJ and X → the headline scares more holders into hedging → and the loop feeds itself. The irony: what starts as prudent risk management ends up looking like evidence of the very risk investors are hedging.

All of this into Anthropic’s closed-door investor day in SF - lots of rumors and talk being passed around on X, but nothing substantive which is worth passing along.
