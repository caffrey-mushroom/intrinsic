Agenda

  • Chinese automakers’ cost control pressure, plus subsequent battery supply selection rationale for vehicle models of different market positionings
  • CATL – technological edges and product quality gaps over peers, product premium updates, plus the future trends of these gaps
  • Development momentum and subsequent potential for Sunwoda (SHE: 300207) and CALB (HKG: 3931), plus solid-state battery competitiveness expectations for CATL (HKG: 3750) and BYD (HKG: 1211)

Key Insights

  • Battery consumption tax is currently borne by battery enterprises, and automakers and battery manufacturers are projected to discuss their respective sharing proportions during the next agreement variation cycle. On average, automakers bear 70-80% of the price fluctuations for lithium carbonate and battery cell materials
  • Currently, regarding LFP technology and manufacturing processes, the differences between major players are estimated to be within an acceptable, narrow range, but CATL still maintains an edge in ternary batteries, with its current ternary battery premium estimated to be above 10% and the corresponding LFP premium in the high single digits
  • Battery enterprises are estimated to lean more towards "securing volume and market share", but expert projects that CATL will not aggressively slash prices. Automakers are currently relatively aggressive in switching suppliers; however, as the life cycle of a new vehicle model is only about two years, they can switch back later if problems arise
  • Automakers are highly proactive about adopting semi-solid-state batteries in vehicles, and expert projects that if the progress of all-solid-state batteries is relatively slow, it cannot be ruled out that major manufacturers will acquire some small semi-solid-state companies. If all-solid-state batteries achieve mature application in the future, market share is expected to consolidate further towards leading players
