Companies: A5G IE, PTSB IE, BIRG IE

Graham Jenkin (GJ) Lecturer, Business & Workforce Management at Charles Darwin University

Max Harper (MH) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • Shifts in the Irish banking industry following PTSB's sale to BAWAG, plus competition from challengers, looking at read-through and defensibility of AIB's (LON: AIBG) position
  • Income outlook, ability to grow NII and expand NIM from rate rises amid rising competition and potential to expand non-NII channels
  • OPEX management, assessing AIB's ability to leverage AI and keep CIR (cost-to-income ratio) below the target of less than 50% towards 2030

Key Insights

  • Irish customers over 40 remain highly risk-averse, preserving AIB's current account stickiness against digital rivals. To counter potential weaker loyalty among younger demographics, AIB can deploy targeted incentives rather than broad back-book repricing
  • Customers may not be thinking about fees on their accounts, but increased visibility of charges such as the EUR 72 annual fee could become a significant price-point consideration, harming retention, despite not being out of step with incumbent competitors
  • AIB's non-interest revenue has hovered around EUR 400m for years, and expert considers surpassing EUR 500m improbable without long-term upgrades to wealth or insurance offerings. Cross-selling could drive growth, but raising average products per customer from three to beyond five remains a target and not reality
  • Proposed Irish Investment Account launching in 2027 could significantly boost savings flows – a previous similar government scheme, SSIA, drove substantial deposit growth, representing a potential opportunity for AIB
