SUMMARY

Overview

The expert manages nearly 5,000 whole-home and condominium vacation rentals, primarily in nonurban beach, mountain and lake destinations across the southeastern United States.

The discussion covered weaker-than-expected 3Q26 performance, stable 4Q26 expectations, encouraging early 2027 bookings, distribution trends across online travel agencies, and the potential for AI tools to redirect travelers toward lower-cost direct bookings.

Key Insights

3Q26 disappointed: Occupancy was flat and average daily rate declined slightly, versus an internal expectation for approximately 3% growth. Higher fuel costs, geopolitical disruption and weaker consumer confidence reportedly reduced demand. Travelers are trading down: Expensive destinations and premium properties underperformed more affordable markets and less costly alternatives, such as ocean-view rather than oceanfront homes. Early 2027 demand appears strong: First-quarter bookings benefit from Easter timing and returning winter travelers to southern destinations. Preliminary second-quarter demand is also encouraging, though the sample is limited. Booking.com faces a serious structural problem: The expert considers its cancellation levels unsustainable for whole-home rentals. Portfolio exposure has been reduced until policies improve. AI could weaken OTA pricing power: Direct bookings are typically materially cheaper. The expert believes better search and comparison tools may help travelers locate credible property managers directly, especially when supported by strong reviews. Airbnb may face greater long-term AI risk: Its narrower product offering could leave it more exposed than Booking.com or Expedia, which provide broader travel services. The expert also questioned whether Airbnb’s exclusive inventory represents a meaningful share of revenue.
