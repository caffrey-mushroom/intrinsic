Agenda

  • Internal deployment scale, active user adoption and daily utilisation patterns of Harvey AI in law firms
  • Demonstrated ROI, efficiency metrics and workflow speed-up across various use cases
  • Impact of AI-driven efficiency on traditional billable-hour revenue models, client fee structures and fixed-fee alternative arrangements
  • Potential threats from LLM (large language model) providers such as OpenAI and Anthropic to Harvey's core value proposition

Key Insights

  • Harvey and Legora are both displacing older machine learning and natural language processing legal AI tools and expert systems, given generative AI's agentic capabilities now replicate what those legacy platforms once exclusively offered
  • Harvey's agent-built workflows create significant switching costs as firms embed institutional knowledge, precedents and know-how directly into platform-specific agents that cannot be easily migrated elsewhere
  • LexisNexis and Westlaw initially prioritised bundling their own generative AI over third-party integrations, but market pressure is forcing them to accommodate platforms such as Harvey, although integrations remain underdeveloped
  • Harvey's edge over Legora stems from its professionalised support infrastructure, including customer success and legal engineering teams proactively deployed to customers. Market leadership between Harvey and Legora alternates every few months as each platform rapidly ships new features
  • Pricing conversations are shifting towards outcome-based models, where AI-driven speed to closing a deal could justify higher fees rather than passing savings to clients
