SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed NVIDIA and TSMC’s optics-on-interposer (OOI) approach and how it compares with co-packaged optics (CPO), near-packaged optics (NPO), and pluggable modules. CPO places optical components close to a switch; OOI would integrate them on the same interposer as computing chips and memory. The expert sees OOI as a potential route to lower power and higher bandwidth, but one that requires more substantial changes to packaging, manufacturing, and system architecture.

Key Insights

The expert views OOI as a longer-term architectural shift, not simply a more integrated version of CPO. It could enable optical links closer to GPUs and eventually support broader optical switching. The central challenge is combining materials with different thermal behavior, manufacturing processes, yields, and lifespans. The expert considers this integration problem more difficult than improving packaging capacity or yields alone. OOI would increase testing complexity: performance issues could stem from the chip, optical coupling, materials, or process, making fault diagnosis and responsibility allocation harder. A prototype around 2030 was considered possible, but the expert expects mass production to take longer. CPO and OOI are seen as complementary because they address different network layers. The expert expressed strong optimism about the longer-term potential for all-optical networks and optical computing if integration challenges are resolved.
