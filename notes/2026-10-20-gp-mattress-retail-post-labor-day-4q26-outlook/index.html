SUMMARY

Overview

An expert from a regional mattress retailer with 24 corporate stores and one franchise in Washington assessed recent demand, consumer behavior, brand performance, and the outlook through 2028.

Third-quarter same-store sales were below expectations, although calendar timing shifted Labor Day demand from August into September. Combined August and September performance was roughly flat, suggesting demand remains near a trough rather than entering a strong recovery. October improved modestly despite housing weakness, inflation, and elevated fuel costs.

Premium demand remains resilient, while low-income consumers are under pressure and increasingly shop online. Mid-market demand has begun recovering, particularly around the $800–$1,000 price range.

Key Insights

The expert expects 4Q26 same-store sales to be roughly flat to modestly positive, supported by a strong Black Friday. Revenue growth is driven by pricing and mix rather than unit volume. Units likely remain lower year over year. Premium mattresses above $2,000 continue to perform well, but accessories and adjustable-base attachments remain weak as consumers prioritize mattresses over optional products. Online mattress penetration may have peaked. Some customers are returning to stores after experiencing durability or service limitations with lower-priced online products. The expert believes insufficient industry advertising has reduced consumer awareness and contributed to declining unit demand. A meaningful industry recovery likely requires lower fuel costs, reduced interest rates, and stronger housing activity. The expert expects better growth prospects in 2028 than in 2027.
