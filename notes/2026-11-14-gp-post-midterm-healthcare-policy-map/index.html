核心摘要

  1. 前CMS政策与数据分析主任认为，在特朗普执政末期加上民主党控制国会的分裂格局下，近期几乎不可能出现重大医疗立法，民主党也有动机把医疗问题归咎于现任政府。
  2. 预计民主党将召开大量听证会，涉及疫苗、事前授权和医疗成本等议题，并可能提出“全民医保”或废除H.R. 1等主要用于制造声势的法案；国会可通过调查、传唤作证和立法显著拖慢或改变HHS与CMS的工作。
  3. 看似边缘的调整可能影响数十亿美元，例如通过风险编码、基准或星级评分对Medicare Advantage削减约100个基点；场地中性支付、医疗补助救济以及《无意外账单法》修补也是可能的两党合作领域。
  4. 专家认为H.R. 1作为现行法律仍将推进，农村医疗转型基金已在第二年拨付，关于医疗补助资格和补充支付的实质性调整更可能在2029年新政府上台后发生。
  5. 340B项目规模已超过每年1000亿美元，远超最初政策意图，医院经济高度依赖该项目，改革难度很大；2027年起有关支付削减将面临法律挑战。
  6. PBM改革虽有两党兴趣，但对具体方案和其对回扣、Part D和利润率的影响缺乏共识；IRA可能推高了保费，国会或审查政府与药企谈成的定价协议，但多为边缘性动作。
  7. 州层面可能更值得关注：德州、佐治亚等未扩展医疗补助的州可能在2028年就扩展进行公投，新任民主党州长可能加强网络充足性、行为健康覆盖和养老院标准的执法。
  8. AI监管将先以审查而非全面规则开始，重点是AI驱动的事前授权拒赔、患者伤害和HIPAA数据隐私；CMS最新HOPD规则中已出现十多个AI相关附加支付代码，值得关注。
  9. 专家认为未来3–5年可能出现更根本的变化，例如基于医疗补助或ACA交易所的两党安全网项目，而赤字压力最终可能促成以医疗支出为核心的两党行动。

SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examines how a newly divided government may shape U.S. healthcare policy. The expert, a longtime healthcare policy professional with experience in federal agencies, Congress, and consulting, expects limited prospects for major legislation in the near term. Instead, congressional oversight, agency implementation, and smaller policy adjustments are likely to drive change.

Potential areas of focus include Medicaid, Medicare Advantage, hospital payments, drug pricing, pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs), the 340B program, and healthcare AI. State governments may also influence coverage and enforcement, particularly through Medicaid expansion and oversight of health plans, nursing homes, and behavioral health services.

Key Insights

Major federal reform is unlikely in the near term. The expert expects hearings and headline-generating proposals, but believes meaningful legislation will be difficult to pass. Congress can substantially constrain agency action. Investigations, testimony, legislation, and oversight can slow or alter the work of HHS and CMS. Smaller policy changes may have large financial effects. Adjustments to Medicare Advantage risk coding, benchmarks, or quality measures could affect billions of dollars. State-level changes could matter materially. More active enforcement of network adequacy, behavioral health coverage, and nursing-home standards may affect patients and healthcare providers. AI oversight is likely to begin with scrutiny, not comprehensive rules. Prior authorization decisions, patient harm, and health- data privacy are likely subjects of hearings. The expert also sees emerging AI-related payment codes as an important area to watch. Deficit concerns could eventually prompt broader bipartisan action. The expert suggests healthcare spending may become a focus if lawmakers seriously pursue deficit reduction.

Healthcare AI developers and users

Adoption is advancing, but privacy compliance, coverage decisions, and payment frameworks create regulatory and reputational risks.
