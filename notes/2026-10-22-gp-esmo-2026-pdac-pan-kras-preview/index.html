SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion reviewed treatment of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, with emphasis on pan-RAS, pan-KRAS, KRAS G12D, and PRMT5 inhibitors. Current Australian first-line options are mainly FOLFIRINOX for fitter patients and gemcitabine plus nab-paclitaxel for frailer patients. NALIRIFOX is generally unavailable.

The expert viewed RAS-directed therapies as potentially transformative but considered durable disease control more important than unusually high early response rates.

Key Insights

The expert favors selective G12D inhibitors over broader RAS inhibitors for G12D-mutated disease, although comparative evidence remains preliminary. Chemotherapy-free RAS inhibition is a desirable goal. Short-interval imaging could make monotherapy trials clinically acceptable while efficacy is established. Vopimetostat plus daraxonrasib produced highly encouraging early activity, but the cohort was too small for confidence. The response rate is expected to decline as enrollment expands. Durability and median progression-free survival are more important than maximizing response rate. A lower response rate with substantially longer disease control would be preferred. PRMT5 and RAS inhibition target distinct vulnerabilities, but true synergy has not yet been demonstrated. The discontinuation of AMG 193 plus daraxonrasib creates uncertainty for the PRMT5 inhibitor class because the underlying rationale was not fully disclosed. Better tolerability alone could differentiate ERAS-4001 and potentially make it a stronger combination partner.
