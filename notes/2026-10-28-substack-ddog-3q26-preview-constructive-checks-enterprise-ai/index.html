Summary

DDOG’s 3Q26 setup remains constructive. Our repeat channel checks point to healthy demand with some seeing acceleration and above-plan performance. Existing-customer expansion is improving, new customers are buying more modules at entry, and enterprise AI deployments are creating additional demand for infrastructure monitoring, application tracing and logs. We see this as a broadening of the core observability opportunity, with DDOG also gaining share as customers consolidate fragmented toolsets and migrate from incumbent platforms. AI-native demand also remains healthy as our check suggests its largest AI customer’s usage has stabilized and resumed growth following its renewal and the guidance reset. This supports a recovery in DDOG ARR from that customer, although recognized revenue lags and is expected to decline initially. Meanwhile, continued training demand from other AI-native customers supports strong momentum into 4Q despite moderation at the other large LLM customer. Budget discipline, logging optimization and uneven partner execution remain relevant. Overall, we expect DDOG could deliver 36% YoY revenue growth in 3Q26, beating guidance by 5–6 percentage points and meeting buyside expectations. We expect the high end of 4Q26 revenue guidance to imply 30–31% YoY growth, effectively signaling stable sequential revenue growth despite a roughly 3-percentage-point tougher comparison.

3Q26 Checks and Outlook — Healthy Demand and Better Expansion

Channel Expert A — Global GSI

  • 3Q26 growth: The practice’s final 2Q26 growth was 34.2%, within the prior 33–35% expectation. Preliminary 3Q26 growth is estimated to have slightly accelerated to 34.6–34.8%. This is in line with the previously raised 32–37% second-half target, with the expert describing underlying performance as slightly better than expected. Strength is broad across the traditional observability portfolio, particularly logs, rather than dependent on a single new product.- 4Q26 and 2027 outlook: The GSI expects approximately 34–35% growth in 4Q26, allowing for seasonality, and discussed an initial 1H27 range of 32–36% that is not yet finalized. These expectations support continued healthy demand beyond 3Q.

Channel Expert B — European Systems Integrator

  • 3Q26 beat and better expansion: 3Q26 growth is trending around 25%, versus a 20% expectation and approximately 20% in 2Q26.- Existing-customer expansion: Existing-customer spend growth improved to 12–15% from 8–10% in the first half.- New-logo mix: New customers increasingly start with two or three modules rather than one. More opportunities converted than planned, supported by upsell, cross-sell and preparation for AI workloads. Budget limits remain, but the check points to a healthier expansion engine than last quarter.- 4Q26 and 2027 outlook: The European integrator expects approximately 25% growth in 4Q26 and a 30% starting forecast for 2027; the expert’s personal upside view is closer to 35%. Customer expansion and consolidation opportunities underpin the outlook, rather than a large immediate contribution from Bits AI.

Channel Expert C — North American Reseller Subscribed

  • Annual growth: DDOG license resale reached approximately $26m in the year ended June 2026, up 73% YoY from $15m, following growth from $6m to $15m (+150%) in the preceding year. These figures exclude services and cover the expert’s North American observability practice.- Quarterly trend: The expert did not provide individual quarterly growth rates, but described growth as fairly evenly spread across quarters. When asked specifically whether the September quarter was above plan, the expert said DDOG had been “above trend all year long, every year,” attributing this to the team’s positioning of DDOG and close vendor collaboration. This supports a consistently strong qualitative trend, while the quantified growth figure remains annual.- Outlook absent reorganization: Based on DDOG’s trajectory and active deal pipeline when the expert left, the practice could have reached $35–40m in the year ending June 2027 under normal operating conditions, implying approximately 35–54% YoY growth. This was the expert’s DDOG-specific assessment; the separately cited 13% growth target applied to the broader observability practice.- Reorganization impact: The reseller subsequently shifted its focus toward services rather than licensing and solution resale. According to the expert’s conversations with former colleagues, results were far below target, and the expert no longer expected the $35–40m outcome to be achieved. The call therefore combines strong historical DDOG momentum and a healthy underlying pipeline with a material partner-execution headwind; it did not provide a revised DDOG revenue forecast.

Customer Expert E — Global Software Platform

  • Spend and usage growth: Annual DDOG spend is approximately $1.2–1.3m, alongside $500–600k on Sumo Logic and roughly $200k on CloudWatch. DDOG usage grew approximately 22% in 2026, versus an average of roughly 26% annually since 2021 and a peak near 40% in 2024–25. Usage remains healthy but has moderated as the customer reduces noise and optimizes consumption; the 22% figure is a usage estimate rather than a separately verified bill-growth rate.- 2027 outlook: DDOG and Sumo Logic contracts expire around July–August 2027. Next-year DDOG growth depends on an ongoing Observe/Snowflake evaluation; the expert did not provide a firm 2027 growth forecast. Continued usage growth and a potential vendor change therefore need to be assessed separately.

Enterprise AI Is Expanding the Core Observability Budget

Checks suggest enterprise AI adoption continues to drive strong incremental consumption of DDOG’s existing products. Agents add API calls, retrieval events, model gateways, vector databases and supporting cloud services. Customers need to monitor the infrastructure underneath these workflows, trace failures across applications and retain the logs required for investigation. This creates monetization through Infra, APM and Logs before dedicated AI modules become a material revenue stream.

Channel Expert A — Core Products Capture the First Spending Wave

  • Core demand: The expert attributed roughly three-quarters of practice growth drivers to traditional products, including logs and security. AI-related products and specialized offerings account for the balance, although the categories overlap: AI-induced infrastructure consumption still appears in the traditional product bucket. The expert also described accelerating demand within non-AI-native customer industries. We therefore view enterprise AI as a driver within core growth, rather than adding a separate AI growth contribution on top of it.- AI-driven spend uplift: For incremental enterprise AI deployments, the expert estimated first-year infrastructure ACV uplift of roughly 8–18%, with larger increases possible in more intensive deployments, and logging spend uplift of approximately 15–35%. These are indicative customer-level estimates, not company-wide growth assumptions. The mechanism matters more than extrapolating the exact ranges: agent adoption expands the monitored estate and the volume of operational data generated by each workflow.

Channel Expert B — Logs Lead the Expansion

  • Logging expansion: The European channel sees the strongest incremental demand in logging, with customers deploying DDOG ahead of broader agent adoption and sometimes before existing Splunk contracts expire. The expert conservatively estimated roughly 20% first-year DDOG spend growth for customers expanding logging, followed by another 10–15% in each of the next two years. That pathway combines AI-driven usage, migration of existing logs and additional teams adopting the platform; it is not a pure AI-volume sensitivity.

Customer Expert D — Canadian Insurance Provider’s Multi-Year Example

  • AI-driven customer expansion: The Canadian business is in its first full year of DDOG use, with estimated 2026 spend of CAD400–450k. The expert expects spend to reach CAD600–650k by December 2028, with most of the approximately CAD200k increase from the upper-end baseline linked to AI-agent-related APM, infrastructure, security and associated telemetry. The mechanism is broader deployment and higher consumption across the supporting architecture, rather than a wholesale replacement of traditional observability with a new AI product.- Spend timing and budget conditions: The timing is important. This is a two-year expansion pathway, not a CAD200k annual increase or a 2027 commitment.- Conditional upside: An additional flex budget could take spend toward a CAD850k ceiling only after demonstrable efficiency gains.- Contract scope: The CAD2.1m three-year contract also includes vendor resources and consulting, so it should not be divided into a clean annual software revenue estimate. The case supports the durability of enterprise observability demand while showing that rollout capacity and ROI govern the spending curve.

Customer Expert E — AI Workloads Expand Existing Monitoring

  • Core consumption before new AI modules: New EKS components, an orchestration agent and AWS Bedrock invocation logs are driving incremental DDOG demand. The customer has not adopted a dedicated LLM Observability module. The expert initially put AI’s contribution at 5–8% of growth, then reconsidered and suggested that most incremental growth may be AI-related. We retain the directional conclusion without treating either attribution as a measured contribution.- AI spending scale: Combined Glean, Bedrock and selected Databricks Genie spending is approximately $8–10k per day, or a roughly $3m annual run rate. The expert’s 6–7% observability-to-cloud-spend ratio describes the existing cloud cost base, not a validated ratio for incremental AI spending, which also substitutes for labor.

Consolidation Is Supporting DDOG Share Gains

Our checks suggest DDOG is winning a larger role as enterprises reduce the number of observability tools they operate. The purchasing rationale combines broader platform coverage, lower maintenance burden and fewer contracts. AI increases the complexity that customers need to manage, while consolidation creates an additional route for DDOG to capture existing budgets.

Channel Experts A and B — Incumbent Replacement and Larger Enterprise Reach

  • Enterprise wins and Splunk displacement: The European integrator reports improving DDOG win rates against Dynatrace in large enterprises, although Dynatrace remains a strong competitor. Both vendors are benefiting from Splunk migrations as customers seek to reduce complexity and cost. Some customers operate DDOG alongside Splunk for the remaining months of an existing contract, which makes the expansion gradual rather than an immediate full-budget transfer. Additional applications, security teams and business entities can then extend the migration over multiple years.- Consolidation pipeline: The GSI has two seven-figure opportunities under discussion: one customer uses AppDynamics and Splunk; the other operates a collection of open-source tools that new management no longer wants to maintain internally. These opportunities illustrate two sources of consolidation demand—legacy vendor replacement and the operating cost of a fragmented in-house stack. They remain pipeline, with possible slippage from 4Q into January because of holiday timing and extended POCs.

Channel Expert C — Historical Resale Gains Demonstrate Platform Appeal

  • Relative channel performance: Alongside DDOG’s resale expansion, Dynatrace resale declined from approximately $20m to $16m to $6m over the same three fiscal years. This supports DDOG gaining share within the covered channel; it is not a measure of industry-wide market share.- Drivers of share gains: The expert attributed DDOG’s progress to broad infrastructure, networking and application coverage, a dedicated observability team and stronger partner incentives. This is useful evidence of DDOG gaining share within a specific sales channel, rather than a like-for-like measure of end-market share.

Customer Expert E — Consolidation Also Creates Renewal Risk

  • Conditional replacement evaluation: The customer is evaluating replacing DDOG and Sumo Logic with Observe/Snowflake at the July–August 2027 renewals. The expert assigns a roughly 50/50 probability, with APM and KEDA scaling gaps still unresolved. DDOG remains deeply embedded, generating 80–90% of alerts, and the extensive dashboards and reporting would require one to three months of parallel operation for a migration.- Lower unit cost can fund broader coverage: The proposed alternative claims pricing close to half the current level, but the expert expects the total observability budget could remain similar if savings fund more retained load-balancer logs. This supports healthy category demand while illustrating that consolidation can redirect spend away from DDOG. Custom-metric and high-cardinality costs are the main reasons for the evaluation.- Migration is not a settled decision: OpenTelemetry deployment is nearly complete, improving portability. Alternatives include a partial migration, another one-year renewal with a gradual transition, or no migration. Glean and MCP connections can analyze across DDOG and Sumo Logic, reducing the urgency to centralize all data. Existing productivity gains and Bits AI’s capabilities raise the bar for a switch.

AI-Native Demand Remains Healthy into 4Q

We flagged OpenAI renewal risk last quarter. Following the renewal and the reset in expectations embedded in DDOG’s guidance, our checks this quarter indicate that OpenAI’s DDOG usage has stabilized after the spending cut in 3Q26 and resumed growth in 4Q26, consistent with the acceleration in OpenAI’s ARR growth in 3Q. The recovery in usage supports growth in DDOG ARR from OpenAI, but recognized revenue lags; we therefore expect OpenAI-related revenue to decline initially before the usage recovery flows through. Anthropic’s DDOG usage growth has moderated, but training demand from other AI-native customers continues to expand. Taken together, we see healthy AI-native demand growth in 3Q and expect momentum to remain strong into 4Q. Overall, we expect AI-native revenue to account for 15.8% of total revenue in 3Q, slightly below 16% in 2Q, and expect its revenue share to rise again in 2027.

Bits AI Shows Production Value but Monetization Remains Early

The near-term constructive thesis rests on core consumption and consolidation. The latest customer check adds evidence of recurring Bits AI use and operational value beyond the POC-stage feedback in other interviews. However, direct spend remains small in that account, and the wider checks do not yet establish a broad paid-adoption inflection. Bits AI may matter for platform retention before it becomes a material standalone revenue contributor.

Channel Expert B — Better Validation and Initial 2027 Commitments

  • Bits AI validation and paid conversion: The European channel reports more Bits AI opportunities and several successful validations, particularly among large high-technology customers. Feedback centers on faster investigation and incident resolution. Some customers have verbally committed to January deployment, but paid adoption remains limited by available budgets. This is progress from last quarter’s predominantly POC-stage discussion; the next test is conversion into funded production deployments and repeat usage.

Channel Expert A — LLM Observability Leads the New Product Set

  • LLM Observability and AI-product mix: The GSI views LLM Observability as the most established part of the newer AI portfolio, while Bits-related products and AI SRE remain early.- AI-product contribution: Its estimate of AI-product contribution to practice spend is around 7–9%, with a possible move toward 10–12% by year-end or 1Q27. The product definition is not fully standardized, and these figures should not be interpreted as DDOG’s disclosed AI revenue mix or the revenue share of AI-native customers.

Customer Expert D — Adoption Capacity and ROI Determine the Rollout

  • Deployment roadmap: The insurance customer-side check reinforces the distinction between procuring capabilities and deploying them broadly. Additional AI/data observability capabilities are on the 2Q–3Q27 roadmap, while the organization is still expanding adoption of its existing platform. DDOG logging is currently a POC and is not the primary logging engine; the expert did not expect a transition for at least six months. Cloud SIEM is also being evaluated, with the broader logging migration timing still open.- ROI and rollout timing: Bits AI SRE is being evaluated against detection and resolution times, investigation coverage, engineering hours and root-cause accuracy. The expert’s anticipated team-cost benefit is a longer-term 2028–29 outcome, dependent on establishing the underlying processes first. The call shifts between SIEM and Bits terminology when discussing POC completion and funding dates, so we do not assign those dates to a confirmed Bits purchase. The relevant signal is willingness to invest if the efficiency case is demonstrated.

Customer Expert E — Recurring Bits AI Use and Retention Value

  • Usage and spend: Bits AI is used for complex or unfamiliar incidents, typically 6–10 runs per weekday and around 200 per month. The expert recalled a substantial price reduction from about $25 to perhaps $5 per run, subject to verification. At the recalled current rate, that implies roughly $1k per month, a small amount relative to the $1.2–1.3m annual DDOG budget; these are customer recollections, not verified list prices.- Operational leverage: MTTR has declined despite nearly 3x as many pull requests and more system components. The roughly 25-person SRE team has remained flat for about two and a half years. The expert attributes these gains to the combined observability and AI toolset, including Bits AI, Sumo Logic and Glean, rather than Bits alone; no precise productivity multiplier was measured. Bits has not been authorized to execute automated remediation.- Retention and data coverage: The expert considers Bits AI materially ahead of Sumo Logic’s equivalent and will test comparable functionality in the Observe/Snowflake POC. Most application logs remain outside DDOG, so investigations still require Sumo Logic or Glean. Bits increases the value of retaining DDOG, but the customer will weigh that advantage against the benefit and cost of consolidating more data on another platform.

Pricing and Log Optimization Still Shape Net Monetization

  • Log optimization and billing: The European channel has not observed customers moving DDOG logs to lower-cost storage. The GSI, however, sees increasing interest in offloading cold logs to alternatives such as ClickHouse while retaining operationally important data in DDOG. Both can be true across different customer sets. Rising telemetry can support net DDOG growth after optimization, but gross log-volume growth should not be translated directly into revenue. The reseller also highlighted unexpected bills as a recurring source of dissatisfaction. Consolidation savings help the value proposition, but cost visibility remains important for sustainable expansion.

Security Adds Cross-Sell Runway

  • Security cross-sell: The GSI sees AppSec, Cloud Security, Cloud SIEM and related modules growing in the high-20s to low-30s, with a low-to-mid-single-digit contribution in its covered business. This is healthy expansion rather than evidence of a new growth inflection. The European integrator expects security to become a larger focus as 2027 budgets open, while the insurance example shows how security and observability can converge in an account. We see cross-sell potential, with implementation capacity and customer budget priorities determining the pace.
