Agenda

  • KLA's (NASDAQ: KLAC) positioning in semiconductor process control as device complexity and inspection intensity increase
  • AI/ML algorithms, GPU computing and image processing in inspection and defect detection
  • Advanced packaging requirements and KLA's technology differentiation
  • Medium-to-long-term outlook

Key Insights

  • Advanced packaging could introduce c30-40% more defect types, supporting process-control spending growth above wafer capacity as improved algorithms limit additional inspection steps
  • AI adoption in defect classification has reached 80-90% for optical inspection tools, but defect detection using AI remains <10% due to limited customer trust in black-box models
  • GPU adoption delivers 30-40x – sometimes 100x – computational acceleration for inspection algorithms, but thermal limits, data-transfer bottlenecks and power constraints restrict overall tool throughput gains
  • KLA's AI-enabled Voyager tool commands USD 8m-12m per unit vs USD 4m-8m for conventional Puma tools, although hardware differences also contribute to the pricing gap
  • Switching inspection vendors can require roughly three months of qualification, followed by a year evaluating initial tools, reinforcing KLA's installed-base advantage against competing platforms
