SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed the outlook for data center power infrastructure, focusing on Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider Electric, and other equipment suppliers. Despite political opposition, permitting delays, and grid constraints, the expert expects data center construction to continue as cloud demand grows. Projects may shift locations, while on-site generation and microgrids become more common.

The discussion covered equipment demand, acquisitions, modular construction, service opportunities, high-voltage direct-current systems, and the implications for traditional UPS products.

Key Insights

The expert considers demand for cloud services the core driver of data center growth and views claims of an industry-wide slowdown as overstated. The main uncertainty is where and when projects proceed. Power availability is increasingly a constraint. On-site generation may help developers avoid waiting for grid connections, while also eventually supporting grid reliability. Large electrical and data center equipment suppliers are expanding toward integrated solutions spanning generation, power distribution, storage, controls, and services. Modular designs and digital twins could simplify engineering and deployment, but broad standardization remains several years away. Adoption of new power architectures will be cautious because operators prioritize reliability. The expert expects conventional UPS systems and mixed AC/DC designs to remain relevant.
