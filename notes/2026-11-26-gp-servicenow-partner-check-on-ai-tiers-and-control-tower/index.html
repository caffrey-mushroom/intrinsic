SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a systems integrator partner, discussed ServiceNow’s shift to new subscription tiers, adoption of AI products, and implications for implementation partners. The conversation also covered cloud-provider funding, competition in AI governance, and how enterprise software companies may adapt their pricing as AI changes how customers use their platforms.

Key Insights

Customers appear to be choosing ServiceNow’s AI-inclusive tiers to access new capabilities, not only because the bundles require it. However, widespread use of AI assistants has not yet translated into broad purchases of additional usage capacity. Growth for the expert’s business comes from expanding beyond traditional IT service management into customer service, HR, procurement, AI, and other workflows. The expert said a narrow focus on ITSM implementation would leave the business in a difficult position. AI adoption is shifting partner conversations from technical implementation toward selecting business use cases, redesigning workflows, and integrating agents. The expert sees AI governance as a promising growth area, with ServiceNow’s Control Tower potentially becoming a key offering as companies manage more agents and non-human identities. The expert expects ServiceNow implementation work to remain partner-relevant. AI agents can assist with workflow creation and testing, but business analysis, architecture, and customer decisions still require experienced staff. The expert argued that replicating ServiceNow with general-purpose AI is not currently a cheaper or better alternative, citing the cost of AI usage and the platform’s accumulated functionality.
