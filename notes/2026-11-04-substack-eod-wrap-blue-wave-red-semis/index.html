核心摘要

  1. 民主党选举大胜后市场出现剧烈轮动，QQQ收涨1%，SPY涨1.6%，VIX下跌2至3点。
  2. 半导体板块下跌3%（盘中一度跌5%），跑输QQQ约4个百分点，为近五年第11大差距，德州敞口的矿企和AI电力股领跌，HUT跌23%、VST跌14%、VRT跌13%。
  3. 超大规模云厂商大涨，AMZN和MSFT各涨6%、GOOGL涨3%，多头认为新建更难使已通电的现有产能更稀缺、护城河更宽，同时资本开支趋平有助于改善自由现金流。
  4. SemiAnalysis称2028年德州规划容量中有16.7GW面临风险，Jefferies估计Hinojosa暂停令下约13.3GW已宣布项目受威胁，UBS称2028年新增产能中30%存在风险。
  5. Gerstner认为2027年43GW建设中有10至15GW可能无法上线，约20%的2027年超大规模资本开支用于前期建设并可能受影响。
  6. 作者认为未来会有更多监管繁文缛节和让步，全美多个规划数据中心将被推迟，但建设仍将继续，呈现更慢更久的格局，费城半导体指数需重回Leopold低点上方才能提振多头信心。
  7. 宏观上油价跌8%至90美元以下，10年期美债收益率降5个基点，金价涨约3%重回4000美元上方，12月加息概率仍约79%。
  8. 个股方面INTC跌4%尽管其CEO通过家族信托以约64.73美元买入15万股，ANET因强劲2027指引涨5%，存储与硬盘股普跌5%至8%，AAPL涨5.3%创历史新高。
  9. 消费和被视为AI输家的软件、IT服务股出现轧空，OTA涨5%至10%，DUOL涨26%，NFLX涨5%，DASH涨8%，WIX涨7.8%，而HUBS因财报不佳下跌4%。

Good afternoon. Another wild day in Tech land as QQQs +1% rallied as much as +2.5% by the early morning before grinding lower all afternoon. SPY was a lot better, +1.6% on the day and VIX fell 2-3 pts. The story of the day was rotation, rotation, rotation! Less risk on or risk off, and more “risk-somewhere-else” as investors reacted to last night’s blue tsunami and a slower DC buildout means for semis (TLDR: not good)

Semis -3% opened green and bled all day (down as much as -5%), underperforming QQQs by 4%, the 11th largest spread in the last 5 years:

Texas-exposed miners and AI power the epicenter of the unwind giving back all gains from yesterday and more (HUT -23%; VST -14%; VRT -13%), while the hyperscalers ripped on the view that those with the most existing capacity just got a lot more valuable (AMZN/MSFT +6%, GOOGL +3%). Lots of debate among investors on the magnitude of the moves in the CSPs. The bulls made the argument that harder new builds make existing powered capacity scarcer, so prices go up and it widens the moat for whoever already owns energized compute, while firms that only promise future capacity fall behind. We showed this chart this morning, showing hypers have the most capacity already permitted and under construction:

Some framed it as a win-win: value of existing capacity goes up while FCF improves if capex flattens and memory prices drop. Yup, the rare tech bull case where building less, spending less, and growing less somehow adds up to being worth more. Hypers are also a lot more politically savvy than Neos at permitting and getting builds across the finish line so another leg up there in a tougher regulatory environment. Capacity can also move offshore as shown by GOOGL’s recently announced $15B DC in Malaysia over ‘27-28. All good points.

A few, fairly quiet pushbacks: Dems ran against Big Tech which isn’t good for Mega-Cap, 34 of 36 winning governors back ratepayer protections, which likely means hyperscalers pay grid costs, & lastly union-labor, water and tax-abatement conditions raise build costs. Also, some pointed to AAPLs +6% move as a reason this was all positioning/safe-haven driven.

Outside of Megacap, the AI-loser and consumer baskets squeezed (OTAs +5-10% on lower oil; DUOL +26% on a better print, etc). UBS said they said “zero real buying” in UBER and BKNG fwiw. Consumer agentic Winners vs. Losers basket -6% as a lot of the names beaten down on AI agent disruption risk unwound. There wasn’t one single internet stock red on my screen today; first time that’s happened in a while as investors shifted to a long unloved sector. Names like NFLX +5% and DASH +8% finally getting some love. TMT Mo -8%.

We dove into the potential impact of the Dem win in Texas in our morning wrap here. SemiAnalysis was out saying they 16.7GW of planned 2028 Texas capacity at risk. Gerstner said 10–15GW of the 43GW 2027 build may not come online. Jefferies estimated ~13.3GW of announced Texas projects are at risk under a Hinojosa moratorium, with Texas roughly a quarter of the US capacity expected online by 2030. Roughly 20% of '27 hyperscaler capex goes toward pre-build activity and is potentially at risk, with '28 build plans facing even greater uncertainty (UBS said 30% of 2028 capacity additions at risk).

MS said the Texas results aren’t in tech prices yet, and noted that in 2018, 2002, and 1974, all had drawdowns of 10% post midterms despite market being higher six months later:

Citriniwas out calling for the peak in AI infrastructure capex as the AI beta trade ends, expecting a “muddle through” as already-funded projects continue but incremental spending and investor expectations moderate. Sounds fair. In our view, it’s not just all of the straight read-throughs to the build, but with Dems in power and feeling good about ‘28, the anti-AI/AI safety noise likely gets louder and comes with a bit more teeth.

The pushback is that Hinojosa platform called for a more stringent set of rules for DCs, but not a complete shutdown and she’s at the mercy of the republican legislative branch in TX as well. DCs create lots of jobs after all. Most construction projects are already under way. Fair points. Our view is there will be a lot more red tape & concessions and plenty of planned DCs across the country will be slowed or pushed out, but the build will continue — a potentially slower for longer build. The bulls did their best narrative gymnastics to spin this as a positive: slower for longer is better, TX isn’t really changing much, *more political oversight could defuse the hyperscaler spending race and ease worries over FCF destruction, issuance and crowding-out, while lowering the risk of an outright shutdown. * Even heard the argument that restrictions give hyperscalers every reason to pull spending into 2027 to lock up power and permits, so capex could go up near term. Ok, ok, ok. While some investors I talked to were sympathetic to some of the arguments, pretty much everyone wanted to see some sort of price action to confirm that this slow-down has already been priced into semis at current multiples. But bulls here facing a really big uphill battle narrative wise. Was just more weight added on to an already burdened AI trade.

Still the AI trade has surprised us many times over the past 3 years and taught us to remain open-minded. In times of muddied narratives and multi-sided debate, we like to resist the urge to out-intellectualize our friends and lean on the charts to settle the argument. Here’s the SOX:

The Leopold lows now provide a nice line in the sand. A break back above would be the first starting point in helping bulls regain some mojo and get the market believing a more rose-tinted narrative.

In macro land, the sweep turned into a peace trade. Oil -8% below $90as Vance’s “the reason we lost is the war” tweet was read as the White House looking for an off-ramp from Iran and Schumer said Democrats will end the war. Yields followed oil lower: 10Y -5bps and 2Y -4bps, the dollar hit a one-month low, gold jumped ~3% back above $4,000 and housing caught a big bid. Fed pricing barely moved: December hike still at ~79% (from ~74% Monday and ~77% Tuesday), or ~20bps for the rest of 2026, with ~55bps of hikes priced through June 2027.

Lots of earnings post-close — we’ll have short recaps once the calls finish

Won’t get into too much individual px action today, but a few things worth calling out…

In AI/SEMIS, mainly a sea of red, but just a few things to call out. INTC -4% as LBT’s Form 4 crossed midday showing he bought 150,000 shares on 11/2 at ~$64.73 (~$9.7M) through a family trust. SPCX -3.9% with Elon's DC and Terafab buildout centered in Texas. ANET +5% held in well after their monster ‘27 guide although bears pushed back on why ANET deserves a multiple with a 3-handle. AMD +1% also held in ok although gave back most of its 6% pop after last night. Memory/HDDs all down 5-8% as read of a slower buildout tilts the balance towards more supply vs. demand. Only bright spot: YMTC potentially delaying their IPO

TTWO +8%:

AAPL +5.3% continued to frustrate bears by hitting all-time highs (slower buildout = lower memory costs = better GM?).

AI loser App software and IT services squeezed :WIX +7.8%; ACN +3.8%; INTU +3.5%; IOT +4.1%; CTSH +3.1%. MNDY +3.4%…HUBS -4% didn’t follow along and rightly so after their print…
