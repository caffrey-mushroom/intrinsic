SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined fiber supply, pricing, connector shortages, and the transition from copper to optical connectivity in AI data centers. The expert remains bullish on long-term fiber demand but believes technical hurdles will delay widespread adoption of near-package optics and co-packaged optics. Corning and Amphenol are positioned as leading contenders as optical connectivity moves inside servers and racks.

Key Insights

North American fiber pricing is rising faster than other regions because of strong demand, logistics, and tariffs. New capacity expected around mid-to-late 2027 should moderate, but not eliminate, price increases. The expert expressed reasonable confidence that major suppliers will meet announced schedules. High-fiber-count cable remains constrained, while lower-count products are more readily available. Buyers increasingly rely on international secondary suppliers. Connector availability is becoming a more significant bottleneck, particularly for newer MMC formats. Installation labor is available but often less skilled, increasing contamination and rework risks. Copper is likely to remain viable for another one or two product generations. Recent 1.6-terabit active electrical cable performance exceeded industry expectations, although power consumption remains critical. Co-packaged optics has proven harder to deploy than anticipated. Near-package optics may serve as an interim architecture and could persist longer if adoption gains momentum. The expert gave Amphenol a slight competitive edge over Corning because of its connector sales experience, customer relationships, and CommScope acquisition. The moderator pursued this view extensively. A one-year optical transition delay should be manageable for major North American suppliers, while a multi-year delay would create greater demand risk.
