AI-driven threats should boost spending on remediation and protection, expanding security revenue pools

Oct 28

READ IN APP

Security could become a substantial source of AI model revenue, with token spending potentially reaching $50–70b as remediation cycles accelerate. The market would remain smaller than the coding opportunity, but still substantial.

AI labs are likely to capture most of this market, but if 20%–30% of the value accrues to security vendors, their opportunity from model revenue alone would be $10–20b. That compares with combined 2026 revenue of approximately $25b for the three security platform vendors, Cloudflare (NET), Palo Alto Networks (PANW), and CrowdStrike (CRWD).

In this note, we review and respond to recent comments from executives at AI labs and cybersecurity companies on security threats, including the PHP vulnerabilities. We draw certain implications from this discussion, including support for the $50–70b model revenue opportunity sized above.

The incremental model revenue opportunity from security

Subscribed The estimate starts with the June 2026 Anthropic Economic Index dataset: cybersecurity and threat detection accounted for 1.42% of sampled first-party API requests in April and 1.52% in May, excluding Claude Code.

As security tasks require advanced models with at least Mythos-level capabilities, we estimate 1.5% share of API usage corresponds to a 4%–6% share of revenue. With global AI model revenue of approximately $200b and an assumed API contribution of 60%, security-related revenue would be roughly $5–7b.

Growth to ten times the $5–7b base assumes response and remediation requirements become more frequent, for example, as monthly cycles compress to weekly cycles and weekly cycles to daily cycles.

In summary, we think the implications are:

  • AI is increasing the efficiency of vulnerability discovery and exploitation, while broadening the range of assets and activities enterprises must protect.- Customers are expected to increase investment in vulnerability remediation, continuous protection, and incident response.- Incumbent cybersecurity companies will benefit if they can translate model capabilities into reduced risk for customers. Products focused only on generic scanning, reporting, and analysis will face growing pricing pressure.- In the near to medium term, AI labs will likely generate most security-related revenue from model usage, rather than from selling security applications.

Demand and architecture: continuous remediation and protection

Recent executive commentary points to several areas of consensus:

  • The speed of addressing security issues is now a greater constraint for enterprises than discovering them. The PHP2Shell incident highlights this bottleneck.- Even after a vulnerability is confirmed and a patch is released, enterprises must identify affected assets, test compatibility, schedule deployment, and check for prior compromise. As security models improve and reveal more vulnerabilities, manual workflows and cross-functional coordination become more burdensome.

This creates two categories of demand:

  • More efficient remediation: linking vulnerability validation, risk prioritization, code changes, testing, and deployment to reduce ticket backlogs.- Protection while remediation is pending: reducing risk through virtual patching, access restrictions, and runtime detection, with rapid blocking when an attack occurs. The hybrid patching approach proposed by Nikesh Arora, remediation bottlenecks highlighted by Logan Graham, and continuous security engineering outlined by Greg Brockman each address aspects of these needs.

These requirements should further integrate security into development and operations (DevSecOps):

  • Development and operations teams work to reduce system defects, while security operations teams focus on identifying and containing attacks. Both require a shared understanding of assets, identities, data, business dependencies, and the ability to execute response actions. Arora and George Kurtz extend protection to AI agents within the enterprise, requiring identity attribution, tool-call tracking, and data access restrictions.- These requirements call for product interoperability and generally support the expansion of security platform vendors.- These executives expect greater security spending, but differ on its timing. Dane Knecht, Cloudflare’s CTO, emphasizes bringing spending forward, while Brockman argues that security should represent a larger share of long-term budgets.

The Division of Work Between AI Labs and Security Vendors

Currently, this division of labor is practical. In simple terms, the AI labs supply the models; the security vendors manage enterprise defense.

The main distinction is whether a task depends on customer-specific context and authority. General-purpose code analysis is reusable across customers and fits scaled delivery by AI labs. Production protection requires ongoing data, system integrations, and reliable controls, making it better suited to security vendors with established deployment capabilities.

AI labs mainly enhance vulnerability reasoning, code remediation, and complex incident analysis, and embed security features into coding agents. Security vendors integrate these capabilities with customer assets, permissions, attack activity, and business constraints to prioritize actions and implement protection across network, endpoint, identity, and cloud systems. Enterprises, software maintainers, and device vendors remain responsible for approving patches, scheduling downtime, and managing production changes.

In-depth analysis and rapid execution should be designed separately. Frontier models can investigate new vulnerabilities and generate patches or detection logic, while validated rules, purpose-built models, and policies handle frequent blocking. This uses model improvements while managing latency, cost, and errors. A model does not need to participate in every real-time decision. OpenAI’s Daybreak partner program and Anthropic’s Cyber Mission already bring AI labs and security vendors together.

Value capture: pricing pressure on analysis, greater value in customer knowledge and execution

Customers are willing to pay for reduced risk. As discovery and analysis costs decline, remediation deployment, control coverage, and response reliability become more important. Value accrues to providers that resolve remaining bottlenecks and are difficult to replace.

AI labs have two revenue paths.

First, as model providers, they can earn usage revenue as security workloads grow, though competition, smaller models, and inference efficiency will constrain pricing.

Second, they can offer security applications directly, embedding scanning and remediation into development tools to capture spending that would otherwise go to application security software or services. The second path offers greater revenue potential but also requires more delivery responsibility.

Security vendors’ main advantages are their ongoing understanding of customer environments and reliable protection enforcement. Stronger models can improve detection and response while reducing manual analysis costs. If customers maintain spending, efficiency gains increase profit; if competition lowers prices, customers benefit more. Telemetry and customer relationships alone are not enough. Vendors must show these assets improve outcomes and cannot be easily replicated by standard interfaces or alternative products.

AI-driven value capture is likely to concentrate at two ends of the value chain: model capabilities and enterprise defense execution. The scope for charging separately for generic scanning, reporting, and preliminary analysis will narrow. AI labs will capture model usage revenue and a portion of security application revenue.

Security vendors will compete for recurring enterprise budgets through cross-system execution, continuous operations, and agent governance. Their ability to retain profits depends on whether customers still need them to manage the entire workflow, from issue discovery through verification of remediation and response. Vendors that can switch underlying models while retaining customer workflows and execution capabilities will have stronger bargaining positions.

We expect AI to support demand for platform vendors that manage customer workflows and execute protective actions, while increasing pricing pressure on point-solution vendors that sell generic analysis.
