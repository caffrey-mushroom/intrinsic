I. The AppLovin vs. Unity litigation

In last quarter’s AppLovin and Unity Previews, we specifically noted that AppLovin’s IAA D28 business faced intense competition from Unity in 2Q26. In the Unity Preview, we highlighted that Unity’s IAA D28 business achieved in just one quarter nearly the growth that its IAP D28 business took several quarters to achieve, which directly hit AppLovin’s core advertising business.

This view was confirmed by the legal dispute between AppLovin and Unity that began in August. A careful analysis of the dispute’s timeline reveals many meaningful clues.

1. AppLovin’s complaint

AppLovin and Unity signed the “AppLovin Header Bidding Agreement” on October 13, 2021, under which Unity connected to AppLovin’s mediation platform MAX as a bidder. Section 2.4(c) of the agreement provides that bid request data may be used “solely to bid on Inventory made available via the Mediation Platform”.

AppLovin alleges that, since at least May 2025, Unity used the Unity Ad Quality SDK to scrape data on ads that AppLovin won and displayed, including creatives, click-through landing pages, advertiser and campaign IDs, device and user IDs, revenue per impression, MAX bidding and waterfall information, and even the coordinates of the most recent screen tap.

AppLovin alleges that Unity’s scraping method was to “hook into AppLovin callbacks and inject Unity code into its ads”, an active behavior rather than passive observation.

AppLovin suspects Unity used this data to train Vector, its competing bidding model, but this is speculation; its public filings contain no direct evidence.

2. Timeline

October 13, 2021: AppLovin and Unity signed the “AppLovin Header Bidding Agreement”.

May 28, 2025: Unity LevelPlay 8.9.0 began bundling Ad Quality; AppLovin says Unity’s data collection began at least from this point.

August 5, 2026: AppLovin reported 2Q26 results. 2Q26 revenue was about 1% below the high end of guidance, short of its usual beat margin, but it gave 3Q26 revenue guidance implying q/q acceleration.

August 21, 2026: AppLovin sent a cease-and-desist letter, requiring Unity to respond within 14 days.

September 4, 2026: Unity replied with a refusal: “Ad Quality does not collect any data from AppLovin”

September 8, 2026 and September 14, 2026: AppLovin issued 72-hour and 48-hour ultimatums in succession, with additional demands.

September 11, 2026: Unity made its first concession, agreeing to notify publishers within 5 business days to disable Ad Quality on MAX, and to release an SDK without Ad Quality within 30 days.

September 16, 2026: Unity made its second concession, agreeing to remotely switch off collection in MAX bidding (including ironSource Ads) and to delete collected data under its normal retention policy; it refused to retrain or take down its model; all conditional on AppLovin confirming in writing that the dispute was settled.

September 27, 2026: AppLovin filed for arbitration with JAMS

October 2, 2026: The court denied the TRO and the request for a preliminary injunction hearing

3. Unity’s stance on the matter

In its September 4 letter, Unity acknowledged the data collection, which it said had taken place “since well before the parties entered into those agreements”.

Unity acknowledged that the collection targeted ads won in MAX bidding, with the data coming from publisher apps and user devices, and with authorization (it denied the data came from AppLovin, but did not deny collecting data on MAX ads).

Unity argues that AppLovin knew about it long ago.

Unity can control the data collection remotely. It can remotely switch off collection in MAX bidding, including Ad Quality deployed within ironSource Ads. Unity does not agree to retrain the Vector model.

Unity alleges that AppLovin’s Ad Review is far more invasive, is enabled by default for all MAX publishers, and also collects impression data for Unity Ads and others; it also argues that MAX gives the AppLovin DSP additional support.

4. Can other DSPs replicate Unity’s behavior?

Back in 2020, Mintegral used the Mintegral SDK to intercept competitors’ ad click data in thousands of apps. After this was exposed, platforms delisted Mintegral, and Mintegral subsequently stopped the practice. AppLovin cited this incident in its filings, saying Ad Quality is of the same nature but more serious.

Most DSPs do not have this capability. However, platforms that have both DSP and mediation capabilities can obtain data through ad quality tools like Ad Review and Ad Quality. The main function of these ad quality tools is to record displayed ad creatives and block ads that are non-compliant, pornographic, click-baiting, auto-redirecting or crash-inducing.

Google AdMob also has its Ad review center, but it only reviews ads sold by Google itself and does not cover ads from third-party networks. There are also independent third-party tools on the market such as AppHarbr and Boltive.

5. The AppLovin vs. Unity case is critical to AppLovin’s results this quarter. Questions we need to resolve going forward include:

If Unity did collect data, was this reflected in AppLovin’s 2Q26 miss and in Unity’s rapid IAA share gains in 2Q26?

AppLovin gave its 3Q26 guidance before it sent the cease-and-desist letter. Did its 3Q26 guidance implicitly assume that the Unity competition would be resolved?

Will a more competitive Unity model raise the AppLovin DSP’s bidding costs and in turn affect AppLovin’s take rate?

After Unity stops collecting data, will the gap between AppLovin AXON and Unity Vector widen again?

II. Publisher feedback on the impact on AppLovin’s acquisition costs

Around these questions, in this round of research we spoke further with five publishers, following up on changes in AppLovin’s and Unity’s bids, impression share and competitive dynamics since Q2, with a focus on the impact on AppLovin’s acquisition costs and take rate.

Before reading the research details, some technical background is needed. AppLovin’s take rate = (advertiser spend - cost paid to publishers) / advertiser spend. If Unity’s bids in MAX rise, it implies that improvements in Unity’s algorithm may make it more competitive when competing for inventory. If AppLovin’s MAX bids also accelerate y/y while Unity’s bids rise, it implies AppLovin has had to raise its bidding costs to protect share. This would also lower the take rate.

Therefore, DSP shares in MAX, and the y/y change in AppLovin’s MAX bids, indirectly reflect changes in AppLovin’s take rate.

Subscribed

1. Publisher A

This publisher covers merge, casual, hyper-casual, and mid-core and hardcore games. Casual games rely mainly on IAA for monetization, while mid-core and hardcore games rely mainly on IAP. This publisher explains quarterly price changes mainly by seasonality: summer ad demand is weaker, so Q3 average eCPM is usually lower than Q2; in Q4, prices strengthen again with peak-season demand.

“Price changes are mainly driven by seasonality. In summer users go on vacation, and advertisers usually spend less. In July and August advertisers don’t spend as much as at other times, and eCPMs also tend to dip a bit.”

Besides seasonality, this publisher also sees publishers setting floor prices more actively as a source of pressure on AppLovin’s acquisition prices.

“Now there are third-party companies offering very simple SDK integrations that help publishers set floor prices intelligently. As more and more publishers use these solutions, AppLovin has to bid higher. Publishers set higher floor prices hoping to earn more revenue, so AppLovin needs to offer higher prices.”

It has also observed that when other ad networks improve their performance and raise their bids, AppLovin raises its bids accordingly to compete for impressions.

“Whenever other ad networks perform well and raise eCPMs, AppLovin also starts raising its bids to compete. For example, when Meta’s performance improves, you see AppLovin’s bids start going up too. Having several strong networks competing with each other helps publishers raise eCPMs.”

2. Publisher B

This publisher mainly covers casual, puzzle and board games, with IAA as its main monetization method and annual IAA monetization of about 8 million to 12 million. It does both user acquisition and ad monetization.

AppLovin’s Q3 eCPM in its apps was up about 60% vs. Q2, while Unity’s rose about 5% over the same period. AppLovin’s larger increase also includes a strategy adjustment in some games to reduce ad frequency and raise the value of each impression.

“For example, users used to watch 10 ads per day, and now we’ve cut that to 6, and we want those 6 ads to sell at a higher price. This is also to reduce the disruption ads cause to players, improve retention, and see whether eCPM goes up. So the difference in AppLovin’s eCPM is large, and it’s not entirely market factors; our own strategy changes play a part too.”

This publisher also feels that e-commerce demand began to have a more noticeable impact from Q3, and expects it to strengthen further during Black Friday and Christmas in Q4.

“Compared with the next few weeks, the impact of e-commerce earlier on wasn’t that big, because Christmas and Black Friday are both very important for e-commerce.”

There is also bidding competition among e-commerce advertisers within AppLovin, and between e-commerce and gaming advertisers. This publisher believes higher bids on the demand side lead to higher publisher eCPMs.

“There are different e-commerce advertisers on the AppLovin platform, and they also compete with each other and raise their bids, so the CPMs advertisers pay go up, and the resulting eCPMs go up too.”

Among ad networks, the competition this publisher feels comes more from platforms like Meta and AdMob.

“Within AppLovin, games also compete with e-commerce advertisers, and e-commerce eCPMs are higher. Among ad networks, Unity isn’t a big source of competition on the monetization side; it comes more from networks like Meta, AdMob, Pangle and Liftoff.”

3. Publisher C

This publisher covers two strategy RPGs and one sports simulation game, with cumulative IAA monetization of about 3.5 million in 2026 up to the interview. This publisher links the July decline in overall monetization eCPM across the three games to holiday user behavior.

“The decline in July was mainly because people were on vacation. Users usually watch more ad impressions, so eCPMs at that time also tend to be lower.”

Of all ad impressions across these three games, impressions AppLovin bought for e-commerce advertisers accounted for less than 1%. This publisher reports that this share did not rise noticeably in Q3, but the eCPM of those ads increased.

“eCPM went up, meaning these brand advertisers are paying slightly more than before, but overall impression share hasn’t grown much.”

On AppLovin’s own acquisition prices, this publisher believes stronger competition from networks such as Unity and Meta may push AppLovin to raise its bids to win impressions.

“Frankly, it’s hard to tell, but I think there is an impact. As Unity becomes more competitive, if Unity, Meta and other networks start winning more share, that will also push AppLovin to raise its bids to win share. At the end of the day, it’s a competition everyone wants to win, and it’s also pushing these networks to keep improving.”

4. Publisher D

This publisher is an Asian casual IAA publisher with average daily ad monetization revenue of about US$700,000-800,000. This publisher does feel that AppLovin and Unity are competing aggressively, but for recent price changes it puts more emphasis on the impact of the Black Friday warm-up and e-commerce demand.

“My feeling is that market demand may have a bigger impact on price changes, not just whether AppLovin and Unity are actively raising prices. Especially around Black Friday or the e-commerce peak season, the impact of promotional demand is more obvious.”

This publisher believes that as e-commerce demand increases, the relative impact of AppLovin and Unity bidding against each other on overall eCPM changes may also shift.

“My understanding is that once e-commerce comes in, even if AppLovin and Unity are still raising prices, the share of the overall price change driven by the two bidding each other up may get smaller. So you can’t just look at this trend and conclude that the two have eased off on actively raising prices, because other factors in the overall market are changing too.”

Comparing ad types, this publisher believes e-commerce ad eCPMs are usually upper-middle, but some high-value gaming UA ads may also pay higher prices.

“E-commerce ad eCPMs are upper-middle, definitely higher than ordinary ads. But there’s also high-value UA, especially competitors’ campaigns, where prices may be higher.”

5. Publisher E

Drawing on cases from its past operations and current advisory work, this publisher covers more than 20 casual, hyper-casual and rewards apps, with annual ad monetization revenue of about US$5 million to 8 million. This publisher has a clear sense that competition drives AppLovin to raise its bids, noting that after Unity and other ad networks raise their bids, AppLovin also raises its bids.

“If we’re talking about competition among ad networks within MAX and its impact on AppLovin, I think yes. When prices from networks like Unity and Digital Turbine go up, AppLovin also raises its bids. From what we’ve observed, that’s indeed the case.”

However, its recent observation is that competing networks’ prices have risen more than AppLovin’s.

“Recently, competing networks’ CPMs and eCPMs have risen more than AppLovin’s. From what we’ve seen, AppLovin’s bids haven’t been that strong.”

On e-commerce, this publisher still sees few e-commerce ads when actually looking at its apps, though more than last year. Based on internal estimates, some e-commerce ads have eCPMs about 20% to 40% higher than gaming ads.

“We don’t have data that lets us precisely compare e-commerce and gaming advertiser eCPMs, only internal estimates.”

“Based on internal estimates, some of the e-commerce ads we see are priced higher, roughly 20% to 40% higher than gaming ads.”

6. Publisher F

This publisher has seen Unity’s bids rise steadily since May, which has affected AppLovin’s bids.

“June was the month AppLovin was hit hardest; AppLovin’s bids in MAX rose markedly y/y.”

“From July to October, AppLovin’s bids gradually decelerated, but y/y growth was still higher than before June.”

June was likely the month of the fiercest competition between AppLovin and Unity, as Unity became clearly more competitive after launching D28 IAA.

After July, AppLovin’s bids began to ease, which may be related to AppLovin updating its algorithm model in July.

“My guess is that AppLovin did a minor iteration of its IAA model in July to respond to Unity’s competition. We’ve also seen some effects since then.”

Publisher F is very happy with the recent competition, believing it has helped publishers earn higher ad revenue.

“The recent competition will definitely reduce AppLovin’s profit; the spread they make reselling ad slots will shrink. But for us it’s a good thing, and I hope the competition gets as fierce as possible.”

III. Changes in AppLovin vs. Unity competition since 2Q26

Given that AppLovin’s results last quarter were below expectations, we also focused on reviewing the pace of competition between Unity and AppLovin since Q2, as well as the changes in prices and impression share observed by each publisher.

1. Publisher A

This publisher has not yet seen Vector take meaningful share from AppLovin, and AppLovin’s recent impression share has been broadly maintained.

“Some people say Unity Vector is performing well now, but honestly, I haven’t seen it. I talk to UA teams often, and I ask whether Unity Vector is starting to work and becoming a bigger UA channel, and nobody has said yes.”

“AppLovin hasn’t gained more impression share recently; it’s basically stayed at the same level. Many publishers are adding more ad networks, hoping to diversify their reliance on AppLovin.”

Its description of the price trend from July to September centers mainly on weak summer demand and a partial recovery at quarter-end.

“Q3 usually starts weak, and August and September aren’t strong either. There may be a slight recovery at quarter-end. Comparing Q2 and Q3, the average eCPM may actually be lower.”

2. Publisher B

In Q3, Unity’s share of all ad impressions rose from about 7% in Q2 to about 11%; AppLovin’s rose from about 32% to 33% to about 38% over the same period.

“(AppLovin’s share of all impressions) went from around 32% to 33% up to 38%.”

“Unity went from about 7% to about 11%.”

This publisher also noted that the absolute number of ad impressions provided by AppLovin decreased in Q3.

“In Q3 there was more and more competition, and the total number of ad impressions AppLovin provided to us decreased.”

On the UA side, this publisher’s overall CPM was low in August, began to recover in September, and then kept rising.

“UA CPMs were low in August, but started rising in September, and since then we’ve kept seeing prices go up. It’s normal for CPMs to climb heading into Halloween and Q4; this time the increase showed up in Q3, which wasn’t entirely what we expected.”

3. Publisher C

This publisher observed that from the end of Q2 through Q3, the prices Unity paid publishers were higher than in Q1.

“From what I’ve seen, from the end of Q2 through all of Q3, the prices Unity paid were somewhat higher than in Q1.”

In September, Unity’s eCPM also rose.

“(Unity’s September eCPM) also went up.”

After further checking monthly data, this publisher confirmed that within MAX, AppLovin’s eCPM advantage over Unity widened in August and September, but AppLovin’s overall impression share declined from July to September. The higher eCPM in September did not bring a recovery in overall impression share.

“This comparison is limited to within MAX. After checking the monthly data, AppLovin’s eCPM advantage over Unity kept widening in August and September.”

“I need to clarify what I said earlier: AppLovin’s overall impression share within MAX declined from July to September. Although eCPM rose in September, it didn’t bring a recovery in overall impression share.”

4. Publisher D

This publisher felt that AppLovin’s and Unity’s eCPMs rose quickly in June and July, and that in July and August Unity began closing in on AppLovin, narrowing the eCPM gap between the two.

“In Q2, especially the second half of Q2, eCPMs were indeed quite high. My understanding is there are two factors: one is that everyone was spending a lot, and CPIs were rising too; the other should be competition, and I think competition is there.”

“Unity was originally behind AppLovin, and in July and August the eCPM gap between the two may have been narrowing. We look at each network’s eCPM curve, but we haven’t done such a detailed comparison of exactly what percentage each one rose.”

Looking back at the trend since mid-year, this publisher felt the slope of the eCPM curve has become gentler than before, but prices are still rising; on recent changes, it also mentioned increased demand from Black Friday, e-commerce and other non-gaming ads.

“eCPMs are still rising, just not with as much momentum as before. Prices across all networks are rising, and with Black Friday approaching there’s more demand from e-commerce ads and other non-UA ads.”

This publisher still felt AppLovin and Unity were bidding aggressively in September, with the competition between the two stronger than with other networks.

“AppLovin’s and Unity’s bids are neck and neck, and the competition is indeed somewhat stronger than with other networks.”

“In September AppLovin and Unity were still pushing prices up, but as stronger ad demand comes in, even if the two keep pushing prices up, they may not necessarily outbid the other demand.”

Compared with other ad networks, this publisher felt AppLovin’s and Unity’s eCPM increases were more pronounced.

“Other networks are also rising with some volatility, but the increases may not be as large as AppLovin’s and Unity’s, because the whole market is trending up now.”

Over the last two weeks or so, eCPMs for some channels and apps have risen quickly, which this publisher links to the Black Friday warm-up.

“Over the last two weeks or so, eCPMs for some channels and apps have risen quite fast, because the Black Friday warm-up has already started.”

5. Publisher E

This publisher believes Unity’s improvement this year showed up mainly in Q2 and Q3.

“Unity’s performance in Q1 wasn’t that strong; it was mainly Q2 and Q3.”

In further discussing intra-quarter changes, this publisher also mentioned that upward momentum eased somewhat in August and September; its view of AppLovin was similar, emphasizing more that this year’s eCPM increase falls short of 2025.

“I think the trend is similar; especially compared with 2025, eCPM momentum isn’t as strong.”

AppLovin’s eCPM still rose from May to September, but the increase over the same period this year was weaker than in 2025.

“Looking from May to September, AppLovin’s eCPM still went up, with fluctuations throughout, but the increase was weaker than last year. September 2026 is definitely higher than May, it’s just that the increase over the same period in 2025 was bigger.”

For the Q3 q/q change, the AppLovin eCPM increase this publisher ultimately gave was single digits, also below its original expectation.

“I think it’s single digits, somewhere between low single digits and high single digits, but this year it’s definitely not double digits. Last year there was double-digit growth; this year there isn’t.”

IV. AppLovin’s bids compared with other platforms

1. Publisher A

The eCPM Unity pays publishers is still about 20% to 35% lower than AppLovin’s; AppLovin is more willing to bid for high-value users.

“The eCPM Unity pays publishers is still about 20% to 30%, even 35%, lower than AppLovin’s; in this respect it’s still hard for it to compete with AppLovin.”

This publisher still believes AppLovin’s advantage comes from combining high eCPMs with large demand scale, and that it is also an important UA channel for the UA teams it talks to.

“AppLovin’s eCPM is still among the highest; maybe only one or two networks can come close, and its ad demand and number of advertisers are also very large.”

“From what I hear from the UA side, for apps that rely on UA, AppLovin is still the main acquisition channel; that hasn’t really changed.”

On the relationship between UA and mediation, this publisher understands that some publishers use AdMob for monetization while buying UA through AppLovin. The concern about continuing to use MAX is more about worrying that switching would hurt UA performance; some specific campaigns are still tied to MAX, which this publisher thinks may be blended ROAS.

“If you want to run UA, you don’t have to use MAX. I know some publishers use AppLovin as a UA channel but use Google AdMob for mediation monetization. AppLovin emphasizes to publishers that switching mediation may lose signals and hurt UA performance, so many publishers aren’t willing to take that risk.”

“For specific campaigns, yes, I think it’s blended ROAS. You can use AppLovin even without its mediation, but there’s a specific type of UA campaign you can only run if you use its mediation.”

2. Publisher B

The average eCPM AppLovin paid in Q3 was about 50% higher than the average of the other ad networks, vs. about 30% to 35% higher in Q2. This publisher believes Google can challenge AppLovin on Android, but overall AppLovin is still clearly above the other networks.

“On Android Google can challenge AppLovin, but overall AppLovin is still clearly above the other networks.”

On e-commerce’s role in this price gap, this publisher believes its contribution is large, while also emphasizing AppLovin’s existing gaming ad performance and factors such as user quality.

“E-commerce prices are indeed high, but AppLovin’s roughly 50% eCPM gap over other networks isn’t fully explained by e-commerce. E-commerce can explain a large part of it, but it’s not the only reason for the gap.”

In further discussing impression share and user quality, this publisher added:

“It’s hard to judge precisely. The factors include e-commerce, MAX, and the higher-quality users we’ve acquired through UA. So you can’t say AppLovin is better only because of e-commerce. It’s usually better at gaming ads to begin with, and e-commerce has added some extra uplift.”

On the UA side, this publisher reports that AppLovin’s D7 and D28 optimized campaigns keep improving, and it particularly approves of the performance on iOS; Android varies by game, and Google performs better for some games.

“The D28 or D7 optimized campaigns we run are getting better and better, and overall we’re satisfied with AppLovin, especially on iOS. Android is more challenging; it depends on the game, AppLovin isn’t always the best, and sometimes Google is better.”

This publisher acknowledges Vector has improved, but at larger spend levels its performance still lags AppLovin.

“We can see Unity Vector has improved, but in our games it still lags AppLovin. It’s usable with small budgets; if you want to put in the same scale as AppLovin, the performance can’t reach the same level yet.”

AppLovin’s importance in UA also makes this publisher cautious about switching away from MAX. It believes the two are not forcibly bundled, but worries that switching mediation would hurt UA performance.

“Using MAX isn’t a requirement. You can use AdMob, CloudX or Unity’s mediation and keep buying UA through AppLovin. But in my experience, the performance is a bit worse.”

“For many games, AppLovin can account for around 50% of ad spend. If you’re going to put that spend at risk, you have to be very sure you’ll do better after switching.”

3. Publisher C

Within MAX, this publisher observed that the average eCPM AppLovin pays is about twice Unity’s, while noting that the two buy different user mixes, with AppLovin skewing more toward high-value users.

“AppLovin and Unity target different audiences. AppLovin mainly targets high-value users, meaning users who are more likely to pay or generate higher value in the games being advertised; the users Unity targets are lower value overall.”

On the UA side, publishers also use both platforms at the same time to acquire different users.

“Most publishers use both AppLovin and Unity for user acquisition and advertising, acquiring different users on the two platforms. So the two aren’t necessarily either-or; it’s more of a complementary relationship.”

This publisher has also heard that many large publishers are exploring AdMob, and more attractive commercial terms are driving some publishers to migrate.

“Quite a few large publishers are exploring the AdMob mediation platform, and there is also some movement from AppLovin MAX to AdMob.”

“It’s mainly about revenue. AdMob is offering publishers more attractive commercial terms.”

How publishers allocate traffic across mediation platforms is also affected by partnership contracts. This publisher mentioned that some contracts require at least 75% or 80% of traffic to go through the partner platform.

“Usually when you partner with a mediation platform, the contract requires at least 75% or 80% of traffic to go through that platform, so you can’t split half your traffic to another platform at the same time.”

4. Publisher D

This publisher feels AppLovin’s overall eCPM is higher, but the advantage varies by region and OS.

“Overall, AppLovin’s eCPM is still somewhat higher than Unity’s, but it varies by country, and in some countries Unity ranks ahead. In Europe and the US it may be higher by more; on iOS AppLovin’s advantage may be a bit stronger, and on Android the gap is smaller.”

Across different mediation platforms, the relative performance this publisher observes also differs.

“Under Google AdMob mediation, Unity may even perform a bit better than AppLovin.”

In impression allocation, AppLovin is still this publisher’s main source of ad demand, and Unity has also improved somewhat. This publisher also actively adjusts traffic across ad networks.

VII. Meta Audience Network’s impact on AppLovin

We also researched the competitive landscape of Meta Audience Network. Although some agencies are not yet actively using it or have not seen clear improvement, North America A and several publishers report that its competitiveness is strengthening and its campaign performance keeps improving, making it a competitive source AppLovin needs to watch.

1. Agency and channel feedback

North America A

In last quarter’s Preview, we had already noted that Meta was stepping up investment in its gaming business again, but at the time, Meta Audience Network’s share of total Meta ad spend in this channel’s sample had only risen from about 2.1% in Q1 to about 2.2% in Q2, still a slow increase. At that point we had not yet seen Meta noticeably affect AppLovin’s gaming budgets, and our competitive focus was more on Unity.

In this round of discussions, Meta Audience Network’s share of this channel’s total Meta ad spend has risen for four consecutive months, reaching 2.7% in September; in our mid-October discussion, it expected October could reach 2.9% to 3%. This channel previously thought the rising share might just be a short-term fluctuation, but this time it leans more toward a sustained improvement.

“Meta Audience Network’s share has kept rising slightly. Four or five months ago, its share of our Meta spend was very stable, almost always around 2.1% or 2.2%. Over the past four months it has risen month by month, reaching 2.7% in September, and I think October may reach 2.9% or 3%. When the share rose before, I found it somewhat interesting but thought it would fall back; now it looks like it may stay at a higher level.”

“The more I learn, the more I see Meta improving in the open gaming ad ecosystem, including new ad formats, better targeting, algorithms and conversion tracking, and it’s starting to work. This may be the biggest change since our last conversation. I’m now more convinced this is a new growth driver. It’s hard to say whether it will contribute 1 percentage point or 3 percentage points next year, but I think it will keep having an effect, rather than being a short-term fluctuation.”

On product, this channel says Meta has added gaming and e-commerce ad formats and is also improving conversion measurement. Beyond gaming ads, it has started to see e-commerce ads entering the mobile gaming network through Meta.

“They’ve launched some new ad formats, like 30-second non-skippable videos in games, and added at least one e-commerce ad format. I haven’t looked at how much these products are being used yet, but they’re out there, and that shows they intend to keep adding products. Attribution has improved too, which helps advertisers better measure whether a conversion came from Meta Audience Network.” “Meta has now decided to get serious about the gaming ecosystem, whereas six months ago I felt it was mostly just maintaining its existing business. What we’re seeing now isn’t just Meta putting gaming ads into the mobile gaming network, it’s also started running e-commerce ads.”

On targeting and data collection, this channel thinks AppLovin’s earlier lead is narrowing. It recalls that AppLovin was still ahead six to nine months ago, but the two are now fairly close; Meta keeps investing in its algorithms and is also improving its bidding capability.

“I think the two are now fairly close on targeting and data collection, but six to nine months ago AppLovin was still ahead. On algorithms, I’d lean toward Meta being better, because it’s putting a lot of resources in. It’s hard to judge precisely, but Meta is currently rolling out one or two new algorithms every quarter, and most of them bring some improvement.”

Meta is also engaging more actively with gaming companies. This channel says that since the start of the year Meta has stepped up product introductions, industry events and outreach to gaming companies, pushing harder than before.

“Six to nine months ago, they were mostly maintaining their existing business and didn’t have a particularly aggressive strategy. But starting in January this year, maybe February, I think January, because they started at CES, Meta has been actively reaching out to gaming companies, going on stage to present their plans, and calling gaming companies to ask whether they’ve seen the new products. At the same time it’s running a fairly large round of market education, pushing on every front.”

North America B

This channel has not seen a clear increase in Meta Audience Network usage. Its brand clients have long tended to exclude ad impressions inside third-party apps and buy only Meta’s own placements, mainly over brand safety and control of the ad environment; this choice is not new this quarter.

“I haven’t seen Meta Audience Network usage go up. A lot of advertisers actively exclude Meta Audience Network and only want to buy Meta’s own inventory, like Facebook and Instagram.”

“This has been the case for a long time, not just Q3. Our clients have never prioritized Meta Audience Network.”

Europe A

This channel has historically used Meta Audience Network little and has limited knowledge of this business. It did not observe strong growth this time; its impression is that it is still gradually picking up small amounts of programmatic ad budget.

“My knowledge is fairly limited. My impression is that it’s not a product that’s growing strongly, it’s just gradually picking up some programmatic ad budget.”

Asia A This channel sees IAA gaming clients increasing budgets on Meta, but the increase is mainly on Meta’s own apps (Instagram and Facebook); it has not yet seen a clear improvement in Meta Audience Network. The small amount of AppLovin gaming budget it mentioned shifting to Meta also mainly went to Meta’s own apps.

“We haven’t seen a clear improvement on the ad network side. What we see more is IAA clients increasing their budgets on Meta’s own apps, that is, Instagram and Facebook.”

Asia B

Some clients are trying Meta Audience Network because it is cheaper and they also want to leverage the data advantage of Meta’s own apps, but this channel has not seen traffic quality improve quickly, nor has it clearly observed Meta taking budget from AppLovin.

“Clients mostly feel it’s cheap and that they can use some of the data advantages of Meta’s own apps. But for this traffic, things like audience quality are hard to improve significantly in a short time. It has its own strengths, but at the end of the day it’s ad network traffic, and there are still some uncontrollable factors around quality.”

Asia C

This channel’s improvement in Meta gaming campaign performance is concentrated in Meta’s own placements such as Facebook Feed, Reels and Instagram, and its view of Meta Audience Network remains weak. In its better-performing campaigns, Meta Audience Network typically accounts for about 5% to 10%.

“Meta Audience Network’s data is much worse than the other placements. Right now the ones performing well are still Facebook Feed, Facebook Reels and Instagram Reels.”

“In our better campaigns, Meta Audience Network is only about 5% to 10% of the mix. If we find that share is above 30%, it usually means the campaign probably isn’t performing well.”

2. Publisher feedback

Publisher A

This publisher observes that Meta Audience Network strengthened again in Q2 and Q3, with improvements in both ad creatives and iOS traffic buying, and it has become the second-strongest ad network in its view.

“They’re very strong, they’ve grown a lot in scale, and the ad creatives have improved noticeably. Also, the industry has been talking about workarounds after the iOS privacy changes, though I’m not sure whether a specific solution has actually been rolled out.”

“On iOS, Meta used to be one of the biggest buyers. After users stopped allowing tracking, they lost a lot of signal. Now it looks like they may have found some workarounds, and Meta’s iOS business has started growing again. That puts competitive pressure on AppLovin. It feels like Meta really is back, it’s become the second or third strongest ad network again, which wasn’t the case before.” “For us, Meta really has become the second-strongest ad network.”

This publisher rates the eCPM Meta pays publishers highly, while still viewing AppLovin as combining high prices with large ad demand.

“Meta’s eCPM is usually strong. I remember when we integrated Meta, their eCPM was very high, and it may still be the highest in the market. But you can’t look at eCPM alone, you also have to consider ad demand and the number of advertisers. Some networks have eCPMs higher than or close to AppLovin’s but don’t have nearly as much demand.”

Publisher B

This publisher lists Meta as one of the sources of increased competition on the monetization side in Q3, with competition also coming from networks such as AdMob, Pangle and Liftoff; Unity is not the main source of competition in its view.

“Inside AppLovin, gaming ads also compete with e-commerce, and e-commerce eCPMs are higher. Between ad networks, competition comes from different platforms. Unity isn’t a major player on the monetization side; it’s more Meta, AdMob, Pangle, and other networks like Liftoff.”

On the UA side, this publisher also rates Meta’s performance well, but thinks it is still quite volatile and requires constant monitoring and adjustment of campaigns.

“Meta performs well, but the results swing a lot, so you have to keep watching it and controlling its delivery.”

Publisher C

This publisher observes that Meta Audience Network picked up most of the impression share that AppLovin and Unity lost in Q2. It attributes the share shift more to how advertisers allocate budgets across platforms.

“Looking at data from other ad networks, it seems that in Q2 it was mainly Meta that took the share AppLovin and Unity lost. At the time, maybe one large advertiser put more budget into Meta’s ad network, which gave Meta more money to bid with.”

“This isn’t a technology or platform issue, it’s about how advertisers allocate spend across platforms. For example, if a large advertiser decides to put several million into one platform, that can change the shares of the whole ad network. If one of them bids aggressively on Meta, Meta’s share goes up and AppLovin’s share goes down.”

The eCPM Meta Audience Network paid in Q2 rose only slightly; the change in impression allocation was more pronounced.

“Meta did grow in Q2, eCPM went up slightly and it paid a bit more per impression, but the change wasn’t dramatic. It was just that more impressions were allocated to Meta instead of Unity or AppLovin.”

Looking to Q4, this publisher expects AppLovin, Unity and Meta shares to stay broadly unchanged.

“It’s hard to predict. But I think Q4 will probably look like Q3, with Unity, AppLovin and Meta shares roughly holding, so I expect Q3 to carry over.”

Publisher D

This publisher senses that Meta is paying more attention to and investing more in its gaming ad business than before, and also mentions that its iOS performance keeps improving, possibly related to adjustments made around ATT restrictions.

“On iOS, they may have made some adjustments around ATT, and performance is a bit stronger. They’re making changes and have started taking this seriously. Overall it’s not that aggressive yet, but it is improving. Our sense is that they used to be fairly laid-back, and now they’re adjusting and putting in more effort than before.”

This publisher also actively adjusts impression allocation across ad networks, with different strategies for each app. Besides bidding performance, ad networks’ promotions and rebate arrangements also affect its allocation.

“Each app has a different strategy, and we also adjust proactively. For example, if an ad network is running a promotion, once we increase its impressions, it gives us some rebates.”

3. How advertisers are actually using it

Gaming company A

The company says Meta Audience Network has improved but has not yet eaten into its AppLovin budget. It is still lowering the previously high budget shares of Meta and Google, while its AppLovin and Unity spend grows with overall ad spend.

“Meta Audience Network has improved, but it’s not taking share from AppLovin.”

“In terms of the trend, we’ve been lowering the budget share for Meta and Google, because it was too high before.”

E-commerce company A

The company still decides on additional Meta Audience Network budget based on new-customer acquisition cost and the stability of incremental ROAS. It thinks Meta Audience Network’s marginal value has not yet reached AppLovin’s, its overall allocation for 2026 has changed little, and it has not cut AppLovin budget because of Meta Audience Network or Unity.

“The core test for scaling up is new-customer cost and the stability of incremental ROAS. So far our 2026 spend on Meta Audience Network hasn’t changed much. Its marginal value hasn’t reached AppLovin’s yet; in this race it’s still the one catching up.”

Impact on AppLovin’s acquisition costs North America A observes that Meta is winning more auctions and paying publishers more. It thinks this mainly comes from better ad performance; as prices adjust, the ROI advertisers ultimately get remains fairly similar.

“It’s hard to compare directly, because prices are changing too, and the ROI advertisers end up with is still similar. What I can see is that Meta is winning more auctions than before, which means it’s paying publishers more. That means either Meta’s returns have improved, or it has lowered its take rate. I don’t think Meta lowered its take rate, so I think Meta’s ad performance is improving faster than AppLovin’s. Both companies are innovating quickly; Meta may just have put in more or better engineers and is improving faster.”

Publisher A has already observed that when networks such as Meta improve and raise their bids, AppLovin also raises its bids to compete for impressions.

“I often see that whenever other ad networks perform well and raise eCPM, AppLovin also starts raising its bids to compete. For example, when Meta improves, you see AppLovin’s bids start going up too. Several strong networks competing with each other helps publishers raise eCPM.”

This publisher also still sees seasonality and publishers’ floor settings as important factors behind price changes.

“Seasonality is one major factor. The other is whether a network has found a way to suddenly improve its performance. Meta, for example, seems to have found a way to buy more iOS traffic. But that change happened more toward the end of the quarter, or in the last two months.”

North America A expects that over roughly the next three months Meta may go into more comprehensive head-to-head bidding with AppLovin. If AppLovin keeps winning impressions by raising its bids, its take rate may come under gradual pressure.

“It’s not fully there yet, but I think over the next three months or so, Meta may go fully head-to-head with AppLovin in auctions. It’s still early, and I don’t think it will be catastrophic for AppLovin. But over time its take rate may come under pressure: if it wants to keep beating a resurgent Meta, it may have to raise its bids, which squeezes the take rate.”

AppLovin’s demand scale on the publisher side and its position in UA remain strong. Even in the Publisher A sample, where Meta Audience Network’s recovery is most visible, AppLovin still combines high eCPM, large ad demand and a large number of advertisers.

“AppLovin’s eCPM is still among the highest, maybe only one or two networks come close, and they also have a large amount of ad demand and a lot of advertisers.”

“From what I hear from the UA side, for apps that rely on user acquisition, AppLovin is still the main acquisition channel. That hasn’t really changed.”

VIII. CloudX progress

In last quarter’s Preview we already flagged CloudX’s potential competition with MAX. In this round of publisher checks we continued to follow CloudX, but did not hear of any clear new progress in its adoption. However, in this round of conversations we also sensed that, as CloudX and other mediation and third-party monetization tools are promoted, publishers are becoming more proactive about optimizing ad monetization, and their awareness and use of floor price settings is increasing. In the past, some publishers may not have known about these settings, or were reluctant to adjust them for fear of hurting monetization; now they are starting to use tools and peer exchanges to more actively pursue higher ad impression revenue.

Floor prices are not unique to CloudX; other mediation platforms have similar features. As more publishers learn about and use these settings, the price AppLovin must pay to win impressions may rise, putting pressure on its cost of buying ad placements.

1. CloudX testing and adoption

Publisher A understands that many peers are watching or testing CloudX, but the industry is still waiting for a large publisher to publicly share a success case.

“I know a lot of people are looking at these options and trying CloudX. The industry is still waiting for a really well-known large publisher to say publicly that it’s using CloudX and that it works well. A case like that could drive more publishers to adopt it.”

Publisher B has not tested CloudX yet, and its largest game still uses MAX. AppLovin is also an important UA channel for it; concern that switching mediation would hurt UA performance is the main reason it stays on MAX.

“We’ve heard of CloudX, but our biggest game is still on MAX. Leaving MAX for CloudX is too risky for us. AppLovin is also the main network we use on the advertiser side, so we don’t want to lose that performance. We’re not testing CloudX right now.”

Publisher F has heard of CloudX but thinks too few DSPs participate in CloudX, so it will not put budget into CloudX for now.

2. Use of floor price tools

Publisher A observes that third-party tools have lowered the technical barrier to setting and optimizing floor prices. Based on peer conversations, it roughly estimates that the share of publishers using such tools has risen from about 30% in Q2 to about 50% to 60% now, and estimates that these strategies can raise publisher revenue by about 10%.

“It’s hard to estimate precisely, but in Q2 it was maybe around 30%, and now it’s probably 50% to 60%. I’m seeing more and more companies offering these services, which shows the demand is real. Peers talk at industry events and share experiences, everyone learns from each other, and they gradually adopt similar techniques to raise eCPM.”

It thinks that more publishers using floor prices to pursue higher monetization prices will increase what AppLovin has to pay to win impressions.

“There are now third-party companies offering very simple SDK integrations that help publishers set floor prices intelligently. As more and more publishers use these solutions, AppLovin has to raise its bids. Publishers set higher floor prices because they want more revenue, so AppLovin has to offer higher prices.”

Floor prices can also be configured within existing mediation setups. Publisher E has used floor prices for a long time, but they have to be requested and configured proactively and are not on by default.

“It’s not something they provide automatically, we asked for it. It’s not a default setting. Many publishers may not even know about it; it’s a setting you have to configure proactively.”

“New publishers don’t even know it exists, or they know but are afraid to adjust it in case it hurts monetization. If you have enough traffic and a good business relationship, this is something you need to do. In my experience, more than half of publishers don’t set floor prices when they start out.”

Publisher D, an Asian casual IAA publisher, also actually uses a strategy combining bidding with waterfall floor prices, paying particular attention to impressions with high historical value that receive low bids in the current auction.

“We’ve built up a lot of data ourselves. If a user’s previous impressions have reached a certain eCPM level and then the bid suddenly drops very low, we’ll also set a waterfall floor price to go with it.”

Publisher B also thinks floor prices are an existing monetization strategy, not unique to CloudX. In practice they still need constant adjustment, and floor prices set too high can leave impressions unsold.

“This isn’t something only CloudX does. Setting floor prices is a monetization method that has been around for a while.”

“Bidding is less work. If you use floor prices, you have to check the ad placements every week, make sure the impressions are selling, and raise or lower the floor depending on the situation. That takes more operational time.”

3. Our takeaways from the checks

From this round of conversations, we sense that more and more publishers are paying attention to floor price settings and, through third-party tools, peer exchanges and proactive configuration, are managing their ad monetization prices more actively. Even if they stay on existing mediation, this shift may gradually raise AppLovin’s cost of acquiring impressions and pressure its existing spread.

X. AppLovin quarterly view and model update

After extensive checks, we try to answer the questions we raised after the initial AppLovin vs. Unity litigation.

1. If Unity did collect data, was this reflected in AppLovin’s 2Q26 miss and in Unity’s rapid IAA share gains in 2Q26?

We think this was already reflected in AppLovin’s 2Q26 miss. For changes in Unity’s D28 IAA in 2Q26, please see our Unity 2Q26 Preview.

AppLovin management gave 2Q26 guidance in early May; with Unity D28 IAA formally launching in late May, Unity and AppLovin competed intensely in June.

This also showed up in our checks: several experts mentioned seeing AppLovin bid higher in MAX auctions in June and July.

2. AppLovin gave 3Q26 guidance before it sent the cease-and-desist letter. Did its 3Q26 guidance imply an expectation that the Unity competition would be resolved?

We do not think it implied an expectation that the Unity competition would be resolved. Arbitration is a long process, and the effect of the data collection stopping, in terms of the models pulling apart, does not happen immediately.

But our checks also show AppLovin’s take rate rising slightly from July, and its bids in MAX also starting to slow y/y.

Considering that AppLovin’s budget may come in above expectations, the take rate is acceptable.

3. Will a more competitive Unity model raise AppLovin’s DSP bidding costs and in turn affect AppLovin’s take rate?

We think this directly affected the take rate.

Several experts said that as Unity bid higher, AppLovin’s bids also rose.

4. After Unity stops collecting data, will the gap between AppLovin AXON and Unity Vector widen again?

It will widen again, but not that quickly.

If Unity does not roll back the data, the old data will still affect its algorithms. But as some of the data goes stale, or as AppLovin updates its models, that effect will fade.

5. Can other DSPs copy Unity and collect data?

Unity’s approach is like opening Pandora’s box; we also understand that after AppLovin sent Unity the cease-and-desist letter, other DSPs are studying whether it is feasible to copy Unity.

Other DSPs could also launch similar ad quality tools.

But unlike other DSPs, AppLovin, Unity and AdMob have all had ad quality tools for years. Launching a new ad quality tool right after the litigation would also confuse advertisers.

Based on these checks, lower barriers to entry for campaigns and advertisers starting to put more effort into Tier-2 ad platforms both give us fairly high confidence in the e-commerce business this quarter. The business-development (BD) issues that had long been criticized also appear to have been largely resolved.

We therefore think AppLovin’s budget came in slightly above expectations this quarter, while take rate dipped slightly q/q, but as bids eased month by month through July-September, take rate also recovered month by month. On this basis, we think AppLovin’s revenue can slightly beat its guidance high end. Although this may be smaller than its 3-4% typical beat, with sentiment steadily weakening over the past three months, we think a 2-3% beat would also be acceptable.

XI. Unity quarterly view

Apart from a few agencies and gaming companies that do not advertise on Unity, our expert checks indicate Unity budgets were broadly above expectations and put bidding pressure on AppLovin.

In last quarter’s Unity Preview and Review we stressed the importance of D28 product improvements for Unity, in particular Unity D28 starting to enter IAA, AppLovin’s core area, which drove strong budget growth last quarter. This quarter we see Unity Ads’ D28 IAA remaining very strong, and based on conversations with agencies and gaming companies, its D28 iOS progress was faster in Q3 than in Q2. iOS has long been AppLovin’s strength in the industry, and iOS is a larger incremental market for Unity.

Unity’s higher bids in MAX also show that Unity can bid for higher-value users. Based on our publisher interviews, Unity’s steadily rising bids reflect that Unity has expanded to higher-value user groups and is able to bid for them. Unlike AppLovin, we do not think Unity sacrificed take rate to compete with AppLovin; its controlled take rate also shows the company can reach satisfactory budget growth without lowering take rate. However, North America C thinks Unity’s relatively low take rate also gives it some room to win advertiser budgets, which differs from our view.

At the same time, we are starting to notice more publishers working to reduce their heavy reliance on AppLovin, and Unity, with its improved bidding capability, has become a helper in reducing that reliance.

Since Unity’s biggest driver, D28 IAA, only fully launched in late May, its main contribution is in 3Q26. We think Unity Ads q/q growth accelerated in 3Q26 and could reach 30% q/q.
