SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a senior pharmaceutical clinical-development leader and AI evaluator, discussed BIOS’s ICA platform for planning and operating clinical trials. AI is already useful for assessing protocol feasibility, selecting sites and countries, supporting recruitment, and applying risk-based monitoring. The expert has used BIOS in several studies and considers it intuitive and effective, while emphasizing that human review and current data remain essential.

Key Insights

Study design is ICA’s strongest near-term use case. The platform can rapidly test eligibility criteria and generate protocol materials, replacing work that can take weeks. The most promising differentiation is linking protocol recommendations to simulations. Modeling how eligibility changes affect recruitment and trial outcomes could strengthen the product. Execution tools are less differentiated. Established systems and specialist providers already cover areas such as trial tracking and patient identification. Eligibility matching also cannot capture patients’ willingness or practical ability to participate. Human input remains important. Safety and monitoring decisions require clinical expertise; site forecasts need local knowledge, and simulation results should be checked with investigators and country teams. The main threat is imitation. The expert believes large pharmaceutical companies and technology providers can reproduce many functions using general-purpose AI. BIOS could strengthen its position through partnerships, customer-specific data, or access to pre-consented patient cohorts. Likely customers include emerging biotechnology firms and small or mid-sized CROs. Large pharmaceutical companies may build tools internally or face more complex integration and data-sharing requirements.
