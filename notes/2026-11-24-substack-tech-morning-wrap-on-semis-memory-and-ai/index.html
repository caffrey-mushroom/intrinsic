HPE: Big Beat and FY27 raise, but mgmt conceded server pricing power is fading and guided OP margins down y/y to 14% in FY27

F4Q26 looked great with a big beat on margins and EPS ($1.43 vs. Street $1.28) on in-line revenue, and the F1Q27 and FY27 guides landed ~10% above Street on revenue and 10–26% above on EPS. AI orders hit a record $5.7B (+88%) with AI backlog at $10.5B, and FY27 revenue growth is now +27–35% (vs. +13–17% prior and Street +18%), with Helios included and the plan covered by inventory and purchase commitments.

However, investors focused on weaker margin guide. Mgmt guided OM to fall from a record 18.4% to 16–17% in Q1 and ~14% for FY27 (~13% implied for Q2–Q4), Q1 GM drops to ~33–34%, and mgmt now ties the step-down more to pricing than to mix, with 2H costs rising faster than prices, although mgmt said on the callback guide embeds prudence on pricing and pass-through similar to the assumptions Q4 beat.

The other nit is FY27 OI guide is ~$590M over Street ($8.95B vs. $8.36B) and ~$400M of that is the Jan qtr ($2.3B vs. Street $1.9B). That leaves Apr-Oct only ~$2.2B of OI a quarter, below 4Q's $2.46B.

One more: FY27 AI networking orders target ($7.5-8B cumulative vs. $3.4B so far) means $4.1-4.6B of FY27 orders: that’s only ~$1.0-1.15B a quarter vs. 4Q's record $1.2B, so a step down despite Helios and Oracle ramping. Neri answered that q against FY26 ("our acceleration is pretty significant"), not 4Q.

The pushback on all these is mgmt typically guides v conservative: they just put up FY26 EPS of ~$4 vs the $2.20–2.40 guided 13 months ago, and FY27 revenue growth has been raised from +8–12% to +27–35% in under six months.

Key Takeaways

  • Callback. The most important callback point: mgmt put pricing ahead of mix as the driver of the C&AI margin step-down, saying its ability to earn margin on top of component costs is diminishing as competitive bidding and customer pushback limit server ASP increases above incremental component costs after FY26’s steep hikes. Mgmt said the margin outlook is not tied to high-cost memory sitting in inventory, quote validity periods are in line with the industry, and the guide embeds prudence on pricing and pass-through similar to the assumptions Q4 beat. On revenue, FY27 remains supply-constrained (networking silicon most acutely, plus select CPUs and memory), the guide is fully covered by inventory on hand and purchase commitments, and faster networking supply conversion is the most tangible upside, followed by server pricing and Helios timing. The Helios assumption includes announced programs plus unannounced opportunities, ORCL lands partly in 2H FY27 with the larger portion in FY28, and AI and Helios deals absorb working capital (partly offset by customer prepayments), so FCF upside is unlikely to match EPS upside.
  • Margins. C&AI OM hit a record 18.4% (last q 17.0%, year-ago 9.3%) vs. a mid-teens guide, lifting total OM to 16.9% (+470 bps y/y) vs. Street 15.8%, with GM of 38.2% (-220 bps q/q) vs. Street ~37.0%. The guide resets that sharply: Q1 OP of $2.2–2.4B alongside a low-20s% q/q opex decline implies GM of ~33–34% (down ~5 pts q/q); C&AI OM falls to 16–17% in Q1 and ~14% for FY27 (raised from ~13%), which implies ~13% for Q2–Q4; and FY27 OM of 14–15% sits below FY26’s 15.1%, so OP (+23–30%) grows slower than revenue (+27–35%). Mgmt attributed Q1’s lower OM primarily to higher input costs only partly offset by pricing, and expects 2H GM to moderate further as AI servers (including Helios) ramp and costs rise faster than prices, while GP dollars still grow. Q1 EPS ($1.35 midpoint) is guided below Q4’s $1.43, and the FY27 midpoint is ~9% below Q4 annualized. At Q3, mgmt framed GM moderating toward historical levels mainly on AI systems growth, traditional-server normalization and deal mix; pricing as the lead driver is the new element. Every 100 bps of C&AI margin is worth ~$0.28 of FY27 EPS.
  • Guide. FY27 revenue growth roughly doubled to +27–35% ($59.4–63.1B) from +13–17%, ~11% above Street ~$55.0B (+18%), with C&AI raised to +30–40% (from +14–18%) and networking held at high-teens to low-20s. EPS of $5.05–5.35 (+27–35%, from $4.40–4.60) is ~10% above Street ~$4.75 (tallies range $4.53–4.77), and FCF rises to at least $5.5B (from at least $5.0B). FY27 revenue growth has gone from +8–12% at the June debut to +13–17% in September to +27–35% now, and FY26 EPS of $3.97 compares with the $2.20–2.40 guided at the Oct 2025 analyst meeting and the ≥$3.00 FY28 target set there. Helios is now in the guide (it was excluded at Q3), mgmt says it assumes only historical win rates on a pipeline that is “multiples” of backlog, and the year is unusually back-end loaded at ~46/54 1H/2H (typical ~48/52) on networking supply conversion, Helios and ORCL timing and large AI deals shipping in 2H. Implied Q2–Q4 revenue growth is ~25% vs. +54% at the Q1 midpoint.
  • Pricing. FY26 growth was price-led: server revenue grew 95% in Q4 (last q +35%) as traditional-server ASP gains offset supply-constrained units, and mgmt said ASPs have been “the biggest growth driver” but that FY27 shifts more toward units, still gated by supply. On the call, mgmt framed the change as FY26’s “significant double-digit increases in commodities” moderating and insisted it will keep passing cost increases through. At Q3, mgmt expected units to strengthen in Q4 on AI inference, but Q4 growth was again ASP-driven, so the unit inflection has slid into FY27. HPE still avoids chasing volume in large AI training deals, so it is not taking AI server share from DELL or SMCI; in networking, it held off on price increases while combining the Aruba and Juniper channels (completed Nov 1), which leaves pricing as a lever for the networking margin ramp.
  • Demand. Total orders grew 88% (FY26 $57.2B, +72% reported and >50% normalized, a ~1.2x book-to-bill), with C&AI orders +107%, server orders up triple digits and networking orders +56%, and mgmt said the pipeline is “multiples of our backlog.” Asked whether the election or a tighter financing backdrop had changed customer behavior, Antonio said it had not and that “everything is status quo”; he expects CY27 IT budgets to prioritize cybersecurity, AI deployments, infrastructure modernization and alternatives to the virtualization layer.
  • AI. AI orders reached a record $5.7B (last q $3.1B): AI systems orders of $4.5B (+130% y/y, last q $2.4B) vs. AI systems revenue of $2.5B (triple-digit growth), and Networks for AI orders of $1.2B (last q $0.7B). AI backlog rose to $10.5B from $7.6B, with AI systems at $8.8B, and FY26 AI systems revenue was $5.8B (+28%) on orders +45% and backlog up ~90%. Helios is the swing factor: each 72-GPU AMD Helios rack carries six HPE QFX5250 scale-up trays, HPE can also sell those trays to other rack builders, and networking is only ~15–20% of rack content, so to the extent the >$1B Helios networking outlook ships as full HPE racks, it brings several billion dollars of lower-margin rack revenue into C&AI, part of why the 2H margin guide steps down. Mgmt’s long-run pitch is that inference (>80% of AI infrastructure demand by 2030) and agentic AI pull workloads on-prem toward higher-value configurations; PCAI posted its largest revenue quarter.
  • Networking. Revenue of $3.06B grew 12% (last q +10% normalized), in line with Street $3.07B and the first clean organic comp since Juniper closed: Campus & Branch +17% (last q +8%) on Wi-Fi 7 and self-driving networking, DC switching +12% (last q -6%), Routing +10% (last q +23%) and Security -2%, with DC and routing partly supply-constrained. OM of 22.1% was in line with the guide but below Street ~22.6% and down 130 bps y/y on variable comp. Orders grew 56% (routing triple digits, DC high double digits, Campus >30%), and cumulative Networks for AI orders reached $3.4B, above the >$3B target set at September’s Networking Investor Day. The new target of $7.5–8B cumulative by FY27-end implies $4.1–4.6B of FY27 bookings, +65–85% vs. ~$2.5B in FY26 but roughly flat with Q4’s $1.2B quarterly pace. FY27 growth and margin targets were reiterated, but Q1 is only +11–13% (~flat q/q at ~$3.0B), so Q2–Q4 must grow ~23%; mgmt expects networking to exceed 40% of company OP by F4Q27, vs. ~25% of segment OP in Q4.
  • Supply. Mgmt now expects memory, NAND and many other critical components to stay constrained to at least 2H CY28, the longest and most specific timeline it has given (at Q3 it expected some 2027 relief from new clean-room capacity, with structural relief tied to wafer capacity). Networking lead times run 28–40+ weeks, networking purchase commitments rose >25% q/q after doubling in Q3, and inventory climbed to $14.1B from $11.8B last quarter and $6.4B a year ago on higher commodity costs, customer orders and strategic buys (DIO of 141 days). Because the guide is already covered by inventory and commitments, upside depends on faster component delivery, especially networking silicon. A longer shortage supports pricing and backlog duration into FY28, but it also keeps input costs rising and gates the unit growth FY27 is supposed to deliver.
  • Customers. Enterprise and sovereign customers are 57% of cumulative AI orders on the slides (mgmt cited >60% over the past four years on the call), and HPEFS has extended no financing to neoclouds or other high-risk customers, with its book weighted to investment-grade credit. Neocloud deals such as the $1.2B Vultr Helios order require prepayments, escrows and strong credit backing, with no change in strategy. Concentration is still rising, since large programs now drive both segments: the multi-billion-dollar hyperscaler inference deal signed after Q3 (for the customer’s internal use), ORCL’s gigawatt-scale networking build (mostly FY28) and Helios, which ties 2H shipments to customer data-center readiness. Enterprise networking revenue grew 16% and service provider 5%.
  • Storage. Storage revenue rose 34% to $1.48B (last q +10%) vs. Street ~$1.36B on several large public-sector and enterprise deals, with storage orders up mid-to-high double digits; Alletra MP passed a $1B run-rate and the X10000 (NVIDIA-certified for file and object) has a “very, very healthy pipeline.” Mgmt guided FY27 storage growth in line with the market, so Q4 is not a new run-rate, and storage margins sit inside the ~14% C&AI guide rather than being broken out. ARR exceeded $3.5B (+12%), mgmt cited traction for its VM Essentials and Morpheus virtualization software (40,000 VMs running internally), and HPEFS revenue was +1% on record financing volume of $2.1B (+43%).
  • Cash. Q4 FCF was $1.9B (flat y/y despite OP +108%) and FY26 FCF was $4.5B vs. the ≥$3.75B guided in September, with the cash conversion cycle 12 days better q/q. The FY27 FCF guide of at least $5.5B (+22%) trails EPS growth (+31% at the midpoint) as Helios, large AI deals and component buys absorb working capital. Capital return steps up: ~$3B of FY27 buybacks (~5% of the market cap at ~$45), a 19% dividend increase to $0.17 per quarter, a new $5B authorization (~$7.3B total) and a ≥75% FCF payout target, backed by net leverage of 1.4x vs. the 2x target after retiring $1.25B of notes. Antonio argued that a company growing revenue ~30% with rising OP dollars and cash returns should not trade at “a multiple that is 8x-9x.”

Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bulls see HPE as a networking-led AI infrastructure company that is still priced like a commodity server vendor. Juniper has turned networking into an ~$11B franchise earning ~22% margins that mgmt expects to grow at a high-teens CAGR through FY29 with mid-to-high-20s margins, the integration is running ahead of plan (synergies now $800M vs. $450M at announcement), and the combined portfolio covers every layer AI traffic touches: Campus & Branch riding the Wi-Fi 7 cycle on the Mist and Central AIOps platforms, QFX scale-up and scale-out switching (including what mgmt calls the first fully liquid-cooled 1.6T Ethernet switch), and PTX/MX routing on in-house silicon for scale-across and DCI, with DC networking alone framed at a low-to-high-50s% CAGR. Helios gives HPE a wedge into AI scale-up networking, since every 72-GPU rack carries six HPE switch trays that HPE can also sell to other rack builders, while ORCL and Vultr show the portfolio winning cloud and neocloud fabrics. In compute, bulls argue the next phase of AI (agentic AI and inference, which mgmt expects to be >80% of AI infrastructure demand by 2030) pulls workloads back on-prem to where enterprise data lives, which plays to HPE’s enterprise and sovereign installed base, its storage attach (Alletra MP at a >$1B run-rate) and its private-cloud and GreenLake software, and that richer, memory-heavy configurations support structurally higher server margins than the 7–9% HPE earned in FY25. A disciplined AI posture (57% enterprise and sovereign mix, no neocloud financing, prepayments and escrows where it does play) also limits the credit risk now hanging over parts of the AI trade.

This quarter gave bulls most of what they wanted on the numbers. Orders grew 88%, AI orders nearly doubled q/q to $5.7B, AI backlog rose to $10.5B, and FY27 revenue growth was raised to +27–35%, with EPS guided ~10% above Street even though mgmt says it assumes only historical win rates and embeds pricing prudence similar to the assumptions that Q4’s 18.4% C&AI margin beat by 300+ bps. The guide is supply-gated rather than demand-gated and fully covered by inventory and purchase commitments, so faster networking silicon delivery, Helios timing and server pricing are all upside levers, and mgmt’s view that component tightness lasts to at least 2H CY28 argues the pricing cycle extends well into FY28 rather than rolling over next year. HPE’s guidance record also supports leaning above the range: FY26 EPS of $3.97 compares with the $2.20–2.40 guided 13 months ago, and FY27 revenue growth has been raised from +8–12% to +27–35% in under six months. The multiple can expand as networking passes 40% of OP by F4Q27, as at least $5.5B of FCF (~8.5% yield) funds ~$3B of buybacks (~5% of the market cap), and as investors start to value the segments separately; at ~8.6x the FY27 midpoint, HPE trades at a steep discount to networking peers and to its own growth rate.

Bears see FY26–27 as a memory-shortage windfall rather than a new earnings base. C&AI, which produced 68% of FY26 segment OP, went from 7–9% margins in FY25 to 18.4% in Q4 because HPE could raise server prices faster than component costs while customers scrambled for supply, which is how FY26 EPS doubled on 23% normalized revenue growth. In the bear view, enterprise hardware over-earned on the memory markup, and that unwinds in two stages: first as cost increases outrun what customers will accept, which mgmt now concedes, and later when memory capacity catches up and prices fall, leaving traditional servers facing ASP deflation against inflated FY26–27 comps, with inventory at 2.2x year-ago levels ($14.1B) and purchase commitments built at peak prices. AI systems, the fastest-growing piece, are structurally lower margin, lumpy and concentrated in a few large programs (Helios, ORCL, a multi-billion-dollar hyperscaler inference deal, Vultr), and HPE’s own decision not to chase volume means it is not gaining share against DELL and SMCI. Networking, the piece meant to justify a higher multiple, is supply-gated with 28–40+ week lead times, depends on Helios and ORCL data-center readiness in 2H, faces ANET, CSCO and NVDA in AI fabrics, and has yet to show its margin ramp (Q4 OM down 130 bps y/y). The end-market backdrop has also turned: post-election pushback on data-center construction has investors trimming 2028 AI capex, which by arithmetic makes 2027 the peak year for AI hardware spend, and some memory analysts have started cutting their shortage estimates.

This quarter handed bears the margin evidence. The Q1 guide implies GM falls ~5 pts q/q to ~33–34% and EPS declines sequentially from $1.43 to ~$1.35, the FY27 EPS midpoint sits ~9% below Q4’s annualized run-rate, and OP growth (+23–30%) now trails revenue growth (+27–35%), with 2H GM pressured by costs rising faster than prices. On the callback, mgmt pinned the C&AI step-down more on pricing than on mix, citing competitive bidding and customer pushback, which squares with industry reports of server OEMs seeking post-signing price increases of up to 45% and a customer suing DELL over one. Implied y/y EPS growth decelerates from ~+108% in Q1 to roughly flat by F4Q27, FCF growth (+22%) lags EPS growth (+31%) as AI deals absorb working capital, and Q4 FCF was flat y/y even as OP more than doubled. Even the >40% networking OP target leans partly on C&AI profit shrinking, since the guide math implies C&AI OP falls ~25% y/y by F4Q27. Hardware multiples rarely expand into decelerating EPS growth and falling margins, and a 54% 2H revenue weighting leaves little room for slippage on Helios, ORCL or component deliveries.

SMTC -12%: Big Jan-qtr revenue guide, but data center only hit its guide, the margin guide is light and there's no FY28 DC number.

HFs leaned short into this and getting paid this morning as DC lighter vs bogeys and GM guide was light. All wasn’t bad though as DC was guided ~+40% q/q to ~$203M. That puts FY27 DC at ~$520M (+133%, >2.3x), ahead of the "more than double" target set at the Oct. 15 Analyst Day and about in line with bogeys.

Mgmt reaffirmed DC >$1B in FY28, citing record bookings, record backlog and customer pull-ins. The new positive is a CopperEdge linear-equalizer line-card win at a second hyperscaler, ramping 1H FY28. Mgmt has seen no DSP-driven pushouts but didn't update August's >70% FY28 backlog coverage, size CopperEdge or reaffirm >50% 1.6T TIA share by January.

Though the OI guide beat Street ($159.7M vs. $152.4M), bears will lean on the GM guide ex the modules being sold (64.3% vs. ~64.6-65% bogeys) but CFO said "64% is not a holding point” on the way to SMTC’s 70% GM target. Mgmt also softened its 1.6T >50% share timing, conceded a couple of ACC proliferation sockets went to the existing solution and pushed photodiodes to a later generation.

Key Takeaways

  • Guide. FQ4 revenue of $480M (+75% y/y) vs. Street ~$450M (+~64%) puts SMTC roughly 1.5–2 quarters ahead of the Street’s prior revenue path. EPS of $1.33 compares with Street $1.29. Mgmt built the guide from DC ~+40% q/q (~$203M), LoRa ~+18% (~$80M), consumer down ~10% (~$35M) and ~$56–57M of cellular modules for the full quarter. Backing those out, the rest of the legacy portfolio (~$105M) falls ~8% q/q despite the 14th week, which looks conservative. Mgmt said the extra week adds ~7% to personnel OpEx but little revenue outside consumer TVS, which ships more linearly.
  • Data center. DC revenue of $145M (+45% q/q, +158% y/y; last q +92% y/y) landed exactly on the $145M outlook given at Analyst Day, with growth across optical, copper and photonics. FY27 now tracks ~$520M (+133%, >2.3x FY26), ahead of the “more than double” target set five weeks ago. The FQ4 guide lifts y/y growth to ~+222% even as q/q growth eases to ~40% from 45%. Mgmt reaffirmed >$1B in FY28, which needs only ~+92% growth. Sell-side models already sit at ~$1.04–1.10B, so the real debate is whether FY28 can repeat FY27’s ~2.3x.
  • Demand. Mgmt cited record Q3 bookings and record backlog and said customers are calling to pull in and escalate orders “well within the lead time.” It reported no pushouts or cancellations, including from the Thai-flood assembly delays and 3nm DSP shortages flagged by Chinese module makers. On CY28 visibility (an analyst said buyside investors see CY27 as “bulletproof”), Hong pointed to backlog, the shift toward linear analog architectures and the breadth of programs. Separately, he said SMTC is “pretty optimistic” it will “smash” the FY30 framework laid out Oct. 15. What’s new: mgmt did not update August’s disclosure that FY28 was already over 70% booked, so CY28 conviction still rests on qualitative evidence.
  • CopperEdge. The lead hyperscaler (widely believed to be Google) is deploying 1.6T ACC in backplanes and added design wins on two new platforms that can serve inference or training. A second hyperscaler chose SMTC’s linear equalizer over a retimer on a major inference-platform line card: ~90% lower power, no added latency, pin-to-pin compatible with retimers. Deployment starts 1H FY28. Mgmt now calls onboard LE a bigger market than ACC backplanes and expects it to go mainstream at 400G/lane, which diversifies CopperEdge beyond one customer. The under-discussed nuance: Hong conceded that in the past month a couple of ACC proliferation opportunities went to the existing solution because hyperscalers “run out of time.” That confirms ACC breadth is gated by platform redesign cycles. Neither program was sized.
  • Optical. 800G TIA share is now “well over 50%” (from ~18% two years ago), and the 1.6T GN1834D TIA (Tower SiGe) won ECOC’s Optical Component Innovation Award. But Hong declined to reaffirm August’s goal of exiting FY27 with >50% 1.6T share. He said only that share will be “higher than today” at year-end and reach that level “in the near future.” 1.6T is expanding across fully retimed modules, with half-retimed (TRO) designs expected late in FY28. High-speed photodiodes were part of the 3.2T content story in August. They are now too late for 200G/lane 1.6T/3.2T NPO sockets and are aimed at a future product.
  • Margins. Adj. GM of 59.3% (+480bps q/q) beat Street 58.9%. Ex-module GM jumped to 64.0% from 59.7%, matching the Analyst Day baseline, and Signal Integrity GM reached 69.8% (from 65.3%). 1.6T FiberEdge/CopperEdge GM is estimated at ~75–80%. The FQ4 GM guide of 58.9% is below Street ~59.7–60.0% (one tally as low as 59.0%). Ex-module GM rises only 30bps to 64.3% despite a richer DC/LoRa mix, because photonics fab costs are running ahead of shipments. Mark called the drag “a pretty short near term” and said “64% is not a holding point.” He cited 1.6T, NPO accretion and linear architectures as the path to 70%.
  • Opex. OpEx of $111.2M (26% of sales vs. 30% last q) ran slightly above most models (Street ~$110–114M). Adj. OM still hit a record 33.0% (+860bps q/q) vs. Street ~31.6–32.5%. The FQ4 OpEx guide of $123M (+11% q/q) is well above Street ~$114–116M, driven by the 14th week and higher R&D for DC, photonics and LoRa. That holds the OM guide to 33.3% vs. Street ~33.7%. Mark expects a similar ~7% OpEx benefit when the extra week rolls off in FQ1.
  • NPO/CPO. Initial NPO revenue is still expected in 2H FY28. Engagements cover essentially all major module makers and ~4 US and 3 China hyperscalers. Mgmt calls NPO “LPO on steroid”: content rises from TIA-only in pluggables to at least TIA + driver at a linear premium, with optional VHP laser insertion. CPO remains a laser-only, CY28+ opportunity for SMTC. Hong argued serviceability concerns (e.g., demands for detachable connectors) favor NPO being adopted first. Form factors still vary by customer, and mgmt would not say which hyperscaler ramps first given ASIC/XPU timing.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bulls see SMTC as one of the cleanest ways to own the power problem in AI networking. Networking already draws 15–20% of data center power. As links move from 800G to 1.6T and 3.2T, linear (DSP-less) analog designs that cut link power by 50–90% go from optional to necessary. SMTC is one of the few merchant suppliers spanning copper (ACC and onboard linear equalizers), optics (TIAs and drivers for pluggable, LPO and NPO designs) and photonics (HieFo InP lasers). Mgmt says that breadth expands addressable content per module by >10x and its SAM from ~$3B to ~$15B. DC revenue has gone from $61M for all of FY24 to ~$203M in a single quarter. Bulls see the advantages as durable: 800G TIA share taken from ~18% to well over 50%, parts qualified at every major module maker, dedicated Tower SiGe capacity, first-mover status in linear equalizers (SMTC formed the ACC MSA) and a laser platform few analog peers have. LoRa adds a second high-margin, non-AI engine (Amazon Sidewalk, Ring, LoRa Plus) growing at roughly twice its 30% CAGR target. The module exit plus net leverage heading to zero leave a cleaner, higher-margin, self-funding company.

This quarter showed the growth broadening across products and customers, not just getting bigger. CopperEdge now has a second hyperscaler in a socket mgmt calls larger than ACC, redesigned “almost on the monthly basis” and set to go mainstream at 400G/lane. Late-October channel checks also suggested the lead customer’s ACC unit TAM is running ~2x prior expectations. FY27 DC is tracking >2.3x against a “more than double” target set five weeks earlier, and FY28 >$1B was reaffirmed on record bookings and pull-ins. Foundry supply is no longer a constraint, photonics has a quantified second 3–4x capacity step for CY27, and mgmt now expects to “smash” a FY30 framework of $3.2B revenue, 70% GM and $12.40–13.00 EPS. Estimates can rise if FY28 DC repeats FY27’s ~2.3x (~$1.2B vs. sell-side ~$1.04–1.10B), and each 100bps of GM is worth ~$0.17 of FY28 EPS. The multiple has room too. Street FY29 EPS is up ~13% since Analyst Day while the stock fell ~28%, compressing the FY29 P/E from ~16.8x to ~10.8x. In addition, Marvell’s pending ~$115B all-stock acquisition of Lumentum, expected to close in 1H 2028, creates a vertically integrated rival that some industry observers argue will push module makers toward independent TIA/driver suppliers like SMTC.

Bears see SMTC as a high-beta (~2.5), late-cycle derivative of the pluggable transceiver build. By FQ4, DC and LoRa approach ~60% of revenue. DC growth depends on one product transition (800G to 1.6T) and on one-time share gains (800G TIA from ~18% to >50%) that cannot repeat. About 71% of sales ship to APAC, home to most module makers, so SMTC is the most exposed link in the chain if hyperscalers digest transceivers bought in CY27. Part of SMTC's share gain came from having supply in a constrained market ("few of us who have the capacity"). As supply normalizes, rival TIA/driver suppliers such as Marvell and MACOM compete harder on 1.6T price and share. Meanwhile, the Marvell-Lumentum combination pairs Marvell's interconnect silicon with Lumentum's lasers and optical transceivers, a bundled offer SMTC cannot match. CopperEdge's first leg (ACC) has struggled to spread beyond one hyperscaler, and onboard LE and NPO are unsized FY28 stories. CPO, where the biggest content prize sits, may be captured by vertically integrated platforms, leaving SMTC with lasers only. Bears argue the bull case rests on three optimistic assumptions: that 1.6T share follows 800G's path, that FY28 DC repeats FY27's pace, and that GM climbs from 64% to 70% while SMTC builds out a capital-intensive laser fab.

This quarter gave bears more to work with than the headline beat suggests. DC and LoRa landed exactly on the Analyst Day numbers, so the upside vs. the raised guide came from the rest of the portfolio, including ~$9M of q/q growth in modules being sold. DC growth eases to ~40% q/q in FQ4, and ex-module GM rises only 30bps despite the richest mix in SMTC's history. The GM and OM guides are below Street while OpEx steps up ~11% q/q. Mgmt also softened its 1.6T >50% share timing, conceded a couple of ACC proliferation sockets went to the existing solution and pushed photodiodes to a later generation. It leaned on customer pull-ins and escalations rather than updating August's over-70% FY28 bookings coverage. In a supply-constrained component chain, that is also what double ordering looks like ahead of a possible CY27 transceiver overbuild.
