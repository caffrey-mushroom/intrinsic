Owning LITE would secure scarce AI laser supply and deepen MRVL's optical stack despite dilution risk

Oct 30

READ IN APP

On October 28, Bloomberg reported that Marvell was in advanced talks to acquire Lumentum, with an announcement possible as soon as the following week. No final agreement had been reached. Bloomberg valued Lumentum at about $83b, compared with roughly $221b for Marvell.

With just $3.92b in cash on its balance sheet, Marvell would likely need to fund a deal of this size largely with stock. At about 27 times Lumentum’s FY2026 revenue of $3.0b, the transaction would dwarf Marvell’s previous optical acquisitions: Inphi (about $10b in 2021) and Celestial AI ($3.25b, announced in late 2025).

Our view is that Marvell would be accepting substantial shareholder dilution—and some pressure on gross margin—to secure three assets: a scarce component in AI optics, the photonics half of a more fully integrated electro-optical stack, and an optical-switching business that complements its electrical switches. We examine the implications below.

Marvell Is Paying to Own the Bottleneck

Subscribed

  • Lumentum’s laser capacity remains constrained. In a Bloomberg TV interview on October 9, CEO Michael Hurlston said the company’s optical components were effectively sold out through early 2029. On its August earnings call, management said EML supply continued to lag demand, pump lasers were effectively sold out, and shipments of some high-power laser categories remained below demand, despite quarterly CapEx of up to $167m. In March, Nvidia invested $2b in Lumentum, alongside a multibillion-dollar purchase commitment and rights to access capacity, and signed a parallel agreement with Coherent.- Bringing Marvell’s 1.6T DSPs together with Lumentum’s EML, CW and ultra-high-power lasers would put two of the highest-value and hardest-to-source components of an optical link under one roof. It would also turn part of the supply risk into an internal allocation decision and strengthen Marvell’s bargaining position with module makers.

Interconnects Are Moving Toward Electro-Optical and Heterogeneous Integration

  • As scale-up domains expand from tens to hundreds and then thousands of XPUs, copper links run into distance and power constraints, pushing more accelerator I/O to leave the package over optical connections. The advantage will go to companies that can co-design SerDes, drivers and DSPs with lasers, modulators and detectors. Marvell has been assembling these capabilities through Inphi for DSPs, Celestial AI for optical I/O between XPUs and memory, and a multi-generation microLED program with Mojo Vision, whose Series B Prime round Marvell led.- The Mojo design combines 300 mm CMOS, GaN-on-silicon microLED emitters, silicon photodetectors and multicore fiber, packing thousands of optical lanes into a footprint the companies compare to a grain of sand. Lumentum adds indium-phosphide (InP) laser sources rated at 120–400 mW for CPO and NPO, with high-volume shipments for scale-up applications planned for 27H2.- We do not believe a single photonic platform can serve every reach. MicroLED is suited to the shortest, densest links within packages and racks (scale-in); silicon photonics powered by external InP lasers is suited to scale-up links across racks; and EMLs and coherent-lite optics serve scale-out and campus links. Ownership of CMOS, InP, GaN and silicon photonics would give Marvell flexibility to select the right medium for each tier and integrate them within one package. Pairing Celestial AI’s optical I/O with Lumentum’s lasers could position Marvell to lead in scale-in interconnects as the market develops toward 2030 and beyond.

OCS Complements Electrical Switching and Broadens the Fabric Offering

  • Management expects Lumentum’s R300 OCS business to generate more than $100m in revenue in the September quarter (FY27Q1), its first quarter above that level, under a multiyear, multibillion-dollar purchase agreement. OCS directs light between endpoints without optical-electrical-optical conversion or packet processing. This makes it suitable for large, relatively stable flows, topology reconfiguration and failover, while packet switches continue to handle fine-grained traffic.- Marvell already sells 102.4T Teralynx switch silicon, and the two companies demonstrated Marvell DSPs and ZR modules operating over the R300 at OFC in March. Together, they could offer hyperscalers packet switching, circuit switching and the underlying optics as an integrated design. We expect OCS to extend beyond its first major deployment into more scale-up and data-center-interconnect architectures over the next two to three years.

Business Implications and Key Risks

  • Tighter Open-Market Laser Supply. If Lumentum prioritizes capacity for Marvell’s own products and custom-XPU customers, independent InP suppliers—Coherent in particular—could gain bargaining power.- Channel Conflict. Lumentum’s Cloud Light transceivers compete with the module makers that buy Marvell DSPs. Some of those customers may shift DSP volumes toward Broadcom, Credo or other suppliers.- Regulatory Approvals. Chinese module makers are major buyers of both Marvell DSPs and Lumentum lasers. SAMR’s review of US semiconductor transactions has included prolonged reviews, conditional approvals and cases that remained unresolved. Intel agreed to acquire Tower Semiconductor for $5.4b in February 2022 and initially expected to close the deal within a year. The US and EU cleared the transaction, but SAMR did not issue a decision. Other examples include Qualcomm/NXP and Applied Materials/Kokusai Electric. US semiconductor deals that SAMR has cleared have faced lengthy reviews and conditions. Nvidia/Mellanox was approved in 2020 subject to commitments to continue supplying Chinese customers; in September 2025, SAMR made a preliminary finding that Nvidia had breached those commitments. Synopsys/Ansys received clearance in 2025 after a 20-month review, subject to ten years of behavioral conditions and two divestitures.
