SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed NaV1.7 as a pain target and reviewed an investigational inhibitor being developed to overcome earlier limitations in drug exposure, potency, selectivity, protein binding, and tissue distribution. NaV1.7 has unusually strong human genetic validation because complete loss of channel function is associated with profound pain insensitivity. However, multiple prior programs have failed to demonstrate convincing efficacy.

The expert compared NaV1.7 with the approved NaV1.8 approach and other pain targets, including TRPV1, TRPA1, PIEZO2, and TRPM3.

Key Insights

The expert still considers NaV1.7 the most promising pain target discussed, despite substantial development risk and a long record of clinical failure. Earlier failures may primarily reflect inadequate bioavailability rather than invalid biology. Effective analgesia may require very high channel inhibition. Tissue distribution was ranked as the most fundamental drug property because potency and selectivity are irrelevant if adequate exposure is not achieved. Central nervous system penetration could improve efficacy because NaV1.7 operates at both peripheral and central nociceptive terminals. Developmental adaptation remains a major uncertainty. Lifelong genetic loss may alter neuronal development differently from pharmacological inhibition in adults. The expert estimated the investigational drug’s acute-pain proof-of-concept probability at above 50% and tentatively expected greater analgesic potential than NaV1.8 inhibition. Orthostatic hypotension was viewed as the most important likely dose-limiting risk. Loss of smell may also indicate target engagement. Combining NaV1.7 and NaV1.8 inhibition could provide additive benefit and improve tolerability versus maximizing either mechanism alone.
