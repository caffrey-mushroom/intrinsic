SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined data-platform demand among pharma and biotech teams, AI adoption, operating costs, and competition between Snowflake and Databricks. The expert described rising consumption as teams process existing data more frequently and bring pilot workloads into production—not simply as a result of storing more data.

Commercial analytics, patient-level analysis, data engineering, and AI-enabled workflows were key use cases. Adoption in life sciences also depends on trustworthy data, governance, privacy, validation, and human oversight.

Key Insights

The expert expects commercial analytics to provide the most reliable platform-consumption growth in 2027. Real-world data has greater upside, while clinical applications may advance less predictably because of longer validation and implementation cycles. AI’s influence on spending decisions is broader than its direct model-inference costs. However, the expert cautioned against assigning a share of consumption growth to AI without workload-level billing evidence. AI agents may increase recurring usage by monitoring performance and investigating exceptions. Narrow, high-volume tasks could be better suited to specialized decision workflows than to general-purpose language models. Platform choice is increasingly about existing data, skills, governance, operating costs, and production readiness. Companies may use both platforms rather than replace one with the other. The expert emphasized measuring AI economics by the cost of a completed, accepted workflow, including integration, quality assurance, retries, and ongoing support—not just token prices.
