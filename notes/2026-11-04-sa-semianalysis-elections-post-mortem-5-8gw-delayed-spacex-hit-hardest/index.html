Elections Post-Mortem Possibilities: SpaceX In Pain, 5.8GW Delayed

Author: Reyk Knuhtsen, Jeremie Eliahou Ontiveros, Muhammad Zuhair, Ellie Holbrook

Date: Wednesday, November 4, 2026 (published 2026/11/05 02:26:55)

The midterm elections have passed, and some of our “pause-candidates,” or candidates calling for a

datacenter pause in some capacity, have won seats. We wrote at length about how to read the impacts,

but let’s assess the situation.

This is our first pass at the most salient campuses that will likely be delayed, where we assume a rough

~2Q pause in any permitting processes. Adding them up, we see 5.8GW could be delayed across the US.

Executive Summary

Delayed capacity falls heavily on SpaceX. We’ve flagged their incredible buildout for some time

now, but as we stated two months ago, their permits were at risk of a Hinojosa win. Next is the

Vantage Stargate project, and Crusoe’s Microsoft buildings at Abilene.

After SpaceX, OpenAI would be taking a big blow, at over 2x the amount of Anthropic’s delayed

MW.

The delay on the chip-side could be overwhelmingly Nvidia’s worry, where only 600MW of the

5.8GW delay is attributed to TPUs.

Most onsite gas equipment facing delay is SpaceX’s, with the nuance being that Elon already

owns most of it, so the turbines sit unused for a while but no OEM loses a sale. What may be

affected are the recent Solaris rental and FTAI orders, which could get delayed or moved to

another site rather than cancelled.

Most of our delays come directly from 2027, and from Texas. We do want to caveat: there is a 5-week

window where the TCEQ pause can be lifted, and permits are issued. ERCOT finishes its review on

December 10, which the TCEQ pause is tied to, and Hinojosa takes office January 19th.

The sites that we see delayed are as follows:

SpaceX’s Starbase GPU campus is the most salient, near-term delay to account for. We suspect the

campus could be up to 2GW, but the air permit is still pending.

SpaceX’s TeraFab GPU campus will likely also be delayed, as no air permit is seen in the register

still. We model this as ~900MW.

Nexus in Hubbard, Texas, 800MW TPU for Anthropic, is now at risk too. The site is only

permitted for ~470MW IT now, and the remainder is subject to TCEQ pause.

Vantage Stargate Texas (1.4GW GPU) may have 700MW delayed outward from 4Q2027. The site

has ~709MW nameplate (545MW IT) permitted, but a further 700MW IT has yet to be approved.

Google/Crusoe Goodnight (800MW TPU) for Anthropic may have ~275MW delayed outward

from 4Q2027. The site has 525MW of grid connection, but the 275MW depends on an ungranted

air permit.

Crusoe Stargate Abilene Buildings 9/10 (672MW) for Microsoft GPU is likely delayed outward

from 3Q2027. The expansion’s air permit for onsite generation is still pending under the TCEQ

pause.

Nscale’s 75MW IT Lease with Figure AI in Cedarvale, Texas is likely delayed outward from

4Q2027. The site has its stormwater permit suspended currently.

In 2028, we had already accounted for the TCEQ pause as mentioned in our last note, as such, the delays

are not nearly as widespread. The main campus we’re confused on is Riot Corsicana, where only one

building has a stormwater permit. We delay one building out of 1H2028, but will dig deeper to see if

anything else arises. The rest of the 1H2028 total is the later phases of the Texas sites above (TeraFab,

Abilene, Vantage and Hubbard, ~740MW), CloudBurst San Marcos (~100MW, no prime-power air

permit), and ~270MW across six Oregon campuses held by Governor Kotek’s DEQ permit freeze

through July 2027.

Secondly, SB Energy Ohio’s Second Phase of 800MW of GPU in 3Q2028 for Nvidia+OpenAI may

likely be delayed outward, depending on pause length. The site has not yet received air permits for its

turbines, its water-quality certification, or its Corps 404 permit.

The Beneficiaries: Where Does Demand Go?

With all these projects potentially delayed, a few segments will directly benefit. In this instance we think

Neoclouds will perform very well. Spot price for GPUs should increase as usable GPU capacity takes a

plunge. This bodes well both for IaaS contracts, on-demand, or Token Factory type products. Through

YE2028, we see the big 4 neoclouds have >6GW of capacity available.

If demand is to spill overseas instead, CoreWeave, Nscale, Firmus, and Nebius are already set to perform

well.

Overseas builders might also see a flood of demand too, as OpenAI and Anthropic look to diversify. We

estimate over 4 GW are available to be contracted through 2028 by the largest builders.
