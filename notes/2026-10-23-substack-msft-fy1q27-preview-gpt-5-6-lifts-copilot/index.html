Copilot seat penetration among customers covered by all three channels continued to rise in the third quarter.

  1. We update our Copilot Seat Model each quarter

Following this quarter’s channel discussions and a review of how orders around quarter-end should be allocated, we have updated our Copilot Seat Model as follows:

  1. End-December 2024: approximately 5.3m seats.
  2. End-March 2025: approximately 8m seats.
  3. End-June 2025: approximately 10.3m seats.
  4. End-September 2025: approximately 12.5m seats.
  5. End-December 2025: approximately 15m seats.
  6. End-March 2026: approximately 22m seats.
  7. End-June 2026: approximately 31m seats, revised down from our estimate of 37m last quarter.
  8. End-September 2026: we estimate approximately 37m seats.
  9. Differences in quarter-end deal closing and seat assignment affected our previous estimates

Some channels reported this quarter that certain orders originally expected to close by end-June actually closed in early July, before our previous interviews. The lag between deal closing and assigning seats to users also affected our previous quarter-end seat estimates. Following this quarter’s checks, we reviewed the timing of these orders and seat assignments and adjusted our historical quarterly estimates.

  1. Sales growth this quarter still came mainly from existing customers expanding deployments

Last quarter, our channel checks indicated that some large enterprises had begun purchasing thousands or tens of thousands of Copilot seats at a time. Growth came mainly from customers expanding their purchases after smaller deployments. We continued to track this trend this quarter. Microsoft’s first fiscal quarter is typically seasonally weaker for sales, but channels continued to see existing customers add seats and some large enterprises expand deployments after completing pilots.

  1. Existing customers continue to expand, while large deals slow at the start of the fiscal year

Sales Partner C reported that seat growth this quarter came more from existing customer expansion, while large deal activity was weaker than in FY26Q4:

‘Growth is coming more from seat expansion than from net new customers. Among enterprise customers, very few are not using Copilot at all. There has been some expansion in Q3 for sure, mostly because of E7 upgrades, Cowork adoption and customers consuming Copilot Studio, among other things.’

‘We typically see huge orders in calendar Q2. They haven’t continued to accelerate this quarter because whoever made the decision has already made it. We expect some additional big orders in calendar Q4, but we haven’t seen similar big order activity in Q3.’

On future demand, Sales Partner C said contract renewals would bring another round of purchases:

‘Generally, after Microsoft’s fiscal Q4, this quarter is a little slower. October to December is when we have another big wave of customers whose contracts are up for renewal. We expect that quarter to be much stronger than this quarter.’

Sales Partner D also observed that growth was coming more from existing customers expanding purchases:

‘It was a solid quarter. Growth was strong, but it was not a massive breakout. What we are seeing is more about quality: there are not that many new customers coming in; it is more about larger expansions.’

Seasonality at the start of the fiscal year still affects deal timing. In last quarter’s checks, some channels had already noted that July-September is typically a relatively weak sales quarter for Microsoft. In this quarter’s follow-up discussions, channels explained again:

‘Microsoft’s fiscal Q1 is always the quarter with the lowest sales, because most renewals happen in April-June, the last quarter of the financial year. Most of our sales also happen in that quarter.’

  1. Customers gradually expand purchases after completing pilots, security and compliance work

Sales Partner D has seen more large enterprises expand from hundreds to thousands of seats. Completing initial pilots and security and compliance assessments has been an important reason for this expansion:

‘Before they scale up to thousands of seats, customers need to run pilots, do security assessments and compliance assessments, and have data security protocols in place. The natural timeline of going from pilot to scaling can easily take a year, which is why we are starting to see more expansion now.’

On how long the momentum in seat additions could last, Sales Partner D said:

‘We are talking about adding seats. I think that momentum will last for another three or four quarters, maybe even up to 1.5 years, or six quarters.’

  1. Large customers deploy in phases, with rollouts continuing for several quarters after signing

Sales Partner E has also seen large customers continue their deployments, with purchases and activation typically taking place in phases:

‘These large deployments are usually executed in phases. Purchases and activation also take place in phases. Seats are contractually purchased, then technically assigned to users, before users gradually reach meaningful, repeat usage.’

‘If a large order takes place in one quarter, that does not mean it will all be adopted and deployed in that quarter. It will be adopted and rolled out gradually over the next consecutive quarters. ... That is a pattern we have observed over the last two or three quarters.’

  1. Actual purchase prices have declined, with differences across contracts

For customers covered by Sales Partner D, actual Copilot purchase prices fell from approximately $25 per user per month about a year ago to approximately $20 in the third quarter. Sales Partner E, however, reported broadly stable prices and discounts in the third quarter.

  1. E7 sales progress

E7 adoption remains low. Bundling is attractive to customers planning broad Copilot deployment who also need identity, security and Agent governance capabilities. Customers still running pilots or deploying Copilot only to selected roles may not need a full upgrade to E7.

  1. E7’s share of seats continues to rise among channel customers

E7 accounted for approximately 5%-6% of seats among Sales Partner C’s customers in the third quarter and is expected to rise to 9%-10% in the fourth quarter. Among Sales Partner D’s customers, the share rose from below 1% in June to approximately 3%-5% in September and is expected to reach approximately 7% in the fourth quarter. Sales Partner E reported a current share still below 10%.

  1. Bundled capabilities and contract terms help drive E7 adoption

Sales Partner C said Microsoft is more inclined to promote E7 adoption than offer large discounts on standalone Copilot. Buying Copilot together with security, identity and governance capabilities reduces the work involved in purchasing and managing separate products and allows discounts to be structured across the overall contract.

  1. E5 plus standalone Copilot remains suitable for partial deployments

Sales Partner E believes that retaining E5 and buying Copilot separately remains reasonable for customers still at the pilot stage or planning to provide Copilot only to selected users.

  1. FY27Q2 sales outlook and progress in usage-based billing
  2. Copilot penetration could accelerate in FY27Q2 versus FY27Q1

Channels expect year-end budgets, contract renewals and the expansion of earlier pilots to support adoption next quarter. Sales Partner C and Sales Partner D expect Copilot seat penetration among their customers to rise further in the fourth quarter. Sales Partner E also said renewals and the release of year-end budgets would support customer expansion.

  1. The share of usage-based revenue is rising, but the near-term impact is likely to be limited

Outside Copilot Studio, GitHub Copilot can incur usage-based fees for additional requests and model usage. Cowork charges for actual Copilot Credits consumed in addition to the M365 Copilot license. We also investigated this usage-based revenue. Channels reported this quarter that fixed seat fees remain the main source of Copilot-related revenue. Usage-based spending is growing as a share but remains supplementary. New consumption is beginning to come from Agent applications in data integration and business processes. After signing, customers still need to define use cases, connect systems and complete testing before sustained consumption builds.

  1. Usage-based spending is rising as a share but remains a minority

Among customers covered by Sales Partner C, usage-based charges rose from approximately 7%-8% of Copilot-related spending in FY26Q4 to approximately 15% in FY27Q1. The scope includes GitHub Copilot and related Agent products:

‘Most of the Copilot revenue we are talking about is still seat based: roughly 85% comes from seats and around 15% is consumption based. Consumption is increasing, but it is still a small portion. Cowork, Work IQ, Agent365 and Dynamics Agents were mostly introduced earlier this year.’

Sales Partner D reported that usage-based charges as a share of Copilot spending, including GitHub Copilot, rose from 6%-7% about a year ago to approximately 15% in FY27Q1 and are expected to reach approximately 17%-18% in FY27Q2. Sales Partner E has seen a similar pattern and expects usage-based spending to increase as more Agents are deployed:

‘Usage-based charges are probably still a minority of overall Copilot spending. But the usage-based portion should rise as customers start deploying Copilot Studio Agents, Cowork premium interactions and some Azure Agent workloads.’

  1. Beyond coding, consumption is beginning to come from data integration and business processes

We also asked which scenarios were generating consumption outside coding. Sales Partner C said many current applications involve connecting data from existing enterprise systems and working with tools such as Excel and Power BI:

‘Outside GitHub Copilot and coding, we see some Work IQ and Cowork use cases where customers are trying to pull in data from small ERPs and other legacy applications. Customers using Dynamics are also starting to adopt Dynamics Agents. Most Cowork use cases still involve integrating data from different sources and are heavily related to Excel and Power BI.’

  1. Customers still need to validate use cases, integrate systems and complete testing after signing

On when these applications could drive more visible consumption growth, Sales Partner C expects a lag between contract signing and sustained use:

‘Customers are only starting to sign contracts, so there may not be a large acceleration immediately in calendar Q4. As the number of applications and integrations increases, we could see a further ramp-up later.’

‘These implementations take longer. Implementing Cowork is not just about purchasing a license. With Copilot, you can purchase a license and give it to the user. Cowork is more for power users and needs an application, a business use case and testing. So the initial rollout is slower, but as adoption increases, token usage will also increase.’

  1. Copilot functionality and usage

Last quarter, we tracked improvements in Copilot’s document generation and handling of long-running tasks. This quarter, we investigated how these improvements work in practice. Feedback from channels and enterprise users indicates that Copilot is becoming more useful for everyday Office tasks, although gaps remain in complex research, specialist software development and multi-step Agents.

  1. Copilot is more useful for everyday Office tasks, while gaps remain on complex work

This quarter’s feedback points to clearer improvements in Excel formula assistance, first drafts of PowerPoint presentations and summaries of enterprise materials. Copilot can reduce some basic processing and content organization work, but complex modeling, complete presentations and long-running tasks still require human input or other specialist tools.

  1. Excel formula assistance and first drafts of PowerPoint presentations have improved

Sales Partner E sees improvements in Excel formula assistance, formula explanations, data summaries and analysis of enterprise workbooks. Complex financial modeling may still require specialist tools or direct coding. Enterprise User A also said employees use Copilot or ChatGPT to work with Excel formulas, code and data, with the choice varying by user and task. Sales Partner E offered more specific feedback on PowerPoint:

‘Copilot has improved for creating first drafts, restructuring presentations, summarizing source content and applying organizational context. Human review remains important for the structure and quality of storytelling required in executive presentations.’

Some channels also said this quarter that users can edit specific components of a presentation and use company templates, reducing the need to repeatedly revise the entire file.

  1. Improvements vary by application, and customers retain competitors for complex tasks

The extent of improvement still varies across applications. Sales Partner D particularly praised Copilot’s Outlook integration and everyday user experience, but remained more cautious on comparisons involving Excel, PowerPoint and long-running tasks:

‘Copilot has narrowed the gap with ChatGPT and Claude, but it has not yet closed it. Excel has improved a lot. PowerPoint is still lagging a bit, and there are still gaps on long documents and multi-step tasks. It wins on availability within the customer’s Microsoft environment and integration with the Office suite, but loses out a bit on raw capability. Most of my colleagues and I use the Claude plugin in Excel and PowerPoint, and it works better.’

Some channels also distinguished between everyday office work and complex tasks this quarter:

‘For fairly simple use cases, such as summarization, content retrieval and lightweight content creation, Copilot is good enough for most companies’ daily needs. But for complex Agents, deep research, detailed research reports and coding, I believe Claude and ChatGPT are still better.’

Support for custom Agents also remains inconsistent across Office applications, and the reliability of multi-step task execution still needs improvement.

  1. Combining better foundation models with enterprise context brings clearer improvements

This quarter’s feedback suggests that stronger foundation models allow Copilot to use enterprise materials more effectively and produce content better suited to the work at hand. Improvements cover information retrieval, context understanding and task execution. Simply providing access to more files is not enough to ensure output quality. For example, when generating documents in Word, Copilot can also draw on chats, meetings, summaries, PowerPoint presentations and images. Some channels described the changes from combining models with enterprise materials as follows:

‘Early on, even when we provided a large amount of work-related material, the results could still be inaccurate because the models were weaker. From GPT-5.6 onward, improvements in context understanding, long-running tasks and reasoning have noticeably improved the output quality from combining work data with the foundation model.’

Response speed also improved this quarter. Some feedback pointed to a clear reduction in Copilot latency and a narrower gap with OpenAI products. Enterprise User B also believes that better speed and stability have made Copilot more useful for Office tasks. Sales Partner D believes that users’ day-to-day work does not become more complex every quarter. As Copilot gradually closes the capability gap, its existing advantages in data, permissions and Office integration should become more apparent. This is consistent with our earlier enterprise AI research: current models can already handle many everyday tasks, while further ROI gains and higher adoption depend more on changes to workflows and employee usage habits. Whether time saved on individual tasks translates into additional output also depends on corresponding changes to the division of work, review processes and delivery arrangements.

  1. Usage momentum improved around July-August; sustained adoption still varies by role and rollout approach

Our checks this quarter indicated an improvement in actual Copilot usage, with some channels pointing to a more noticeable change around July-August. Activity still varies substantially by role, and customers are using training and the promotion of use cases to increase actual use of purchased licenses.

  1. Weekly active usage and satisfaction metrics have improved

Some channels provided the following feedback this quarter on weekly active use within the purchased seats they track:

‘Weekly active users as a percentage of purchased seats had fallen to single digits at the low point and have recently recovered to approximately 30%-40%. The more noticeable inflection was around July-August.’

‘Sustained weekly use is about 30%-55% of purchased seats, with large variation by role. Some user categories can reach 80%-90%, while others may be only 0%-20%.’

Some channels also reported this quarter that NPS scores for Word, Excel and PowerPoint were approximately 2-3 points higher than in July.

  1. Roles and rollout approaches affect sustained adoption

Sales Partner D said existing licenses are shifting from being unused or rarely used to more frequent usage. Customers are investing in training, exploring and sharing use cases, and working with partners to increase actual use. Sales Partner E also observed higher activity among customers that define use cases by role, appoint internal champions, provide scenario-specific training and role-based prompts, and reclaim inactive licenses. Enterprise User B offered a specific example of how task allocation has changed: approximately 60% of Office-related tasks now use Copilot and 40% use ChatGPT, whereas ChatGPT accounted for most usage initially. This reflects improvements in Copilot’s quality, speed and stability, as well as ChatGPT usage limits and Claude’s more limited availability within the company.

  1. Copilot Cowork progress

In this quarter’s checks, we have seen some customers begin using Cowork for workflows that run across applications and continue over time. Adoption remains at the stage of validating use cases and gradual rollout. Customers need to assess the value delivered by each task, running costs and whether employees can use it consistently. This quarter’s channel checks indicated a positive response from Cowork customers, with adoption accelerating around July-August. For example, feedback included:

‘Customers who have gained access to Cowork are very enthusiastic about using it, and their feedback after using it is also very good, much better than the feedback we previously collected on Copilot Chat. Taking both into account, I feel growth should still be quite fast.’

‘A product change around July-August helped accelerate adoption. Cowork used to be hidden under Agents in the sidebar and was not easy to find. After the change, its entry point was elevated to the same level as Chat and Code, making it much more visible. Promotion also increased, making it easier for users to discover and use the product.’

  1. Usage is expanding from one-off interactions to ongoing workflows

Cowork use cases are beginning to include scheduled execution, ongoing monitoring and consecutive tasks. One observed use case involves moving information from emails into an internal enterprise ticketing system and continuing to track the resulting tasks:

‘After receiving an email, a customer can use Cowork to build an automated flow that organizes the information in the email into an internal ticket management system and continues to monitor the relevant tasks over time. We have already seen such cases. From a use-case perspective, it is closer to tools like Codex, where tasks can be deployed and executed.’

Document creation can also be broken down into consecutive steps, such as collecting information, organizing the structure and generating content.

  1. Access to data in the Microsoft environment is attractive, while customers continue to validate individual use

cases

Existing connections to Office materials and permissions encourage customers to try Cowork in enterprise workflows. Customers are still testing specific use cases individually and gradually connecting the relevant systems. Sales Partner C is still selecting suitable users, testing business value and completing system integration. On the product experience, Sales Partner C said:

‘Copilot Cowork is still new and, if you ask me, it is not as mature as Claude Cowork. But within the Office 365 data it can access, I think it is reasonably good.’

Sales Partner E also believes Cowork and Agent capabilities are beginning to help Copilot handle longer, multi-step tasks. Existing identity, permissions and Office infrastructure support adoption.

  1. Wider adoption requires companies to validate task value and manage usage and budgets

Fixed seat purchases are relatively easy to budget for. Cowork involves ongoing execution and usage-based charges, so rollout also requires clarity on which tasks are worth running, who pays and how usage is controlled. Training and usage management will affect customers’ eventual ROI.

  1. Validate task value first, then train employees and expand usage

Enterprise User B believes users already familiar with Claude or Codex may not immediately increase Cowork usage. Other employees need training in Skills, browser operations and multi-step tasks. The company envisages reading calendars and work records across applications to fill in timesheets automatically and enter them into the HR system. Adoption, however, depends on how much time this process saves and what it costs:

‘If that costs $100 per employee just for that task, I would never do that. So it really depends on the cost. What I would probably do is select the users who can really make a difference, validate the use case and running costs, and then roll it out to other functions. But we need to train people first.’

Enterprise User B further distinguished between fixed workflows and open-ended use: employees can directly use a task already configured for one-click execution. If employees are free to set their own tasks, they need guidance and boundaries; otherwise, they may waste credits and become frustrated.

  1. Usage-based billing requires more detailed budgeting and cost allocation

Some channels also pointed out this quarter that management needs visibility into consumption by department, user and task before moving from pilots to sustained use:

‘Forecasting the license budget is very easy: you multiply the number of users by the fixed fee per person. But with consumption-based billing, you don’t know how much a user might use. Companies need to know which department and cost center used how much, how to allocate budgets and how to forecast.’

Other feedback from our checks suggested that companies can already control costs through credit limits and model access permissions.

  1. Copilot model routing and running costs

We also examined the models behind Copilot and their running costs. Some channels said that when users leave the default Auto mode enabled, Microsoft can select models by task and route routine requests to lower-cost models, reducing the cost of processing those requests.

  1. Default routing helps control the cost of routine tasks

Some channels provided the following feedback on default mode and the use of Microsoft’s own models:

‘About 60%-70% of users keep model selection on Auto mode. In Auto mode, the model router chooses which model to invoke for a particular task. Where the router chooses MAI, those cost savings come through. Other users manually choose the model they want a response from, and Microsoft cannot directly push them to switch models.’

Some channels estimated that MAI models account for about 18% of requests and argued that daily tasks do not always require the strongest model:

‘In terms of pure benchmarks, MAI is still below the level of frontier models. But not every task needs that level of model capability; not every task is that complex.’

Other feedback suggested that routine requests are increasingly routed to cheaper OpenAI and Anthropic models, while also noting how automatic switching affects the user experience:

‘They are routing routine AI requests to cheaper OpenAI and Anthropic models rather than using their own Phi and MAI models. I think that is going quite well. However, when I use Copilot, I have also had worse results because it switches me to a weaker model. The ability to specify which model to use is also more limited in Copilot than in other applications.’

Preferences have also changed among users who actively select a model. This quarter, some channels said that users more often chose Anthropic models before GPT-5.6, whereas choices between OpenAI and Anthropic models became more balanced after GPT-5.6. Sales Partner E also cited prompt design and task orchestration as factors affecting output quality in practice.

  1. Complex tasks add pressure to running costs

In our checks this quarter, some channels estimated that power users account for about 20% of users, with requests costing about $12-$13 per user per month to process, compared with about $5-$8 for other users. Complex agents and long-running tasks may trigger multiple model calls, information retrieval and tool execution from a single user request, increasing both latency and cost. Under fixed seat pricing, a higher share of complex tasks adds cost pressure; usage-based pricing provides an additional revenue source for this consumption.

  1. Competition with ChatGPT, Claude and other products

In our checks this quarter, customers continued to recognize Copilot’s pricing and ease of adoption within existing Microsoft environments. As Copilot becomes more usable for daily tasks, these advantages carry more weight in product selection. Capability gaps in specialist tasks, however, still lead customers to retain competing products.

  1. Most channels recognize Copilot’s price advantage; functional improvements further strengthen value for money

Enterprises with a substantial Microsoft installed base can lower the incremental cost of adoption by reusing existing Copilot licenses or purchasing through broader contracts. As Copilot becomes more usable for daily tasks, customers can more readily put existing licenses to work.

  1. Broader contracts and bundled purchases lower the incremental cost of adoption

Sales Partner C said that more tasks closely integrated with Office 365 are moving to Copilot, with pricing, employee adoption and ease of rollout among the considerations. Sales Partner E also believed that lower costs and existing security protections help customers adopt AI while controlling costs. This quarter, some channels also noted that bundled discounts in large enterprise agreements can lower the incremental cost of adopting Copilot:

‘For enterprises with a large number of Office seats, E7 is very attractive. With a contractual discount, you are almost getting Copilot for free, and you are also getting governance. Microsoft can blend that discount across the entire E7 product line and make some very attractive pricing offers.’

Sales Partner D was more cautious about the price gap with competing products. The effective Copilot purchase price for its customers has fallen from about $25 to about $20 per user per month, but large OpenAI and Anthropic customers also receive discounts, so Copilot’s price advantage over competitors may not be particularly large.

  1. Reusing existing licenses can cover more daily tasks

Enterprise User B also proposed reallocating existing Copilot licenses to more active users to handle some daily tasks subject to usage caps on other platforms. With frontier models and Office and SharePoint integration, the enterprise sees good value in reusing existing licenses. It described its actual payments and usage limits as follows:

‘We never paid for tokens in Copilot except in Copilot Cowork. You could use Copilot in Excel and PowerPoint with no issues. I am not sure if it was unlimited, but across thousands of users, I have never encountered somebody telling me they were running into Copilot limits.’

  1. Existing identity, security and procurement systems reduce barriers to enterprise adoption

Enterprises already using Microsoft extensively can retain many existing identity, permissions and vendor management arrangements when adopting Copilot. Introducing a new AI vendor requires a fresh assessment of data access, storage and governance requirements, while business teams also need to justify the additional purchase.

  1. A new vendor requires additional justification for the purchase

Sales Partner D said that enterprises generally prefer to have fewer vendors:

‘When onboarding a new supplier such as OpenAI or Anthropic, you need to demonstrate why we cannot do this with Copilot, since we already use Microsoft.’

Some channels also said this quarter that third-party products require new security, compliance and permissions reviews. These processes may extend the time needed for adoption, particularly in regulated industries.

  1. An enterprise’s integration experience illustrates the impact of permissions and data storage requirements

Enterprise User B’s experience connecting ChatGPT before March 2026 provides more specific context. The enterprise faced two main obstacles at the time: access controls and data storage requirements.

‘We were struggling to get information security approval for the Microsoft 365 connectors into ChatGPT. Initially, ChatGPT did not always respect access controls in SharePoint. If you could connect to things you were not entitled to see, that was challenge number one. Challenge number two was that OpenAI was copying our files to its own servers and systems. That was not compliant for some client work requiring data to be stored in the European Union or in specific countries.’

The enterprise began enabling Teams, Outlook and calendar connectors in March 2026. SharePoint required folders to be reorganized and responsibility for content and governance requirements to be clarified before access could be enabled gradually by client or business team. This work continued into the summer.

  1. Microsoft’s native integration retains advantages as Computer Use improves

As ChatGPT and Claude improve their Computer Use capabilities, we examined this development in our checks this quarter. We focused on whether it enables competitors to handle Office and cross-application tasks more effectively, weakening Copilot’s integration advantage within Microsoft environments. Feedback this quarter suggests that native connections still reduce additional steps when information and workflows are already concentrated in Microsoft environments. Neither Sales Partner D nor Sales Partner E has observed a resulting slowdown in Copilot purchases among the customers they cover. Sales Partner C also considers Computer Use to represent only a small share of overall use cases today.

  1. Native calls reduce disruption and additional execution steps

Some channels said this quarter that executing tasks through native API calls reduces the disruption that browser or desktop operations can cause to a user’s current work:

‘Computer Use may take control of the mouse, switch pages and interrupt what the user is doing. If the information is already in the Microsoft environment, API calls can complete the task while users continue their own work and leave other workflows to the product.’

Enterprise User B also noted that once a tool is allowed to operate a computer, the information security team still needs to assess which actions it may perform. On execution efficiency and cost, the enterprise finds direct access to information through Work IQ more convenient than navigating a browser step by step:

‘If you are talking just about the Microsoft ecosystem, yes, there is some automation they can do, but it will consume more tokens because you need to click to open SharePoint, search for the right file and click into the folder. If you are already integrated with Work IQ, it is just faster and cheaper.’

‘Even with better Computer Use in ChatGPT, you need to go into the browser, open the application, see what is in the application, and then give the context back through pictures or frames to ChatGPT.’

  1. Competition remains strong within individual applications; cross-application integration favors Microsoft

Competitors can still compete strongly when they enter Office applications directly through plugins. Some channels distinguished between tasks within a single application and those spanning multiple applications this quarter:

‘Claude can connect to Word, PowerPoint and Excel through plugins, and competition within an individual application remains strong. But when tasks span multiple applications, especially when they involve information within the Microsoft environment, Copilot and Cowork still have an advantage.’

Enterprise User B’s use also illustrates the convenience of accessing information across applications. It has combined Teams meeting summaries with task assignments and project scheduling templates to generate work plans for the following two weeks. The enterprise compared how the two products receive task context:

‘You need to describe to ChatGPT which meeting you want a recap of, and sometimes ChatGPT does not understand which meeting. So you need to write much more about what you want to achieve. With Copilot, you tag a meeting: you do a slash, then select a specific meeting and tag additional context. That could be a person, a PowerPoint deck or Excel data. That is much more precise and straight to the point, even compared with ChatGPT with the connectors enabled.’

  1. Implementation is easier within Microsoft environments, with partner resources providing another advantage

The deployment advantages reported by channels mainly concern customers already using Microsoft products extensively for data, identity and workflows. Existing connections reduce some implementation work, and partners familiar with Microsoft products can more readily provide support.

  1. Multiple channels said that Microsoft customers can deploy Copilot more quickly. The table below shows channel

estimates of the time required for different projects. Sales Partner E provided implementation planning ranges by scenario.

Source: Funda channel checks

Sales Partner E said that this advantage is more apparent for customers whose main data and workflows are already in Microsoft 365, Dynamics 365, Power Platform or Azure:

‘For Microsoft-centric customers, Copilot can be faster to integrate because identity, permissions, Teams, SharePoint, Power Platform and Office application integration are already part of the platform.’

  1. Deployment differences also reflect partners’ implementation experience and resources. Sales Partner E noted that

enterprises are often customers of both Microsoft and its service providers, and existing relationships help implementation and rollout. Sales Partner C also stressed that actual projects still require work on permissions, data labeling and identity management. The associated consulting and implementation support affects both timing and cost. Views differed on whether the technical integration itself is simpler. Some feedback did not consider Copilot inherently easier to integrate, attributing faster deployment more to the number of partners and available skills:

‘Microsoft has many more partners available to help enterprise customers integrate Copilot. If a project’s systems integrator or service provider is equally familiar with Claude and OpenAI, deploying ChatGPT may take about as long as Copilot. But there are currently fewer partners with those skills.’

  1. The scope of an enterprise deployment also affects the time to initial rollout. Enterprise User A estimated about two

weeks for an initial standalone ChatGPT deployment, compared with about four weeks for Copilot deeply integrated into its enterprise environment, which involves more governance and permissions work. For subsequent model updates, the enterprise finds it easier to obtain approval for new models through Copilot. The standalone ChatGPT version requires a separate review and may lag the latest release.

  1. Copilot is more likely to become the default tool for daily office work, while specialist users retain

competitors

Feedback this quarter suggests that enterprises more commonly use several products according to task and role. Copilot handles more daily work within Microsoft environments, while ChatGPT or Claude remains in use for specialist software development, deep research and some complex analysis.

  1. Daily office work and specialist needs support the use of multiple products

Sales Partner D said that even when a new vendor requires additional approvals and integration work, power users are willing to wait an extra four or five weeks for integration to gain access to frontier capabilities. Easier implementation therefore does not lead enterprises to abandon competing products entirely:

‘Most clients currently follow a dual strategy, with Copilot plus either Anthropic or OpenAI. For convenience, and because Copilot is seen as good enough for daily needs, the default is usually Copilot. The other tools are provided to power users.’

  1. Enterprises allocate models and usage by role and task

Some channels also said this quarter that enterprises tier model access and usage allowances by role and task. General users receive basic capabilities, while only those with coding or deep research needs receive stronger models and higher allowances. This quarter, we continued to track how enterprises purchase and use models through Azure, with a focus on adoption of open-source models, fine-tuning and customization services.

  1. OpenAI remains the main model provider in channel samples

OpenAI still accounts for a large share of the model mix across the three sales partners’ customers. The shares of Claude and open-source models vary with the customer mix and the scope of purchases covered.

  1. Among the customers covered by Sales Partner C, OpenAI accounts for approximately 60%-65% of Azure model spending,

  1. Sales Partner D roughly estimates that OpenAI accounts for approximately 65% of its customers’ Azure model

  1. Across Sales Partner E’s portfolio of large Microsoft enterprise customers, OpenAI accounts for approximately

As Azure has expanded its model selection, some customers have moved their Claude usage from other channels to Azure.

  1. Sales Partner C roughly estimates that Azure’s share of Claude usage among its customers has risen from

As noted above, Sales Partner C also said Agent365, Cowork and similar use cases were increasing the use of Anthropic models. With Microsoft actively promoting Claude, we believe growing Claude demand could bring more model consumption to Azure.

  1. Open-source adoption is rising, but its share remains small; customers still weigh total costs before expanding

production deployments

Open-source model adoption increased this quarter, but its share of consumption remains low across channel samples. Lower prices and deployment flexibility are the main attractions. When expanding production use, enterprises still consider deployment, governance and operating costs.

  1. Among Sales Partner C’s customers, open-source models accounted for less than 5% of Azure model spending in

FY26Q4. Their share increased by approximately 2-3 percentage points in FY27Q1 and remains below 10%. The partner explained customer demand as follows:

‘One of the main concerns customers have about using open-weight models is security, governance and compliance. Microsoft is adding these models to Azure Foundry and providing compliance and security capabilities through Purview and other products, which is helping adoption. Customers do not want to put everything on frontier models, which are expensive. They want to separate the workloads that need to run on frontier models from the simpler tasks that can run on open-weight models. Hyperscalers offering these capabilities, rather than customers having to do it themselves, is increasing adoption.’

  1. Sales Partner D estimates that approximately 25% of its Azure AI customers have an open-source model in production,

but open-source models still account for only approximately 4%-5% of its customers’ Azure model consumption. The partner said:

‘Interest in open-source models is increasing because you have more control. You can run them locally or in the cloud and train them on your own data. There is also a realization that OpenAI and Claude will be very expensive if you really scale usage, so open source is attractive from a cost and flexibility perspective.   1. Sales Partner E has seen limited production adoption so far. Many customers test open-source models during

evaluations or proofs of concept, but few run them in production. Production use is concentrated in cases where cost control, deployment flexibility, data residency and customization are priorities.

  1. On promotion, Sales Partner C and Sales Partner D have not seen aggressive campaigns or additional discounts

specifically for open-source models. Sales Partner E said Microsoft offers partner incentives tied to Foundry consumption and promotes model adoption through integration with Agents and Azure services, as well as joint solution design.

  1. Sales Partner E believes the cost advantages of open-source models need to be assessed for each task:

‘Customers can use these smaller models for classification, summarization, extraction and other focused tasks. They also have the potential for customization and control over deployment, which reduces dependency on a single proprietary model family. But a lower token price does not automatically mean a lower production cost.’

‘Customers need to compare the total cost of successfully completing a business task, including infrastructure, evaluation, governance and operations. At the end of the day, what ROI are they bringing to their organization, whether they use AI Foundry or another vendor’s technology?’

Beyond token consumption for open-source models, we are also tracking whether fine-tuning, custom deployments and similar services can generate additional revenue for Microsoft. This quarter, we asked channels about pricing and actual purchases. Feedback indicates that a small number of customers have purchased these services, but most projects remain experimental. We expect the impact of these services or products on Azure revenue to remain very small.

  1. Sales Partner D said the share of customers purchasing fine-tuning and customization services is increasing, but
