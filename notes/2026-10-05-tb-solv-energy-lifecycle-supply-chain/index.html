Agenda

  • Solv Energy's (NASDAQ: MWH) integrated EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) and O&M (operations and maintenance) model as a structural moat
  • Rapid data centre energy demand forces developers towards fast-track project delivery and custom procurement
  • High-voltage material shortages and skilled labour constraints limit rapid capacity scaling capabilities
  • Grid dependence compels data centre developers towards combined cycle cogeneration and grid upgrades

Key Insights

  • Solv's limited notice to proceed to full notice to proceed conversion rates run 80-100%, as clients typically complete financing, land procurement and interconnect agreements before reaching 90% design stage
  • Third-party subcontractors charge 30-40% pricing premiums over self-performance, although civil vendors offer relief through competitive bidding, partially offsetting higher costs from smaller local firms
  • Fuel and consumables constitute c15-20% of total project value, a figure that has become increasingly difficult to forecast, given recent diesel price volatility affecting hundreds of pieces of equipment daily
  • Prefab modular substation skids built in lower-cost factory settings, such as Alabama vs California, are gaining traction and could significantly reduce weather-related delays and overall labour costs
  • Wage increases of 5-7% per year or higher are expected, as skilled labourers gain negotiating leverage amid inflation and labour shortages, particularly on the union side
