Key Takeaways

  • Guide. The Q4 guide of $1.695–1.725B (+18.7% y/y at the midpoint) and EPS of $2.19–2.25 sits ~4% / ~9% above Street ($1.649B / $2.03), with non-GAAP OM of 44–45% vs. Street ~43.2%. FY26 revenue rose $102M at the midpoint to $6.387–6.417B (+20.9% y/y vs. Street $6.32B, +19.4%), ~4x the $23M Q3 beat, and EPS rose $0.30 to $8.40 (Street $8.15). It is the third straight raise and came in ahead of investor expectations after the big Q2 raise. The FY revenue guide now sits ~$450M above the initial $5.95B, though ~80% of that increase traces to Hexagon (~$160M) and China upside (~$200M); stripping both out leaves ~14% growth. Mgmt still called the outlook prudent and noted Q4 is usually its strongest quarter.- Recurring. Recurring revenue grew 18% y/y (79% of mix vs. 78% in Q2), down from ~24% reported in Q2, when Hexagon added ~4 pts and mgmt put normalized growth at “high teens to 20%.” The 18%-vs.-20% comparison raised on the call mixes Q3’s reported rate with Q2’s normalized one; like for like, and assuming a similar Hexagon lift, organic recurring slowed to roughly mid-teens. With 2H recurring acceleration the key pre-print focus, this is the quarter’s main blemish, and the beat came from upfront revenue (+42% y/y) instead. Mgmt tied the slowdown to China timing in the year-ago quarter (China was 18% of Q3’25 revenue) and pointed to FY25’s 11% as the better yardstick; averaging Q2 and Q3, recurring is ~21% reported and mid-to-high teens organic.- Callback. Mgmt said the raise reflected a better Q3, improved visibility and strength across the business, led by record hardware demand and IP, and that the outlook remains prudent. Despite a lighter renewal year, bookings and backlog benefited from add-ons as customers expanded usage beyond initial plans, but customers are not yet paying significantly for agents; increased use of existing tools is what is driving more license purchases. The Q3 charitable contribution was in the “tens of millions,” which implies OM would have topped the 44.5% high end of the guide without it. On AI, CDNS wants to stay model-neutral and not be tied to one provider despite discussions with OpenAI this year, arguing Maestro’s edge comes from deep integration with its EDA tools rather than the underlying model.- Backlog. Backlog hit a record $8.2B (+17% y/y) and cRPO $4.2B (+20% y/y). Both barely moved q/q (backlog +~$0.1B, cRPO flat), implying Q3 book-to-bill of only ~1.06x. YTD bookings growth slowed to ~26% from ~55% through 1H (both analyst figures mgmt didn’t dispute), implying Q3 bookings fell y/y against a strong year-ago quarter. Looking ahead, mgmt said 2027 carries a larger renewal base than 2026 and backlog should be higher y/y, but it stopped short of calling 2027 a stronger year.- AI. Active Super Agent engagements topped 100 across ChipStack, ViraStack, InnoStack and AuraStack, vs. 20+ for ChipStack and 25+ for ViraStack cited in Q2, with ~10 public endorsements this quarter (mgmt says only ~1 in 10 customers ever goes public). Proof points: MediaTek put ChipStack into production verification (50–70% better coverage), Renesas cited 34x productivity from InnoStack, Nokia 5x from AuraStack, and Honda, using the new ChipStack RTL agent, saw 24% less area and 18% less power vs. pure foundation-model code. Mgmt credits agents for driving heavier base-tool usage behind the recurring step-up from FY25’s 11%.- Maestro. CDNS launched Maestro, a portfolio of domain-specialized models (small decision models up to frontier-class open-weight models, including NVDA’s Nemotron) post-trained on CDNS tools and data, plus a router that escalates to frontier models only where they do better, expected in just ~5–10% of EDA use cases. Mgmt cites up to 30% better quality and up to 5x token efficiency vs. general-purpose models. Customers can post-train Maestro and run it on-prem or in the cloud, while CDNS keeps barring them from post-training generic models on CDNS tools. CDNS will charge for model usage on top of EDA and agent licenses, and the CFO stressed that customers can bear their own model compute and that CDNS won’t become a general-purpose AI infrastructure business. Incremental vs. Q2, when mgmt said customers were demanding LLM choice: Maestro productizes that with a CDNS-owned model layer, a clear divergence from SNPS’s OpenAI partnership (GPT-Synopsys).- Hardware. Palladium/Protium posted another record quarter with new logos and growing hardware-cloud usage, and mgmt expects a record hardware year. The change vs. Q2: then hardware was still supply-constrained; now the CFO says there are “no capacity or supply chain constraints,” with inventory up another $58M q/q to ~$449M (+$72M in Q2) to ship against a significant backlog (one report still describes demand as exceeding available capacity). Demand is very strong across several large customers, including INTC; mgmt gave no 2027 hardware outlook beyond calling design-size growth a megatrend (quote #3).- Margins. Non-GAAP OM of 44.1% was in line (Street 44.1–44.2%) but down ~350 bps y/y and 140 bps q/q, as non-GAAP G&A jumped to $81M from $54M in Q2 ($39M a year ago) on the timing of annual charitable contributions (Q3 this year vs. Q4 last year). The FY26 OM guide rose to 44.7% from 44.25%. FY26 incremental margin is only ~45% (+$1.1B revenue, +~$500M op income) because Hexagon adds little profit; ex-Hexagon, mgmt puts it in the usual 54–55% range, expects Hexagon headwinds to largely fade in 2027 (as with BETA) and targets ≥50% incremental margins. EDA and SD&A carry the highest margins, then IP, with hardware lowest. GAAP OM fell ~550 bps y/y to 26.2%, below the 27.5–28.5% guide, GAAP EPS of $1.05 missed the $1.11–1.17 guide, and roughly 60% of the non-GAAP EPS beat came from lower other expense and share count.- IP. IP grew 24% y/y to an estimated ~$237–245M vs. Street ~$235–237M (segment dollars are derived from CDNS’s rounded mix), normalizing from >40% in Q2, which mgmt had cautioned not to annualize. Drivers: AI infrastructure, custom silicon and chiplets across interface, memory and foundation IP; Tensilica had a record quarter and a space company chose CDNS IP for a custom chip program. IP is near a ~$1B run-rate, with foundry diversification (the INTC 14A deal announced in Q2, Samsung) still ramping into 2027. On royalties, mgmt noted they already exist in Tensilica and design IP but called them “just one vector,” a contrast with SNPS’s ~$1B royalty ambition.- EDA. Core EDA grew 20% y/y (18% in Q2), ahead of Street in dollars, with 16 new digital full-flow wins, including very large customers adopting the full digital flow (synthesis and signoff, not just Innovus). Displacements broadened to analog (Spectre FX at a marquee infrastructure silicon provider, multiple 2nm projects) and PCB (Allegro at a leading EV OEM), and verification was strong in big digital and memory designs at hyperscalers and memory makers.- Customers. Systems companies are now ~45% of the business, and new chip designers include space and finance companies. Q3 brought a wide-ranging expansion with a marquee systems customer and a top telco that named CDNS its primary design partner. The CEO called design activity the strongest he has seen, with analog/mixed-signal and memory recovering alongside AI, helped by physical AI.- China. China was 16% of revenue (15% in Q2, 18% a year ago; one report puts Q3 at 14%), is growing 30%+ in 2026 and is tracking ~15% of FY26 revenue vs. the 12–13% initially assumed (~$200M of upside). The guide again assumes export controls stay substantially similar.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bulls see CDNS as one of two toll roads on every advanced chip and system design, with ~80% recurring revenue and a product set that gets more valuable as AI raises design complexity. Design activity is the strongest mgmt has seen, and the customer base is broadening well beyond traditional semis: systems companies are now ~45% of the business, every hyperscaler is building custom silicon, and new designers range from AI startups to space and finance companies. The structural tailwinds are multi-year: chiplets and 3D-IC multiply verification and IP needs, a more diverse foundry landscape (INTC 14A, Samsung 2nm, Rapidus) requires new IP ports and DTCO work, and physical AI pulls SD&A into new markets. The moat is the middle layer of mgmt’s “three-layer cake”: signoff-accurate physics engines and silicon-correlated data that take decades to build and that foundries certify. Bulls argue agents raise the value of that layer rather than replace it, because every agent action still has to run CDNS engines to produce a design that works, and agents run far more design iterations than engineers do.

This quarter fed that thesis. FY26 growth is now 21% (~18% organic), the fastest in years, with a Q4 guide ~4% above Street and a third raise in three quarters. Recurring growth of 18% is still well above FY25’s 11%, and mgmt attributes the step-up to agents driving more base-tool usage, which is the bull mechanism at work. Super Agent engagements topped 100 with measured gains at Renesas, MediaTek, Nokia and Honda, and Maestro gives CDNS a credible answer to the frontier labs: own the model layer, route among third-party models and keep the customer interface. Hardware is no longer supply-constrained, so record demand can convert into shipments. Estimates can move higher on several levers: 2027 is a heavier renewal year in which agent and model value can be priced into larger multi-year deals; Hexagon integration should lift incremental margins back to the usual 54–55% or better; IP should pass a $1B run-rate on foundry diversification; and buybacks of more than 50% of FCF shrink the share count. Clear evidence of direct agent or Maestro monetization would let the multiple re-rate toward the high 40s P/E range CDNS held for most of the past five years.

Bears argue AI is the first real threat to EDA’s business model in decades, and that the best of this cycle is already in the numbers. The structural worry is that agents and frontier models become the designer’s front end, where design intent and workflow live, and push EDA engines toward commoditized back-end services priced on usage. AI could also raise engineer productivity enough that customers need fewer licenses per design, while frontier labs, hyperscalers and customers’ own agents capture the value of the new layer. CDNS still discloses no agent revenue, and mgmt concedes customers are not yet paying significantly for agents, so the AI upside remains a promise. Customers are already scrutinizing token budgets, and startups are going after the agent layer too (NVDA also works with ChipAgents). Meanwhile SNPS has laid out explicit long-term targets and a royalty model, and CDNS declined to offer a framework of its own. The cyclical worry is that 2026 benefits from things that may not repeat: ~80% of the ~$450M raise since the initial guide traces to Hexagon and China upside, two record hardware years leave a tough comp, China is growing 30%+ under an export-control regime that can change at any time, and IP is lumpy by nature.

This quarter gave bears several data points. Recurring revenue, the cleanest read on AI-driven software consumption, slowed to 18% from ~24% in Q2 (roughly mid-teens organic) just as bulls were looking for 2H acceleration; the beat came instead from upfront hardware and IP, the lumpier part of the model. Q3 bookings fell y/y (book-to-bill ~1.06x), cRPO was flat q/q, OCF missed Street by ~25% on receivables and inventory, GAAP EPS missed its own guide, non-GAAP OM fell 350 bps y/y, and roughly 60% of the non-GAAP EPS beat came from below the line. Maestro also brings new costs: post-training models, running a router and possibly giving away model value to win the interface. Estimates and the multiple could compress on a hardware digestion year in 2027, tighter China export rules (16% of revenue), weaker renewal pricing if customers refuse to pay for agents, a slower Hexagon payback, or evidence that agents reduce seat counts. If investors conclude EDA is a middle-layer utility rather than the owner of the AI design workflow, the multiple would drift toward mature-software levels.

Let’s start with AVGO…

AVGO / Anthropic: $63B chip financing package announced – Broadcom

Bank of America, Blackstone, Citi and Morgan Stanley announced a $63B financing structure for an SPV to buy Broadcom AI accelerators and networking equipment for lease to Anthropic, with deliveries scheduled for 2027. Banks are arranging first-lien financing; Blackstone is the sole committed second-lien provider. The announcement formalizes funding for another large block of AI compute.

AVGO: DA Davidson upgrades to Buy/$400 as Anthropic/OpenAI funding keeps AI ASIC cycle intact

DA Davidson upgrades AVGO from Neutral and raises PT to $400 from $350 after reading Anthropic’s S-1, arguing an Anthropic IPO/capital raise followed by additional OpenAI funding should support Broadcom’s AI expectations over the next several years. The firm says AVGO is effectively pricing in near-zero probability of both funding events materializing, creating meaningful upside if AI customer capex remains funded. The takeaway: the AI/ASIC cycle looks more durable than current valuation implies.

AVGO: KeyBanc confirms Anthropic ASIC win, raises OpenAI volumes and says META Apollo remains in play

KeyBanc says AVGO has officially won Anthropic’s ASIC, a potentially sizable late-2028/early-2029 program with ~5–10M lifetime units, while correcting its prior view that META’s Apollo training ASIC had gone to MTK; that award remains open and AVGO is still bidding. OpenAI Jalapeño ramps with ~100K units in 2H26 and lifetime volumes rise to ~1–1.5M, while AVGO also owns Google’s TPU v9i Whale Fish and is expected to retain the higher-ASP v10i Merope/Blade Runner. Despite some share shifts, KeyBanc still sees AVGO as the dominant AI ASIC supplier and positioned to exceed ~$115B/$230B of AI revenue in FY27/FY28. OW/$575.

AVGO: Wolfe says $63B Anthropic financing materially de-risks CY27/28 AI revenue, with RVS the trade-off

Wolfe says the new $63B financing covers most of Anthropic’s CY27 AVGO purchases, making execution of the already-guided revenue much more likely and improving confidence in bullish CY27/28 supply-chain forecasts. AVGO is backstopping ~72.5% of the financing through residual-value support, with ~$45B maximum exposure on this tranche plus ~$29B from the prior deal, though that exposure amortizes toward zero over five years. Wolfe views the key tail risk as eventual AI compute overcapacity reducing residual values, but sees the near-term financing as clearly constructive. OP/$500

MELI: Citi upgrades to Buy as pullback creates attractive entry despite heavier investment cycle

Citi upgrades MELI to Buy with a $2,050 PT, arguing recent weakness over-discounts competitive/macro concerns while Brazil GMV should still grow ~30% in 2027 and MELI continues to outpace the broader market. The trade-off is margins: sustaining share gains likely requires deeper investment, with EBIT margin potentially below Citi’s ~7% 2027E forecast. Credit/FX remain the key risks, but Citi sees >20% upside and continued competitive strengthening, particularly as higher Brazil rates constrain rivals’ ability to invest.

META: BNP sees Muse as potentially >$8B revenue opportunity, but cost-to-serve must fall ~10x for economics to work

BNP estimates Meta is currently spending roughly $180/year per average Free Muse user, while current Power/Maximum subscription pricing remains far below cost-to-serve, making efficiency the key gating factor. Management is targeting a ~10x cost reduction over the next year, which BNP believes could enable gross-profit breakeven as early as 2027 and >$8B of incremental revenue by 2028 through advertising plus transaction fees. With ~25% of Muse users already shopping and Japan rollout underway, distribution/adoption looks promising, but monetization depends heavily on inference costs falling as planned.

INTC: Needham says $2.3B Classiq deal positions Intel as a full-stack quantum platform

Needham says Intel’s acquisition of Classiq, the largest quantum-computing deal to date, adds a critical software layer to its silicon spin-qubit hardware and manufacturing capabilities. Classiq’s high-level quantum programming and algorithm-synthesis platform could become increasingly important as the industry moves toward fault-tolerant systems, while also validating the broader quantum ecosystem and potentially lifting attention on peers like Horizon Quantum. The key risk is whether other quantum hardware vendors continue supporting Classiq once it is owned by a vertically integrated competitor.

PT moves to $150 from $180 on a lower 17x multiple, but BofA reiterates Buy and sees the recent de-rating as excessive.

WDC: Evercore says capex step-up is demand-backed, with ~3-year payback and long-term EB visibility

Evercore argues WDC’s higher capex is an investment in areal density and EB output rather than broad unit-capacity expansion, supported by customer visibility through 2030-31. Capex is moving toward ~8% of revenue versus a 4–6% LT framework, which Evercore estimates can support ~278EB/416EB of additional annual shipments in FY27/FY28, with incremental capex adding ~250–450EB per year as HAMR/ePMR complexity rises. Despite the spend, ~70% incremental GM and limited opex intensity imply roughly a 3-year payback, making the step-up economically attractive if demand holds. OP/$575.

CRWV: Bernstein cuts PT to $54 as revised funding math improves, but capital intensity still outweighs backlog growth

Bernstein lowers its incremental funding estimate for CRWV’s signed backlog to roughly $44–68B of new capex with ~$18–42B still needing to be raised, well below its prior ~$75B requirement, after switching to a power-based methodology. Including capacity targeted through YE28 lifts gross capex needs to ~$95–122B, but Bernstein expects a meaningful portion to be funded by new contracts and backlog growth. The core concern remains unchanged: shortening contract duration means each new dollar of growth requires more incremental capital, creating a structurally tougher funding model despite the lower near-term estimate. Underperform, PT $54 from $51.

ALAB: Citi stays Buy/$430, sees Dec-Q upside as PCIe 6 + Scorpio X drive higher AI content

Citi expects Sep-Q roughly in line, but sees ~12% revenue / ~21% EPS upside to Dec-Q vs. Street as ALAB content per accelerator ramps on Scorpio X and sales expansion drives content toward $1,000+ by Dec-Q. AI retimer revenue is modeled +34% in CY26 to ~$630M, with PCIe 6 adoption and ~20% higher generation-to-generation ASPs supporting the next leg of growth. Key debates are Scorpio scale-up demand, UALink competitiveness versus Ethernet, optical/CXL execution and customer diversification. Buy/$430.

SOFTWARE

MS argues ecommerce advertisers should monetize low-value/repeat in-game impressions rather than crowd out core gaming spend, making new ad verticals a positive for both U and APP. ORCL: Citi comes away more confident on AI monetization across Apps + Data after AI World Day 1
