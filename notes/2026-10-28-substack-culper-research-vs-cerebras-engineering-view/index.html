Consider me a mostly neutral, activist investor.

My background is in lab testing and post-silicon validation. I have experience with TSMC N5 (Cerebras uses this node), N5A, N5P, N4P, N3E, N3P alongside Samsung Foundry 5LPP, 4LPE, 4LPX (original and fixed version) and SF3 and Intel 18A.

There will be some discussion by sources cited on the possible root cause of the alleged issue at Cerebras. My view is much simpler.

Foundries give rules in PDK.

If you design and simulate against these PDK rules, you will not have electromigration issues.

Only way to fail electromigration is gross incompetence or intentionally running your chip outside allowable temperature and voltage conditions.

In this case (TSMC N5), the standard max VT corner is 110C at +5% voltage.

Let’s go through the Culper short report in order now.

Right off the bat, we have wrong information. Meta runs Nvidia GPUs with dummy FLOPS to avoid stressing the power grid. It is the famous “pythorch powerplantnoblowup” flag.

Retards, you cannot just calculate jack shit based on power draw alone. Temperature and voltage are needed as well and you do not have this info.

The new (attempting to ramp) CS-4 system is supposed to be much better in terms of thermals. What matters is voltage and max temperature across the waver. Power is irrelevant if you designed against PDK correctly.

This is honestly a compliment to Feldman for managing to sell SeaMicro to AMD. Dude is good salesman.

It is true that the Cerebras/OpenAI deal is massively in favor of OpenAI. Given that it was negotiated at fixed cost and Cerebras was retarded (or perhaps retardedly desperate) and did not secure power or equipment, they get hurt by rising prices directly.

Thermal miss-match of the 84 reticles in WSE do not just come from workload. Also from process variation.

From a design perspective, all these material-science and physics things are abstracted out. The PDK has rules and EDA software simulates your design against these rules. In general, it is difficult to fuck this up as long as you do the work and are not sloppy. The tools are good.

HOWEVER

BIG HOWEVER

If in post-silicon validation you fix not-reliability problems (functional, performance) by juicing voltage or enabling certain design-specific tuning registers to juice a particular sub-circuit, then you might be fucked on reliability.

It is entirely possible that Cerebras changed some settings they should not have and this results in some subsystem on the wafer to experience unacceptable electromigration.

This is the most plausible explanation for the many early failed CS-3 systems. Gut feeling I think this is the problem, not WSE warpage. Current density is the killer in reliability.

In theory, CS-4 should be better given the huge (alleged) improvements in power delivery and cooling, but given Cerebras track record…. I guess we find out in 6 months once CS-4 hardware gross margin flows through the financial statements.
