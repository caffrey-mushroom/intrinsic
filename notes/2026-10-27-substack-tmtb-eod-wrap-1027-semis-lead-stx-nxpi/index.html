Good afternoon. QQQs +1.3% opened +41bps and ground higher most of the day as the AI vibes kept rebuilding after Monday’s S-1, with Semis +3.9% leading for a second straight session while software +35bps got left behind again. As we wrote yesterday, we think the ST semis trade set up was cleaned up a bit following a better received Anthropic S-1, tea leaves pointing to a good Oct ARR #, along with positive financing news from META, AVGO, and SPCX all of which helped remove some uncertainty from the Lab ARR and funding debate, the two issues weighing most heavily on the trade in recent weeks.

More broadly, as we discussed this weekend, the past few weeks have forced investors to better understand the reflexivity of the AI trade, with Lab ARR at the center of it all. Even after two strong semis sessions, the vibe feels different from past rebounds: less exuberance, more appreciation for dependencies across the AI ecosystem that aren’t going away. We think that’s healthy and mature.

Neoclouds ripped as AI credit calmed and the Lab ARR reflexivity did its thing on the upside (NBIS +11%; CRWV +8%; IREN +8%). CRWV’s 5Y CDS back to ~1,071 from a 10/15 high of ~1,188, still ~26% wider than at the end of September:

AI losers went back to, well, losing: IT services (ACN -5%; CTSH -4.5%; EPAM -3%) and app software (HUBS -5%; MNDY -4%; ADBE -4%; INTU -3.4%) with no news beyond HUBS’s downgrade. META -1.6% slipped even as its $25B stock sale ended 5x oversubscribed.

Ok, back to ARR (you thought we were done?). Everything continues to tie back to Anthropic ARR expectations at the moment and it seems that’s all we’ve written about over the last 2 weeks. The tea leaves here kept improving. Ramp CEO Eric Glyman said overnight that 5.5 may have "hit an inflection point" with "a better Q4 for them than Q3" (the last official Ramp release was 10/11). Ramp economist Ara Kharazianposted new Ramp data at 12:50pm:

We think “remains below peak” is a glass half empty way of looking at things given it compares it with the one week spike in Fable usage, so ex- that spend is hitting new highs. We’ve talked about the caveats to Ramp spend (enterprises move slower and 48% of Anthropic revs come from marketplaces), but it’s still been directionally a good barometer for ARR (this chart shows +25% m/m spend, which would correlate to ~$76-$78B in Anthropic ARR based on historical).

Our view is that a good ARR # likely removes some of the worst fears/overhangs around Lab ARR that have been going around the last few weeks, but unlikely to resolve the more MT/LT debates around ARR growth sustainability. Bogey for Oct settling in around $75-76, which is a bit higher than I wrote yesterday, with anything higher than $78B viewed positively as would mean organic growth has reached June/July highs again.

Moving on…

Post-close, a couple mediocre reports from STX and NXPI:

STX -6%: Beat Street but came up short of the buyside bar on every line, and the Dec guide ($4.8B / $9.20 EPS) is well above Street but below the ~$4.9–5.05B / $10–10.50 bogeys. Exabytes flat q/q. Tough for a still somewhat crowded long following WDC’s better print

WDC is -2.5% AH in sympathy.

Sept-Q revenue of $4.23B (+61% y/y, +17% q/q) vs. $4.14B Street and ~$4.27–4.31B bogey. GM of 57.8% was ~20bps above Street but ~200–250bps below the ~59.8–60.3% bogey. EPS of $7.68 vs. $7.42 Street and ~$8.00–8.25 bogey.

The pushback is volume. Exabytes were 220 EB, +1% q/q (+21% y/y), ~15 EB short of Street’s ~235; nearline 198 EB, also +1% q/q. So nearly all of the q/q step-up was price/mix: implied revenue per TB rose ~16% q/q to ~$19.2 (+33% y/y). WDC did +7% q/q exabytes last week. That puts the exabyte debate on STX now, after it was the main knock on WDC.

Dec guide: $4.8B ±$100M (+13% q/q) vs. $4.50B Street and ~$4.9–5.05B bogey. EPS $9.20 vs. $8.54 Street and $10–10.50 bogey. Op margin guided “toward mid-50%” on ~$300M opex implies ~60–61% GM vs. 59.7% Street and the ~62–63.5% bogey. For context, WDC guided Dec revenue ~12% and EPS ~21% above Street; STX is ~7% and ~8% above.

Good bits: record FCF of $1.48B with capex at 5% of revenue, $400M of buybacks in the quarter, debt down to $1.6B after another $750M retired in Oct, and the “vast majority” of data center nearline supply allocated through CY28, with some cloud agreements out to CY30. Haven’t gone through the call yet.

NXPI -8%: 3Q landed at the low end of the bogeys and the 4Q guide came in below both Street and the ~$3.9–3.95B buyside bar. Not the TXN-style beat-and-raise bulls wanted, after a run into the print.

Analog ripped today (ON/MCHP +6%, ADI/TXN +4%) and NXPI was +3%, up double digits off the 10/14 low so giving back some of the pre-eps rally. Rest of analog -1% to -3% AH in sympathy.

3Q revenue of $3.79B (+19% y/y, +8% q/q) vs. $3.77B Street and ~$3.79–3.82B bogey. GM of 58.6% matched Street but was ~20–50bps short of the ~58.8–59.1% bogey. EPS of $4.20 vs. $4.12 Street and $4.20–4.25 bogey. Segments mostly in line with Street: Auto +7% q/q vs. the MSD guide, Industrial & IoT +39% y/y, and Comm Infra & Other (where data center sits) +54% y/y, ~$10M ahead.

The 4Q guide is the main issue. $3.85B at the midpoint is +2% q/q, right on the long-run 4Q seasonal but below $3.87B Street and the +3–4% q/q buyside wanted. GM guide of 59.0% in line with Street and at the low end of the bogey; EPS $4.36 vs. $4.38 Street. 4Q annualizes to ~$15.4B, so Street’s $16.1B for C27 (and the ~$16B 2027 target still in the deck) needs ~4–5% growth off the exit rate.

Good bits: FCF of $1.21B (32% of revenue) vs. ~$1.05B Street, channel inventory held at 11 weeks, and a new $5B buyback (~8% of market cap) on top of $1.3B remaining. Call ongoing.

In macro land, rates went sideways into tomorrow’s FOMC: 10Y ~5.00%, flat after a morning rally faded, and 2Y +~2.5bps to ~4.73%, after October Conference Board confidence came in at 91.7 vs. 81.0 expected and a mediocre $70B 5Y auction. With the Fed in blackout, hike odds for tomorrow sit at ~10%, but December is 80-90% priced for another +25bps (~20-23bps for the rest of 2026) with a March ‘27 hike priced too. WTI -2% to just under $100 after Saudi, Turkish and Pakistani officials met Iran in Tehran and agreed to keep talking, outweighing a Houthi strike on Saudi’s Ras Tanura refinery. BTC ~$80.5K (+80bps) after dipping below $80K early.
