SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed Sjögren’s diagnosis, current treatment limitations, and the prospects for dazodalibep. Community diagnosis remains difficult because gland biopsies, salivary-flow testing, ocular assessments, and formal disease scores are not routinely used. Earlier identification is important because established gland damage may be irreversible.

Patients are broadly divided into systemic disease with organ involvement and symptom-predominant disease characterized by dryness, fatigue, and pain. Existing immunosuppressants and rituximab can help systemic manifestations, but symptom- predominant disease remains particularly difficult to treat. No therapy is currently approved specifically for Sjögren’s disease.

Key Insights

The expert viewed dazodalibep as especially promising because it targets B-cell and T-cell interaction rather than focusing exclusively on B-cell depletion. Phase 2 efficacy was characterized as modest but clinically encouraging, particularly because benefits appeared across systemic activity and patient-reported symptoms. Improvement across dryness, fatigue, and pain would be more persuasive than a result driven by one component. However, a substantial fatigue benefit alone could still be valuable. Systemic patients would likely receive the drug first because uncontrolled disease can cause organ damage and is associated with lymphoma risk. Approval and clinical utility could remain viable even if efficacy is demonstrated only in systemic disease. Strong symptomatic results could have the larger long-term impact because most patients have substantial dryness and related symptoms. Wider availability of effective therapy could transform diagnosis by encouraging earlier testing, objective assessments, and formal scoring in community practice.
