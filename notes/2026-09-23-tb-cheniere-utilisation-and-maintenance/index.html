Agenda

  • Cheniere Energy's (NYSE: LNG) operational performance and competitive advantages against Venture Global, focusing on facility design, equipment availability and OPEX
  • Utilisation sustainability, maintenance and cost implications of running above nameplate capacity
  • Expansion and project execution, focusing on operational challenges at Corpus Christi and Sabine Pass facilities, integration with existing infrastructure and modular train design limitations
  • Long-term operational outlook, feed gas nitrogen bottlenecks, and Baker Hughes gas turbine seal upgrades

Key Insights

  • Sustaining facilities at 110-115% of the nameplate capacity doesn't meaningfully accelerate equipment degradation, though rising nitrogen in feed gas acts as a capacity derater by increasing cold-end refrigeration duty
  • Cheniere's mega-train architecture provides a structural O&M and availability advantage, with cUSD 0.40-0.45 per metric million British thermal unit of O&M and c99.5% equipment availability compared to Venture Global's modular design, which is now approaching USD 2.00 per metric million British thermal unit
  • Upcoming Baker Hughes mechanical seal upgrades for gas turbines could provide Cheniere with a meaningful cost tailwind by largely eliminating seal losses, which can cost USD 0.05-0.06 per metric million British thermal unit at current facilities
  • Although transitioning to mid-scale modular trains at Corpus Christi Stage 3 introduces near-term cyclical instability and boil-off gas fluctuations, initial train stabilisation takes 30 days vs 60 days for mega-trains
