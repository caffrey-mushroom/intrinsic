Lots of questions and potential implications of Jensen getting ousted - lets go over a few.

The first is whether NVDA keeps its supply edge when its TSMC capacity rested on Jensen’s relationships. Second, does NVDA cut prices to defend share from AMD and ASICs? Then there’s the neocloud complex and all the deals Jensen made: NVDA has been the AI ecosystem’s lender of last resort. Does a more cautious successor reined in by the board, without Jensen’s speed (60 direct reports, no committees), unwind those deals rather than extend them? (MRVL, LITE, CRWV the co’s who have benefitted most from Jensen’s dealmaking). It also seems safe to expect a real crackdown on AI chips reaching unfriendly nations, with TheInfo already reporting this morning export curbs widening to 7 countries including Singapore. Whether Jensen gets ousted or not, it’s consensus NVDA now having to be cleaner than clean in how they operate.

NVDA: Board weighs Huang’s removal – Reuters / WSJ

Nvidia’s board met Wednesday to consider removing Jensen Huang, Reuters reports; the WSJ says directors are preparing an ouster. No decision or successor has been announced. The leadership crisis adds uncertainty ahead of November 17 earnings, after the board had publicly backed Huang on Sunday.

NVDA: Five downgrades as Huang succession/governance risk overwhelms otherwise intact fundamentals

William Blair, Oppenheimer, Evercore Melius and Bernstein all downgrade NVDA as reports of a potential Jensen Huang departure, expanding allegations and board investigations create a major leadership/sentiment overhang with no obvious successor. Oppenheimer moves to Perform and removes its $315 PT despite unchanged CY26/27/28 EPS of $9.40/$16.84/$23.27, warning Huang’s outsized strategic role creates execution and potential “brain drain” risk; Evercore cuts to In Line/$186 from $465 and Bernstein to Market Perform/$200 from $400, both arguing prolonged investigations/succession uncertainty should drive further P/E compression. William Blair similarly cuts to Market Perform, adding slower AI buildout, regulatory scrutiny and competition to the execution risks.

Melius: “We thought we had experience, but nothing prepares you for what has happened at $NVDA over the last few weeks. The only situation remotely close to this we can recall was HP in 2010. Waiting to see what that Board would do (and who they would hire) that time didn’t work out.”

None of the firms are calling out a fundamental collapse: DC/AI demand, earnings and FCF remain strong and valuation has compressed sharply, but the group broadly sees leadership visibility and governance risk dominating the stock near term, with AMD/MRVL/AVGO among potential beneficiaries.

AMD: William Blair upgrades to Outperform as INTC slips + NVDA turmoil improve competitive setup

William Blair upgrades AMD from Market Perform, no PT, as INTC Diamond Rapids slips to mid-2027 while Venice ramps with strong demand and server rev is expected to more than double next year. Helios is now live at Anthropic, META, MSFT, OpenAI and ORCL, with MI500 due 3Q27, while NVDA’s issues could strengthen AMD as a second source; after an ~18% pullback, valuation has fallen to ~31x/21x CY27/28 EPS. Risks remain custom ASICs, Arm, HBM/CoWoS supply and warrant dilution.

SK Hynix’s HBM4 yield has reportedly risen above 70%, supporting its production ramp and competition with Samsung and Micron. The company has not disclosed the figure. Chosun cautions that differing measurement methods make manufacturers’ reported yields difficult to compare directly.

NVDA: Singapore reporting change draws fresh scrutiny – WSJ

A WSJ analysis questions Nvidia’s switch from customer billing locations to headquarters for geographic revenue reporting after it blocked Megaspeed in June 2025. United will block third-party personal AI agents from booking flights on its website, citing privacy, unintended purchases and passenger attestations.
