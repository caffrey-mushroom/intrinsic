Third Bridge · 2026-10-19

Analyst-led • 29 Sep 2026

Companies: ARM US, INTC US, META US, NVDA US, NET US, SMCI US, AMZN US, MSFT US, QCOM US, AMD US, 2454 TW

Santosh Shetty (SS) Technical Director at NXP Semiconductors NV

Saad Maqsood (SM) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • Arm's (NASDAQ: ARM) strategic transformation from a pure IP licensor to a merchant silicon vendor
  • Customer deployment dynamics across lead partner Meta (NASDAQ: META), alongside order ramps with OpenAI, Cloudflare (NYSE: NET) and Super Micro Computer (NASDAQ: SMCI) server rack integrations
  • Supply chain bottlenecks and wafer allocation at TSMC (TPE: 2330)
  • Ecosystem positioning against open-source RISC-V server architectures
  • Commercial trajectory towards Arm's USD 15bn AGI (Arm General Infrastructure) CPU revenue target by FY31

Key Insights

  • Arm's single-thread performance gap vs x86 remains a real enterprise barrier, with x86 cores reaching 5GHz while Arm data centre cores historically peaked at 3-3.8GHz until recently
  • Expert believes the biggest underappreciated risk is silicon execution – falling behind on specs such as PCIe Gen6/7 or DDR5 directly threatens market share. Furthermore, foundry blockage is main bottleneck concern. "You have to wait for 6-8 months after delivering GDS to get the first silicon"
  • Qualcomm and Nvidia building their own Arm-based CPUs reduces royalty revenue, yet the overall market expansion means Arm still benefits from broader ecosystem growth
  • Meta and OpenAI debuting homegrown RISC-based data centre processors at Hot Chips signals a credible alternative path that could gradually erode Arm's hyperscaler CPU footprint over time. Arm's CPU role in hyperscaler stacks is just one component, leaving it vulnerable as RISC-V cores become increasingly competitive
