DCD Connect London 2026 Conference Note Nicolas Bontigui, Reyk Knuhtsen | Tuesday, September 29, 2026

DCD Connect London brings together the operators, developers, investors and equipment suppliers behind Europe’s datacenter buildout. It is one of DatacenterDynamics’ Connect conferences, which also run in the US, the Middle East, India, Asia Pacific and Latin America.

Industrials

  • Munters quotes 1 year for a chiller, and Excool confirms the market lead time for air-cooled chillers at ~1

year today. Munters mainly points to axial fans’ supply, with compressors also short, both at 30-35 weeks. We covered this for Industrials subscribers in our recent note covering the EC fans supply crunch. Excool confirmed the supply chain issues, says EC fans exceed 35 weeks and, as we also covered in our Heat Exchanger note, says HXs are constrained because of limited tier 1 suppliers Excool says it can deliver in ~20-25 weeks and is winning projects on that Adiabatic cooling barely registers. Munters discusses it with clients, but most go for an air-cooled chiller or straight to a dry cooler, and Excool counts only a few deployments in EMEA

  • A major colo´s procurement team indicated Vertiv quoted roughly 50% above peers, at ~$170K a chiller.

OEMs indicated that chiller prices continue to rise in line with component cost increases.

  • Vertiv CDU lead times reach ~6-12 months while Liquidstack manages to deliver in ~3 months from

customer approval.

  • CDU quality issues: CoolIT sees leaks and the joints between components as the most likely cause.

Every major OEM buys from the same high-quality suppliers such as Alfa Laval for HXs, Belimo and Victaulic. LiquidStack flags third-party commissioning, sometimes skipped or run poorly, like filling the primary loop with tap water instead of treated fluid. The minerals and chlorides then scale and corrode the HX plates Manufacturers can approach CDUs, sometimes seen as a commodity and standardized product, differently. Reinforced units using stainless steel are more robust when it comes to leaks but cost more and take longer to manufacture Another EU equipment supplier delivers a 6.5MW CDU in September that fills over half a shipping container. Its pipes are too big for a FAT, so only electrical and controls run in the factory Liquidstack supplies CDUs to Microsoft, Cerebras and Nebius

  • Armada quotes 3 months lead time for a 2MW Leviathan module and 6 months for their Orion 10MW

block. Eaton quotes ~20-25 weeks for an ePOD. Flex / Anord Matrix puts itself at ~40-45 weeks for a 2MW pod. Armada indicates it is strong on execution and holding lead times. Has spare capacity, with manufacturing capacity in Ireland and Arizona Flex offers both LV-only pods and LV + MV pods, and names MV switchgear currently the longest lead time item Eaton is sending 20 ePODs to Berlin and is also present in Zaragoza Flex is working with Tillion in Zaragoza, while its US customers include CyrusOne and EdgeConneX. Flex is supplying busbars to Google in the US

  • Armada says it is close to Schneider Electric, and it uses Motivair/ Schneider mechanical equipment in

their modules. Eaton also uses Schneider gear inside its pods because customers ask for it As we covered in our LEGO datacenters deep dive, Schneider sets up a factory near the customer for projects >1GW

  • Weichai quotes a 6-month production time on 1,000 units a year of capacity, possibly across diesel and

LNG, and is building another plant in China. Weichai does not seem to have US LNG customers yet, and its “5GW” for 2027 does not seem to be ordered yet AVK is supplying 80x 3.2MW gensets (~256MW) to Nscale Portugal and tendering for the next building. AVK says the capex there is now funded, so it is just a PPA, with no rates given.

Datacenter

  • Microsoft signed 120MW with Pure DC at €140/kW/month. A 20MW deal in Spain went for

€125/kW/month, and Verne signed 11.7MW with Microsoft. Conference chatter says Spain’s datacenter sustainability decree will probably be pushed back or watered down

  • Nebius signed 22MW in Slovakia and is looking at powered land in Latvia. A neocloud, likely Nebius, took

72MW in Bulgaria, with the rest of the site under exclusivity up to 1GW. At the Bulgarian site we count only the 72MW as signed

  • Verda took 5MW at Stellium Newcastle, so the other ~50MW may already be signed. A Chinese

hyperscaler, likely Alibaba, wants 20MW in southern Poland.

  • AMD is looking to backstop European capacity, but only on 3-year terms. AMD has no real AI

deployments in Europe right now, and it does not like selling unless the developer has the rest of the infrastructure worked out. We see 3 years as short cover for a much longer-lived asset

  • Hut 8’s River Bend phase 2 is for inference and has diesel gensets. Diesel for inference cuts against

Microsoft’s zero-generator push, but diesel hangs on longest at inference sites, where uptime matters most. This aligns with our Industrials note the UPS-less era Part 1 Era4 is a waste management business that wants to burn the waste for onsite generation to power its datacenter. It has 2,000 Blackwell GPUs in UK

  • Other datacenter updates: Separately, Nabiax’s first phase is a spec build 20k GPUs are shipping

through Hamburg to a site outside Berlin. A neocloud is involved, we think is likely Polarise BE Group sold 40MW and its Pennsylvania site can expand to 300MW+

  • As a curiosity, a London university Math PhD indicated that has not done any math himself for 3

months, other than prompting LLMs.
