SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, an experienced advertising executive and former search-platform employee, discussed Google’s slowing advertising growth despite rising search queries. They attributed the mismatch to a combination of queries growing in less-monetizable markets, slower ad rollout in those regions, and possible loss of valuable commercial searches to AI assistants. Meta was presented as gaining advertiser spend through improved targeting and tools for businesses.

Key Insights

The expert sees Google’s main risk as losing commercially valuable queries, especially for considered purchases such as software, travel, health, and education. Informational and productivity searches may increase usage without generating comparable ad revenue. Google can likely improve AI search monetization by adding more ads, but preserving user experience may limit the number shown. The expert urged faster execution, suggesting a rollout toward three ads per AI search result within six months. AI-driven personalization could support sustained price increases across ad platforms. The bigger uncertainty is whether Google can maintain its share of commercial searches. The expert believes search overall could expand substantially as AI helps users compare options and make purchases. Google’s eventual market share depends on how effectively it responds to AI competitors.

Competitive Landscape

Company or product

Positioning and competitive dynamics

Google

Strong ad relevance and improving conversion tools, but faces pressure from shifting user habits and uneven AI-search monetization. Could respond with more AI ads, personalized assistance, and advertiser automation.

Meta and its AI business tools

Gaining advertiser interest, particularly among smaller businesses, through data integration, improved returns, messaging, and more flexible ad delivery.

AI assistants, including ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, and Muse

Attract users seeking help with complex decisions and planning. The expert said ad tests on ChatGPT remained difficult to scale; a more capable shopping or personal-assistant experience could pose a larger threat.

YouTube and competing video platforms

YouTube remains attractive for performance and brand advertising, but faces more video inventory from services including Netflix, Roku, and Amazon. Google’s automated campaigns may also shift spend between YouTube and Search.

YouTube
