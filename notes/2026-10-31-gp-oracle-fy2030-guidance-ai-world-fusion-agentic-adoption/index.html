SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, an enterprise technology adviser, described strong client interest in Oracle’s AI World announcements, especially agentic features in Fusion applications and the use of OpenAI as a voice-enabled interface. Clients with established Oracle cloud deployments are exploring a further transformation toward more automated workflows. The expert believes these capabilities could encourage organizations still using on-premise applications to migrate to Fusion.

The discussion also covered Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI), including large AI infrastructure projects, multi-cloud and sovereign cloud offerings, implementation efficiency, and the pace at which contracted backlog could become revenue. The expert distinguished major AI infrastructure commitments from the broader applications and cloud business, which they said is also gaining momentum.

Key Insights

The expert characterized the event as unusually energetic and said agentic applications and voice interaction generated the most client interest. They view Oracle’s multi-cloud approach as a way to complement existing AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud investments, rather than requiring customers to choose one provider. The expert believes configuration records, quarterly updates, and AI-assisted implementation can shorten projects and reduce delivery risk. They argued that the backlog outlook should be considered alongside growth from existing customers expanding cloud and AI consumption. The pace of that expansion remains difficult to model, particularly for new AI-unit charges. A key adoption signal to watch is how many Fusion customers begin using agentic features, beyond basic embedded AI. The expert relayed a view from an industry leader that Oracle is substantially ahead of SAP in agentic capabilities; this is an opinion, not an independently verified comparison.
