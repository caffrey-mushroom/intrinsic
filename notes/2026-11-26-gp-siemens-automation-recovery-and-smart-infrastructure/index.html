SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed Siemens Digital Industries (DI) and Smart Infrastructure (SI), focusing on automation demand, software monetization, competition, data centers, and capacity constraints. The expert, a former Siemens senior leader with experience in both divisions, described a broad recovery, but emphasized that performance varies by region and industry.

Key Insights

Automation demand is improving, led by semiconductors, power, aerospace, and defense. Restocking is not a major driver. Europe is recovering in some areas, including machine tools, while European automotive remains weak. Siemens has stabilized in China but is unlikely to regain its former market-leading position. Its localized, lower-cost products have narrowed the price gap with local competitors and should help defend its position in Europe. Software growth is strong, supported by acquisitions and increasing recurring revenue. The expert expects further growth, but said Siemens must continue investing to keep pace in electronic design automation (EDA). DI profitability remains below several peers despite its software contribution. SI is positioned to benefit from data-center investment and electrification. Project delays may moderate growth, but the expert considers cancellations unlikely; utility and energy-automation demand provide additional support. The moderator pursued further detail on competitive risks, pricing, and project delays. The expert’s responses generally characterized Siemens’ near-term outlook as resilient, while identifying EDA investment and execution of capacity expansion as important challenges.

Inovance

Emerson, Honeywell, Rockwell

Major automation competitors, particularly in the US. Emerson matched Siemens’ overall growth in the period discussed.

Synopsys and Cadence

Leading EDA competitors with faster growth and substantial investment capacity. Siemens must sustain investment to defend its position.

PTC and Dassault

Compete in PLM and simulation. Siemens outgrew PTC and Dassault in those areas, according to the expert.

Schneider Electric

Strong in energy management, but less prominent in automation. Its PTC acquisition could strengthen its offering after integration; the expert sees limited immediate risk to Siemens.

Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider Electric, and ABB

Strong data-center competitors, with Siemens smaller than most. The market is capacity-constrained and demand extends beyond data centers.
