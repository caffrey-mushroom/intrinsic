SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed the semiconductor automated test equipment market, focusing on Teradyne and Advantest. AI accelerators, advanced chip packaging, and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) are increasing testing requirements and spending. The discussion also covered localized Korean testing equipment, Chinese competitors, and demand for testing AI networking and optical interconnect components.

Key Insights

The expert views Teradyne’s AI-driven revenue mix as structurally supported by more complex testing, greater use of chiplets, and customer diversification of test capacity. However, reliance on a small number of large customers and semiconductor capital-spending cycles create risks. Advantest’s SoC share gains reflect rapid expansion in AI and high-performance computing testing, rather than a loss of Teradyne’s business. The expert characterized 2025 as a turning point when AI and HPC made up most of SoC testing demand. Lower HBM stack heights can increase the number of stacks requiring testing, substantially expanding the addressable market, even when total memory capacity is unchanged. Korean suppliers’ recent orders are for wafer-level burn-in and screening, not the high-speed ATE supplied by the two market leaders. The expert expects only modest pressure on Advantest’s memory share. A roughly $1 billion opportunity for CPO testing was not directly confirmed. The expert instead estimated about $1.2 billion in annual ATE spending for the broader AI networking and interconnect category.
