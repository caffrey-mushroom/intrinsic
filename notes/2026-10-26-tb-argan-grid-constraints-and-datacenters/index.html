Agenda

  • Execution capabilities and project delivery timelines across core markets
  • Technical compatibility of combined-cycle plants with behind-the-meter data centre loads
  • EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) gross margin expansion drivers and profit outlook through to 2030
  • Competitive positioning relative to key EPC peers including Black & Veatch and Kiewit

Key Insights

  • OEMs are manufacturing equipment speculatively on contingency, building US-based facilities and pursuing international financing structures to bridge delivery gaps caused by surging demand and constrained domestic capital markets
  • Expert believes simple-cycle projects can actually be more complex than combined-cycle builds, contradicting the common assumption that fewer components automatically mean simpler, less demanding execution
  • Utilities are the single-biggest driver of project timelines, dictating when plants enter the queue, receive backfeed and achieve commercial operation dates, often overriding everything else on the schedule
  • Regional transmission organisations are actively being restructured to incorporate predictive maintenance, real-time plant health monitoring and new interconnection criteria, reshaping how plants are built and operated going forwards
  • AI loads fluctuate in milliseconds, while traditional grid loads shift over minutes or hours, making combined-cycle plants fundamentally mismatched for behind-the-meter AI applications
