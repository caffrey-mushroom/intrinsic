EARNINGS

APP 3Q26: Next-Gen Model, Only Partly Rolled Out, Re-Accelerates Gaming to ~50% and partially helps flip the Share-Loss Narrative

Overall, a very solid print for what was a somewhat a crowded HF short coming into the print and a stock that has derated to a mid teens p/e multiple. Used to be LO darling but has been largely de-grossed — likely needs another good print to get its mojo back, but this was a good start.

Gaming, where bears saw share loss to U, was the bright spot. It grew nearly 50% y/y (their first gaming growth disclosure in a year) on a partial rollout of new ad models, driving most of the 3Q beat ($2.185B vs. ~$2.10-2.12B bogeys), first acceleration after four straight decels (although the 2 yr stack decel’d) and biggest beat vs. street since 1Q’25. The 4Q guide, counting only model gains already live, beat bogeys on revs ($2.38B mid vs. ~$2.33-2.36B) and EBITDA ($2.01B vs. ~$1.93-1.96B). Foroughi said share-loss checks “tend to end up being flawed” and called 2Q’s miss “our own foot fault,” but mgmt gave no take rate, gaming vs. consumer split, full-rollout timing or e-com revenue: “You sort of have to make your own guesses.” Foroughi called the 3Q model rollout “a model transition as significant as Axon 2.0,” and asked if it’s Axon 3.0, put it on that level “from a technology standpoint in terms of technical complexity.” It’s “still in partial rollout,” the 4Q guide counts only gains “already live,” and there’s no timing on the full rollout.

The nits are the 4Q guide’s +8-10% q/q (vs. +14% in 3Q), but their usual ~5.5% beat on the mid would take 4Q to ~+15% q/q. No 2027 number, but 2026 now tracks to ~$8.33B (nine months reported plus the 4Q guide), so Street’s ~$10.1B for 2027 assumes just +21% growth, well short of the “roughly 30%” a year Foroughi says the business can compound at long term. At 30%, 2027 revenue would be ~$10.8B. Overall, much better than feared after U/META share-loss scare, and the higher base de-risks 2027 and likely gets some nascent bulls back on board hoping for e-com to ramp and beats to continue in ‘27. Bulls will say APP can grow revenue ~30%+ in CY27 and deserves high teens CY27 EV/EBITDA which = ~$500+.

Key Takeaways

  • AI model. The beat came from a partial rollout of the architecture mgmt only previewed on the 2Q call. The new models are bigger and trained on larger compute clusters. Mgmt now puts the architecture on par with Axon 2.0 in technical complexity. The biggest gains come where data is sparse: newer and smaller advertisers and new categories, which is where consumer sits. Mgmt won’t commit to a full-rollout date or a pace of gains. It does say the new generation has “a lot more room to optimize” and a “much longer roadmap,” and could eventually extend across media and client types (gaming and consumer models are separate today). The uplift came with no new data added to the system, so feeding in more data is a second lever still ahead.
  • Gaming. Gaming grew nearly 50% y/y, re-accelerating alongside the rollout, and drove most of the ~$100M beat vs. the high end. Mgmt now says gaming could grow faster than its earlier framing over the long term (quote #2). The category looks healthy. MAX supply is still growing at a “very strong double-digit” annual rate, vs. low double digits or single digits for most ad markets. Mgmt also says weak App Store in-app purchase data misses payments that have moved off-platform. Supply costs are rising as publishers monetize better, so APP has to keep improving its tech faster than market pricing rises to grow faster than MAX.
  • Callback. The key nuance was the scope and cost of the rollout. It covers “parts of traffic, parts of the system.” Mgmt declined to size what remains and cautioned that the impact may not scale linearly. APP is already “well over” its original three-year $1.3B cloud commitment ($1.1B paid through 3Q) and is still adding capacity, yet mgmt does not expect margin compression. Its reasons:
  • Training cost doesn’t scale linearly with revenue; most compute goes to serving ads.
  • After the 2Q surprise, APP split model development from serving and added spending controls on large training runs.
  • Any future step-up in compute that pressures margins would show up in guidance.

Mgmt also gave its clearest explanation of why channel checks missed. APP can lose share of wallet on lower-value impressions while conversion and pricing gains elsewhere more than offset that, and checks can’t see the offset.

  • Competition. Mgmt called 2Q “our own foot fault.” It noted that several competitors grew in the same quarter APP posted its biggest result (“not zero sum”). It dismissed negative channel checks as sampling a small sub-segment whose respondents “probably aren’t working that hard.” On pricing, CPMs rise as targeting improves, but mgmt says advertiser ROAS is higher than three years ago on far larger spend. APP doesn’t manage to or disclose a take rate, and conversion is “a little bit over 1%.” Mgmt framed take rate as rising when APP out-innovates the market, holding when it keeps pace and shrinking when it falls behind. That is the cleanest explanation yet of the 2Q air pocket. Mgmt also stressed that MAX gives Axon only win/loss and clearing-price data, never what competitors serve. On the callback, it said it doesn’t view the dispute with U over data collection as material to the P&L.
  • Consumer. Advertiser spend grew double digits q/q and triple digits y/y heading into the Q4 peak. Street estimates put consumer at ~$250M of 3Q revenue, ~12% of the total. In 2Q, spend had already exited 28% above 4Q25’s seasonal peak. Mgmt says new-customer acquisition is accelerating. It cautioned, however, that APAC-led pixel growth partly reflects merchants spinning up product-specific sites just for APP. Success still skews to mid-market advertisers who are either sophisticated or supported by APP’s team. Fully self-serve SMB is “further into the future,” and the partnership channel is “still a work in progress.” Mgmt softened its tone on creative, from “the biggest hurdle in our system” last quarter to “I wouldn’t call it a barrier.” It conceded that gen-AI video tools have lagged because Western LLM labs are “focused on something else.” New formats such as dynamic product ads are the workaround for now. On the callback, mgmt said there will be no added consumer disclosure for the foreseeable future.
  • Guide. The 4Q revenue midpoint of $2.38B (+43.6% y/y, +8–10% q/q) is ~5% above Street. The adj. EBITDA midpoint of $2.01B (~84.5% margin) is ~6.5% above. Together they imply FY26 revenue growth of ~52% and adj. EBITDA growth of ~56%. The guidance approach is unchanged: it covers normal seasonality, consumer scaling, model gains already live and higher training compute, with no credit for the part of the rollout not yet released. Some on the buyside see +8–10% q/q as light for the seasonal peak. It is below both 3Q’s +13.6% and last year’s 4Q guide of +12–14%, which turned into +18%. APP has beaten the high end in 14 of the last 16 quarters by mgmt’s count. A beat matching last year’s ~3.6% would put 4Q near $2.48B (+~14% q/q). Q4-to-date is too early to read, since holiday budgets ramp from mid-November (callback).
  • Margins. Adj. EBITDA margin of 84.3% was ~90bps above Street and above the ~83% the guide implied, on 87% q/q flow-through (70% in 2Q). GAAP gross margin rose ~70bps q/q to 89.0%. Cheaper model serving pushed data center costs down as a % of revenue even as training compute rose. That reverses 2Q, when compute costs pushed adj. EBITDA below the guide range. Mgmt manages to EBITDA dollars and FCF rather than margin %, and says every dollar of compute is “gated on return.” The 4Q guide implies ~84.5%, above the low-80% long-term framework mgmt gave last quarter.
  • CTV. Pilot campaigns are live and the product is “looking very promising.” Mgmt said not to “bank on it at all” for near-term numbers. That is a clear shift from 2Q, when CTV came last in the supply roadmap (after non-gaming apps and the open web) and was gated on having excess consumer budgets. On the callback, mgmt cited improving attribution and early signs of incrementality, with a potential unlock next year. Mgmt sees CTV as a rare supply source that could move the needle, because no true top-to-bottom-funnel performance offering exists there. It compared CTV to mobile 10–15 years ago.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

The bull thesis is that APP is the best-executing AI performance-ad engine outside the walled gardens, and that its economics compound with every model improvement. Better predictions lift advertiser ROAS, budgets step up at the same ROAS targets, and APP keeps a larger share of the value created. Supply is not a ceiling, because MAX inventory is still growing at a strong double-digit rate. Bulls see three long-duration drivers. First, gaming still has large conversion headroom: conversion is only ~1.3%, and sustaining even ~20bps of annual improvement could put revenue ~15% above Street by 2030. Second, consumer expands the addressable market rather than standing alone. It is already a ~$1B run-rate business growing triple digits, and every new advertiser adds data that improves the model. Because gaming and commerce bid in the same auction, commerce demand lifts the value of lower-value impressions, and showing users a more varied mix of ads improves gaming conversion. Third, there is optionality the Street doesn’t model: CTV, non-gaming apps and the open web, MoPub-style acquisitions of data and customers, and a shift of performance ad dollars from the mobile web into full-screen in-app ads that average 35-plus seconds of watch time. The durable advantages are scale (the largest feedback loop in mobile user acquisition), MAX’s position as the neutral mediation layer that pays publishers the most, and engineering talent density that has historically put APP “many quarters or years ahead” on breakthroughs. Underneath it all is an 84%-margin, capital-light model that converts ~60–65% of revenue to FCF.

This quarter ticked nearly every bull box. The 2Q air pocket proved to be timing rather than a structural problem. A partial rollout of an architecture mgmt compares to Axon 2.0, the transition behind two years of ~70%+ ad revenue growth, re-accelerated gaming to ~50% y/y. It produced APP’s largest-ever sequential dollar add with no new data in the system, and it did so while several competitors were also growing, which undercuts the view that share gains are zero-sum. Mgmt raised its gaming growth ceiling for the first time. Because the 4Q guide gives no credit for the unreleased part of the rollout, estimate revisions should keep coming. FY27 Street revenue has moved from ~$10.1–10.2B pre-print to ~$10.5B (+~26%), and bulls think the full rollout plus consumer makes 30%+ achievable. On margins, 87% flow-through with data center costs falling as a % of revenue shows compute can scale without breaking the model, so each $1B of revenue upside adds roughly $0.85B of EBITDA. Even after the after-hours move, APP trades at ~12.5x FY27 EBITDA, ~16x FY27 EPS and a ~6% FY27 FCF yield. That compares with a four-year average of ~18x NTM EBITDA, 35x+ a year ago, and ~21–22x where U and RDDT trade today. Buybacks are running at ~5% of market cap a year at the 3Q pace. If APP’s data-collection claims against U hold up, U’s recent momentum could prove short-lived. With the quarter rebuilding credibility on execution, bulls see a path back toward the historical average multiple as APP strings together beats through the full rollout.

The bear thesis is that APP is a phenomenal business that is fundamentally hard to forecast, and so deserves a discounted multiple. Growth depends on lumpy R&D breakthroughs that mgmt explicitly won’t put on a schedule. Disclosure is thin: no gaming/consumer split, no take rate and no added consumer KPIs. And 2Q showed that a single quarter without model lift can take ~20% off the stock in a day. Gaming is fully penetrated (“We have every mobile gaming company live probably”), so growth depends on capturing more of advertisers’ value, which is exactly what competitors are going after. U’s Vector-led Strategic Grow revenue is growign 125%. . LFTO and Moloco are scaling their own machine-learning stacks, and Google is entrenched on Android. On the callback, mgmt itself acknowledged APP is losing share of wallet on lower-value impressions. Mgmt also says take rate shrinks if APP stops out-innovating market pricing. And because commerce and gaming bid in the same auction, e-commerce bidders can push marginal gaming advertisers toward cheaper networks.

Bears think the bull case leans too hard on two assumptions. The first is that this architecture compounds for years the way Axon 2.0 did; competitors have since rebuilt their stacks, so APP’s lead may be shorter this time. The second is that consumer can keep roughly doubling. A couple of years in, it is ~12% of revenue, success still depends on hand-holding and creative investment, and self-serve SMB is years away. Growth also skews to APAC merchants sourcing from the Asian supply chain, a trade-policy exposure mgmt did not address.

This quarter gave bears more than the after-hours move suggests. The 4Q guide implies only +8–10% q/q in the seasonally strongest quarter, below last year’s +12–14% guide, with y/y growth slowing to ~44% at the midpoint. Even a typical ~3–4% beat leaves two-year stacked growth (~+148%) slower than 3Q’s (~+162%). Mgmt also flagged a new seasonality headwind (quote #3), which makes the February 1Q27 guide the next pressure point. FCF conversion guidance slipped, 3Q FCF was slightly below Street, and conversion is structurally lower than in FY25 as cash taxes rise, so FCF per share will lag EBITDA. Investment gains also flattered the GAAP EPS beat. On costs, APP is already well over its cloud commitment and still adding capacity, mgmt says the models will be “starved for data,” and the callback flagged that a future step-up in compute could pressure margins. Bears therefore see margin risk, plus the chance of a large data acquisition as APP reaches for growth.

HUBS -8%: Q4 Guide Steps Down to 12% CC and Net New ARR Is Now Declining, Pushing FY27 Toward ~11% and feeding the AI loser bear case

Not the print bulls wanted, even though there weren’t many bulls lef theading into the print. Mgmt didn’t do themselves any favors on the call and 4Q guide is feeding the AI loser bear case. 3Q was largely ok and grew 17% cc, beating the ~15.5-16% bogeys, but the 4Q guide of 12% missed the ~14.5-15%, which mgmt put on AI changing how customers find and buy software. KPIs got cut too: the CFO now sees FY NRR down ~2pts, net new ARR down y/y and 4Q net adds “below the 5,000 level”. Mgmt all but told the Street to anchor 2027 on the ~12% exit, so FY27 revenue is resetting from ~+14% to ~+10–11% Asked if 2027 starts at 12%, CFO said the headwinds “will continue in Q4 and into 2027”; mgmt wouldn’t say if trends stabilized, give a 2027 number before February or date its lower-ASP seats-plus-credits pricing beyond new EMEA customers.

On AI disruption, its LLM connector users show 13pts higher gross retention, but the CEO called the connectors a “nascent” lead source that doesn’t yet offset weaker inbound. Overall, worse than feared even after the four October sell-side downgrades and everyone hating it to put it lightly, with 2027 growth now pointing below the well below ~13% bar.

Key Takeaways

  • Demand. The two headwinds first flagged in Q2 (customers want proof that AI works in their own environment before buying; tighter budgets plus AI changing how buyers discover software) “became more pronounced” in Q3, and commentary implies trends weakened into quarter-end. Agent trials grew 64% q/q, but conversion needs partner and solution-consultant hand-holding, which lengthens cycles. At Goldman in September mgmt framed this as a normal platform-shift adoption curve; now it says this “is not going to be a single quarter or a two-quarter transformation.”
  • Callback. Callbacks were downbeat. Mgmt put the downmarket at the center of the slowdown, with Google’s shift to AI Mode disrupting the inbound, product-led funnel faster than AEO, LLM connectors and social can make up for it. Mgmt was candid that it has not decided how broadly to roll out the new pricing (Q4 is the first scaled pilot read), declined to size the NNARR decline, said Q4 NRR steps down from Q3, flagged FY27 GM pressure and, on 2027, pointed back to the 12% Q4 exit, declining NNARR and lower NRR as the starting point.
  • Downmarket. Net adds of 5,258 missed Street (~5,750) and landed at the low end of the 5–6K guide (~7.0K in Q2, 10.9K a year ago), the lowest since 1Q20, and mgmt pulled the Q4 net-add guide, saying only “below” ~5K (Street ~5.8K) vs. a 9–10K quarterly pace entering 2026. Mgmt conceded that new top-of-funnel channels (intent-based marketing, LLM connectors, AEO) “don’t really meet” the decline in inbound demand, and that downmarket buyers are more price sensitive because “AI is a new line item.” The relaunched Starter (self-updating CRM, agentic onboarding) and the pricing pilot are the fixes, but mgmt said they will “take time.”
  • Upmarket. The expected offset slowed sharply: $120K+ ARR deals grew 19% y/y vs. 38% in Q2, as larger buying committees with CEO, CTO and board involvement stretch cycles. Mgmt cited ~3x higher win rates when it engages multiple stakeholders on deals with 5+ committee members and has merged Sales and CS upmarket to smooth the path from trial to first AI use case to expansion. Multi-hub lands held up (62% of new Pro+ customers, +2pts y/y) and active core seats grew 34%.
  • NRR. NRR fell to 101% (-1pt q/q, -2pts y/y) vs. Street ~103%, and FY26 NRR is now guided down ~2pts (~101.5% vs. ~103.5%), the second cut this year after starting 2026 at “up a couple of points” and moving to ~flat in August. That implies Q4 NRR near 100% vs. the usual seasonal uptick, raising the risk of sub-100% in 1H27. Gross dollar retention is stable in the high 80s, so this is an upsell problem: core and persona seat upgrades remain a tailwind but are “now being more than offset” by weaker cross-sell, contact-tier and edition upgrades and cost optimization at renewal; ASRPC of $12,038 (+4% cc vs. Street $11,856; +2% cc last q) was flattered by fewer small Starter adds.
  • NNARR. Mgmt now expects FY26 net new ARR to decline y/y, the second reset this year: HUBS entered 2026 expecting NNARR to outpace cc revenue growth and in August cut that to growing below it. For the full year to decline, 2H NNARR has to be down meaningfully more, and mgmt declined to size it before February. The pricing pilots are too small to explain the drop; it reflects demand, with lower sales productivity after the spring credits retraining also a drag.
  • Pricing. The seats-and-credits pilot (Nordics since April, Benelux since June) expanded to all new EMEA customers in October: hubs are replaced by a single go-to-market seat, the Starter-to-Pro price jump shrinks in Marketing, Content and Data Hub, and all usage-based features move into credits. The first quantification shows lower ASPs but more Pro+ customers, 2–3x higher credit consumption and “stronger new ARR” vs. non-pilot regions. There is no timeline for a global rollout or installed-base migration (more in February), though existing customers can already swap seats for credits; mgmt frames it as a replay of the 2024 seat changes (ASP down first, then bigger and stickier cohorts), while bears read it as an effective price cut that signals competitive pressure.
  • AI. Usage keeps building: 64% of Pro+ customers use AI features, 20% use HUBS agents (+11pts YTD, but only +1pt vs. 19% in August), Breeze Assistant reach is 61% (+24pts YTD vs. 57% in August) with CSAT at 69%, monthly agentic actions are 5x since January (vs. ~3x at Goldman in September), credit consumption is ~2.5x, and Pro+ customers have deployed 5,600+ custom agents since July (vs. 3,000+ at Analyst Day). Data Agent plus Prospecting Agent with intent signals, and Customer Agent (2M+ support issues resolved in Q3), show the clearest ROI. The gap is monetization: credits are not yet moving NRR or NNARR, and AI revenue contribution remains small.
  • Headless. Customers created 22M CRM objects through LLM connectors in Q3 (+157% q/q), and connector users show 13pts higher GRR (vs. ~12pts cited in September) and upgrade paid seats at 3x the rate of non-users. HUBS says it is the most popular CRM connector on both ChatGPT and Claude, which is mgmt’s best evidence that headless use is additive rather than disintermediating. Monetization is early: only the Data API is monetized today (usage above “human” thresholds), with Context and Work APIs not yet priced.
  • Competition. Mgmt said elongated cycles are not competition-driven, upmarket deals face the same competitors and are not falling out of the pipeline, and bigger committees are lengthening cycles rather than driving losses. Downmarket, customers are experimenting with AI tools and capping spend across experiments. Mgmt did not call out any specific competitor or rival pricing, but the Starter-to-Pro price compression, rising competition from AI start-ups and native AI tools downmarket, and checks suggesting HUBS’ AI products lag peers in adoption keep the debate open.
  • Margins. Non-GAAP OM of 22.6% (+270bp y/y) beat Street (20.3%) by ~230bp, with the OI beat ($26M) larger than the revenue beat ($22M) as headcount fell q/q and opex grew 11%. GM of 84.2% was roughly in line but down 90bp y/y (subscription GM 85.4%, -130bp) on AI costs, and mgmt still expects FY27 GM to decline as COGS outgrows revenue, offset by opex leverage. The Q4 OM guide of 22.9% missed Street (24.3%) because a “significant portion” of the October reorg savings (~7% RIF; $60–70M Q4 severance) is being reinvested in retention, AI hiring and AI product. FY26 OM is ~21% (+~2.4pts) and the FY27 23–24% target was reaffirmed rather than raised, which frustrated some given the growth reset, though mgmt signaled it could go further on costs if trends persist.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bulls see HUBS as the scaled front-office platform for SMB and mid-market companies, with 311.7K customers, ~4,000 solution partners, about half of ARR outside the US and gross dollar retention that has held in the high 80s through two quarters of disruption. Their core argument is that AI raises, rather than erodes, the value of the unified customer data and workflow layer HUBS owns: models can draft emails and resolve tickets, but inside a business they need trusted, current context on pipeline, contacts and history, and SMBs lack the resources to build that themselves. The long-term drivers are intact in that framing: upmarket expansion, multi-hub consolidation (62% of new Pro+ customers land with multiple hubs), core-seat growth (+34%) and a new consumption layer in credits that monetizes work done by agents rather than seats. The partner channel acts as a forward-deployed implementation arm for AI, and ease of use remains HUBS’ edge with buyers who do not have AI teams.

This quarter gave bulls more product evidence than any print this year. The revenue beat (2.4%) was bigger than Q2’s (~1.5%), OM reached 22.6% with headcount down q/q, and AI usage outran the September run rate: Breeze Assistant reach of 61%, monthly agentic actions at 5x YTD, credit consumption at ~2.5x and 5,600+ custom agents. The connector data directly rebuts the headless-disintermediation bear case, since customers working through ChatGPT or Claude create more objects in HUBS, retain better (+13pts GRR) and buy seats at 3x the rate. The EMEA pilot is the bigger tell: it repeats the 2024 seat-pricing playbook that lowered ASPs first and then produced larger, better-retaining cohorts, and this time it comes with 2–3x credit consumption and stronger new ARR. Bulls also note that mgmt’s guidance method is unchanged and the Q4 guide is flat q/q despite stable churn, so a normal ~2% beat would put Q4 near 14% and make the 2027 reset look overdone.

Bears argue that HUBS’ growth engine (content- and SEO-driven inbound demand feeding a product-led SMB funnel) is being structurally impaired by AI search, and that the company is trying to change product, pricing and go-to-market all at once while its core acquisition channel shrinks. By mgmt’s own description, the replacement channels are too nascent to fill the gap, and net adds have roughly halved from a 9–10K quarterly pace to ~5K, with Q4 lower still. Downmarket, AI tools compete for the same budget (”AI is a new line item”), and over time AI-native CRMs or frontier-model agents could commoditize the SMB front end, a risk bears see reflected in a pilot that narrows the Starter-to-Pro price gap. Credibility is the other issue: NNARR and NRR expectations have each been reset twice in two quarters, and the net-add guide has been withdrawn.

The quarter supplied the bear case with its best evidence yet. NNARR is now expected to decline for the full year, which requires a sharper decline in 2H; NRR is heading to ~100% in Q4, with seasonality pointing below 100% in 1H27; and upmarket, the supposed offset, slowed to +19% in $120K+ deals from +38%. GM fell 90bp y/y and is guided down again in FY27, FCF guidance was cut by $30M, and the October reorg savings were reinvested rather than dropped to margin, which suggests less flexibility than bulls assume. On the bull assumptions, bears note that credit consumption has not shown up in NRR or NNARR, that the pilot lowers ASPs first, and that a global rollout, let alone any installed-base migration, could compress ARR before usage catches up. They also argue that an FCF multiple flatters the stock while SBC still runs at ~13% of revenue.

MU: Micron pauses construction on a planned $4.5B Taiwan memory fab – Bloomberg / EBN

Micron has largely halted construction in Taoyuan, Bloomberg reports. Executives are concerned a local labor agreement could be overturned. The pause puts Fab 11E’s targeted 2028 completion at risk. Existing Taiwan production continues.

Data centers / META / CRWV: Election raises permitting risk across three key states – WSJ / WSJ interview / Bloomberg

Texas, Georgia and Ohio account for over 40% of planned U.S. data-center capacity, per Evercore ISI. All three elected governors who campaigned on slowing construction. CoreWeave’s 2031 unsecured bonds traded as low as 79.5 cents on the dollar. Meta sees a clearer path to expanding existing sites and said one governor-elect had already requested a meeting about jobs and investment.

SpaceX / NVDA: Goldman and Apollo approach buyers for $40B GPU financing – Bloomberg / Reuters

Some investors were asked to take stakes of up to $500M ahead of formal syndication, Bloomberg reports. The financing backs a previously agreed lease of Nvidia hardware. Deliveries are expected in the first half of 2027, with leases running just over five years.

OpenAI: Enterprise sales overtook consumer sales as coding tools gained traction – WSJ / Reuters

Greg Brockman said OpenAI struggled in the first half, then saw revenue growth pick up in Q3 as businesses adopted its coding tools following the launches of GPT-5.6 Sol and ChatGPT Work. He believes OpenAI could be ready for an IPO in 2027.

Hon Hai / AI servers: October sales rise 46% to NT$1.31T – Foxconn / Bloomberg

Foxconn reported October revenue of NT$1.305T, up 45.7% year over year and 12.7% from September. Demand for AI servers helped drive the increase at Nvidia’s manufacturing partner.

META / AI coding: Meta buys Conductor to help developers manage coding agents – Axios / Conductor

Conductor lets engineers run several coding agents in parallel and manage their work in one place. The startup has closed new sign-ups and says it plans to introduce a new interface early next year.
