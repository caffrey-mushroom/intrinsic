Agenda

  • Business resiliency update for Aeroméxico (NYSE: AERO) following JV continuation decision
  • Regional scale differences with competitors
  • Connectivity improvements through fleet overhaul
  • Disciplined operational changes and outlook

Key Insights

  • Aeroméxico's Mexico City hub operates at severe capacity constraints, reduced from 54 to 40 operations per hour, making slot ownership its most critical competitive asset. Fleet transition from regional jets to narrow-bodies on domestic routes represents an underappreciated lever, enabling meaningful up-gauging
  • Viva-Volaris integration risk is significant, as the two may remain internally competitive, potentially offering a huge tailwind for Aeroméxico, especially as domestic capacity is expected to rationalise following deal completion
  • Growing revenue per available seat-kilometre/yield close to 10-11%, which Aeroméxico saw through 2026 YTD, will be challenging to sustain in H2 2026 and FY27 as revenue per available seat-kilometre-dilutive capacity is reintroduced and there are limited accelerating demand drivers
  • Premium revenue mix reaching c60%, up from c43% in Q2 2026, is unlikely for Aeroméxico, as Mexico lacks comparable concentrations of large multinationals that drive corporate premium. There are also significant gaps in loyalty programme, particularly around non-air partnerships, single-bank exclusivity and inability to redeem points for ancillary services
