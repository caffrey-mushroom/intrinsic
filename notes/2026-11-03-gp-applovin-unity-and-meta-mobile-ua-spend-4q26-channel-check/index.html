SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a mobile-game developer who buys advertising to grow game audiences, described current user-acquisition spending and performance across major ad networks. Spending decisions depend on return on ad spend (ROAS), the ability to scale campaigns, and the mix of ads shown within games. The expert also discussed e-commerce advertising, Android development, and a planned ad-supported product using CloudX.

Key Insights

AppLovin remains the strongest partner for scale and returns, but campaigns have recently struggled to consistently spend to daily targets. The expert has not seen a sudden, sustained performance jump attributable to an algorithm upgrade. E-commerce advertising brings new money into the mobile gaming ecosystem and can provide steadier ad rates over repeated impressions. However, game developers also need performance campaigns to attract new players; too much emphasis on brand ads could weaken that audience-replenishment cycle. Google’s recent gains are partly linked to improved Android game stability. AI-assisted development and testing have helped address device fragmentation, with potential for further growth. Unity has improved from prior declines, but its scale remains limited and recent performance has flattened. The expert favors maintaining a mix of networks rather than shifting all spending to AppLovin, where returns would likely deteriorate at higher budgets. Meta’s share has fallen as influencer-style creatives have become less effective and its audience network has delivered less volume. The expert could not determine whether this reflected broader platform weakness or competition from larger advertisers. CloudX is being tested for a new ad-supported product, but the expert does not view it as a near-term replacement for AppLovin MAX. MAX remains important for ROAS campaigns.

Competitive Landscape

Platform

Position and observations

AppLovin

Largest partner and best ROAS; reliable returns but less consistent delivery against spending targets. Competes for impressions with e-commerce advertisers.

Google

Growing share, supported by Android improvements and broad inventory, including Play Store and YouTube placements.

Unity

Smaller-scale option with improving returns and budgets; recent results appear stable rather than accelerating.

Meta

Spending has declined amid weaker performance from some creative formats and reduced audience-network volume.

CloudX

In beta for a new product, with access to exchange inventory and brand demand. Not considered a near-term MAX competitor.
