核心摘要

  1. Datadog 26Q3收入同比增长35.2%，比指引高约6个百分点，接近预览中36%的预期，非AI业务增速连续第五个季度加速至二十多个百分点高段。
  2. 26Q4收入增速指引为25%至26%，低于买方高端预期的30%至31%，市场一致预期约为26%。
  3. Q3非GAAP营业利润率为27.9%（指引约23%），FCF利润率21%，账单增长30%，RPO增长38%，cRPO增长三十多个百分点中段。
  4. CFO指出Q4指引受三因素影响：最大客户按最低承诺额计入、大型科技和AI客户以更高承诺和更低价格提前续约、以及12月中旬至1月的消费季节性放缓。
  5. 作者据此反推最大客户OpenAI的最低承诺约为其Q3支出的40%，指引其余部分隐含核心业务增速约25%，低于Q3估计的28.9%。
  6. 作者预计26Q4收入增速约34%，区间约33%至35%，核心业务增速每变动1个百分点影响总增速约0.9个百分点，价格下调约拖累总增速1个百分点。
  7. AI原生客户已超过850家（上季约750家），其中39家年支出超过100万美元，公司整体NRR维持在120%出头。
  8. 作者认为客户与定价变化使季度收入更难预测，但非AI需求依然稳健，AI原生企业正复制云原生企业从自建转向采购DDOG的路径。

Executive Summary

DDOG’s 26Q3 revenue grew 35.2% YoY, about 6 percentage points above the guided growth rate and close to our preview’s 36%, with non-AI growth accelerating for a fifth straight quarter. However, the 26Q4 guide of 25–26% fell short of the high-end buy-side expectation of 30–31%, versus consensus of about 26%. The largest customer is included at its minimum commitment despite spending well above it in Q3, while several large customers renewed early at lower prices. We estimate 26Q4 revenue growth of about 34%. These customer and pricing changes make quarterly revenue harder to forecast, while non-AI demand remains solid.

Results: Q3 Meets Buy-Side Expectations; Q4 Guidance Falls Short

Source: Company, FUNDA estimates

  • Q3 revenue growth was broadly in line with buy-side expectations. The full-year guidance midpoint rose by $60m, broadly matching the $57.1m Q3 revenue beat against the prior guidance midpoint, leaving implied Q4 revenue guidance little changed since August.
  • Non-AI growth accelerated ~1pt to the high-20s, and infrastructure monitoring, about half of ARR, grew mid-30s.
  • Non-GAAP OPM was 27.9% versus ~23% guided; FCF margin was 21% on collection timing.
  • Billings +30%, RPO +38%, cRPO up mid-30s%.

Minimum Commitments, Renewal Pricing and Seasonality Shape the Q4 Guide

The CFO identified three factors affecting sequential Q4 revenue growth. These factors do not, by themselves, indicate weaker underlying demand.

Subscribed

  • Largest customer at its floor. It spent substantially above its minimum commitment in 3Q, but 4Q is guided at the floor, more conservatively than 3Q.
  • Early renewals. Large tech and AI customers whose usage far exceeded their commitments renewed early at higher commitments and lower prices. This weighs on near-term revenue growth. These renewals follow no fixed pattern and can recur.
  • Seasonality. Consumption softens from mid-December into January.

We Estimate Q4 Revenue Growth could reach 34%

The CFO also said 4Q growth excluding the largest customer is low-to-mid 30s% in the guide. That lets us back out both the OpenAI floor and what the rest of the guide assumes.

  • Using our OpenAI estimate and an assumed 33% ex-OpenAI growth rate within the guided range, the implied minimum commitment is about 40% of Q3 spending. The rest of the guide implies core growth of about 25%, versus our Q3 estimate of 28.9%. Both the largest-customer assumption and core growth account for much of the gap to our forecast.

Bridge from the guide to our 26Q4 view (YoY growth)

The range is ~33-35% depending on AI-native pricing and OpenAI usage; each 1pt of core growth moves total growth by ~0.9pts.

Management did not quantify how much of the Q3 revenue beat came from the largest customer. Our forecast of about 34% total growth assumes ex-OpenAI revenue exceeds the level implied by a 33% ex-OpenAI guided growth rate by about 4%.

AI-Native Customers Broaden; Renewals Reduce Near-Term Pricing

The AI customer base continues to expand: over 850 customers, compared with about 750 last quarter, including 39 spending more than $1m annually. Revenue from these customers is growing faster than the company overall. Companywide NRR remains in the low 120s.

  • 3Q: core is derived from the disclosed 1pt acceleration and AI-native is the residual (~16% of revenue, versus 15.8% in our preview).
  • Q4: OpenAI holds its Q3 exit run-rate, and AI-native ex-OpenAI grows about 8% QoQ, well below its recent pace. Our model assumes price cuts reduce total revenue growth by about 1 percentage point.

AI natives are also replacing in-house tooling with DDOG, the build-to-buy path cloud natives took, with gains led by model developers and inference providers.
