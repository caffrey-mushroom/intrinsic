Agenda

  • Margin pressures and claims inflation drivers
  • Evolution of insurer-repairer relationships and panel recruitment considerations
  • Competitive environment and consolidation outlook for repair providers

Key Insights

  • Claims severity inflation is likely to persist over the next 2-3 years, as fuel, materials and trade labour costs escalate alongside expanding restoration requirements for high-severity water and mould damage
  • Temporary accommodation for displaced policyholders is seeing costs increase to USD 600-800 per night, where long-term rental supply shortages are exaggerated by Airbnb conversions, creating margin headwinds for insurers
  • Catastrophe repair EBITDA margins are expected to remain capped in the low-to-mid-30s because surging mobilisation expenses, trade inflation and prolonged claim cycle times erode the operational leverage gained from higher job volumes
  • Accelerating retirements and subcontractor shortages are constraining tradesman capacity, prompting insurers to prioritise larger providers capable of mobilising national networks and bypassing unsustainable, margin-on-margin subcontracting structures
  • Regulatory pressure from the updated General Insurance Code of Practice is expected to favour large national providers, as compliance guarantees and obligations are harder for smaller regional operators to deliver
