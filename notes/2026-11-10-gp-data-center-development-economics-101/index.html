SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed the development economics of large data centers, focusing on land, permits, power supply, construction schedules, financing, and customer contracts. The discussion centered largely on projects in Texas and other U.S. markets, where grid capacity, equipment access, and local approvals can shape project timelines and costs.

Key Insights

The expert expects proposed Texas policy changes to slow development, likely by several months to about a year, but does not expect them to stop projects. Developers offering protections for ratepayers, responsible water use, and community benefits may be better positioned. Access to grid power is increasingly difficult. The expert expects on-site generation to become more common, especially where utilities cannot deliver power on a suitable schedule. Financing has become more challenging, particularly for developers without investment-grade customers or strong balance sheets. Deals may be delayed or abandoned while investors wait for regulatory and customer risks to become clearer. The expert emphasized that cost overruns remain a major risk, even with careful planning. Fixed or not-to-exceed EPC contracts and strong equity partners can help, but do not eliminate exposure. A typical project may rely on a single customer. Short customer contracts can constrain loan terms and cash flow, while longer agreements may support more favorable financing and stronger returns over the project’s life.

Competitive Landscape

Market participant

Position and considerations

Hyperscalers

Often seek exclusive use of a facility and may require redundancy. Their credit quality and contract duration influence financing terms.

Neocloud providers

Some may move quickly, including signing firm agreements early, but their credit strength can affect financing availability.

Utilities

Grid connections can offer a lower-complexity power path, but queue backlogs may delay service for years.

On-site generation providers

Can help developers meet earlier power needs, but require substantial upfront capital, equipment financing, and fuel access.

EPC contractors

Not-to-exceed pricing is available from some providers, generally at a higher cost; fixed-price options are limited.
