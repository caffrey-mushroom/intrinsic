SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined whether generative world models could weaken Ubisoft’s advantage in large-scale games. The expert argued that AI will initially accelerate individual production tasks rather than create polished, commercially viable games from a single prompt.

Ubisoft’s established franchises, proprietary data, creative direction, and brand recognition remain meaningful advantages. However, lower development barriers and faster AI adoption by Chinese publishers could intensify competition.

Key Insights

AI-generated environments are already becoming usable within conventional production pipelines. Fully generated AAA games remain much further away because they require coherent design, narrative, systems, and creative oversight. Ubisoft’s main defense is not world size but its ability to connect gameplay, story, lore, and curated content into a cohesive experience. AI could materially reduce unsustainable AAA development costs across coding, art, localization, testing, live operations, and environment creation. Publishers are expected to retain most of these savings. Large back catalogs should become more valuable as AI lowers remastering and platform-porting costs. Brand owners are better positioned than studios imitating older gameplay without the associated intellectual property. Human-made positioning may persist among smaller games, but the expert expects mainstream publishers to adopt AI broadly as consumer resistance declines. Younger players continue to value exploration, but may favor mobile, user-generated platforms, and culturally native experiences over traditional consoles. The moderator pursued the expert’s particularly strong view that Chinese publishers have established a substantial AI-adoption lead over Western studios.
