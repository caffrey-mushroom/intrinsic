SUMMARY

Overview

A former clinical services leader assessed a national home infusion provider specializing in immune globulin therapies. The discussion covered acquisition-led expansion, referral conversion, payer contracting, site productivity, infusion-suite utilization, revenue-cycle management, and the outlook for specialty and acute infusion services.

The provider’s strongest established branches generate steady chronic-patient volumes. New locations typically require substantial time to secure accreditation, build referral relationships, and develop patient census.

Key Insights

Immune globulin remains the provider’s core advantage due to strong payer contracts, clinical experience, and market reputation. Specialty-drug growth is constrained by payer carve-outs, preferred-pharmacy rules, and unfavorable reimbursement. Smaller branches can outperform larger operations when documentation and billing controls are stronger. Revenue-cycle weaknesses contributed significantly to the closure of the enteral nutrition program and continue to leave collections unrealized. Ambulatory infusion suites are materially underused. Existing home patients are difficult to convert, making new referrals the primary growth opportunity. The strongest growth case depends on improved specialty contracts, continued immune globulin leadership, better collections, and greater automation. Key risks include weak suite utilization, reimbursement pressure, authorization failures, and operational complexity at larger sites.
