Expert Perspectives:

I. Overall Operational Overview

In the first half of 2026, Midea Group Co., Ltd maintained steady overall operational growth. The overseas business served as the core growth engine, the To B business (Building Technologies, KUKA robotics) delivered standout performance, and the domestic home appliance business demonstrated strong operational resilience in a soft demand environment.

Core Data by Business Segment:

  • Overseas business: Europe grew nearly 10%, the US grew 11%, and the Asia-Pacific region grew the fastest. Across the full year, only the Middle East contracted due to geopolitical conflicts, while all other regions achieved growth.
  • Building Technologies: Grew over 10% overall in the first half, with domestic operations up about 8% (high single digits) and overseas up 11%–12%.
  • KUKA Robotics: Grew over 10% in the first half, driven by over 20% domestic growth (reaching 30%+ in Q2) and single-digit overseas growth. Domestic market share rose to second place, trailing the market leader by only around 200 units.
  • Domestic air conditioning: Maintained positive domestic sales growth of nearly 1% in the first half, outperforming peers against broader industry headwinds.

Core strategic stance: Persist in the "top two" strategy across core categories. Stabilize market share in air conditioners, aggressively capture share in refrigerators, stabilize share in washing machines + dryers, gain share through new offerings in kitchen appliances, and stabilize share in small appliances such as microwave and toaster ovens.

II. Overseas Business

(I) Growth Momentum

Overseas profit margins continue to expand. Currently, OBM margins are less than 1 percentage point below OEM margins, and margins are set to trend higher as operating scale expands. The OEM segment has historically generated the highest margins across the group's consumer (To C) operations.

The US market performed well in the first half, with concurrent expansion in contract manufacturing. In the tariff environment, certain traditional players lost cost advantages, leading orders to shift toward Midea. Europe offers high visibility and remains the core focus region for future overseas growth.

(II) Global Factory Footprint and Tariff Mitigation

The company currently operates approximately 45 factories overseas across both To B and To C segments, covering almost every major region. Among newly added overseas capacity, roughly 30% operates under localized manufacturing and dispatch, while 70% continues to be exported from China (European tariffs are only 2–3 percentage points, presenting limited impact).

  • North America: Over 90% is supplied by overseas facilities, primarily Thailand, with modest volumes from Egypt. A local North American joint-venture plant with Electrolux and a factory in Mexico began operations in Q3, effectively neutralizing tariff impacts.
  • Europe: Facilities in Turkey and Spain (non-dedicated appliance facilities that can be converted through capacity optimization) and the Egyptian plant are available to supply the market. Midea is the only Chinese manufacturer operating an active heat pump plant in Europe; local European manufacturing mandates for heat pump commercialization form a significant competitive barrier.
  • Latin America: Despite tariffs reaching 60%–80%, local manufacturing in Brazil leaves operations unaffected.
  • Africa: Sourced primarily via production and supply from Chinese and Egyptian factories.

Management views the probability of comprehensive additional European tariffs as low.

(III) Overseas Factory Operating Model

New factories typically begin with assembly, gradually introducing sheet metal fabrication, coating, stamping, and other processes in Phase II. Core components (such as compressors) are still manufactured in Chinese factories and shipped to overseas plants for assembly; lower-tech parts with local cost advantages, such as packaging materials, are procured locally. The Thailand facility has engaged local suppliers, and select core component vendors have accompanied Midea overseas to build plants (while also supplying Haier Smart Home); overall rollout remains gradual, however, as not all suppliers possess the capability to expand overseas.

III. Domestic Home Appliance Business

(I) Air Conditioning Industry Outlook

The air conditioning industry faces pressure throughout 2026, primarily stemming from three dynamics:

  • Subsidy roll-off: National subsidies declined in scale this year, with coverage shifting from online to offline channels, markedly weakening policy stimulus.
  • Subdued demand: Consumer sentiment remains cautious with no clear inflection point in sight, and a wait-and-see attitude prevails.
  • High input costs: Upstream commodity prices (particularly copper) remain elevated; while plastics and steel prices have softened, copper shows no sign of retracing.

Delivering nearly 1% positive domestic shipment growth in the first half was no small feat for air conditioners. Lower base effects will ease comparative pressures in the second half, though demand pressure persists in absolute terms. Peak air conditioning installation tracking runs from May to August each year and has passed. August installation card tracking turned positive year-on-year, though a renewed downturn in September remains possible. 2027 is expected to improve over 2026, but the impact may be limited even if subsidy policies continue—subsidies persisted this year, yet consumers adopted a wait-and-see posture, with none of the concentrated rush to purchase seen last year.

(II) Market Share and Competitive Landscape

Midea's air conditioning market share remains within the 30%–35% band. Offline market share expanded significantly in the first half (to around 34%–38%), largely absorbing share from second- and third-tier brands; online share contracted slightly, mainly because aggressive Q1 pricing by Gree Electric Appliances,Inc.of Zhuhai (introducing "Kinghome" units) lifted Gree's share.

In terms of competitive advantages, Midea leverages scale effects, commodity hedging, and proprietary installation networks. For products priced at 1,500 RMB, Midea maintains disciplined cost control, whereas sub-scale competitors struggle to simultaneously cut costs, protect margins, and compete on price. Marginal players (second- and third-tier brands) face compounding pressure, enabling Midea to outperform peers even under a defensive posture.

Competition in the B端 space intensified this year as Haier Smart Home and Hisense pursued aggressive pricing to win commercial accounts, but Midea's B端 execution continued to outperform rivals. Haier's performance on the C端 side was unexceptional. In August, installation card metrics for Midea's premium products even outperformed Haier's entry-level units, driven by: Midea's market share running far ahead of Haier; Midea's stronger air conditioning distribution supported by proprietary installation teams; and a comprehensive product suite matching targeted brands and SKUs to different price points.

Xiaomi's home appliance market share has declined continuously since Q4 of last year, continuing its slide through the first half of this year, accompanied by a marked decline in market attention.

(III) Refrigerators and Washing Machines

Refrigerator and washing machine demand has long been driven by replacement cycles, leaving it less sensitive to national subsidy swings. Midea's market share in these categories trails the market leader by a wide margin; the business has steadily competed and captured share by introducing new products to fill category and price-tier gaps, sustaining acceptable performance overall.

(IV) Cost Pass-Through and Gross Margin

Passing costs through to end consumers remains difficult due to weak consumer demand; Midea primarily absorbs input cost increases through internal efficiency gains and cost-reduction initiatives. Price hikes attempted early in the year led to immediate share losses, prompting subsequent rollbacks. Price increases have been completed in the US market, but domestic increases have not been implemented, requiring close observation of leading industry peers' moves.

Second-half gross margins are expected to track first-half trends. Cost pressure from plastics has eased and steel prices have trended lower, but copper remains elevated with no sign of a pullback. Some commodity pressures will lighten in the second half.

Selling expense ratios are trending down across both domestic and overseas markets. The full-year target centers on stabilizing profit margins, with overall operational expenses held under strict discipline; the goal is to lower the domestic selling expense ratio by approximately 2 percentage points and the overseas ratio by around 1 percentage point.

Regarding the technical path of substituting aluminum alloys for copper: While viable from a performance standpoint—with aluminum piping demonstrating equal or superior stability and performance in Japanese timber-framed residences compared to copper—it has not emerged as a mainstream industry alternative.

(V) Brand Matrix

Hualing's inherently low price point aligns with consumption downgrade trends. Hualing is an acquired brand and achieved modest market share gains this year.
  • COLMO: A premium brand that benefited materially from national subsidies across the prior two years. Two years ago, amid depressed foundational demand, expectations for the brand were restrained under a "nurturing" approach. The brand delivered solid winter growth this year before softening somewhat; its overall retail scale stands at around 10 billion RMB, accounting for a modest share of revenue.
  • Little Swan: Also an acquired brand, operating within the washing machine segment.

(VI) Channel Reform (Initiated in August)

In August 2026, Midea initiated lower-tier channel reforms across the air conditioning business. The core shift was eliminating traditional "cold opening" practices (which required distributors to place advance orders and make prepayments to hold inventory) in favor of an on-demand "order-based pickup" model—distributors place orders with the company only after securing customer orders, with retail store purchases triggering replenishment, removing the need for advance channel stocking.

Reform Impacts:

  • No material impact on total sales revenue, altering only sales cadence.
  • Notable impact on cash flow: Operating cash flow in Q3 and 1Q2027 will register substantial year-on-year declines, as positive contributions from advance dealer prepayments in historical periods disappear. Beginning in 3Q2027, year-on-year fluctuations will narrow.
  • Healthier and more transparent inventory: Post-reform, total channel inventory (including distributor holdings) fell from historical levels of 3–4 months down to 1–2 months. Channel inventory was previously opaque; full visibility now enables precise management of omni-channel stock levels.
  • Inventory risk shifts to the company balance sheet, but overall channel inventory levels have declined.

Reform Rationale: In an environment of weak consumer demand, repeatedly offering policy incentives to push inventory and demand prepayments from distributors eroded dealer profitability, creating a vicious cycle. The objective is to partner with distributors through challenging cycles while enticing distributors of rival air conditioning brands to switch to Midea. Excluding air conditioning, other product categories transitioned to order-based pickups long ago; following this restructuring, the air conditioning business is not expected to revert to the traditional model.

IV. To B Business

(I) Building Technologies

Building Technologies grew over 10% in the first half, with domestic growth of roughly 8% and overseas expansion of 11%–12%. Domestic momentum was driven primarily by new products and emerging use cases, while overseas growth benefited from government-backed initiatives.

Regarding profit margins: Margin contraction in Building Technologies over the past year was largely due to losses at the acquired Toshiba Elevator business; Toshiba Elevator turned profitable this year, removing this negative drag and positioning Building Technologies margins for recovery.

(II) KUKA Robotics

KUKA grew over 10% in the first half, with domestic growth exceeding 20% (surpassing 30%+ domestically in Q2) alongside single-digit overseas growth. Domestic market share rose to second place, trailing the market leader by only around 200 units by volume; management expresses confidence in securing market leadership by late this year or in 2027 (Estun 10%, KUKA 9.7%, Inovance 9.2%).

Growth Drivers — Customer Diversification:

Driven by higher internal component sourcing ratios, workforce rationalization and cost containment in Europe, upstream procurement discipline, and downstream commercial expansion, margins will progressively repair upward. US operations delivered robust performance, achieving double-digit growth in both revenue and profit; European operations remained comparatively subdued. KUKA currently has no plans for a standalone listing.

(III) AIDC Thermal Management

Addressing thermal management demands from AI data centers, Midea has committed 1 billion RMB to build a dedicated liquid-cooling manufacturing facility, with division management expressing strong confidence.

  • Product Portfolio: Broadened from legacy chillers and precision air conditioning to a full suite covering CDUs (Coolant Distribution Units), manifolds, fan walls, and cooling towers, with cold plates temporarily omitted due to capacity and aftermarket considerations.
  • Customer Acquisition: Tier-one overseas accounts including Meta, Google, OpenAI, and NVIDIA are in active discussions regarding orders.
  • Team and Capacity: Liquid cooling headcount has expanded to 300–400 personnel, backed by continuous increases in R&D and manufacturing capacity deployment.

Strategic Pivot: Midea previously favored standardized product lines (yielding high mass-production margins) and allocated lower initial investment to AIDC due to high customization requirements across clients and regions. Seeing high visibility in the AIDC market, the group increased R&D commitments, transitioning from a standardized product mindset toward building bespoke solution engineering capabilities.

DPS (dividends per share) will remain stable with an upward bias, with BPS (book value per share) stability providing the foundation for predictable dividend distributions.

(II) Capital Expenditures

Annual capex will not exceed 15 billion RMB over the next three years, allocated primarily to facility optimization with an emphasis on overseas plants. Expanding revenue contributions from To B and overseas operations will drive structural margin expansion across the consolidated group.

(III) Foreign Exchange Management

Midea's foreign exchange gain/loss performance materially outperforms peers, underpinned by accumulated hedging discipline:

  • Upon securing committed orders, price quotations are provided to clients based on baseline gross margin targets; once orders are booked, forward exchange rates are locked immediately against target gross margins to insulate profitability.
  • Building on initial locks, financial derivatives including forward contracts and currency swaps are utilized to capture incremental yield.
  • FX hedging instruments remain accessible to all market participants, but directional positioning and execution timing require accumulated experience that cannot be replicated easily across peer groups.

(IV) Balance Sheet

Free cash reserves stand at approximately 240+ billion RMB, maintaining conservative stability without material shifts. Interest-bearing liabilities total roughly 80 billion RMB.

Topic 8: Channel Reform

Q: What impact will the August lower-tier channel reform have on 2026–2027 sales?

A: Channel restructuring has no impact on underlying aggregate sales volume, shifting only the operational sales cadence. Operating cash flows in Q3 and 1Q2027 will experience notable YoY contraction: Under historical "cold opening" cycles, upfront distributor order prepayments provided positive working capital contributions, which have now been phased out. These cash flow headwinds concentrate in Q3 and Q1 annually; YoY volatility will moderate starting in 3Q2027. Operating cash flow will register pronounced declines in Q3 this year and 1Q next year.

Q: What was the primary strategic rationale for the channel reform? Was it intended to reduce costs?

A: The restructuring was not designed for cost reduction. Amid soft end-consumer demand, repeatedly providing channel incentives to compel distributor inventory loading and prepayments impaired dealer margins, fostering a vicious cycle. The objective is to partner with distributors through cyclical downturns, while leveraging healthier channel dynamics to attract distributors from rival air conditioning brands to switch to Midea.

Q: What were overall channel inventory levels for air conditioning in 1H? Who assumes inventory risk following the reform?

A: Midea's inventory profile remains healthy, consistently managed at 1–2 months, outside of transient pre-618 promotional builds in April. Post-reform, inventory holding shifts onto Midea's balance sheet, but total omni-channel inventory has decreased. Channel inventory held by distributors was historically opaque, with combined distributor and corporate inventories spanning 3–4 months; distributor holdings are now fully transparent, allowing omni-channel inventory to be maintained at 1–2 months, compressing aggregate channel inventory. The operating model functions on an on-demand order fulfillment basis: dealers order only against verified demand, with consumer checkout triggering upstream replenishment.

Q: Could the air conditioning channel model revert to historical practices? What is the status of other product categories?

A: Excluding air conditioning, other product categories transitioned to order-based fulfillment long ago. Air conditioning had lagged, but following this restructuring, the model is not expected to revert to legacy practices. While inventory holding shifts to the company balance sheet, aggregate channel inventory has declined.
