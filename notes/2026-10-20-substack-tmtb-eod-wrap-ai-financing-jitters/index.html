Breadth flipped from yesterday’s narrow bounce (~70% of the S&P down) to ~85% of S&P stocks up by late morning. The drivers & debates at the center of the AI semis trade (financing, power supply, labs ARR, frontier vs. open source) continue to be the focus of conversations this week. We’ve spent the past week modeling Anthropic out given the details we received last week, and the lower Q3 ARR raised a handful of questions we hadn’t thought about enough, ones we suspect a lot of investors are grappling with as well and will be thinking about as the S-1 comes out (we’re working on a longer post). A lot of good back and forth in #anthropic-ipo in Slack on the topic, which we’ll recap below:

OJ here: backing out large customers and distillation from the disclosures, OJ gets only ~$5.5B of net new ARR in Aug/Sep, which makes the $100-110B year-end target look “completely fanciful,” with maybe ~$120B of FY27 revenue, nowhere near the $190-200B FY28 targets floating around in August. Arun Dhar here framed it with compute expects: 40GW of incremental compute next year, ~20GW on average through the year, means ~$280-320B of revenue for the compute providers, and with consensus converging on ~$200B of FY27 revenue for OpenAI + Anthropic (~$120B incremental), ~$180B has to come from everyone else (GOOGL, META, AMZN/MSFT’s own workloads, other model providers, enterprise AI) at zero gross margin: “Its a tall ask if the labs aren’t going to do the heavy lifting.” Lala here put the 40GW at ~$2T of capex, equal to all the AI compute built to date (“a 100% growth next year isnt enough”), and ALC here said the best case is the 2027 GW “underdelivers... say 20-25GW.” The bulls pushed back. atl42 here: “100% growth next year is actually pretty conservative” for a company on a path to 10x this year, so the question is the 4Q26 exit rate, and the way it resolves here is an S-1 that justifies the unit economics plus 4Q momentum on the roadshow that surprises to the upside. Ted here noted we asked the CSPs the exact same questions last quarter, “And then Microsoft was up 15% and AMZN up 20%.” Arun here argued this round is different: people aren’t questioning capex vs. ROI but the commitments, and he doesn’t see the handover from frontier to non-frontier demand happening “without indigestion.” Akh here called it “covid redux” in some interesting framing:

The question bretzel left open for the next two weeks here: what can the hyperscalers say on their prints to reignite confidence, when the worry will be how much of the beat is Anthropic/OpenAI and how sustainable it is if they slow down. Arun’s closing take here: “the despair in this chat prob best sign SOXX gonna rip soon.” Below from Tech L/S tells you everything about the vibes among AI semi investors right now, which is as low as we’ve seen it all year:

Put it all together, and it reinforces our point from yesterday: turning over the rocks is healthy, but semis investors need time to assess what’s underneath and rebuild conviction. This feels different from the bouts of indigestion we’ve seen over the past 2–3 years where new highs always felt on the horizon as the debates now center on whether lab revs can support commitments already made, making another capex raise or rev beat this EPS season less obviously reassuring. As you can tell from the back and forth above, the AI investor debate is maturing alongside the trade itself, with greater scrutiny replacing the go-go days of May/June and the unquestioned conviction of the past few years…interesting times.
