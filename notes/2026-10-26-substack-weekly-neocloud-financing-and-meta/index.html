It’s probably an understatement to call the last week a train wreck for AI infra trades. According to Goldman Sachs, global hedge funds had one of their worst days on Thursday as semi/infra longs sold off while popular shorts squeezed higher. During our Weekly Call on Wednesday, we shared our takeaway from investor conversation after Anthropic’s numbers, which was unanimously poor. Across our conversations, the implied revenue run rate fell short of expectations, some investors cut exposure, and there was little appetite to defend the AI trade. Subsequent company conversations sounded more optimistic, but offered no quantifiable update to establish a reacceleration into year end. The problem for investors is the lack of a credible basis for underwriting next year’s growth. With that said, Opus 5.5 has been widely approved by users since launch with stronger adoption, and we would continue to monitor the progress.

CDS concerns add another dimension: near-term cash coverage can coexist with doubts about debt repayment and business-model visibility several years out. We do not think Anthropic’s numbers alone establish that the AI infrastructure thesis has broken. GPU rental pricing remains a more important test. Neolabs have been significant incremental buyers of on-demand neocloud capacity; if their next funding rounds become harder to secure, that pressure can feed directly into rental demand and pricing. Our Microsoft work also points to rising neocloud financing costs improving Azure’s relative position. We discussed this link between Neolab funding, GPU rental demand and neocloud pricing on our Wednesday weekly call.

This week’s earnings underline why revenue growth and funding need to be assessed together. Meta’s revenue and Muse commentary were encouraging, but higher capex and financing plans increased valuation pressure. SPCX’s compute-led beat came with expansion ambitions that require substantial external funding. Bloom Energy beat and raised guidance, yet negative operating cash flow and rising receivables weighed on the shares. Strong demand still has to turn into cash.

Our latest enterprise work shows that most companies in the latest six-company survey expect slower AI spending growth as procurement, model routing and ROI controls improve. We believe this actually aligns with our view that incumbent enterprise SaaS platforms’ AI products are gaining ground, including MSFT Copilot and NOW Assist. At the same time, our OCP checks and memory research still show demand pressing against physical constraints. We remain constructive on optical interconnects and AI storage, while watching funding availability, rental pricing and the conversion of installed capacity into revenue.

Research|LLM: DeepSeek V4.1-Flash-1017 Reaches the Top Tier of Open-Weight Models, Still Behind Proprietary Frontrunners

Oct 19

DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash-1017 on October 17, an update to the V4.1 Flash model launched on September 10. The API model name is unchanged. Pricing remains $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens, with a 50% off-peak discount. The weights are available under the MIT license. The new version outperforms its predecessor on all 11 benchmarks reported by DeepSeek: its Terminal-Bench 4.0 score increased from 31.2 to 42.3 and its DeepSWE score from 74.2 to 77.0. In our view, it now offers the best balance of capability and cost among open-weight models for coding and agent tasks.

Subscribed

Preview & Review | META 3Q26: Revenue and Muse Impress, Financing Raises Concerns

Revenue reached $64.6b, up 26% YoY, and the midpoint of 4Q guidance was about 1% above expectations, supporting our positive revenue view. Muse’s commercialization commentary was encouraging, but the proposed non-voting Class C stock and 2026 capex rising to $250b increased financing concerns and near-term valuation pressure.

Preview & Review | SPCX 26Q3: Compute Drives a Broad Beat as Debt and Capacity Expand

Revenue of $14.58b and adjusted EBITDA of $8.81b exceeded our preview’s $13.0b and $6.7b estimates, with compute delivering the growth we had anticipated. The company guided 27Q1 revenue to $33–37b, but its longer-term ARR ambitions require substantial new capacity, external monetization and financing to come together.

Review | BE 26Q3: Beat-and-Raise Overshadowed by Negative Cash Flow

Revenue of $1.236b and adjusted EBITDA of $299.5m beat consensus by 15% and 25%, respectively, and 2026 revenue guidance rose to $4.4–4.6b. Operating cash flow of negative $27m and free cash flow of negative $181m weighed on the shares as contract assets and receivables increased; improved collections, new customers and Project Jupiter approvals remain the key catalysts.

Preview | INTC 26Q3: Utilization and Pricing Support Gross Margins

Agentic AI is supporting server CPU demand, while capacity shifts from PCs to servers and better pricing could lift gross margins above guidance. We remain positive on server growth and higher ASPs, with memory shortages constraining PC shipments and advanced-node capacity expansion still requiring execution.

Preview | Kioxia FY27Q2: Price Increases Moderate, eSSD Demand Remains Strong

We expect revenue to rise 31% QoQ, roughly in line with guidance at constant exchange rates, with blended ASPs up 24%. Enterprise SSD demand remains the main support: contract prices rose 25% in 26Q3 and we expect a further 15% increase in 26Q4, despite the slower pace of price gains.

Preview | SNDK FY27Q1: Solid Fundamentals Face AI Capex Concerns

AI storage demand and tight NAND supply continue to support pricing, with enterprise SSD revenue approaching half of the total. Concerns about the durability of AI capital spending are weighing on sentiment, but our work suggests high prices can persist longer than the market expects.

Deep | LLM: DeepInfer Shows the SSD Requirements of Agent Inference

DeepSeek’s disclosed KV-cache configuration points to roughly 20–25TB of physical storage per accelerator in our calculations, highlighting the storage demands of agent inference. Higher Rubin throughput could make a fixed 16TB-per-accelerator assumption too conservative, with actual deployment and cache-retention periods still important variables.

Preview | Ibiden FY27Q2: Server CPU Substrate Pricing Lifts FY28 EPS

Our checks indicate mid-single-digit server CPU substrate price increases beginning in FY27Q3, with better Intel substrate yields also supporting margins. We raise FY28 EPS from JPY267 to JPY282 and will watch EMIB-T progress and the expansion timetable.

Preview | EMC 26Q3: Gross Margin Upside and Further 4Q Price Increases

We raise our 3Q gross margin forecast to 38.1%, supported by price increases in lower- and mid-end CCL. We expect another high-single-digit price increase in 4Q and a rising high-end product mix as Google TPU v8 and AWS Trainium 3 ramp.

Preview | FTAI 26Q3: Awaiting the First Power Deliveries

We expect revenue near consensus and adjusted EBITDA about 8% above it, but no Mod-1 deliveries in 3Q limits the immediate power-business catalyst. The first roughly ten units expected in 4Q, the initial SPV closing and the December investor event are the next milestones to watch.
