  7. 就NXPI并购传闻，CEO王奉民在电话会称更大平台可加速营收放量且公司持开放态度，但作者认为潜在收购价已从此前传闻的95-100美元下修至约70美元（或含现金+CVR）。

I can’t remember the last time a company that was holding revenue so steadily for so long have such a major cut in their next quarter guidance.

This one stings because they are one of the better Edge AI companies out there (in my opinion) and they are being crushed by the memory constraint.

On the surface the Q3 print was actually fine.

Record revenue of $115.2M and record AI rev.

But Q4 guidance came in at $70-$75m which is a ~37% sequential drop.

I believe the company itself is executing very well on the engineering side, butt he environment they are operating in right now is ruthless.

So let’s unpack everything here and see what we have after this drop.

Why is the Q4 guide so bad?

Well, data centers are soaking up all the global DRAM supply and memory prices have spiked.

Ambarella’s customers are getting squeezed. Now keep in mind that they don’t buy or resell memory so their gross margins aren’t getting hit (basically staying constant in Q4).

But roughly 35% of their rev still comes from consumer IoT devices where memory is already a massive part of the BOM. Once they burned through their older, cheaper DRAM inventory and had to buy at market prices, they get stuck and raise retail prices and crush demand, or they eat the cost and ship at very low margin. So a lot of them choose to just pause instaed.

When it comes to Enterprise IoT and Auto, Amba management says they are only seeing pushouts. So when customers can source memory, they want to ship. But in consumer IoT they are seeing pushouts plus some outright cancellations because seasonal consumer products miss their window and they just don’t come back.

This is really the story of the stock being down so much.

For me, this investment thesis was always dependent on an inflection at the end of CY 27, into CY 28.

Right now their average chip sells for about $15. And a big chunk of this is still tied to lower margin consumer cameras. The entire point of their new product roadmap is to move upmarket and build smarter, more powerful chips that run AI locally on the device (drones, security systems, robots, cars etc.) And sell for $20+. Thats the idea. So you have an expanding market and a step up in ASP.

Even with the Q4 reset and the DRAM situation, they still expect full year FY28 rev to grow. (Just as a note, their FY 28 is basically just CY 27). FY28 should be heavily weighted toward the second half. And they aren’t even assuming DRAM gets better next year. They are betting that cusotmer will save their scarce memory for their highest margin flagship products, which is where all of these newer chips sit.

They have a bunch of new products coming out, which again is why I think from an engineering perspective they are doing a great job. Let’s quickly touch on them.

  • CV75 & CV72 (5nm). These are the current growth drivers. These are upgraded AI chips for security cameras, dashcams, commercial video and so on. This pulls up their ASP.
  • CB7 (4nm). This is a more powerful step up for heavier AI tasks like autonomous drones. Booked its first real production rev in Q3 across 3 customers.
  • X7 (4nm). This is a new direction for them. Typically you have customer that build their deveice around an Amba chip. X7 is a standalone AI “add on” chip that plugs in alongside a customer’s existing processor (ARM or x86). This let’s amba sell into edge servers and industrial hardware they couldn’t easily reach before
  • CV8 (2nm) Next gen, cutting edge. Sampling with customers right now and supposed to hit production in 2nd half of CY27. Big part of the H2 recovery bet, with a second 2nm chip taping out soon (more details in Jan)
  • Cooper platform. Softare tools. Working with Liquid AI and optimizing Google’s gemma 4 models so customers can run AI on amba chips using LESS memory. Which is a direct work around for the DRAM shortage.

Sicne they don’t have a massive direct sales force, they are also leaning on a growing partner network with Mancina, Capegimini, 10+ software partners, and newly added Sigma connectivity. All of this is to get these chips into bigger accounts.

We can’t blame everything on the memory environment.

Like I said, I am very impressed with their engineering. But their R&D is very expensive for the company size. This is a reason we really need to see a hockey stick type inflection.

$58m in operating expenses. When rev is $115m and they make ~$11m in operating profit, great. fine. But when a supply chain shock knocks rev down into the ~$70m range, now that fixed R&D expense flips them to a ~$15m operating loss.

Mgmt acknowledged this and said they are reviewing discretionary spending to cut costs during the downturn, while explicitly protecting chip design and AI software. They also didn’t buy back a share in Q3 even though they have a $50m buyback open. Which I appreciate. Protect your $282m cash pile.

There was somewhat of a weird yellow flag in here that I need to check out more. Days Sales Outstanding (how long it takes customers to pay) jumped from 32 to 47 days.

Otherwise, management said they scrubbed the Q4 forecast hard and removed everything they doubt will ship. So ideally that guide is near the floor and its a low bar that they can beat. But of course if something even more extreme happens with DRAM, their isn’t much of a contractual floor.

Now NXPI

This wasn’t a major part of my original thesis, obviously, as it’s more recent.

But it was a supporting factor in my recent additional purchase.

This was from just a few days ago.

I was really hoping we would get this news pre earnings today. And when we saw that halt after hours, I got very excited. (come to know it was just because of the Q4 guide. Maybe if they didn’t halt it wouldn’t have dropped so much honestly).

So where do we stand on this?

Luckily an analyst asked them on the call.

"Excellent. Then maybe a larger picture question. If you could talk about, Fermi, how you feel, but also how the board feels about strategic alternatives versus staying standalone. I know you guys have felt like in auto, you were maybe a little subscale. Are you just feeling overall you might be subscale, and there could be some larger opportunities, whether it's with model companies, the labs, or otherwise to tap into this larger AI opportunity? Or just anything else that you're thinking regarding a tie-up?"

Fermi Wang (President and CEO):

"Right. First of all, our view and our board's view about potential M&A never changed. I think that we all know that if there is a bigger platform who can have our technology, I think they can scale the revenue faster than we have seen before. Also, if there's anybody approaching us, we'll definitely have an open attitude toward this potential collaboration. That have never changed. But at the same time, the way I run this company is always we have to assume that we're going to continue to run as independent until there's some party interested in talking to us about M&A. All those three things I just said didn't change, and I don't think that with the current problem we are facing, it doesn't change our view about the M&A either."

So a few things stand out.

1 - I would expect this answer “Also, if there’s anybody approaching us, we’ll definitely have an open attitude toward this potential collaboration.” That is safe and doesn’t spill any beans.

2 - But this… “I think that we all know that if there is a bigger platform who can have our technology, I think they can scale the revenue faster than we have seen before.” Why? Why say that? You don’t have to say that. Now this is 100% my opinion, but this comment here leads me to believe that there is truth to the rumors.

3 - When I think about NXPI being disinterested after these earnings (they will have done extensive DD of course), this line is encouraging “I don’t think that with the current problem we are facing, it doesn’t change our view about the M&A either.”

Keep in mind, NXP wouldn’t be buying Amba for their consumer camera exposure. They want 2nm vision/perception IP to plug into their auto + industrial sales channels (hypothetically).

What DOES change (again, in my opinion) is the price.

That floated $95-$100 amount is quite far off the table.

My purely speculative amount would be around $70/sh.

That is the floor that I believe amba would sell at, and I think that’s getting close to the ceiling that NXPI would be a buyer at after this big dip.

So, let’s see.

If a deal gets done soon, I would think it would be a structured payout. Something like cash upfront plus a CVR tied to H2 FY28 milestones. Otherwise maybe talks pause until the stock stabilizes.

So, what do we think of the valuation now?

We are looking at $396m in FY27E revenue now with that Q4 guide.

MKT cap as I write this is ~$1.24b

Cash & marketable securities is $282.8m

EV is ~$959m

EV/FY27E sales = ~2.4x

So the market is valuing their entire 5nm/4nm/2nm Edge AI port and auto/robotics pipeline in under $1b in EV.

I know that Q4 guide is really bad.

But they are still expecting to grow next year, even with the DRAM environemnt.

So if I am thinking a few years out we will (hopefully) have an improved DRAM environment (maybe sometime late 2027, early 2028) along with their higher ASP products + new pipeline coming on board.

Who knows how far forward the market will be willing to look.

Maybe the punishment continues.

But for me, given what we know today, I am holding shares and willing to sit through an ugly 2027 on hopes that 2028 looks better and/or NXPI comes through. If we do get a meaningful improvement in share price in the meantime I might trim some of my position and wait for more clarity. But we are a bit of a ways off from that.

CY28 is really the year of focus now.
