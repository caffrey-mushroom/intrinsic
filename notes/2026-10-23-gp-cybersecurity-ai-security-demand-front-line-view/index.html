SUMMARY

Overview

A cybersecurity sales expert discussed enterprise demand across endpoint, network, cloud, SIEM, and AI security. The market is splitting between consolidation around large platforms and rapid growth in narrowly focused AI security startups. Enterprises increasingly view AI as both a productivity tool and an additional attack surface, but most remain uncertain about what controls they need.

Security budgets are expected to rise because AI has introduced new risks rather than eliminating existing ones. However, many customers are delaying commitments while evaluating agent governance, observability, and control technologies.

Key Insights

CrowdStrike was characterized as the clear endpoint-security leader, supported by extensive endpoint data, threat research, and close AI ecosystem partnerships. AI security remains immature. Customers are initially prioritizing visibility into deployed models and agents before implementing controls. CrowdStrike’s next-generation SIEM shows promise but remains less mature than established alternatives. Palo Alto Networks wins through executive relationships, broad bundling, and aggressive pricing, although product complexity and integration challenges can disadvantage it. Zscaler has a strong portfolio and satisfied customers, particularly where buyers prioritize specialized capabilities over bundled platforms. Microsoft security adoption is viewed as primarily driven by enterprise licensing and Azure relationships rather than security- team preference. Google’s acquisition of Wiz could create a formidable cloud-security platform, particularly for cloud-native, compute-intensive companies. Large enterprises are unlikely to rely heavily on open-source security tools because the perceived operational risk outweighs potential savings. A major AI-related security incident could accelerate board-level spending.
