SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a cybersecurity leader with nearly 20 years of experience, discussed AI and agent security in China and related markets. Enterprise spending is not yet consistently tracked as a separate AI security category; it is typically distributed across areas such as data protection, identity and access management, cloud security, operations, compliance, and model evaluation. Adoption is uneven, particularly in regulated sectors, where procurement and compliance requirements influence decisions.

The expert emphasized that security needs depend less on the number of AI models deployed than on which business processes they support, the data they can access, and the privileges they receive.

Key Insights

AI security becomes more urgent when agents enter production workflows with the ability to change data, run code, make payments, or operate enterprise systems. The expert expects technical demand to emerge before substantial revenue growth. Standardized procurement, faster deployment, recurring subscriptions, and integration into existing platforms will be important indicators of commercial momentum. Security requires ongoing lifecycle management, not just a pre-deployment assessment. Operating costs include maintaining evaluation data, tracking model and prompt changes, managing tool permissions, reviewing risky actions, and coordinating across teams. A balanced approach automates low-risk tasks while applying stronger checks and human review to higher-risk actions. Excessive manual controls can undermine the efficiency gains of agents. The expert sees AI security as a layered market in which established vendors, cloud platforms, and specialist providers can coexist.
