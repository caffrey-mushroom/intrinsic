SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a cybersecurity strategy and channel professional, described a strong market with robust demand and pipeline extending into the next planning period. Interest is rising in agentic AI security, identity and data protection, while firewalls, SASE, and endpoint detection remain steady growth areas. The discussion covered Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, Okta, Cloudflare, and competing security providers.

Key Insights

The expert expects cybersecurity demand to remain strong and potentially intensify, driven partly by AI-related threats, vulnerability discovery, and the need for faster patching and compensating controls. Large platform vendors are positioned to capture the largest share of budgets, but smaller specialists are gaining customer interest, particularly in AI security, data security, email protection, and AI-enabled security operations. Organizations face growing product sprawl. The expert noted that a mid-sized enterprise may manage dozens of security tools, increasing the need for consolidation and managed services. Vendors are using free trials, bundled modules, longer contract terms, and service credits to support renewals and adoption. The expert said renewal increases commonly range from 5% to 10%.

Competitive Landscape

Company or group

Positioning and competitive observations

Palo Alto Networks

The expert’s strongest vendor relationship and largest business. Firewall refreshes, Prisma Access, and Cortex XSIAM are key contributors. AI security interest is building, though the expert sees a runtime-visibility gap versus some specialists. Higher cost can make alternatives attractive for simpler requirements.

CrowdStrike

Growing, with interest in flexible licensing and newer SIEM, identity, and exposure-management offerings. Its products may displace legacy SIEM providers and vulnerability-management specialists.

Okta

Cloudflare

Business was flat for the expert. Auth0 generated some wins, but broader product expansion was limited. The expert criticized its partner approach and said direct competition can complicate channel sales. CyberArk’s identity offering is a competitive threat.

Business grew by roughly 10%, with a strong pipeline and positive views of its technology. The expert said it still needs to strengthen partner alignment and compete more directly in SASE.

Fortinet, Check Point, and others

Fortinet remained healthy in the expert’s customer base, particularly in hardware and firewalls. Check Point can offer a lower-cost option for less complex needs. Vendor selection depends on technical fit, usability, staffing, and price.
