SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a sales leader at a regional LTL carrier, discussed freight demand, pricing, network changes, grocery and temperature- controlled shipments, and competitive pressures. The carrier operates a focused terminal network and specializes in larger LTL shipments that can be expensive through traditional carriers, as well as refrigerated freight. The discussion also covered national carriers, brokers, data-center construction, and Amazon’s entry into LTL.

Key Insights

LTL activity has been firm since the second quarter, with freight availability improving recently. The expert views the recent strength in dry freight as likely broader than the carrier’s own business, while refrigerated demand has been relatively steady. Closing underperforming terminals and concentrating on profitable lanes improved service and margins. The expert said the carrier had expanded too far, and now intends to focus on local service areas around its terminals. Growth is expected to come mainly through existing brokerage relationships, offering more capacity and competitive pricing on established lanes. The expert sees traditional door-to-door sales as less effective than targeted inside sales. The expert sees no clear ongoing shift from LTL to truckload, or vice versa. Some larger shipments moved to dedicated truckload when pricing was unusually competitive; some have since returned to LTL. Data-center projects are a major source of demand for flatbed and other open-deck truckload capacity, but have had little effect on the expert’s LTL business. Amazon has not yet been seen as taking meaningful market share. Its technology and shipment visibility could pressure other carriers to improve their systems.
