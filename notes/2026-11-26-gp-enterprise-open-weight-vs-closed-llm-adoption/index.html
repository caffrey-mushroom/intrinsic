SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a technology leader at a large online retailer, oversees AI for software development, internal business teams, and customer-facing shopping tools. The organization is expanding its use of open-weight models while relying on a leading closed- model provider for more complex or higher-risk work. It uses a cloud platform to host open models and an internal gateway for routing, governance, and cost controls.

Key Insights

Risk and task complexity determine model choice. Open-weight models are used for code reviews, simpler coding tasks, and internal knowledge workflows. Customer-facing shopping and health-related interactions remain on closed models because errors could create compliance and reputational risks. The expert sees a meaningful quality gap today. Closed models perform better on complex coding, long-context work, tool use, and reasoning. Open models are not yet trusted for large refactors or other high-complexity tasks. Tooling can make model switching difficult. Existing integrations, security controls, and workflows are tailored to the closed- model ecosystem. The organization is developing model- and harness-independent tools, but migrating established systems requires engineering work. Savings must account for downstream costs. Cheaper models can increase review time, bug fixing, and the risk of building against incorrect requirements. The expert views a quality gap below roughly 10% as a point where lower-cost models become more attractive. The expert expects open models to take a larger role. Over the longer term, they anticipate open models handling most routine work as capabilities improve and per-task costs decline. Proprietary data and evaluation systems are expected to be especially important investments.
