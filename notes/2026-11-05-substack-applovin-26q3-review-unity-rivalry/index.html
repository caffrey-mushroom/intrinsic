2026.11.05 | Substack | APP | AppLovin 26Q3 Review & Unity Rivalry

原标题：Review|APP 26Q3: From Being Caught Off Guard to Responding Effectively

#internet #gaming

Substack · 2026-11-05

核心摘要

  1. AppLovin 26Q3收入21.85亿美元，环比增长13.6%、同比增长55%，比指引上限高4.8%，调整后EBITDA为18.42亿美元，利润率84%。
  2. 公司本季度回购及代扣股份共410万股、金额14亿美元，4Q26收入指引为23.65-23.95亿美元，中值对应环比增长8.9%、同比增长43.6%。
  3. 超预期主要来自核心移动游戏业务的模型改进，游戏业务同比增长近50%，且新一代AXON模型仍处于部分推出阶段。
  4. 消费者广告主支出环比增长两位数、同比增长三位数，新模型在新广告主、小广告主等数据稀疏领域提升最明显。
  5. 作者渠道调研显示一家北美渠道的非游戏广告主数量从6月的58家增至9月的75家，远超此前预期的63家，其中约80%为电商客户。
  6. DSP专家估计Unity此前获取了约30%至50%的MAX相关数据，可使预测准确率提升约5%、D28 ROAS提升约10%、冷启动期缩短约30%。
  7. AppLovin自9—10月起限制部分用户属性数据访问，Unity的出价增速自8月底9月初放缓，AppLovin在iOS上的竞价胜率约为Unity的三倍、Android上约为两倍。
  8. 作者认为AppLovin的take rate在6月低点后迅速恢复，Unity冲击最严重的阶段可能已经过去，AXON 3正式推出后take rate有望进一步改善。
  9. 管理层表示CTV试点产品前景良好，但短期内不会对业绩产生增量贡献。

I. Results at a glance

  • Revenue of $2.185bn, up 13.6% QoQ and 55% YoY, 4.8% above the high end of guidance.
  • Adjusted EBITDA of $1.842bn, up 59% YoY, with an 84% margin. Incremental EBITDA margin was 87% QoQ.
  • The company repurchased and withheld a total of 4.1 million shares for $1.4bn, a substantial increase from the previous quarter.
  • 4Q26 revenue guidance of $2.365bn to $2.395bn, implying 8.9% QoQ and 43.6% YoY growth at the midpoint; adjusted EBITDA guidance of $1.995bn to $2.025bn, with a margin of 84% to 85%.

II. Earnings call highlights

The quarterly beat and gaming reacceleration

  • Gaming drove most of the quarterly beat: “Revenue grew 14% sequentially and exceeded the high end of our guidance by approximately $100 million, primarily driven by the core mobile gaming business due to model improvements during the quarter ... Our gaming business grew nearly 50% year-over-year, re-accelerating right alongside the rollout.”
  • Consumer continued to scale: “Consumer advertisers’ spend continues to have healthy growth, with spend up double digits quarter-over-quarter and growing triple digits from the same quarter last year.”

The next-generation AXON model and the comparison with AXON 2.0

  • Management confirmed a new generation of models: “I would put it from a technology standpoint in terms of technical complexity on that level. In terms of business, look, this is the next generation of the model. ... But the reality is, this gives us a lot bigger path in the future in terms of processing more data and spinning up bigger clusters and hopefully getting better output.”
  • The rollout remains partial, with more room for further optimization: “We are still in partial rollout, so it has to roll through all of our models. There is still a lot of complex R&D work there, but it gives us a much longer roadmap than the old models would have given us”
  • The largest performance gains are in data-sparse segments: “It also improves performance most where data is sparser, newer advertisers, smaller advertisers, newer categories, exactly where our consumer vertical lives.”

The impact of consumer ads on gaming ad impressions

  • Consumer ads diversify the ad mix and improve targeting: “Instead of showing the same user 20 gaming ads in a week, we might show them 15 product ads and five gaming ads. ... It just means that the model can get much more targeted, so those same five impressions that would have driven the conversion are still going to sit there, and now all of a sudden you have all these product ads.”
  • Improving conversion remains the near- to medium-term priority: “We’re still very low on what we think is the possible conversion rate inside this gaming ecosystem. ... That’s really the focus area. That’s the largest needle mover in the short, medium term. Long term, for sure, supply expansion is something that we’re going to be focused on.”

Performance brand ad budgets shifting to apps

  • Performance brand advertisers have different objectives: “So, I would split up performance brand into true performance where they’re optimizing the incremental ROAS or like a down funnel purchase transaction. Then you’ve got performance brand that still is looking for audience reach. A lot of the mobile web advertising and a lot of what’s called, I guess, traditionally open web, is more of the latter.”
  • Budgets focused on purchases could shift to apps: “We try to price the discovery all the way to the point of transaction. So, if that’s what they care about and they’re able to track it all on digital, then yes, we think a lot of those dollars will shift into the app space. ... If you then gross that up for the whole space, this is one of the largest advertising opportunities that there are outside of social and search, maybe the largest outside of social and search.”

MAX data access

  • MAX sees bids, but AXON has limited data access: “The only data that is available, to the MAX system, of course, we see all the bids. We do not know what people bid for. But the only data that is available to our Axon models or our system are, did we win the ad or did we not win the ad? What was the price for the winning auction? And then everyone who loses gets the price for the losing auction. That is it.”
  • MAX does not see the specific ads other companies serve: “We do not have visibility into an auction cleared, someone else won it, they served exactly X. ... That exact X is the ownership of the advertising company ... It is an output of their models. That remains with them. There is nothing that gives us magical data access from the mediation.”
  • All bidders have the same data access: “In fact, when we built MAX, what we did was we commoditized it all. We said everyone is going to have the same access to data as we will as a bidder into MAX, because what we wanted was a fair marketplace. ... that fairness is what drove the price of every single impression to be the highest possible.”

MAX supply, inventory costs and take rate

  • The MAX market continues to grow strongly: “Yeah, I mean, look, it is still strong double digits. I think last quarter we told you in a single quarter it grew double digits. ... I think most ad spaces in terms of just supply growth and value of that supply are not growing all that fast, probably like very low double digits to possibly single digits.”
  • Growth comes from more supply and higher impression value: “there are two drivers to MAX growth, more supply and the cost going up. So cost effectively, companies getting better at monetizing the impressions, the value of the auction goes up, the publisher makes more.”
  • Technology improvements must keep pace with rising inventory costs: “Now, on the other side of that, we are either going to have to pay out more for publisher inventory or improve our own technology at that same rate, or improve our own technology more than that rate to grow our business even faster than that market. ... We have been improving our technology better than the market, and therefore our business is growing much faster than the market is growing.”
  • Take rate is an outcome, rather than an optimization target: “we don’t talk about take rate in the business, and frankly, it happens after the fact. When you have a conversion rate that’s a little bit over 1%, if we were buying on 1,000 impressions, somewhere around 987 of them we’re losing money on. ... On the other ones, you have almost an infinite take rate because you’re making a ton of revenue with a very small amount of outlay. So it’s just not a function that we optimize to”
  • Take rate depends on technology improvements relative to the market: “If our engineers created a massive uplift in the next two weeks, we’d be able to take more because we out-innovated everyone else in the market. ... If we innovate at the rate of the market, the market pricing stays consistent, we’re sort of where we always are, which is pretty consistent. If we fall way behind ... our take rate will contract because the market pricing will get better than the rate of improvement on our technology.”

CTV pilots and the long-term opportunity

  • CTV pilots are live, with no near-term earnings contribution expected: “We’ve got pilot campaigns going now. The product is actually looking very promising. It’s early. It’s a whole new market, so I wouldn’t bank on it at all for being accretive to numbers anytime soon.”
  • The long-term opportunity is full-funnel performance advertising: “there’s not a true top of funnel to bottom of funnel performance offering on connected TV. ... If we can figure that out and be the first to market with something that’s scalable there’s a lot of available inventory that’s under monetized there. It’s sort of what mobile looked like to us 10, 15 years ago when we started the business.”

III. Our take

  1. Why did we gradually become more positive during our research?

Subscribed

As we noted in our last review, the key issue for AppLovin’s recovery had shifted from improving its own models to competing with Unity. We initially worried that Unity would build on the competitiveness it had shown in May and June, forcing AppLovin to keep raising its bids for inventory and putting further pressure on take rate.

In subsequent conversations with publishers and DSP contacts, we did hear that Unity was putting pressure on AppLovin in auctions.

Some contacts also reported that bid increases had slowed in August and September. The pace of AppLovin’s bid increases on MAX had also moderated since July. We therefore noted in our preview that ad budgets were tracking slightly ahead of expectations and that take rate was gradually recovering from its June low. These shifts gradually led us to believe that competitive pressure on AppLovin might be milder than we had initially feared, and gave us greater confidence in its quarterly performance.

  1. The new model is already driving meaningful growth in gaming ad budgets

Management said this quarter that model improvements in core mobile gaming were the main reason revenue exceeded guidance. Gaming revenue grew nearly 50% YoY, with the new model still only partially rolled out.

As we wrote in our preview, a large gaming client was already using AppLovin’s next-generation model. The improvement was not a step change, but it still supported higher budgets: the client’s spending on AppLovin rose more than 24% QoQ, well above its initial expectations. This was one reason we became more positive on the gaming business in our preview. The quarter’s results provide further evidence that model improvements can still generate meaningful incremental revenue in a gaming business that is already large.

  1. The e-commerce advertiser base is expanding

As we noted in our preview, AppLovin is lowering entry barriers for e-commerce advertisers and expanding its reach among small and midsize clients. One of our North American channel contacts reported that its non-gaming advertiser count rose from 58 in June to 75 in September, well above the previous forecast of 63. Around 80% were e-commerce clients. Management said this quarter that new customer additions were accelerating, while Consumer advertiser spend continued to grow by double digits QoQ and triple digits YoY. This is consistent with what we had seen in our research. The growing advertiser base is becoming another driver of e-commerce budget growth.

  1. Further conversations after our preview

After publishing our preview, we also spoke with an expert at a DSP that competes with both AppLovin and Unity. The expert has never worked at either company. The discussion helped us check our views and better understand how competition between Unity and AppLovin is changing. The following points increased our confidence in the views set out in our preview.

  1. MAX data helped Unity optimize its models faster

The expert estimates that Unity previously obtained roughly 30% to 50% of the relevant MAX data. Unity already had its own models, and the data added information on clearing prices, impression revenue and cross-network samples to help identify high-value users, improve longer-horizon predictions and shorten the cold-start process.

For model performance, the data could improve prediction accuracy by about 5%, lift D28 ROAS by about 10% and shorten the cold-start learning period by about 30%. It could also help identify long-tail users more accurately and produce more stable CVR estimates. This helps explain why Unity’s D28 product attracted budget growth relatively quickly after its expansion into IAA.

  1. AppLovin can limit data access, but must also preserve MAX’s competitiveness

The expert noted that AppLovin began restricting access to some user-attribute data during September and October, while basic auction feedback and statistics remained available. This shows that AppLovin has some control over access to its data. It also has to balance those restrictions against publisher monetization and MAX’s appeal to other ad networks. The expectation we heard was that some restrictions may need to ease by December at the latest.

  1. Unity’s bid growth slowed in August and September

Unity’s budgets grew quickly in June and July, and its bids were aggressive. From late August and early September, however, the pace of increases in its bids or eCPM slowed. The pace at which it was closing the gap eased from the rapid improvement seen in the middle of the year.

This is consistent with what some publishers told us during our preview research. It also supports our earlier view that competitive pressure remains, but the squeeze on AppLovin may be milder than we initially feared.

  1. AppLovin retains an advantage in eCPM and auction win rates

The eCPMs AppLovin pays publishers remain higher than Unity’s, with a wider gap in midcore and hardcore games. Its auction win rate has also been recovering after an earlier decline. From what our contact can observe, AppLovin’s win rate is about three times Unity’s on iOS and twice Unity’s on Android. It continues to have a particularly clear advantage in higher-priced impressions.

This feedback also supports the eCPM cushion we discussed in our first point. Unity is becoming more competitive, but AppLovin’s existing advantages still give it some room to respond.

  1. Other platforms face barriers to adopting similar methods

We asked in our preview whether other platforms could obtain data in similar ways. They can technically try to do so, but the value of the data also depends on the models they already have. Continuing to turn the data into better ad performance requires further algorithm development, MMP postbacks and a proprietary conversion feedback loop.

The expert made clear that the competing DSP had chosen not to take the same approach. AppLovin’s legal action against Unity also sent a clear warning to other platforms considering similar methods.

  1. Short-term data benefits may last through year-end, but longer-term gains depend on Unity’s own capabilities

Our contact’s view is that the extra near-term benefit from the data could last two to three months, through year-end. The gains already built into the models, in prediction accuracy, data features and the ability to iterate, could also endure. More important now is whether Unity can keep improving through its own data and conversion feedback loop, and translate better ad performance into larger budget allocations from advertisers.

At least based on what we have heard so far, AppLovin has gradually regained growth momentum.

  1. How do we view the outlook?

After missteps in its response last quarter, AppLovin has returned to its familiar beat-and-raise pattern. Both this quarter’s results and next quarter’s guidance exceeded expectations. Combined with the early Q4 seasonal pickup and budget growth we had already seen in our research, this suggests to us that the company may be back on its previous growth trajectory. We are more optimistic about Q4 ad budgets than before.

What makes us more optimistic about the long-term narrative is that the impact on AppLovin’s take rate this quarter ultimately proved limited. Take rate recovered quickly after taking a hit from Unity’s D28 IAA product in June. This also suggests that AppLovin began taking action after being caught off guard in June. If take rate holds at current levels, we can infer that the worst of Unity’s impact on AppLovin is behind it.

As management noted this quarter, changes to AppLovin’s model architecture allow it to collect data on a much larger scale. This also suggests that we may see further improvement in take rate when AXON 3 is officially launched.

If Unity is unable to continue collecting the data and AppLovin’s data advantage over Unity widens as a result, that would change the long-term narrative.
