There are a few things we need to talk about.

Some of these stories get right into the question we have been having around this trade for a while.

How far ahead is the market going to look?

Because we can keep getting strong quarters, huge spending numbers, more capacity coming online, and still have investors start getting nervous about the growth a few years out.

That’s always been the concern with hyperscaler capex.

I want to discuss the SemiAnalysis revisions and Blackstone’s comments with that in mind.

Then there’s Lambda actually running CPO at scale, the US tightening who can rent access to AI chips, and some interesting work on making memory cheaper to add.

Let’s get into it.

SemiAnalysis

The big number going around is a roughly 40% cut to the 2030 AI accelerator spending forecast.

Thats a biggg revision. But first remember that these are cuts to SemiAnalysis’s own previous forecasts.

The update is dated November 13 and was circulating today. The full institutional model isn’t public, so I’m using reporting on the changes here.

  • 2026 stays around $392B.
  • 2027 goes from $770B to $627B.
  • 2028 goes from about $1.05T to $758B.
  • 2029 goes from about $1.35T to $796B.
  • 2030 goes from about $1.53T to $923B.

Look at that for a second. They still have spending going from $392B this year to $627B next year. Then $758B. There is still a ton of growth in this model.

Their expectations for the later years have come down a lot.

The reporting says their frontier-lab revenue and monetization forecasts are still above consensus.

The reasons given are slower incremental revenue growth at the labs, harder financing, and delays getting sites developed.

They also brought down shipment assumptions, HBM pricing and NVIDIA selling prices. So some of the spending revision comes from less hardware, and some comes from paying less for it.

This has always been the concern on hyperscaler capex.

The market will try to see a slowdown years ahead and start adjusting for it.

We can keep getting significant growth for quarters and the market might still look past that because it’s thinking about where the growth goes after.

Imagine a supplier puts up another great quarter. Revenue is up, backlog is up, guidance is up.

That can all be true while investors are getting less willing to pay for the earnings a few years out.

Investors can keep revising those longer-term expectations even through strong quarters.

And that’s what we are seeing over these past few weeks.

Blackstone and Amazon

Blackstone did have a more cautious take on new development today.

On the BXDC call, their CIO said building would be more difficult after the election and probably somewhat lower than they would have expected a few weeks ago. He still expects plenty of growth.

I think the Amazon deal helps explain how Blackstone can say that and still want to own these buildings.

BXDC agreed to buy a 75.1% interest in two joint ventures holding 13 AWS buildings. 647 MW, with a $5B gross property value. These are powered shells. Amazon keeps a minority stake and keeps using the buildings.

Lambda has CPO running

We’ve spent a lot of time talking about when CPO starts getting deployed at scale. Lambda now has it connecting more than 10,000 Blackwell Ultra GPUs in Quebec.

For the relevant optics stocks, the question is how quickly spending starts moving toward this design. We now have a large customer deployment to put against that timeline.

Lambda’s reason for adopting it is pretty straightforward in that it uses less power on the network and you leave more available for GPUs.

Cool to see it actually deployed!

The US is tightening who can rent AI computing power

Say a Chinese company rents a big cluster of AI chips sitting in another country. It uses them over the internet while the hardware stays in that data center.

That’s the arrangement the US Commerce Department is tightening restrictions around.

The new controls cover data centers supplying large amounts of computing power to companies based in China, Macau and other restricted countries, including through parent companies and intermediaries. They also add licensing requirements for covered chip shipments to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil.

There are exceptions for certain US-company arrangements and systems used exclusively to recommend things like products or social-media content. The customer, its ownership and what it’s using the chips for all affect how the restrictions apply.

For a cloud provider, this gets into who can pay to use the machines. If serving a customer requires government approval, that can complicate the revenue an operator was counting on. And if it becomes harder to fill that capacity, it can feed back into how much equipment the operator orders next.

Marvell and Penguin

Marvell and Penguin announced some interesting memory work today. There are two timelines here.

The first is CXL expansion cards using Marvell controllers, including the ability to reuse DDR4. Those are expected to be available for qualification and deployment by the end of this year.

The bigger shared-memory appliance using photonics is further out. Samples in the second half of 2027. Penguin is targeting 16 TB of DDR5 plus 576 GB of HBM3E and 57.6 Tbps of aggregate bandwidth.

Think about an inference workload that needs a lot more memory and only a little more compute. Today you can end up buying more accelerators to get the memory you need, then barely using the extra compute you just paid for.

If you can add the memory separately and share it, you have another option.

Of course, the GPU still has to get to that memory. How much latency do you add? How much bandwidth do you actually get for the workload? What does the whole thing cost and how much power does it use?

Marvell supplies the controller and photonic technology.

Penguin handles the appliance and the software to allocate the memory.
