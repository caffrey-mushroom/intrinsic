SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed Apertura’s CNS-directed gene-replacement strategy for tuberous sclerosis complex, or TSC. TSC1 and TSC2 mutations drive neurological and multi-organ disease, including epilepsy, cognitive impairment, autism-related behavior, kidney tumors, and lung complications.

The expert identified cognitive and behavioral impairment as the greatest unmet need, followed by seizures and progressive organ damage. Existing therapies mainly suppress symptoms, while gene replacement could potentially address the underlying biology across the brain and peripheral organs.

Key Insights

Presymptomatic patients would be the ideal treatment population, particularly those identified through familial testing. In practice, many patients have spontaneous mutations and are diagnosed only after neonatal seizures, cardiac tumors, or other manifestations. Cognitive function and behavior should be the highest-priority outcomes, followed by seizure control and prevention of organ progression. The strongest value proposition is durable disease modification, potentially including seizure freedom and reduced growth of kidney and other lesions. TSC2 may offer greater clinical need because it is generally more severe, but its larger gene requires a technically complex dual-component delivery method. The available preclinical findings support payload delivery and improved survival in mice, but the expert did not consider survival alone sufficient proof of meaningful human benefit. The moderator pursued the expert’s concerns about surrogate capsids, dose translation, tissue distribution, and whether biological activity will translate into clinically relevant outcomes.
