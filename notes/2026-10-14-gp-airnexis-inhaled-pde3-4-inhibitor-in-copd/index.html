SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed AirNexis Therapeutics’ AN01, an inhaled dual PDE3/4 inhibitor intended to provide bronchodilator and anti-inflammatory effects in COPD. The expert treats a substantial number of moderate-to-severe COPD patients and generally escalates from standard inhalers to nebulized PDE3/4 therapy or biologics.

Persistent exacerbations, hospitalizations, and the absence of therapies that clearly modify disease progression or mortality remain major unmet needs. The expert viewed AN01’s short Phase 2b dataset as insufficient to establish meaningful differentiation.

Key Insights

AN01 needs long-term evidence showing fewer exacerbations, hospitalizations, and oral corticosteroid courses, alongside improved lung function and quality of life. The expert considered four weeks of data highly inadequate and preferred approximately one year of follow-up. Incremental FEV1 improvement should exceed 100 mL when AN01 is added to existing bronchodilator therapy. A dry-powder formulation offers only a modest convenience advantage over nebulization. It may also trigger coughing and still requires adequate dexterity. Physicians are unlikely to switch stable patients from Ohtuvayre solely because AN01 uses a dry-powder device. The expert would be surprised if AN01 demonstrated greater clinical benefit than ensifentrine, given their related mechanisms. Likely use would be late in the treatment pathway, particularly after triple inhaled therapy and potentially ensifentrine, in patients with recurrent exacerbations and low eosinophil counts. Insurance restrictions and requirements to try established therapies first could materially limit adoption.

Clinical Data

The expert regarded AN01’s FEV1 endpoint as standard and valid but found the observation period too short. Phase 3 should evaluate exacerbation frequency, time to first exacerbation, hospitalization, corticosteroid use, quality of life, and sustained pulmonary function against triple therapy.
