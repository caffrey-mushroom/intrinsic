SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion reviews Phase 3 HARMONi-GI1 results for first-line ivonescimab plus chemotherapy versus durvalumab plus chemotherapy in advanced biliary tract cancer. The expert considers the enrolled patients broadly representative of clinical practice and emphasizes the disease’s aggressive course, limited early detection, and need for treatments that extend survival, shrink tumors quickly, preserve quality of life, and potentially enable surgery.

The expert views the results as highly promising, while noting that further follow-up and more detailed safety reporting would be useful. Trial stratification by disease status and tumor location is considered important because biliary tract cancer includes biologically distinct subtypes.

Key Insights

The expert considers the survival and response results among the strongest reported for first-line treatment in this disease, and expects the regimen could become a preferred option if approved. Benefits across tumor subtypes are seen as encouraging, particularly the response rate in gallbladder cancer. However, subgroup results do not establish that every subtype benefits equally. Higher response rates may create conversion-to-surgery opportunities for some patients with locally advanced disease; the transcript provides no conversion or resection outcomes. The expert attributes the results to combined PD-1 and VEGF targeting, while presenting this as a biological rationale rather than proven explanation. Key ongoing needs include earlier diagnosis, better later-line options, subtype-specific evidence, and treatments that improve tolerability and quality of life.
