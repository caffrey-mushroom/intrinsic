  • 11/10 — This note updates our channel checks to answer one question: when does security-vendor budget growth show up, and how large is the increase?

Three new takeaways

  • Near-term security incidents do not necessarily translate into immediate revenue growth. PHP2Shell has largely been addressed through patches, existing tools, and budget reallocation. Fewer than 15% of customers added budget immediately, limiting the direct revenue impact on vendors this year. Persistent or severe incidents, together with sustained offensive and defensive activity, are more likely to drive ongoing increases in enterprise security budgets.
  • The budget growth is expected to come through in 2027: security-spend growth among covered customers is expected to rise from 10–12% in 2026 to 13–15% in 2027. The increase is concentrated in AI security, AppSec/software supply chain, identity, and cloud security, while mature categories are broadly flat.
  • The more important shift is how automation changes the cost of labor, rather than one-off revenue from incidents. Scan frequency is rising 3–10×, alerts requiring human handling 1.5–3×, and remediation windows are compressing to 24 hours or three business days. In 2027, 10–20% of low-value outsourced work could be automated, rising to 25–40% over the next 2–3 years.

Near Term: Patches and Budget Reallocation; New Purchases Are Mostly Services

Subscribed

PHP2Shell’s two vulnerabilities were disclosed on October 20, with patches released the following day. PHP is used by roughly 70% of websites, and the NSA advised taking systems offline if they could not be updated immediately. Most security leaders we interviewed installed patches and restarted PHP processes within days, using WAFs or virtual patches to block exposure until remediation. For older versions without patches, they limited request sizes and disabled upload-progress tracking to reduce risk. Compared with Log4j, PHP is less common in large-enterprise core systems, its deployment footprint is easier to identify, and remediation is simpler — usually without the need to buy new tools.

Near-term incremental purchases are concentrated in external attack surface management (EASM), incident response, and remediation personnel. Scanning tools that only identify issues still need remediation engineers to act on the findings. Customers generally prioritize incumbent vendors (PANW customers add more PANW products, while Cisco customers first check their existing licenses). This is consistent with the purchasing-channel findings in our November 5 note.

Current-year budgets are difficult to increase materially, and the PHP incident did not cause widespread actual losses. Roughly 25% of enterprise customers have contingency funds, while 70–75% have relatively rigid budgets. In response to severe risks, companies may spend more than $1 million, and a large enterprise’s one-off emergency response could exceed $150,000 and approach $500,000. More commonly, companies reallocate existing budgets, accelerate already-approved projects, or draw on next-year funds. Incremental spending is concentrated in EASM, SCA, and vulnerability scanning, while higher-priced products and longer-term capability buildouts are generally deferred to the next budget cycle. GSI experts said fewer than 15% of customers added budget immediately.

Medium-Term Structural Change: Automation Reprices Labor

The more persistent pressure is on remediation rather than vulnerability discovery. We made this point in our October 28 note; this report quantifies the implications.

Scan frequency and result volume rise first. US Co. 2 plans to move scans from monthly to weekly; US Co. 4 estimates that after shifting to continuous scanning, frequency rises 3–10× and raw results 2–5×, while deduplication and risk-scoring leave results requiring human handling up 1.5–3×. The remediation window for high-severity vulnerabilities also compresses from 1–3 weeks to 24 hours or 3 business days.

As remediation becomes the bottleneck, companies are spending more to improve efficiency. They prioritize active attack paths and high-severity vulnerabilities, pay for log ingestion based on usage, and no longer retain all logs at the highest level of detail. Commercial vendors provide telemetry, scanning, runtime protection, and compliance capabilities, while internal teams manage unified risk data, integration, policy, and orchestration. High-impact actions, such as shutting down production systems or changing critical configurations, still require human approval.

This is also changing the economics of outsourcing. US Co. 2 expects to automate low-value tasks such as alert triage first in 2027, reducing low-value outsourcing spend by 10–20%. Over the following 2–3 years, as platform consolidation and AI automation mature, overall outsourcing demand could fall by 25–40%, freeing budget for automation, architecture upgrades, data integration, and AI governance.

2027 Budget Accelerates, but Spending Trends Diverge Across Enterprises

Based on our channel checks, we expect security-spend growth among covered customers to rise from 10–12% in 2026 to 13–15% in 2027, driven by newer categories such as AI and cloud security, while traditional categories grow at single-digit rates. Resellers’ average security-business growth also increased quarter by quarter, from 10% in 26Q1 to 13% in 26Q3.

Source: FUNDA

This growth outlook does not apply uniformly across enterprises. Some large companies continue to accelerate spending, while those that made sizable one-off investments are growing more slowly off a higher base (US Co. 2 grew about 40% in 2026, including a migration to a single platform, before returning to 20–25% growth in 2027). Asia Co. 1 has a smaller base and is more sensitive to industry conditions; its preliminary 2027 growth estimate is 30–50%, with spending focused on AI workflow visibility and alert-handling personnel.

Source: FUNDA

Baseline budget growth comes mainly from inflation, business expansion, and upgrades to core security capabilities. US Co. 4 estimates these factors contribute about 5–10%; incidents and AI-related offensive and defensive activity add another 3–5%, bringing total growth to 13–15%. Companies facing strict regulation and complex supply chains could reach 15–20%. An enterprise’s level of automation determines how well it can absorb the additional workload: companies with stronger automation may need only 2–3% more budget, while those with weaker foundations may need 5–10% or more. The additional spending is mainly for efficiency; headcount is unlikely to increase in proportion to vulnerability volume.

Spending is also being brought forward. US Co. 3 said budgets are typically set about six months in advance. When a risk event occurs, companies may bring forward projects planned for the following year and reallocate funds. As a result, part of the acceleration in 2026 reflects projects being pulled forward, and growth could ease later.

Category Mix: Incremental Spending Flows to AI, Application, Identity, and Cloud Security

The 2027 budget increase is concentrated in four categories, while mature categories are broadly stable. AI security, AppSec and software supply chain, identity, and cloud security are gaining budget; SIEM/MDR, firewalls, and VPN are largely flat. US Co. 1 noted that firewall costs are stable, VPN spending rises with user count, and three-year contracts lock in annual fees. AI-related products, by contrast, are often signed on one-year contracts, making AI security more likely to receive incremental budget.

Source: FUNDA

AI security and AppSec are seeing the largest budget increases. US Co. 4 expects AI and data security to rise from 5–10% to 10–15% of budget (spending growth of 40–50%), and AppSec to increase from 10–15% to 12–18% (including SCA and SBOM). Europe Co. 1 plans to increase AI security spending by 30% and AppSec by 20%.

Identity-security spending is growing with user seats, non-human identities, AI agent identities, and API token governance. A company with 1,000 users and 100 agents may need to pay for 100 additional agent identities. US Co. 4 includes PAM, DLP, and data security in its identity budget, which rises from 20–25% to 22–27%. Cloud security spending is growing alongside cloud adoption, particularly in CSPM, workload protection, CASB, and zero trust.

AI and token-related spending still represents a small share of overall security budgets. Purchasing channels remain consistent with our November 5 findings: customers typically buy through cloud-provider gateways or security-vendor offerings, and we have not seen customers purchasing directly from Anthropic. We therefore do not repeat that discussion here. Vendor consolidation will also affect budget allocation. Europe Co. 1 plans to reduce its number of strategic security vendors by 15–20% over the next 18–24 months, cutting low-value outsourcing, overlapping tools, and one-off consulting to free up budget for automation and the categories gaining incremental spend.
