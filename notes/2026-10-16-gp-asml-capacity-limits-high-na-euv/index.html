SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined ASML’s ability to meet rapidly rising semiconductor lithography demand through 2028. The expert focused on low-NA EUV capacity, supplier constraints, pricing power, China-related risks, installed-base upgrades and high-NA EUV adoption.

ASML holds an effective monopoly in leading-edge lithography but depends on a complex supplier network and a small group of major semiconductor customers. Demand visibility is supported by long-term agreements, fab construction progress and close customer collaboration.

Key Insights

The expert viewed the planned EUV expansion as achievable for 2027 but substantially more challenging from 2028 onward. Smaller suppliers, workforce availability and synchronized capacity growth are greater risks than major optics suppliers. Customer demand is validated through visible fab preparation, including installation infrastructure. ASML is avoiding commitments for 2029–2030 until both customer readiness and its own capacity are clearer. Allocation decisions likely consider order timing, fab readiness, long-term relationships, customer loyalty and volume. Contract protections are expected to discourage speculative bookings. The recent price increase was characterized as an exceptional adjustment linked to AI-driven value creation and supply-chain funding. Repeated market-based increases could undermine ASML’s stable, value-based customer relationships. China restrictions create meaningful short-term revenue exposure. The larger long-term threat is that continued restrictions accelerate domestic Chinese lithography development. Installed-base upgrades remain attractive because they can increase productivity much faster than adding new fabs or systems. High-NA EUV adoption may initially be gradual, then accelerate once maturity and cost advantages clearly exceed low-NA multi-patterning.
