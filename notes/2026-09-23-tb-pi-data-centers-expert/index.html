Questions

Could you describe what Pi Data Centers does and the products and services it provides?

Expert: They are basically a data centre company. It's been 10 years since they started. They have their location in a place called Mangalagiri. It's in a state called Andhra Pradesh in India. Their built-up area is about 2 lakh feet squared and they have about 1,100 racks on the floor. Their annual revenue would be, in terms of rupees, INR 0-250 crore. Their typical clientele industry-wise ranges across almost all segments, finance, education, medical, government and so on. Their largest clientele spread is in finance, fintech, small banking and other clients, primarily. They are also into certain manufacturing companies and 11-20% is in government. They have about 60 employees and they have headquarters in the city of Hyderabad, India.

What would you say is the value proposition of Pi Data Centers' products and services?

Expert: Primarily, co-location services. That'll be the biggest product area and revenue-gathering area. They're not into cloud. They're not into services as such.

Analyst: What do you mean by co-location?

Expert: It means that if you want to rent out, suppose if a company has some servers on their own and they want to put it in an area where it gets all the electricity, cooling and maintenance. They put it into one of these co-location centres and they run it from there. The role of the co-location centre is to maintain the uptime availability of those servers. The client maintains the remaining maintenance of the servers, builds applications on it, connectivity to connect to it remotely and all that.

What would you say is the full range of industries and business sizes that could use Pi Data Centers' products and services? What might be the company's TAM?

Expert: Mid and large would be the range. Small industries, it's not geared towards small segments. Mid-to-large and enterprise-grade clientele it would reach, from companies who would have a monthly invoicing ranging from close to INR 10 lakh-25 lakh to over INR 50 lakh per month billing rates. They would be able to afford that. Why does this matter? Renting out from cloud providers, the same infrastructure, would be very expensive and many of the co-location companies here aren't owned by one single entity. There are several entities that own and they take a part of it. They rent it again. It passes through many intermediary companies. That's not the case here. That is one of the attractions towards this company.

Analyst: Which industries might the company target?

Expert: Because they are a tier 4 certified company, which clears out a lot of banking regulations. They have several certifications and they have also banking customers. Banking, finance, fintech is a major reach for them. At the same time, they're into other manufacturing also because where the volume is high, they're able to handle that capacity also.

Could you profile Pi Data Centers' typical target customers across industries and business sizes? What might be the company's sweet spot based on other metrics?

Expert: The sweet spot is the cooperative banking sector because they are not too heavy on regulations and they're not too heavy on paying for high availability and all that, because they don't mind taking a downtime of five minutes or something like that. What happens is that when you go for the other regulated banking sectors, or say, fully regulated sectors need very high availability environments, which will be very expensive. When you have that sector, in terms of profits also, it is reduced. That's why they go after the semi-regulated banking sectors, where they're able to make them happy with the current requirements as well as there's a good profitability margin also in that sector.

Expert: 41-50% comes from cooperative banking, small banking and such people. Comparatively.

Analyst: Might there be a close second industry?

Expert: The closest second would be one level up from cooperative banking, the other private banks. That would be the next segment. Beyond that would be the manufacturing segment.

Analyst: How do you determine the business sizes Pi Data Centers caters to? What might make an ideal medium and large company?

Expert: Based on their annual revenue growth, they would target INR 500 crore-plus companies, which most of the banking sector easily achieve that goal because what they're looking at is good paymasters with the monthly billing. They relax up to three months for the billing, 45 days. Many clients pay up within the 60-day period, so that is one take on why they target those kinds of companies.

In the women's SME area, they have their own cooperative banks and all that.

Analyst: How is the price decided for co-location services? What factors come into play? Are there any services that are provided while the infrastructure is with Pi Data Centers?

Expert: Pricing is quoted in terms of per rack. INR 6 lakh-15 lakh a year or a monthly price of INR 10,000-15,000 per rack per month. Depends on the power it consumes. Depends on the amount of the size of the rack and all that. They come to that conclusion. They have managed racks and unmanaged racks. Sometimes customers bring their own men into the data centre to manage their racks. We provide that. Some other people will expect the data centre to manage that. That'll be the cost of managed services on that.

What might be a use case for each segment? Could you discuss a user journey of a customer starting with Pi Data Centers? What value and services are added to them? How do co-location services work on the back end until the end of the engagement?

Expert: Typically, the customer would want to host their infrastructure. They would give something called a bill of materials. Based on the bill of materials, Pi Data Centers will prepare a quotation to host them. What would it cost per month in terms of power, cooling and support? That is resource support. Then, there would be a few levels of something called requirements meeting criteria based on, we want 80% availability, 90% availability or 99% availability. Based on this, they will architect how the co-location should happen. They will start from there. Based on customers' cost, requirements, necessity, regulatory compliance and all that, they arrive at the final solution.

Analyst: What might be a use case for the greenfield projects? How do they work?

Expert: Greenfield basically is very much a non-IT project. It will be a civil contract build of the structure, engaging service providers and internet service providers, contracting with the electricity board of the locality for power and the water supply department for the water requirements and then all the permissions and all the clearances they need to get, whether they need to have a generator capacity with a diesel tank, clearances from fire department, planning for what size it should be, what is the availability of the local grids for electricity, manpower, all those things come into the picture. Based on the size, do they want 1-2MW up to 30MW or 60MW capacity of the data centre? They will size that sitting with the client and they have a team of experts who come into the meeting and begin the discussions. They move on to different areas and implementation. Many partners also come into the picture who have specialised departments. There would be a specialised diesel manufacturer who would come. There would be a UPS manufacturer who would come and so on. Once they build that, then the IT segment, how many racks of capacity did they plan for, installing the racks, giving the power to the rack all the way and then hosting the servers. The IT part, then again, if the client wants further into the IT, they will also provide services on maintaining those servers also at the client premises, wherever the data centre is built. That is the end-to-end greenfield approach.

Analyst: What might be a use case for the brownfield projects?

Expert: Brownfield is, suppose the client already has a data centre but is unable to manage that or has run out of capacity. That's where you come and you expand it or modernise it even. There are many modernisation projects there. You can modernise it, throw away all the old stuff and bring in the new stuff and then complete it to a client's specifications, basically.

Analyst: Is the design and architecture of the greenfield and brownfield projects completely handled by Pi Data Centers, or do customers come into play?

Expert: It depends. If the customer has got a very reputed architect for the physical building infra, he would want them. Then the design comes from us. It could be vetted by their known architect. To maintain certain tier levels, like tier 1, 2, 3, 4, there is a specification requirement that has to be met. Based on that, that's why we have our own teams. Basically, they engaged because they have been working with them for such a long time. They have their own teams who would, without wasting much time and without R&D, they would straight away go and build a plan.

Analyst: What do you mean by tier 4 data centres? What are the regulations?

Expert: That is very highly regulated. It has to meet a lot of requirements. What it means is the data centre can never go down. It's 99.999% availability. You should have fully redundant systems, like four diesel generators. Two of them fail. Two should pick up and if one of them fails, one should always be there. Redundancy is built into every part, from diesel generators to power, to racks, to servers, to the very end, very expensive in the end. There is the Uptime Institute, which is in the UK, that certifies them. They certify them all the way from the physical, all the way to the very last. They should meet certain criteria. Every year, they should renew the certification to maintain tier 4. That is why it is a double-edged sword, basically. You build a tier 4, but you are competing in the market with tier 3 plus segments where the cost would matter a lot. You already have a tier 4. There's past 10 years' experience, the clients really don't need a tier 4 unless they're hyperscalers wanting to rent from you. When hyperscalers come, they will handle most of the tier 4 requirements in a different way. That is why nowadays nobody is truly building a tier 4 data centre.

Analyst: How might customers build their own cloud? What might be the process? Does Pi Data Centers factor in anywhere during that?

Expert: Some clients want us to build a private cloud on their own servers. Basically, it's like virtualising the whole server and having more people, giving more environments to people than the physical server. One physical server can give about 15 virtual servers, for example, for 15 applications to run. That is nothing but the cloud. You're lifting up the whole physical infra into a virtualised environment. It could be compute, storage, network and now GPUs, basically, are all virtualised and presented so that more people can work with more environment reach. That is called cloud, basically. There are some automation features that come into the cloud which are restricted if they stay on the hardware itself. The platform layer, which we call PaaS, platform-availability-as-a-service, is nothing but the cloud layer, where if the client doesn't have the expertise, there is a cloud team that will plan and build for them. If it goes beyond the capability of this cloud team, there is a cloud build partner also who is engaged to build a private cloud for the client, especially when it comes to an AI cloud, which is a little bit complicated because there are GPU servers, there is capacity planning and there is something called GPU slicing and then the containerisation and all those things come into the picture, which is readily available in a public cloud. Due to certain restrictions, people want an AI cloud in a private cloud, in a private environment.

Which of Pi Data Centers' three segments do you anticipate being the fastest growing and potentially driving the largest revenue share in future?

Expert: Right now, co-location would be their biggest bet. Both greenfield and in-house builds are also coming, but the problem is that, with both, the second two segments, like greenfield and in-house, many requirements come from state governments, like the Assam government or remote areas, remote states, because they have already built a state data centre here and many governments approach them to replicate that. For that, what happens is the process of tendering and putting forth something called surety amounts, all those is a huge process, which really doesn't make business sense, so they don't take much. Though there are many such opportunities, they don't really go after them. Their focus is more on co-location, which is simple, fast, continuous generation of revenue, no headache and only expansion, basically. One room gets over, the next room gets filled and then they expand and all that.

It's not a floor-by-floor thing that you see in many cities because they're in a tier 2 city, they have a lot of land available. They can expand horizontally rather than vertically. That's why they do that and so they're able to go after that expansion.
