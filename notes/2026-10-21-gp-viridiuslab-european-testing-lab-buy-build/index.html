SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined a European testing, inspection and certification group built primarily through acquisitions of small regional laboratories. Its portfolio is concentrated in environmental testing, with additional exposure to chemicals, life sciences, industrial testing and specialist niches.

The group generally acquires local market access, customer relationships and trusted brands rather than differentiated technical capabilities. Growth has primarily come from acquisitions, followed by price increases at previously underpriced laboratories. Germany is the largest market, followed by Italy.

Key Insights

The expert viewed insufficient scale as the group’s central weakness. Larger competitors achieve better equipment utilization and spread IT, sales, quality and reporting costs across more samples. Acquired laboratories often have outdated systems, aging workforces and limited standalone prospects. Integration is difficult because procedures, technology and pricing remain fragmented. Cross-selling has delivered limited results due to inconsistent commercial processes, separate brands and differing customer requirements. Capital spending has generally focused on replacing equipment rather than materially modernizing operations. The strongest EBITDA opportunities are higher equipment utilization, standardized customer interfaces, self-service tools and automated reporting supported by machine learning. Specialist life-science services were considered the most attractive assets. The expert believed a future owner could create greater value by separating commodity environmental operations from higher-value life-science laboratories. Cash generation was described as stable but unexceptional, while prior EBITDA improvements were characterized as mostly short term.
