SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined Qualcomm’s return to data-center computing through server CPUs, custom silicon, inference accelerators, connectivity IP and open software. The expert characterized the company as a late but credible entrant whose strengths in low- power processing, telecommunications, DSPs and integrated systems could support meaningful AI market share.

The company’s AI strategy targets both customized chips for major hyperscalers and standardized accelerators for smaller customers. Its longer-term opportunity may improve as workloads shift from training toward inference, autonomous agents and physical AI, where power efficiency and cost per token become increasingly important.

Key Insights

Qualcomm’s long-term AI revenue goals were viewed as conservative rather than excessive, particularly given the projected market size. The C1000 server CPU was considered competitive but delayed. Its customized ARM CPU technology and efficiency could differentiate it, although commercial deployment remains several years away. Cost and energy per token may become Qualcomm’s strongest competitive metric as power and cooling represent a substantial portion of inference ownership costs. NVIDIA’s CUDA ecosystem remains a major advantage, but the expert no longer considers it an impenetrable barrier. Open software and AI-assisted coding should make model migration easier. Qualcomm’s HBC memory architecture may provide an approximately 18–24 month lead in cost- and power-efficient near- memory processing, though competitors could reproduce the approach. Qualcomm is expected to remain outside the top position in AI accelerators but could become a top-five supplier. In 6G, Qualcomm was viewed as the likely early leader, with MediaTek as its closest modem competitor.
