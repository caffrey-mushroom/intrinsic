US stocks are about to become a lot easier to trade while most of us are asleep.

We talked briefly about 24/5 trading recently.

I wanted to spend more time on one company that could benefit, and the more I looked through the brokers’ calls, the more interesting this got.

They’re opening up access to US stocks around the world.

At the same time, customers are starting to let AI agents place trades for them.

I think both could bring more activity into the market. And there’s a company already sitting in the middle of a lot of those orders.

Nasdaq is targeting December 6 for its expanded trading schedule.

That would give someone in Korea a more established exchange on which to trade US stocks during their own day.

Brokers already offer overnight trading through other venues, so there’s actual activity to build on.

The SEC measured $7.4 billion in average daily overnight stock trading in August. Still only 0.8% of US equity dollar volume.

Then on top of this I looked at what’s happening with agents, as I was curious if this could potentially be another growth engine for this company. Robinhood reported almost 200,000 agentic accounts and more than $1.6 billion of cumulative trading volume through them.

Interactive Brokers and many others will be opening up autonomous trading too.

There’s money moving through these products already, even if it’s early.

Below I’m going through how this company makes money, which brokers already send it orders and how agents could add to its growth. Then we’ll get into the earnings and what the stock could be worth.

The Business

The company is Virtu Financial, $VIRT.

When you buy a stock through a brokerage app, the broker can send that order to a market maker like Virtu. Virtu provides a price and fills the trade. It does this across stocks, ETFs and other financial products around the world.

Say it’s willing to buy a stock for $100 and sell for $100.02. There’s two cents between those prices. If it buys and sells successfully, some of that gap can become income.

The stock keeps moving, so Virtu has to manage what it holds and hedge its exposure while paying trading costs. Its technology does that across enormous numbers of transactions, trying to keep a small amount from each.

Q3 adjusted net trading income reached $704 million, up 50.7% YoY. Normalized adjusted EPS was $1.77, up from $1.05.

Asset managers also pay Virtu for execution software, trading algorithms and tools that help them evaluate their trades. That business earns commissions and fees. Market making drove most of the latest earnings improvement.

Overnight Trading

Virtu was already filling overnight orders when Blue Ocean launched its trading venue in 2021.

A broker adding overnight access needs firms willing to keep quoting prices and filling orders. Virtu already has the connections, the trading systems and people working across regions. Management has talked about passing activity between its existing teams as the trading day moves around the world. That gives it a starting advantage over somebody building the operation from scratch.

Interactive Brokers said on its Q3 call that overnight trades exceeded 10 million, compared with 3.5 million a year earlier.

Bad news can hit overnight while markets Virtu uses to hedge are closed.

It can lose money on those positions before it gets a chance to adjust.

And it’ll be competing with firms like Citadel Securities and Jane Street for the orders.

Customers getting better prices would leave dealers earning less per trade, so Virtu could handle more volume without seeing the same increase in profits.

AI Agents

How many strategies would people actually run if they had the time?

Following companies, checking prices and placing each order takes work. An agent can take on some of that, and I think it could bring people into trades they would otherwise skip.

You can describe a strategy to an agent, approve the rules and have it watch for the conditions that trigger an order. Public already offers that workflow. The customer can get on with their day while the system keeps checking.

Robinhood said on its Q3 call that customers had put more than $250 million into agentic accounts.

Interactive Brokers expects AI to help customers process more information, follow more companies and implement strategies more easily, leading to more trading. Its autonomous trading launch followed in October.

Schwab said trading through AI chat tools and software connections had roughly doubled over six months. Management associated AI-connected customers with stronger engagement, though that observation leaves open how much the tools caused the activity. People who already trade frequently may be the first to connect them.

Exchange operator Cboe said agents could make trading strategies easier to run. On its Q3 call, it said it still couldn’t measure how much activity they were adding.

So my hypothesis is that agents can be a net volume driver.

Some people will use them to monitor a long-term portfolio and barely trade.

Others will run strategies they previously lacked the time or tools to carry out.

The Connection

Robinhood’s Q3 routing report shows Virtu received 33.89% of its covered S&P 500 stock orders in September and 35.95% of the covered orders in other stocks. These are the categories of orders where the customer leaves the routing choice to the broker.

Public also lists Virtu in its Q3 routing disclosure. Its orders pass through clearing firm Apex, which connects them to trading firms including Virtu.

Customers are adopting agents at brokers that already use Virtu to fill stock orders.

Uneven Quarters

Cboe’s market statistics put average US equity volume at 16.9 billion shares a day in Q3, down from 20.2 billion in Q2 and 17.6 billion a year earlier.

Virtu’s daily adjusted net trading income moved from roughly $12.9 million in Q1 to $11.6 million in Q2 and $11 million in Q3. Still a very profitable business. Just less activity and opportunity than earlier in the year.

Virtu can earn more when prices are moving around and there are more opportunities to trade and hedge. During calmer periods, it may earn less from each trade. So even with more customers and longer hours, profits will still swing from quarter to quarter.

Average daily adjusted net trading income rose from about $4.8 million in 2023 to $6.4 million in 2024 and $8.6 million in 2025. Normalized adjusted EPS rose from $1.84 to $3.55 to $5.73 over those years.

You would be buying a business with that history of uneven earnings. Longer hours and agents could lift activity over several years, with plenty of quiet quarters along the way.

Funding Growth

Its invested-capital measure increased from roughly $2.47 billion at the end of 2025 to $3.39 billion in September. Management said the increase came roughly half from retained earnings and half from debt. That supports more positions and strategies, alongside the collateral required to keep trading.

Retaining earnings can be attractive if the extra capital produces good additional profits. Strategies do have limits, though and other firms can crowd into them and managing bigger positions can get expensive.

Virtu is putting more money into trading, and its buyback authorization expired in April. Earnings growth now depends more on what that larger operation can produce.

Interest expense rose almost 30% YoY in Q3, so the extra trading capital has a higher hurdle to clear too. A slowdown would leave Virtu with less income coming in while those financing costs keep running.

The Valuation & Chart

At $59, Virtu trades at about 8× the $7.39 per share analysts expect it to earn in 2027. If it can keep earning around that level, I think $67–$74 is reasonable. And that’s without putting a number on what overnight trading or AI agents could add.

The part I like is how Virtu held up last quarter. US trading volume fell 16% from Q2, but Virtu’s adjusted earnings per share only slipped 3%. That’s encouraging, especially in a market where money is moving in and out of different sectors so quickly.

There’s still real downside if trading gets quiet for a while. If Virtu’s net trading income fell 20% from Q3 and other major costs stayed roughly steady, I estimate earnings could fall toward $5 per share. At 8×, that’s around $40–$41.

For now, I think this is an interesting business for me to watch in a market like this.

The chart is chopping right now and I don’t have any interest in playing the middle ground. If we find some support lower closer to my bear case I will revisit.
