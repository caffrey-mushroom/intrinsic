Agenda

  • Distribution relationship roll-out for Sealed Air and Veritiv
  • Consumer demand resiliency through cost pressures
  • Reinvestment into ageing infrastructure
  • Ability to mitigate product cannibalisation

Key Insights

  • Historically, 50-60% of Sealed Air's protective packaging volume moved through distribution, with larger equipment sales typically going direct, structurally limiting the channel shift. Other distributors carrying Sealed Air products will likely suspect preferential pricing or lead-sharing with Veritiv, risking relationship deterioration across network
  • Cushioning is showing promising growth as customers shift preferences, providing potentially mix-driven margin tailwind. Improving margins above mid-to-high teens in longer term depends on SKU rationalisation, mix shifts away from void-fill, polypropylene and polyethylene terephthalate in both the Americas and Europe
  • Protective demand in 2026-27 should return to 1-4% normalisation growth rates, driven by reduction in void-fill. Risks of further growth deterioration are somewhat unlikely, though pressures of ships in own container and alternative packaging mailers are materially challenging
  • Instapak remains Sealed Air's highest-margin product due to exceptional shipping economics, but European sustainability legislation could effectively outlaw it, and similar regulations may eventually follow in North America
