Good morning. QQQs +70bps as investors digest a wave of headlines pointing to a possible diplomatic off-ramp between the US and Iran around Hormuz. The reports first began circulating during Thursday’s session, with Iran later confirming it had sent Washington a proposal that would restore the framework of the June MOU within seven days, after which the two sides would begin talks on Tehran’s nuclear program. Yields ticking down 3-4 bps across the curve although Crude +2.5% is staying bid.

In Tech land, Semis +1.8% helped by the big AKAM/Anthropic deal annoucned after the close for “accelerating CPU workloads”. AKAM +19%. CPU names bid: ARM +4% / AMD +2% / INTC +2%. META -50bps looking to digest some recent strength. SNPS +3% on an HSBC upgrade while TWLO -4% on an HSBC downgrade. DDOG +1.5% as Wolfe says checks show increasing likelihood of being involved with META’s Muse.

Overnight, mainland China and Korea were closed but otherwise Asia mixed: TPX +1.31%, NKY +1.3%, Hang Seng -1.01%, HSCEI -1.21%.

Happy Friday. Let’s get to it…

AKAM: $11.6B Anthropic deal to support Anthropic’s “accelerating CPU workload demands” transforms CIS growth outlook, with potential expansion to ~$20B

Sell side generally positive on the deal, saying Anthropic’s 7-year ~$11.6B commitment is a major validation of AKAM’s cloud infrastructure strategy, with an option for another ~$9B that could take total contract value toward ~$20B. Revenue contribution is minimal in 2026, builds to roughly $150–300M in 2027 and ramps through 2028 toward a ~$1.7B annualized run-rate, materially accelerating CIS growth. The deal requires substantial upfront capex and likely pressures FCF near term, but sell side sees the stronger revenue visibility, premium CPU economics and potential follow-on frontier-lab wins as enough to shift AKAM from a “value” story toward a higher-growth compute asset.

Read-throughs from Claude:

AKAM’s cloud already runs on AMD EPYC. The current top tier is 5th-gen EPYC and the one before it is 3rd-gen. I found no Intel or Arm servers anywhere in their lineup. The dollars are modest for AMD. My rough math is about $1–1.4B of CPUs over 2027–28. NVDA previously flagged Akamai’s Vera deployment plans. Vera targets agentic workloads (code, orchestration, data processing) with high-bandwidth LPDDR5X.

JBL. The 8-K has Jabil holding Akamai’s $1.7B of memory on consignment and buying it back at cost as it uses it. That means Jabil is building the servers. It’s big revenue at thin margins.

Lenovo (992 HK / LNVGY). It signed a new 7-year hardware deal with Akamai on 9/23, likely the second server supplier.

SNPS: HSBC upgrades to Buy/$700, sees business model shift + agentic AI turning Synopsys into an AI beneficiary

HSBC says SNPS is moving toward a licensing-plus-royalty model in Design IP while agentic AI could shift EDA from human-seat subscriptions toward higher-value usage/consumption as agents orchestrate more design work. HSBC sees these changes driving revenue growth to ~15% in FY27 vs. ~11% consensus and lifting the earnings CAGR from ~7% in FY24–26 to ~28% in FY26–28. Upgrades to Buy from Hold and raises PT to a Street-high $700 from $490.

DDOG: Wolfe raises PT to $340, says agentic AI + Muse could drive another leg of observability demand

Wolfe says ecosystem checks show agents materially increasing observability intensity as every tool call, retry and handoff needs to be traced, with traces costing roughly 2x metrics and already driving higher consumption. AI coding tools are also accelerating software-development velocity by ~2–3x, creating more code/services/pipelines for DDOG to monitor, while Wolfe’s initial conversations suggest an increasing likelihood of DDOG becoming directly involved with Meta’s Muse. Wolfe’s upside case now implies ~32% FY25–28 revenue CAGR vs. ~25.5% consensus and sees DDOG as one of the best-positioned ways to play inference/agentic demand.

AAPL: Bernstein sees December-quarter margin downside from sharply higher memory costs; keeps OP/$370

Bernstein’s BOM/mix work points to December-quarter Apple gross margin of ~46.2%, ~50bps below consensus, and EPS of $2.87 vs. $2.92 Street as higher DRAM/NAND costs outweigh premium iPhone mix. International price increases provide some offset, but memory accounts for ~90% of the estimated $168 y/y device-cost increase on a 256GB iPhone 18 Pro Max.

Memory: Memory / Nanya: CXMT expansion raises 2027 commodity-DRAM pricing risk despite HBM shortages – Commercial Times

> A Money Weekly analysis republished by Commercial Times argues memory pricing could diverge in 2027, with HBM remaining tight while CXMT expansion pressures mature DRAM. It says CXMT’s fifth-generation process increases dies per wafer by at least 50%, and estimates the company holds about 15% of global DRAM wafer capacity but 11% of bit output. The analysis estimates roughly 70% direct competitive overlap with Nanya after accounting for customers and qualifications. It also cites Acer chairman Jason Chen’s expectation of lower average memory costs next year.

ERIC/NOK: BNP says RAN outlook is stable overall but mix shifts away from North America; AI-RAN remains too early to call

BNP says Dell’Oro’s 2026–27 RAN outlook is broadly unchanged, but North America has weakened toward the low end of the prior range while China/rest of Asia ex-India improved. Nokia is more exposed to North American MNO capex, so near-term cuts are a headwind, though 2027 should see some recovery as spending normalizes. On AI-RAN, BNP says Ericsson remains more cautious while Nokia is gaining traction with a more aggressive edge-inference approach, but the market is still too early to determine which strategy wins.

AMZN: Plans $3B Indian quick-commerce investment through 2030 – Reuters

Reuters reports Amazon plans to invest $3B in Indian quick commerce by 2030, including $1B by end-2027, citing two sources. Plans include expanding Amazon Now’s neighborhood warehouses from roughly 750 to 1,300 by April and improving inventory software and demand forecasts. Amazon declined to confirm the spending but said the business exceeded $1B in annualized gross sales.

Optical networking: CPO production ramp expected in 2028–29 as pluggables persist – Commercial Times

Commercial Times cites researcher Lin Wei-chih forecasting limited 1.6T co-packaged optics shipments in late 2026, 3.2T system validation in 2027 and a larger production ramp in 2028–29. He expects pluggable, linear-drive, near-packaged and co-packaged optics to coexist over the next five years. The report puts conventional 1.6T pluggable power consumption at roughly 22–28W, potentially reaching 40–60W at 3.2T. Optical-engine manufacturing, fiber alignment, testing and packaging yields remain constraints.

TikTok / Social media: First US teen-harm trial starts September 28 in Alabama – Reuters

The state alleges addictive design and misleading safety claims, seeking penalties and other relief. At least 27 other states and Washington, DC have also sued.
