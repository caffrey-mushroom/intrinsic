SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined Equal Group’s orthotics and prosthetics business, including patient-care clinics, products, distribution, and digital operations. The group has expanded primarily by acquiring clinics across Europe, the US, and other markets.

The expert viewed this expansion as largely additive rather than organic. The central concern is weak integration across IT systems, purchasing, product catalogs, management, and commercial strategy. Many owned clinics continue buying competitors’ products instead of sourcing internally, limiting procurement benefits and margin improvement.

Key Insights

Equal’s most serious weakness is the absence of a unified post-acquisition integration strategy. Clinics operate with substantial autonomy and reportedly treat the internal product division like an external vendor. Equal lacks a comprehensive product portfolio and clear market positioning beyond its large clinic network. The expert strongly favored expanding orthotics, which serves more patients earlier and for longer periods than prosthetics. Future growth opportunities are strongest in the US and Germany, although successful expansion requires better integration rather than additional acquisitions alone. Recommended priorities include implementing a groupwide CRM, consolidating product and purchasing data, rationalizing low-value products, centralizing supplier contracts, and influencing hospital prescribers before patients reach clinics. Reimbursement pressure and clinician shortages are major structural risks.
