SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a former energy trader at the company, discussed how trading can support an oil and gas producer, the economics of building a trading operation, and opportunities in LNG, carbon storage, and hydrogen. The discussion also covered market outlooks for oil, gas, LNG, and carbon.

Trading can help producers sell directly to buyers, capture more of the value chain, and use storage, transport, and destination flexibility to improve returns. It can also support hedging and, within limits, trading strategies beyond the company’s own production. The expert characterized the company’s energy business as primarily an integrated asset trader, with some potential to expand into merchant activity.

Key Insights

The expert sees trading as a way to add roughly 2–4% in returns to an asset portfolio, generally with limited risk and without major capital investment. Marketing fees account for part of this opportunity; optimization and merchant activity can add more. Scale matters. A trading desk can be marginally viable at around 50,000–60,000 barrels per day, but roughly 100,000 barrels per day is a more attractive scale. Growing toward 150,000–200,000 barrels per day could move the business into a higher market tier. LNG contracts can create value through flexible sourcing and delivery, but a small, concentrated portfolio limits trading options. The expert said a portfolio about three times larger, with more diverse supply sources, would be more capable of active trading. Carbon storage could become a trading opportunity as volumes expand, but its economics depend partly on government support and carbon policy. The expert was skeptical that hydrogen will gain broad energy-market demand soon, citing transport challenges, production costs, and efficiency losses.
