It was also worse than the April 2025 tariff week. SOX said hold my beer to NDX, declining 17%, its 4th worst week ever. We summed the dynamic earlier this week: "Consensus has gone from explosive multi-generational cycle at the end of June to "we're speed-running the semis downcycle" in a matter of 4 months. Quite unprecedented."

AI Semis headwinds

The headwinds facing the AI trade haven’t been helped by macro with a hawkish fed set to tighten in Dec (and another 25bps by March), and financial conditions already having tightened by 100-150bps, which in the past have come along with 10-20% SPX drawdowns. Worries about Iran keeping Brent bid aren’t helping. The good news: yields have started falling and bulls are hoping that Nov data cools and Fed is at peak hawkishness, but Nov’s CPI release isn’t until 12/10 so unclear if we get much movement before that.

Two things seem necessary to stop the market bleeding (a bounce not withstanding): 1) something that looks like a Fed pivot or 2) the headwinds facing the AI trade to begin to abate.

The latter won’t be easy as the list of things dogging the AI trade seem to grow bigger every week.

Let’s quickly go over the (non-exhaustive) list of narrative headwinds:

  • Questions around demand and the next great use-case after coding. Dario himself said last week that coding is “starting to get more mature” and bigger markets he named (science, life sciences, robotics) are years out. Cyber has potential to be the next fastest growth vector, but questions remain. Still we’re hearing encouraging positive signs: last week we called out some positive rumblings and this week we a friend passed along news that a large Tier 1 bank signed a 9-figure cyber deal with OAI. Pushback has been that the cyber market just isn’t big enough, but we’d note that the coding tools market was only a $25B market in 2024 and AI coding blew past that, so we’re open-minded.
  • Efficiency at both the hardware and model level could drive less demand for chips
  • Pricing pressure & model commoditization as prices get cut fast. A couple very recent examples from this week of pricing pressure at the frontier: GOOGL's Neon is at $3/$15 vs $4/$20 for Opus 5.5, and OpenAI is already discounting 6.2.
  • Enterprises could slow AI spend growth in 2027. FUNDA had a good enterprise survey out last week showing 62% expect slower growth and 9% flat or down next year. Half of those slowing cite budget caps and ROI gating, 39% engineering cost optimization, a third the high base, and 27% current use cases saturating while new ones are slow to scale.

  • Semis estimate revisions have peaked. In the three months to July, the typical name's 2027 EPS estimate went up ~18%; in the latest three months it went up ~9%, and about a third of names have been cut since 10/31. The SOX topped on 6/22, right when the raises stopped getting bigger. With financing tighter, demand questions, the Fed hiking and 2028 builds running into power and politics, it's hard to see the raises speeding back up anytime soon.

  • Jensen’s departure adds fuel to the fire: Without Jensen, we likely get a more conservative NVDA. He bankrolled a lot of the neocloud buildout, ran the key supplier relationships and pushed hardest on the technology edge, and all of that is now in question just as Southeast Asian capacity that was rented to Chinese firms could come back onto the market.
  • Concerns over the pace of the DC buildout given Democrats are in power.
  • Not enough powered shells for 2028’s chips.
  • Does Anthropic suck flows from semis? Investors I talk to are already internalizing that labs can do well while semis lose: if lab revenue stalls, capex falls even harder, and if it booms, the labs need fewer chips per dollar of revenue (ANT's revenue is up ~45x in six quarters on only ~10x more compute). In addition, scarcity let NVDA and memory charge premium prices. As supply catches up, compute cost per token falls, meaning lab margins could widen while chip margins shrink.

Does that mean that investor $’s shift to ANT? Investors seem to already think the trade between semis and labs has already decoupled (it’s obviously still somewhat coupled, but we think ANT has much better upside skew than semis at the moment)

  • Capex growth fades fast into 2028. On Street estimates, which are increasingly in question, ‘28 capex grows just 14% vs. ~50% in ‘27 and 88% in ‘26. Hard to get much torque out of semis with capex growth decelerating this sharply:

  • Recursive self-improvement (RSI), good or bad? Net good for chips near term since it requires a lot more training compute, but the risk is more efficiency gains we’ve already started to witness
  • Diffusion lags capabilities: Hey, Anthropic solved Riemann! But does that help AI adoption at all? Unclear.
  • OpenAI wobbling (at least that’s investor perception): Company is burning cash flow, couldn’t raise as much $ as they wanted recently and had to lower valuation, has lost share since Opus 5.5, GPT 6.2 benchmarked well but drew harsh early reviews and Altman conceded there's "more work ahead of us on making the models nice to work with." The semis ecosystem needs both labs to thrive to hit estimates. It doesn’t help that GOOGL is now back at the frontier following their Neon launch.

  • ROI concerns
  • VC money meets a hiking Fed. 80% of the top 1% of AI spenders on Ramp are VC-backed vs 13% of the median spender (Apollo). Last cycle VC dollars fell 34% in 2022 and 30% in 2023 during fed hiking cycle. And Jensen is gone as well: NVDA was roughly ~15-16% of 2026 late-stage VC dollars.

The good news for the AI trade: enterprises are still ramping spend and token growth is still climbing, so adoption is still very 'early.' GOOGL: Can inroads in coding make up for search slowdown?

Google surprised us and almost everyone else this week by releasing Neon, a very competitive model for coding. We passed along the benchmarks this week, putting it at or better than Opus 5.5 while being 25% cheaper. We liked Theo’s review of the model here. His takeaway is not only that Neon is competitive with Opus on benchmarks, but more importantly it surpasses almost all other models on coherence.

His argument is that we’ve reached a point where leading AI models are already extraordinarily intelligent. Increasingly, the biggest differentiator isn’t what they can theoretically solve, but how reliably, consistently, and naturally they turn that intelligence into useful work (coherence). Better coherence means less supervision, greater trust, and more dependable execution, with potentially significant implications for AI adoption, diffusion, retention, and monetization.

Some key quotes from Theo’s review (keep in mind, he is typically a big Google skeptic):

This might be my favorite model right now. I don’t know where this came from. Google just out of nowhere cooked something that is a generational leap that is also smaller and cheaper and generously subsidized in their Gemini subscription plans.

This is the first real Opus 5.5 competitor, and it’s a very, very real competitor. I have pushed this model to its absolute limits, and honestly, it has been hard to find them.

This is a phenomenal model... I cannot believe that Google has a model that I would currently prefer to everything OpenAI has out, but they do.

Specifically, I find Gemini 4 Neon to be more coherent than any Anthropic model, comically more coherent than any OpenAI model. But more importantly, now that I’ve really pushed it for code work in particular, I find that Gemini 4 Neon is also more capable.

By coherent, I mean a few things. I mean, I find it easier to read and understand. I find it stays on task better. It understands me better. It completes work better. It feels more like a collaborative partner. And when it works with itself, it’s also more collaborative and thoughtful as well.

It is incredibly capable. It is incredibly coherent. It takes all the things I loved about Muse Wonderful, turns them up a tiny bit, and then takes the capabilities of Astra and other models, minus the 3D, and makes them more coherent over long horizons and somehow delivers solutions faster.

They should have named this model Gemini 5. This is a generational gap, in particular on the coherence side.

The new frontier is not the capability frontier. The new frontier is coherency. And this is where OpenAI is currently behind some of the Chinese labs.

They made a model that is smart enough to do real work, is good enough at staying focused in long-running, long-horizon tasks to complete really hard work and is coherent enough to stay on task and explain what it is doing consistently. It’s exceptional. It’s a phenomenal model.

The engineers at Google in DeepMind loved using this model so much that they begged and pleaded to rush it out because they wanted people to stop hating Gemini models so much

I have been struggling to convince the best AI and agent dev people I know to try this model because they were so burned by Argan and Carbon. Every time they do, they’re like, ‘Wait, holy shit This is a generational gap.’”

Outside of search fears, we’ve continued to really like the GOOGL narrative given its moat: TPUs, cloud, frontier models, attractive economics and pricing power at scale, massive distribution, and one of the strongest balance sheets among hyperscalers with the stock trading at less than 15x ‘28 EPS.

A few weeks ago, we dove into AI Overviews, coming to the conclusion that while 2027 search growth could be near the mid single digits, if GOOGL figures out how to monetize AI queries at just a fraction of what search is monetized at, ‘28 could see accelerating search growth. A couple pieces of good news on this front:

  1. Buyside consensus has already come down significantly for Q1 and Q2 search growth with most thinking somewhere around

6% and some on the sell side like Wells Fargo sub 5% in Q1. In Q4, search is tracking about ~2ppts below street (~8.5% vs. 10.2%) according to 3p data. Buyside already there around 8%.

  1. According to

AWR, AI Overviews CTR surpassed regular search results on mobile for the first time ever in October (41.5% vs. 36.3% for the #1 link), and on desktop for the first time since Dec 2025 (26.3% vs. 24.3%). That suggests cannibalization fears may be overdone, since people still click through after reading an AI Overview. But it’s one month, and it doesn’t cover AI Mode.

We still have issues getting over the fact search could possibly hit MSD growth in the first part of 2027. Given Neon’s reception, and to help us get over this fear, we asked ourselves: how much coding market share would be needed to make up for this search shortfall?

1ppt of ‘27 search growth is about $2.5B. Assuming the coding market is $75B currently and goes to $120B next year, GOOGL needs 2-3ppts of coding share to make up for every 1ppt of search growth short fall. Not a Herculean task.

We still have trouble getting over the decelerating search growth in ‘27, but the setup here seems a lot better than it was a month ago and we continue to think downside is limited. Not involved yet, but getting more interested, especially near $250…
