SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a UK-based cooling-equipment executive with extensive industry experience, discussed data center demand, supply- chain bottlenecks, cooling technology shifts, and pricing. Recent business was slightly better than expected, though market activity was uneven. EMEA projects faced redesigns as customers adapted specifications to available components; activity picked up again in November.

Demand remains heavily focused on AI infrastructure and newer cloud operators, while traditional colocation activity appeared quieter in the expert’s experience. Projects are growing dramatically in scale, with some European proposals exceeding a gigawatt and delivery schedules extending several years.

Key Insights

Supply is now a greater constraint than manufacturing capacity. Fan availability is the most acute issue, with compressors also severely constrained. Some manufacturers cannot fully use their production capacity because parts are unavailable. Chiller lead times have stretched sharply. Turbocor compressor availability has deteriorated quickly, prompting customers to take alternative compressor technologies more seriously. Liquid cooling is becoming central to system design. The expert expects larger, more customized CDUs and greater use of integrated liquid-cooling solutions. The company is prioritizing CDU projects, then adding air-side equipment. European growth could accelerate. The expert suggested that restrictions or reduced construction in the US could redirect demand and supply-chain capacity toward Europe, especially markets with available low-carbon power. This was presented as a possibility, not a firm forecast. Prices are rising, but margins may not improve. Cost increases are being passed through unevenly, and the expert expects margins to remain flat or face some pressure as companies balance higher costs against competitive project bids. Alternative suppliers present both opportunity and risk. New sources may ease shortages, but qualification, reliability, warranty, and long-term support remain concerns.
