Oracle Projects Update – Lights Off at Oracle’s Lighthouse

Author: Ellie Holbrook, Reyk Knuhtsen

Date: Monday, November 9, 2026 (published 2026/11/09 22:31:03 Taipei)

We now see one of Oracle’s four Stargate sites due to start operations in 2027 missing Oracle’s planned

dates: Lighthouse in Port Washington, Wisconsin slips to late 2028/early 2029. Project Jupiter in Doña

Ana County, New Mexico can still make 1H2027 if the air permit is approved in 1Q2027. We believe the

Saline Township site in Michigan and the first phase of the Shackelford site in Texas are still on track for

2027 delivery, although Shackelford’s roughly 1GW expansion is held by the Texas air permit pause,

which we now expect to last until the legislative session ends on May 31, 2027. We also believe that

trucked gas supplied Oracle’s Bloom Energy fuel cells in Utah.

If you want to fully catch up on our Project Jupiter coverage, our May and June notes on the site cover

its earlier gas-supply problems, while our note in March details the project’s first round of air permitting

woes.

Executive Summary

Lighthouse (Port Washington, WI) slips to 3Q2028 first commercial AI operations, ~3-4 quarters later

than the 2H2027 guidance, with the load ramping into 1Q2029. Let’s walk through the timeline.

The permanent grid connection is contingent on multiple things. The first is substation “Decker,”

which is waiting on PSC to accept the application as complete, a decision within 180 days, and a

preliminary schedule of +31 days to start construction, +7 months of construction. The review can

be shorter than 180 days, but we assume six months for now; similar previous cases took 7.5mo–

1yr. The earliest for Decker is then February/March 2028, but is still not sufficient until load

smoothing arrives in June 2028.

The load “can operate up to the point that ATC criteria is exceeded,” which limits load

swings to 25MW in any 5 second period. Beyond this, a load-smoothing device needs to be

in place.

Enter E-STATCOM, a 600MW 5-second supercapacitor-based device at Decker that absorbs

those swings, with an in-service date of June 30, 2028 (according to MISO+ATC), and ATC

states “The end-use customer intends to align the load ramp with the E-STATCOM in-

service window.”

However, Decker is not enough for the full load alone. Connecting Decker only to the existing

Edgewater–Saukville 345kV line “is not viable.” The load “will require additional 345 kV

transmission sources to serve it reliably”

ATC’s preferred combination is North West plus South East, subject to the PSC’s route selection,

however, upgrading the transmission lines takes 9 months, and only begins after the South East

work is done.

On South East, the Adell substation and Adell–Holland 138kV lines are expected to take ~8

months of construction, starting around the same time as Decker. Not much additional time

added.

However, once the South East work is completed, the transmission lines conversion+rebuild

begins and takes 9 months. This places final transmission work completion around

~1Q2029.

(Onsite gen is not likely at the moment, as Oracle stated on their 1QFY27 earnings call that “In

Wisconsin, we’re not doing on-site generation.”)

We believe Oracle is on the hook for power

ATC states that E-STATCOM is paid for by OACS (Oracle), and for the substantial transmission

and distribution work, OACS signed a Payment and Cancellation agreement, “that obligates

OACS to reimburse Wisconsin Electric for all Project costs if the Project is terminated or

cancelled.”

If Wisconsin is take-or-pay, and Oracle is responsible for delayed power, there may be

another instance of Oracle paying rent on a non-powered datacenter.

Positive Scenarios for Lighthouse:

The South East design includes a temporary 345/138kV transformer at Cedarsauk/Saukville

intended to support customer load before full-project completion. This documents staged-service

intent, but the associated operating MW and timing are not disclosed.

Project Jupiter (Doña Ana County, NM) moves forward to 2Q2027 from 2029, and can meet Oracle’s

1H2027 guidance, if the air permit for the Bloom Energy fuel cells is granted in 1Q2027.

Oracle’s latest guidance still states 1H2027 delivery. We brought the start forward because the

Green Chile pipeline’s federal review now plans for first gas by mid-2027, while we also

discovered a local gas line can feed the first 100MW if the FERC decision is delayed. This leaves

the Bloom fuel cell air permit as the main constraint. A hearing on the permit planned for

December 7 was vacated on November 6 at the request of YGI and NMED, and a new date for a

hearing has yet to be decided. The exact timeline is up in the air, but NMED already issued a draft

permit for the plant in July, and the hearing officer and the Secretary can shorten the post-hearing

deadlines, so the permit could be approved in 1Q2027.

YGI’s sworn statement allows three months from approval to the first 100MW of generation, fed

by the local gas line, which gives first power in 2Q2027, within Oracle’s 1H2027 guidance.

The ramp beyond the first 100MW needs the Green Chile pipeline and start-up power, for which

YGI has only an unsigned proposal from El Paso Electric.

Meeting 1Q2027 for air permit approval requires every step of the assessment to go as fast as

possible.

Shackelford (TX): the first 420MW phase is on time, but the additional 1GW timeline is tied to the

Texas permit pause.

The first 709MW of VoltaGrid gas engines (~420MW IT) is permitted with equipment already on

site, and the first building completes 1Q2027, so it is in line with Oracle’s 1H2027 start date. The

air permit expanding the site to 2,582MW (+1GW~) is stuck on Texas Governor Greg Abbott’s

permit pause. The permit waits for the legislative session that ends on May 31, 2027.

However, there is potential to stage all the VoltaGrid engines on its parcel next door and

prep them for a permit approval after the session, which may not substantially delay the

completion timeline.

Saline Township (MI): end of 2027.

The project is fully approved but the two 345kV stations that feed the site, built by ITC

Transmission, are scheduled for November 30, 2027. We are moving first grid power to the end of

2027 and the 974MW IT ramp through 2028, a delay of two quarters compared with our previous

expectations. Both Oracle and DTE, the local utility, now guide for around the end of 2027.

Utah: evidence that Oracle’s Bloom fuel cells ran on trucked CNG.

We understand trucked compressed natural gas first supplied Oracle’s Bloom fuel cells at Novva’s

West Jordan campus and now Novva’s Jenbacher engines, the first Bloom deployment we have

seen on non-pipeline gas.

Wisconsin – Lights off at Lighthouse until 3Q2028

Construction is advancing at the ~900MW IT Lighthouse campus in Port Washington, north of

Milwaukee, developed by Vantage Data Centers. Vantage’s construction update says two of the four

buildings are enclosed and the other two are in structural construction. What is lagging behind is the

power supply.

On September 15, 2025, We Energies, the Milwaukee utility owned by WEC Energy Group, sent the

load request for Oracle America Cloud Services, the Oracle subsidiary buying the power, to American

Transmission Company (ATC), which owns and builds the high-voltage grid across most of Wisconsin.

ATC’s filing to MISO, the regional grid operator, two days later details the cost and timeline at the time:

A site this size can be connected to the grid either through the utility’s local distribution network or

through a new high-voltage transmission connection.

We Energies’ testimony to the PSC calls a connection through its distribution network ‘impractical’:

So ATC must build the Decker 345kV substation, two transmission lines and upgrades to existing power

infrastructure. ATC’s current application puts the project at $2.48B to $2.72B depending on the route, of

which $1.1B is the stabilizer, all paid by Oracle America Cloud Services apart from a small regionally

shared piece. For the transmission and distribution work OACS signed a Payment and Cancellation

Agreement that obligates it to reimburse We Energies for all project costs if the project is cancelled, and

posted the security on August 9, 2026; the matching agreement for the stabilizer is still to be signed. The

whole project needs to be approved by Wisconsin’s Public Service Commission (PSC).

But the PSC closed ATC’s first application on September 10 after ATC made substantial changes to the

project, including route modifications, temporary bypass lines added late, changes to the scope of line

upgrades and revised costs, although the order makes no finding on the merits of the project itself:

ATC refiled its application on September 14 with the stabilizing device included, but on October 19 the

PSC ruled the refiling incomplete and listed 35 missing items, including the second load request, which

covers expansion toward 3.5GW in the 2030s, along with questions on the stabilizing device:

ATC answered on the afternoon of November 6 with 78 filings, including a revised application. The PSC

has not yet said whether the application is complete. It took 31 days on the first application, so we

estimate early December for the decision on its completeness to come through.

Another wrinkle in the plan is that Woodview Lane, a small 138kV project, must be completed before

ATC can convert an existing line to 345kV for the Lighthouse connection. ATC had planned to speed up

the timeline from nine months to six at an extra $3.1M, but on November 2 told the PSC it no longer

needs to, “Due to a change in the schedule for the Ozaukee Project”, ATC’s name for the Lighthouse

transmission project. If Woodview construction starts in January (as planned by ATC) and then finishes

around October 2027, this leaves no room for the December 2027 in-service date in ATC’s original

request.

The PSC review – comparable reviews took 232 to 355 days

The PSC then has 180 days to decide, plus one 180-day extension. It used the extension on this project’s

first application and in its last two comparable cases: Racine Western Feed, 232 days from acceptance to

signed order, and Grid Forward Central Wisconsin, 355 days with a full environmental impact statement.

After the PSC issues the written order granting ATC the certificate to build, ATC’s schedule puts the start

of construction 31 days later. ATC then estimates about seven months on the line connections into the

Decker substation and says the substation itself takes most of the 17-month construction program. ATC

also notes that Oracle’s load (OACS, Oracle America Cloud Services) may be energized before the

whole program is finished, but gives no date for that. Assuming the PSC accepts the application on

December 7, the three review paths give these dates:

The Decker connections therefore arrive between February and July 2028 depending on the review path.

But there’s another hurdle to overcome before the site can reach first power in earnest… The grid

stabilizer.

The grid stabilizer

ATC’s planning rules limit how fast a customer’s load may change, to protect nearby generators and

equipment. ATC’s assessment found the Lighthouse load would breach the rules and that the device ‘is

required to be in service when load variability exceeds ATC Planning Criteria’, 25MW in any five

seconds:

The solution is an E-STATCOM, a supercapacitor-based device at the Decker substation that absorbs or

supplies up to 600MW for five seconds to smooth the swings. ATC asked MISO to approve it on

September 30 with a requested in-service date of June 30, 2028, and MISO staff’s November 3

recommendation letter gives the same date:

Then the PSC asked, in item 1.35 of its October 19 letter, whether the E-STATCOM must be in service

before the large load. ATC answered on November 6:

So the campus may draw some power before the stabilizer, but only within the 25MW-per-five-seconds

limit, which AI training load exceeds, so Oracle’s ramp-up at the site depends on the stabilizer’s start

date.

How firm is June 2028?

ATC told the PSC on November 6 that it has chosen Hitachi Energy to supply the stabilizer, that

the contracts to build and operate it were expected in early November, and that the lead time is

‘approximately 15 to 18 months, measured from the date construction starts to the date the E-

STATCOM is in service’. ATC also said that this timeline estimate is subject to change.

ATC’s request to MISO gives 22 months from the start of major spending, which from September

2026 lands in July 2028. By that timeline, a June 2028 start needs construction to commence by

early 2027.

Oracle could in principle hold a first building within the swing limit itself, with batteries behind

the meter or chip-level power smoothing. But ATC’s assessment says ATC and Oracle ‘determined

that the E-STATCOM is the only viable and commercially available solution for this proposed

load’, and lists Oracle as paying the $1.1B for the device.

We expect the PSC to accept the application in December and order within its 180-day limit in

June 2027, ATC to have the Decker connections built by February 2028, and the campus to start

drawing power once the stabilizer is in service on June 30, 2028. That puts first power in 3Q2028

and the full 900MW campus in 1Q2029.

Temporary power is enough for site testing, not for running a building

Vantage told the Ozaukee Press on September 23 that building handovers would start at the end of 2027,

and its FAQ puts all four buildings in operation in 2028.

Vantage’s interim service agreement with We Energies, filed with the PSC in March 2026 with its dates

redacted, covers only the early phase:

Drawings show 20MVA of temporary utility service stepped down through a 10MVA transformer,

enough for roughly 7 to 15MW of IT depending on the power factor and the facility-to-IT ratio. A

separate switchgear slot is labelled for a future VoltaGrid connection.

The diagram below is the only sign of any onsite gas plan: we found no gas engine air permit application

or gas pipeline to support one, and Oracle’s co-CEO, Clay Magouyrk, said on the September 10 earnings

call that ‘in Wisconsin, we’re not doing on-site generation’.

ATC’s planning study, filed as Appendix D to the application, says the same about any temporary

supply: ‘Due to the size of the load, there is no viable temporary solution to serve the load’ (PSC REF

567681, section 9.1).

New Mexico – Project Jupiter finds its gas supply but awaits the

Bloom air permit

Project Jupiter is a four-building Oracle campus in Doña Ana County, developed by BorderPlex Digital

Assets and STACK Infrastructure. Yucca Growth Infrastructure (YGI) will own the on-site power plant,

with up to 2,462MW of installed Bloom fuel cells and a proposed 1,600MW operating cap.

Our May and June notes put first power in 2029 because of delays to the Green Chile pipeline, the gas

lateral proposed by Transwestern, an Energy Transfer company. YGI has since identified an existing

New Mexico Gas Company (NMGC) line for the first 100MW, which avoids waiting for Green Chile’s

FERC approval. We believe the air permit for the Bloom fuel cells is now the main hurdle to first power,

and we put first generation in 2Q2027, on a 1Q2027 air permit.

The latest satellite imagery shows the four datacenter buildings under construction and the power-plant

parcel graded with roads in and no fuel cell installations visible:

As part of a filing to defeat a motion to dismiss the air permit application for the fuel cells, YGI

disclosed a pipeline on October 19 in a sworn statement by Jacques Greyling, its contact for fuel supply

and construction:

The declaration describes supply through February 2027, so availability for a later commissioning date

remains an assumption.

County land records show YGI granted NMGC a pipeline easement over part of the site on June 25,

2026, but did not disclose any delivery volumes, term or price. A July 2026 sworn declaration by an

Oracle vice president in the county bond case says Oracle and YGI signed gas supply, power purchase,

option and construction agreements that are ‘contingent on certain activities commencing by December

2026’, without saying which activities or who supplies the gas.

The NMGC line cannot fuel the plant at full capacity. At the proposed 1,600MW cap, YGI estimates

consumption of 299 million cubic feet a day (MMcf/d), roughly 12 times the small line’s cited capacity.

Scaling to full capacity still requires the Green Chile pipeline.

Air permit issues return

Last month YGI filed a redesign of the plant in its air permit application, proposing a 1,600MW

operating cap and fewer, taller exhaust stacks, which Oracle says would mean permitted emissions fall

about 35%. NMED had already issued a draft permit for the original design on July 29. The redesign

means the New Mexico Environment Department (NMED) must re-run its air modeling for the smaller

plant and issue a revised draft permit before a hearing can be held.

The Air Quality Control Act gave NMED until November 23, 2026 to grant or deny the air permit. But a

hearing has to come before any decision, and the hearing was set for December 7. So this deadline fell

two weeks before the earliest point at which NMED could make a decision.

If nothing changed, NMED would have to deny the permit on November 23 because it ran out of time.

This then prompted YGI to waive the deadline on November 3, removing the November 23 requirement.

On the same day YGI and NMED asked to cancel the December 7 hearing because NMED still has to

redo its air modeling for the smaller 1,600MW design YGI filed in October. NMED said in the motion

that it will not seek denial or dismissal unless it reaches the finding the Act requires:

On November 6 the hearing officer cancelled the hearing. No new date will be set until NMED files a

revised draft permit. The record is updated weekly and there are status conferences every two weeks

from November 19. This means the timeline now depends on how long NMED’s air modeling takes:

An air permit in 1Q2027 needs NMED to file its revised draft permit by mid-December, which assumes

about six weeks for the modeling runs it could not start until YGI answered its questions on November

  1. The 30-day public notice then puts the hearing in late January, and the hearing officer and the

Secretary would have to shorten the post-hearing steps, which on their default deadlines take about 105

days: 30 days for closing arguments after the transcript, 30 for the hearing officer’s report, 15 for

comments and 30 for the final order. On those defaults, a January hearing gives a decision in May; a

decision by the end of March needs them cut roughly in half.

Three things could push the decision into the second half of 2027: NMED gave no estimate on

November 4 of how long its review will take, one intervenor has asked for a further 90-day extension,

which NMED and YGI oppose, and a new governor takes office on January 1 and chooses the NMED

secretary who signs the final order. A draft permit in February instead of December puts the hearing in

April and the decision between August and October.

YGI allows three months from approval to the first 100MW, fed by the NMGC line. A 1Q2027 permit

gives first generation in 2Q2027, inside Oracle’s 1H2027 guidance; a decision between August and

October gives 4Q2027 to 1Q2028. Start-up power is the one open item that could add time: YGI has a

proposal from El Paso Electric but no signed agreement. A denial and appeal would take longer:

The plant also needs power for commissioning. YGI told NMED on October 26 that it has a proposal

from El Paso Electric for commissioning and black-start power but no signed agreement. Since then the

county has voted to notify the Project Jupiter companies of a potential breach of their industrial revenue

bond agreements over the proposed power plan.

We understand Oracle sent a force majeure notice to the Project Jupiter developer in September citing

power delays, seeking to push back the start of rent by up to three years; STACK said its financial

commitments were unchanged.

Green Chile – gas volumes depend on pipeline approvals

The plant’s 299MMcf/d requirement is about 290,000 dekatherms a day (Dth/d) at the tariff’s heating

value. Green Chile’s contracted deliveries first exceed that at the 317,000Dth/d step, about a year after

first gas. With our June 2027 first-gas assumption, that covers the capped plant around mid-2028. Full

contracted deliveries reach 400,000Dth/d after 17.5 months. With the first 100MW running on the

NMGC line from June 2027, the ramp after that follows Green Chile’s contracted steps rather than

Bloom’s install rate of 80 to 100MW a month: roughly 600 to 750MW of generation by the end of 2027

and the full 1,600MW cap around mid-2028, when deliveries reach the 317,000Dth/d step:

We expect first gas in June 2027, with May possible, against Transwestern’s February 1, 2027 estimate.

Construction needs a Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) certificate, a Bureau of Land

Management (BLM) right-of-way grant and FERC’s notice to proceed. Comments on the environmental

assessment closed October 5; on comparable orders the certificate comes in December, the grant and

notice by February, then a 17-week build. A spring grant pushes first gas to 3Q2027. Nothing has been

filed on the FERC docket since October 19.

Oracle’s Green Chile Ventures holds the full 400,000Dth/d contract for 17.5 years, with no termination

right for a plant air permit delay. Bloom has allocated about 150MW to the first phases.

Texas – Shackelford’s first phase is on time, the expansion waits

on the pause

The first 709MW of VoltaGrid engines at Vantage’s Shackelford County campus, about 420MW of IT,

has been permitted since July 2025 and is being tested, with the first building due to complete in

February 2027 on the state licensing schedule. The air permit expanding the site to 2,582MW (TCEQ

182467, replacing the 709MW air permit) cleared review by the Texas Commission on Environmental

Quality (TCEQ) on October 23 with zero comments and zero hearing requests, and waits on Abbott’s

pause and Hinojosa’s promised moratorium on datacenter permits, which we covered on November 4.

Abbott leaves office on January 19. His September 21 pause runs until ERCOT finishes its review of the

large-load applicants, and Governor-elect Hinojosa, who called the pause a stunt, promised on October

22 her own moratorium on ‘all data center approvals until laws can be passed by the Legislature to

protect Texans’. We therefore expect the expansion permit after the session ends on May 31, 2027, with

December possible only if Abbott lifts the pause before he leaves. The expansion adds roughly 1.1GW

of IT at the same engine-to-IT ratio as the first phase. Engines can be staged on VoltaGrid’s own 140-

acre parcel nearby but not installed before the air permit. The buildings complete from February 2027

through May 2028, so a permit by mid-2027 still lets VoltaGrid install the first 700MW of the expansion

through the second half, which keeps our 4Q2027 date for the next 700MW IT.

Michigan – Saline Township

The Michigan campus in Saline Township, developed by Related Digital, has all the required permits,

with construction underway.

The grid connection, though, is two new 345kV stations built by ITC Transmission, which owns the

high-voltage grid in DTE’s territory, and ITC’s schedule filed with MISO puts them in service on

November 30, 2027; but DTE’s own substation has no published start date. The contract ramp runs from

January 2027 to the full 1,383MW on December 1, 2027, but Oracle may defer the steps by up to 12

months, and DTE’s rate-case forecast assumes the load starts in January 2028. So first grid power comes

at the end of 2027, in line with Oracle’s planned 2H2027 start, and the ramp to 974MW of IT runs

through 2028, about two quarters later than our model previously.

Utah – Oracle’s Bloom fuel cells appear to run on trucked CNG

We understand trucked compressed natural gas (CNG) first supplied Oracle’s Bloom fuel cells at

Novva’s West Jordan campus in Utah, where Oracle is the tenant, and now Novva’s Jenbacher gas

engines.

Oracle’s July 2025 registration with Utah’s air regulator covers up to 54.6MW of Bloom fuel cells at

Novva West Jordan, as temporary power until Novva’s 72 Jenbacher engines arrive:

The application assumed up to 12 months of fuel cell use and engines arriving by June 2026, which are

now there and operating.

Bloom said on October 22 that it had delivered power to Oracle’s Utah site in under two months, while

Certarus said on May 14 that its new hub southeast of Salt Lake City was the primary gas supplier for a

60MW hyperscale datacenter in the area, due to start operations in early summer 2026.

The 60MW figure is consistent with the first 17 of Novva’s 72 Jenbacher engines, about 56MW, which

Utah air-quality records show stack-tested at full load between February and April 2026, with the rest

scheduled through October. So CNG is likely also feeding the engines until the enlarged gas main is

ready.

The resource plan of Enbridge Gas Utah, the gas utility, has an enlargement of a West Jordan gas main,

part of it paid by one customer, due by fall 2026, and says the large requests on that side of the valley are

largely datacenters and power generation:

What to watch

Wisconsin: the PSC’s completeness ruling on ATC’s November 6 answers, which starts the 180-

day review, and whether ATC files the stabilizer construction and service agreements it expected

in early November.

New Mexico: the November 19 status conference, NMED’s weekly record supplements, its

revised draft permit and a new hearing date; FERC’s Green Chile certificate, then the BLM grant.

Texas: the datacenter permit pause after Governor-elect Hinojosa takes office on January 19, and

the legislative session that ends May 31.

Michigan: DTE’s first quarterly report on the Oracle load, due in the MPSC docket by December

31, and MISO’s next status update on ITC’s stations.
