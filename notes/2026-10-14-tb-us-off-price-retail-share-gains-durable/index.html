Agenda

  • US off-price retail demand outlook, younger shopper adoption and durability of trade-down customer spending
  • Ross (NASDAQ: ROST) vs Burlington (NYSE: BURL) – expansion runway, new store sales maturation, localised merchandising and cannibalisation risks
  • Competitive outlook – market share opportunities across Ross, Burlington and TJX (NYSE: TJX), category mix shifts and sourcing benefits from department store contraction

Key Insights

  • Expert believes off-price demand should remain structurally durable beyond the current macro cycle, as trade-down shoppers increasingly convert into repeat customers and younger consumers normalise treasure-hunt shopping
  • Ross should remain the strongest near-term market share gainer, with growth driven by Northeast US expansion and greater emphasis on shoes, accessories and home rather than relying on apparel
  • Although expert believes Burlington remains c7-10 years behind Ross in its off-price evolution, they note leadership is increasingly replicating the Ross playbook and expect Burlington to surpass Ross within 5-7 years
  • New store openings, c100 annually for both Ross and Burlington, typically outperform chain comps for three years, creating a repeatable sales growth mechanism embedded in their expansion models
  • Fewer department stores actually increase goods availability for off-price, as vendors must still hit sales targets and redirect inventory elsewhere to compensate
