原标题：Digital Realty – A Record Backlog Meets a Power-constrained Build Cycle

#datacenter #power #capex

Third Bridge · 2026-10-26

核心摘要

  1. 专家认为行业存在过度建设，预计到2030年适度回归正常。
  2. 柴油发电机交期长达4年，是建设瓶颈之一。
  3. EMEA地区建设成本约1760万美元/MW。
  4. 伦敦数据中心出租率约72%，部分市场供给已经偏多。
  5. Digital Realty有310个数据中心，运营约3.1GW、在建1.4GW、可建约8.5GW，覆盖50多个城市；EMEA约978MW IT负载、年化租金约18.4亿美元。
  6. 阿姆斯特丹出租率降到约79.7%，企业续约时缩减租赁，留下不适合AI高密度的“困住空间”。

Analyst-led • 19 Oct 2026

Companies: EQIX US, DLR US, AMZN US, MSFT US

Vincent Kelly (VK) Adjunct Lecturer- Infrastructure & Sustainability, MA Data Centre Leadership & Management - Self Employed at Anglia Ruskin University

Dean Tsopanides (DT) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • Trends and developments impacting Digital Realty (NYSE: DLR) and the broader data centre REIT operating environment
  • Demand outlook following sub-1MW and interconnection bookings reaching a third consecutive record, signalling enterprise AI inference shifting towards connected colocation
  • Market constraints and challenges
  • Mid-to-long-term outlook

Key Insights

  • Digital Realty's connectivity ownership is its core competitive moat – carrier-neutral hubs position it better than hyperscalers for AI inference distribution, which demands proximity to dense urban populations
  • Expert posits that the industry is overbuilding capacity and expects moderate normalisation or levelling out through 2030 rather than a sharp correction. His reasoning assumes that AI firms are likely unable to sustain capital development as ROI models remain unproven, triggering consolidation and bank-led restructuring
  • Diesel generator lead times are currently four years minimum, with Cummins' pipelines fully booked, meaning data centre builds ordered today won't receive critical backup power infrastructure until 2030, highlighting challenges in procurement supply chains
  • EMEA data centre costs run cUSD 17.6m per megawatt vs USD 15m-16m in the Americas, driven by higher land costs, stricter regulation, elevated labour and GDPR compliance requirements
  • Falling EMEA occupancy rates – London at 72% and Amsterdam dropping to 79.7% – reflect enterprises downsizing leases at renewal, creating "trapped space" in rooms too small for new AI-density requirements
