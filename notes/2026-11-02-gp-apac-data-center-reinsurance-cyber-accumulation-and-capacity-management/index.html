SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a risk analytics professional with extensive actuarial and modeling experience, discussed how reinsurers can assess growing data center exposures across the Asia-Pacific region. The central challenge is accumulation: one cyber or infrastructure event may affect operators, tenants, and their customers across cyber, property, business interruption, and technology liability policies.

The discussion covered exposure mapping, geographic diversification, capital modeling, treaty wording, claims liquidity, and ways to expand capacity without taking on poorly measured correlated risk.

Key Insights

Geographic spread alone does not ensure diversification. Shared cloud platforms, vendors, power, cooling, and network dependencies may connect facilities across borders. The expert sees contract clarity as a stronger immediate capacity driver than improved dependency data. Clear event definitions, aggregation periods, coverage boundaries, and limits can directly constrain losses. Facility-level data is essential. Missing site coordinates, capacity, tenant information, or dependency records may make an otherwise attractive risk unacceptable. The expert recommends comparing models using consistent scenarios, using ranges rather than point estimates, and applying more conservative assumptions to tail losses. Growth should slow when severe-scenario net losses approach board limits or retained exposure rises beyond appetite.

Facultative reinsurance

Slower and more expensive, with separate wording that may complicate recoveries.

Retrocession

Helps transfer cross-line or event-level accumulation and protect capital.

Adds another recovery layer and generally follows underlying underwriting terms.

Parametric and insurance- linked solutions

Can add capacity and provide faster liquidity for defined outages.

Basis risk limits usefulness for physical damage, liability, long-tail interruption, and losses tied to incident-specific findings.
