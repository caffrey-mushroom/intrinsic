SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed povetacicept’s RAINIER and RUBY studies within the expanding IgA nephropathy treatment market. The expert manages approximately 100–120 patients with the disease and increasingly uses APRIL or combined BAFF/APRIL inhibitors as initial disease-modifying therapy.

The expert expects povetacicept to deliver efficacy and tolerability broadly comparable with atacicept and sibeprenlimab. Kidney- function preservation was considered more clinically important than reductions in proteinuria, hematuria, or pathogenic IgA biomarkers.

Key Insights

APRIL-pathway therapies were characterized as transformative because trials have shown stabilization or slight improvement in kidney function rather than merely slower deterioration. Povetacicept is expected to produce positive kidney-function results consistent with a class-wide effect, although cross-trial superiority claims would be unreliable. Selective APRIL inhibition currently appears as effective as combined BAFF/APRIL inhibition. No evidence yet establishes a meaningful advantage from BAFF blockade. A proteinuria-only approval would leave povetacicept at a mild-to-moderate disadvantage versus agents with longer randomized kidney-function data. Physicians are unlikely to switch stable, responding patients. Earlier approval, established reimbursement processes, and monthly rather than weekly dosing can strengthen adoption. The most important unanswered question is which patients fail to respond. Hematuria, recent biopsy findings, disease severity, and inflammatory activity warrant further subgroup analysis. No emerging mechanism currently appears likely to displace APRIL-pathway therapy in typical patients.
