  2. 作者认为黄仁勋回归消除了英伟达停止支持neocloud和供应商这一最极端的尾部风险（CRWV、NBIS涨20%），打破了融资收紧、建设放缓、股价下跌的反身性循环，SMH反弹到490至500美元并不意外。
  3. 但作者也指出这不代表风险解除：黄仁勋可能更谨慎地使用资产负债表，美联储仍要加息，加州总检察长调查和国会听证（12月1日、12月3日）仍在继续，东南亚对华GPU销售难以恢复。

  5. SMTC盘后跌5%：1月季度收入指引4.8亿美元高于预期，CEO称FY28数据中心收入将在FY27约5.2亿美元的基础上翻倍，但毛利率指引64.3%略低于预期。
  6. ZM跌2%：企业业务增速创13个季度新高，FY28收入约53.2亿美元的市场预期被CFO称为合理起点，略低于买方预期，并维持80%毛利率。
  7. 其他要点：ANT跌1.3%，受谷歌Neon模型价格仅为Opus 5.5的25%以及开源叙事加强的压力；MU、SKHY涨约20%，跑赢SNDK；CRWD以全股票收购ZS后跌9%，交易价值降至约每股237美元；ORCL称OpenAI合同已全部获得融资，涨12.4%。

Good afternoon/evening. QQQs +7% after NVDA’s (+32%) board put Jensen Huang back in charge, adding $1T of market cap, the largest one-day gain for any company. Pretty productive first day of work.

Semis +14% was the 4th biggest day ever. Stocks were already up 3-4% premarket before the news hit and the Jensen news just added fuel. It didn’t take much as stocks were deeply oversold and positioning in Tech had downshifted significantly coming into the holiday shortened week. To give some context for the move over the last week, we are still down 4% from Friday the 13th and still down 1.5% from Wednesday’s close.

Plenty of 2-way debate over how sustainable this “Jensen Jump” is (doesn’t sound as good as “Leopold Low” I know). Some good debate in TMTB Slack here. Our view is that Jensen played an integral part in several parts of the AI trade: without him, we likely would have had a more conservative NVDA. The big concern was that NVDA’s support for neoclouds & suppliers would freeze up without him. Now that he’s back, it works as a relief valve for the most draconian outcomes for each of those (CRWV/NBIS +20% led semis today).

Still, Jensen back doesn’t resolve those issues completely. It’s not unreasonable to think he’ll likely be more careful about using NVDA's balance sheet to support customers and suppliers than he was before. It doesn’t resolve the litany of other issues plaguing the AI trade, and there’s quite a bit of them, just read our weekly from yesterday. Jensen’s leaving also shone even more light on the risks of the AI trade — you can’t turn that light off. It also doesn’t resolve the Fed wanting to hike.

This whole affair also makes AI even more politicized than it already is. It’s obvious the stock market decline (“AI is too big too fail”) was an impetus in getting Trump & the WH to push for Jensen to come back in a timely manner. But the other side of the aisle wasn’t placated: California AG Bonta said the move changes nothing for his probe (which Bloomberg reported Friday covers potential criminal violations), Schumer said Dems will investigate, Warren called it a “reckless decision,” and Huang faces closed-door House interviews on 12/1 and a public Senate Banking hearing on 12/3.

In terms of other read-throughs, it’s unlikely the SEA→China GPU sales are coming back any time soon and China Open Source will likely still face big headwinds. Case in point: the commerce dept added Megaspeed to the entity list this afternoon. With Jensen back at the helm, he’ll likely continue to push for open-source which will keep pressure on the labs so negative ANT/OAI. AVGO (+2%) & GOOGL (+6%) were beneficiaries from a weakened Jensen-free NVDA. AMD’s (+8%) mixed: Jensen's return closes the share opening a more conservative NVDA might have left, but AMD still benefits from a healthier AI trade overall.

Where do we come out?

Putting it all together, we think Jensen's return takes the worst near-term tail risks off the table. More importantly, it breaks the reflexive loop the bears were feeding on: NVDA pulls back on financing, the neoclouds can't fund capacity, the build slows, falling stock prices make investors more depressed, causing them to save their pnl before Jan 1st, all of it making the next round of funding even harder for the buildout (& for VCs looking to fund the next AI startups). The SMH was about 6-8% higher before Jensen’s resignation so a continued rally to somewhere close to $490-$500 wouldn’t surprise us, but this isn’t an all clear and would continue to expect a fairly tough investing environment after that. Still: we’ll stay open-minded as we’ve seen the AI trade’s reflexive power on both the up as well as the down.

Outside of Semis, Software +4%, AMZN +3.5% and GOOGL/META +6% led hyperscalers. Yields were just up a tad and Oil -4%.

Post-close Earnings:

HPE -7%: Big beat-and-raise on revenue and EPS, but after a +17% day the FY27 guide implying margins down y/y not clean enough

FY27 growth 27-35% (vs. 13-17% in Sept), led by Cloud & AI, while OM stays at 14-15% (Street 15.2%). The worry going in was how long 4Q's memory-driven price increases last. Mgmt sees memory supply tight "to at least the second half of calendar 2028" but price increases slowing, with AI, incl. AMD's Helios, weighted to 2H, and didn't say what normalizing memory prices would do to margins. Asked about Cloud & AI OM going to ~14% from 18.4% in 4Q, the CFO cited FY26's "fairly steep" price increases; Neri called it "a natural progression."

Bears will lean on the FY27 OI topping Street by only ~$590M at the mid (EPS $5.20 vs. $4.71) on ~$6.3B more revenue. The Jan qtr is ~$400M of that OI, leaving ~$2.2B a quarter for the other three (vs. $2.46B in 4Q). HPE slid from -6% to -8% during a question on the first FY27 Networks for AI target, $7.5-8B cumulative (from $3.4B), implying orders below 4Q's $1.2B a quarter. Also new: a $5B buyback authorization. Overall, de-risks FY27 revenue but not margins, not enough after a +17% day.

SMTC -5%: Big Jan-qtr revenue guide, but data center only hit its guide, the margin guide is light and there's no FY28 DC number. Looks decent enough overall.

DC only matched its $145M guide, but the $480M Jan-qtr guide beat the ~$452-462M bogeys. HFs leaned short on 1.6T DSP shortages. Asked about 2028 demand, CEO Hou put FY27 DC at ~$520M and said "we're going to be double that" (~$1.04B, in line with the bogeys). Mgmt has seen no DSP-driven pushouts but didn't update August's >70% FY28 backlog coverage, size CopperEdge or reaffirm >50% 1.6T TIA share by January. Though the OI guide beat Street ($159.7M vs. $152.4M), bears will lean on the GM guide ex the modules being sold (64.3% vs. ~64.6-65% bogeys) but shares pared from ~-8% to ~-3.5% in Q&A, starting right after the CFO said "64% is not a holding point” on the way to SMTC’s 70% GM target.

ZM -2%: Enterprise accelerates to a 13-qtr high and margins rip, but revenue only hits the low end of the bogeys and the Jan-qtr guide comes in under Street.

Enterprise accelerated organically, but FY28 light of bogeys. The Jan-qtr guide (+3.9%) missed the bogeys, but with Online flat for the year, as guided, Jan-qtr Enterprise still grows ~8%. On FY28, the worry going in, CFO said Street's $5.32B is "a sensible start" (+4.3% vs. FY27's +4.8%), under the ~$5.35-5.38B bogeys, with no margin or EPS; asked if the guide is conservative, she cited a "consistent forecast methodology", and cRPO's slowdown got no direct answer. On ANT's Claude Meetings beta, Yuan called ANT a partner that is "not building a real-time video collaboration product". On AI costs, the CFO kept 80% GM "well into the future.” Overall, better than the guide looked, de-risking Street's FY28 after the ANT-proxy selling, but slightly light of bogeys. Should be fine and won’t change bull vs. bear debate.
