SUMMARY

Overview

The expert described enterprise demand for unified communications (UCaaS) and contact center platforms (CCaaS), alongside the growing influence of AI. Buyers are moving from legacy on-premises systems to cloud services to reduce costs and simplify management. UCaaS is increasingly viewed as a commodity, while contact center offerings can help vendors win broader customer relationships.

AI is reshaping the market. Agentic systems can handle customer interactions, potentially reducing demand for human agents and per-seat licenses. Meanwhile, agent-assist and quality-assurance tools aim to improve human interactions rather than replace them.

Key Insights

The expert expects significant consolidation and sees established UCaaS and CCaaS providers as needing to develop agentic capabilities quickly to protect their businesses. Zoom was identified as a particularly strong competitor, with effective pricing, growing contact center momentum, and substantial investment in agentic AI. The expert views Sierra as a leading agentic provider, crediting its enterprise traction, adaptability, and market momentum. The expert expects smaller providers may eventually be acquired. Vendors are acquiring specialist AI companies when in-house tools fall short. The expert distinguished agentic AI, which may reduce agent seat counts, from agent-assist and QA tools, which can enhance service quality and support revenue growth. The Microsoft outage may create openings for competitors and strengthen customer focus on service-level agreements. However, the expert does not expect most customers to pay for duplicate systems; they generally prefer one provider and built- in resilience. AI tools can surface guidance during calls, automate call reviews, identify performance gaps, and provide insights. The expert estimated these tools could cut new-representative ramp time by about half. The expert has not seen meaningful customer interest in personal AI agents calling businesses on customers’ behalf.
