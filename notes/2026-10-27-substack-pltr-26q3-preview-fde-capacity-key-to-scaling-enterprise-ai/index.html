PLTR's deployment edge and scarce FDE talent should support upside

Oct 27

READ IN APP

This report first examines the bottlenecks to scaling enterprise AI adoption, with a focus on FDE demand and the changing competitive landscape, before turning to our Palantir 26Q3 earnings preview.

Enterprise AI budgets continue to grow, but progress from pilots to scaled deployments remains uneven. Customers are increasingly focused on tangible business returns, with projects that improve costs, efficiency, or decision-making more likely to secure continued funding. FDEs play a central role in integrating AI into existing business workflows, but the limited supply of experienced engineers and the difficulty of replicating project expertise remain constraints on broader adoption. As more vendors build out their FDE teams, competition is extending beyond model capabilities to deployment expertise. Palantir has an advantage in executing complex projects and drawing on its accumulated deployment experience. For 26Q3, we expect U.S. commercial revenue to maintain strong growth, with a modest pickup in U.S. government revenue growth. We forecast total revenue of approximately $2.319bn, around 7% above the high end of company guidance.

The Bottleneck to Enterprise AI Budget Expansion

Unclear ROI Could Slow Enterprise AI Budget Growth

  • Our enterprise survey: Enterprise AI spending growth may slow next year, with respondents citing unclear ROI or realized benefits falling short of expectations.- McKinsey (August 2026): Only 37% of respondents report that AI contributes to enterprise EBIT, essentially flat y/y; roughly 20% say operating costs, including tokens, are already constraining AI usage.- Gartner (2026, 1,303 respondents): Only 22% of organizations have scaled AI across multiple business units or adopted an AI-first operating model.- Insufficient returns are already shaping follow-on decisions: some projects remain stuck in pilot and struggle to reach production, while some live deployments risk cuts to incremental budget because benefits have fallen short.

AI Projects With Measurable Returns Still Win Budget

  • Palantir’s U.S. commercial business has grown more than 100% y/y for several consecutive quarters, and growth is still accelerating.- PwC 2026 AI Performance Study (1,217 executives): 20% of companies captured 74% of the AI-driven economic value in the sample; these leaders are roughly twice as likely as their peers to have redesigned workflows.- BCG (2026): Among the most mature “future-built” companies, 61% have shifted from fragmented pilots to multi-year, enterprise-wide programs.- Consultant interviews: Compared with assistive-tool projects, projects embedded in core business processes carry budgets at least 5x larger and deliver 5–10x higher ROI.- Case study: An inventory management system geared toward automated decision-making can reduce a customer’s inventory costs by more than 10% annually. In the cases we reviewed, annual project spend was roughly $2–3mn, with reported ROI of 3–5x. By contrast, document organization, Copilot, and knowledge-base applications make it much harder to translate value into operating results.

Palantir Benefits From Its FDE Experience and Platform

  • These projects are often highly disruptive for customers: they require a deep understanding of the business, cross-functional organizational alignment, data integration, and systems build-out.- Deloitte (April–June 2026, 501 U.S. executives, all of whom had at least begun agent pilots): Only 15% have achieved scaled, orchestrated multi-agent deployments; 72% lack unified, accessible data; and 67% view integration as too costly and complex. These barriers closely mirror what we heard in our project interviews.- FDEs help customers identify problems and drive them to resolution, while the platform provides the context layer, data integration, and the support required to run business workflows.- Our research: Delivering similar projects without the Palantir platform typically takes longer to develop and deploy.

Experienced FDEs Are Scarce; Replicating Know-How Matters

  • Talent development is inherently slow: FDEs must simultaneously understand the business problem, determine the implementation path, and drive internal alignment at the customer.- Unlike the SAP era: Back then, staff mobility among the Big Four, Accenture, and other consultancies spread implementation know-how quickly. Palantir talent rarely moves to consulting firms, so some firms have large delivery teams but lack people who have executed comparable projects and a proven implementation methodology.- Palantir has itself said growth could be even faster with a deeper FDE supply. The company is easing the constraint through in-house talent development programs and an expanding partner network; Fujitsu became a Palantir Global FDE Partner in 2026.- External FDE build-outs by other vendors expanded meaningfully this quarter:

In financial services, insurers, banks and asset managers are also building systems on the platform to drive revenue or reduce operating costs. Production AI Projects Could Lift Token Spend

  • Preliminary data: Token spend on these projects has in some cases doubled year over year.- To be confirmed: Our team is conducting more granular research to confirm the magnitude and durability of this growth.

New FDE Teams Need Time to Build Delivery Experience

  • Model vendors, hyperscalers and consultancies are building larger deployment teams and partner networks, which we expect to drive a new wave of enterprise AI initiatives.- Timing: With a typical project lasting 3–6 months, we preliminarily estimate that the newly hired FDE cohorts will need roughly six months and 1–2 completed projects before they can independently originate and lead projects.- What we are watching: Whether a handful of lighthouse projects emerge with clear ROI and a repeatable implementation methodology. With a large headcount base, the odds of high-quality lighthouse cases rise, which should in turn drive broader enterprise adoption of complex AI projects.

U.S. Government: We Remain Positive on 26Q3 Revenue and Expect a Modest Acceleration in y/y Growth

  • Historical relationship: Based on our tracking of government contract data, U.S. government obligations in a given quarter are positively correlated with the sequential increase in U.S. government revenue the following quarter. We use this lagged relationship as the basis for our revenue forecast.- Q2 obligations as a read-through to Q3: Disclosed U.S. government obligations in 26Q2 were roughly $632mn, essentially flat vs. $630mn in Q1.- Our model implies a sequential revenue increase of roughly $130mn in Q3.- Q3 revenue forecast: Building on actual Q2 U.S. government revenue of ~$809mn, we forecast Q3 U.S. government revenue of ~$940mn, up ~94% y/y from $486mn and ~16% q/q. On this basis, y/y growth in U.S. government revenue should tick up modestly from ~90% in Q2.

Key Changes in the Government Business This Quarter

  • Q1: Institutionalization of programs and validation of applications.- Q2: Expansion of platform capabilities and the partner ecosystem.- Q3: Defense programs moved into production procurement and extended into day-to-day operational workflows such as munitions management — the most substantive change this quarter, providing more concrete support for the medium-term durability of the U.S. government business.- International: Room to expand remains, but whether new partnerships convert into procurement at scale, and how adoption and renewals of existing European programs play out, warrant continued monitoring.

U.S. Defense: Moving From Program Validation to Production Procurement, With Deeper Integration Into Day-to-Day Operations

  • TITAN Initial Production Orders (September): Two Army orders totaling ~$192mn, of which Palantir received ~$127mn and Anduril ~$65mn.Covers eight initial production systems (four Advanced and four Basic), slated for delivery over roughly the next 18 months.- The program’s transition from prototype to production procurement provides a more concrete foundation for follow-on deployment, software upgrades, and sustainment.

Army Munitions Management Modernization Order: ~$48.1mn, consolidating nine legacy systems.

  • A 12-month base period for development and deployment, plus up to five option years.- The application covers day-to-day workflows including munitions planning, inventory, distribution, and auditing; sustained usage could support a longer service lifecycle.- The option years still need to be exercised and should not be treated as locked-in revenue.

Takeaway: Combined with prior progress on Maven and NGC2, this strengthens our conviction in the medium-term durability of the U.S. defense business.

Federal Civilian: Order Conversion at Existing Customers and Broader Deployment Channels Remain the Focus; New Use Cases Need to Be Validated Case by Case

  • What to track: For customers such as DHS, USDA, VA, IRS, FDA and CDC, the focus should shift from agency coverage and contract-vehicle ceilings to actual task orders and deployment scale. Contract structures and values differ across agencies, so it would be inaccurate to describe this uniformly as “$1bn-scale BPAs at six major agencies continuing to ramp.”- The FAA signal: Palantir’s defense advantage does not automatically translate into a competitive edge across all civilian use cases. The SMART airport deployments demonstrate government demand for AI-enabled airspace management, but the contract went to Air Space Intelligence and should not be counted toward Palantir’s growth.- New deployment channels: FedStart and the Knox partnership announced in October open a new path for third-party software to be deployed into government. If more vendors deploy through Palantir’s accredited environment, the company’s sources of growth could broaden further.- Revenue mix: The U.S. government revenue mix is diversifying this year (see chart below).

International Government: Regional Expansion Opportunities Remain, but Pilot-to-Procurement Conversion and European Renewal Risk Need to Be Factored Into Forecasts

  • Eastern Europe: In September, Lithuania’s President and Karp discussed expanding local engineering and product deployment capacity and explored establishing a regional technology hub serving the Baltics and NATO’s eastern flank; defense cooperation with Poland has moved into discussions on implementation arrangements and data governance.- Asia: The Fujitsu FDE partnership strengthens local implementation capacity, while LIG Nex1’s defense R&D collaboration targeting the UAE illustrates a path into third-country government markets via industrial partners.- European risks:UK FCA: An October 6 report that the FCA did not renew a short-term financial crime analytics contract shows that pilots do not necessarily convert into long-term procurement; available information is insufficient to attribute the non-renewal entirely to political resistance.- UK NHS: The program faces more explicit political and public-trust pressure, alongside disputes over actual utilization and program benefits; Parliament has called for exit options and alternative suppliers to be considered, which could further weigh on deployment, expansion and renewal.- Takeaway: Risks to the European business span political acceptance as well as proof of product value and procurement conversion, and warrant continued monitoring.

2027 Revenue Growth Outlook

The key variable we can underwrite for 2027 is U.S. commercial growth. Palantir should add roughly 200 net new customers in 2026. Based on our channel checks, net adds could rise meaningfully in 2027; assuming 400 net new customers at roughly $2mn of first-year spend each, new customers would contribute ~$0.8bn of revenue.

Company-wide net dollar retention (NDR) currently stands at 157%, and U.S. commercial should run well above that (~200%). Assuming U.S. commercial NDR of 170% in 2027 (which our channel checks suggest is achievable), we arrive at $3.5bn × 1.7 + $0.8bn ≈ $6.75bn of U.S. commercial revenue, implying ~93% growth on the 2026 base of $3.5bn — still a very high growth rate.

Government: The Street currently models 2027 government revenue growth of 36% (vs. 85–90% in 2026). While we cannot size 2027 government revenue through channel checks, current trends (quarterly order flow and penetration across government agencies) suggest growth should not decelerate that quickly. We assume government growth of 60–70%. Overall, we see a high probability of 70%+ revenue growth in 2027, and by extension expect the pace of deceleration in subsequent years to be slower than the Street anticipates.
