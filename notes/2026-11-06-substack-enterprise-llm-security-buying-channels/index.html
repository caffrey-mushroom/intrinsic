The PHP2Shell incident has put LLM security capabilities back in focus. Our channel checks suggest that customers are unlikely to increase security spending materially this year. Interest in higher spending is more evident over the medium term.

Executive Summary

  1. AI labs are getting better at finding vulnerabilities, often faster and at lower cost. This puts pressure on point

solutions whose main offering is scan results or labor-intensive penetration testing. But the opportunity is not limited to scanning: demand is also moving toward platforms that manage assets and policies, as well as products that protect WAFs and APIs.

  1. Most customer spending is still directed toward remediation, incident response and governance. These jobs depend on

systems already running in the customer environment—endpoint agents, firewalls and identity tools that collect telemetry, block threats and support remediation—as well as teams that can investigate and handle incidents. AI labs generally do not own this infrastructure or provide those operating teams. Channel checks also point to two practical constraints: people are still needed to carry out remediation, and sensitive data must stay within controlled environments. As a result, new spending tends to go to vendors customers already use.

  1. For now, partnerships are the more common route to market. AI labs supply the models and tokens; security vendors

connect them to customer data, enforce policies and build the models into existing products. The leading security models are also being made available through partners or tightly controlled programs, rather than through unrestricted direct access.

How Enterprise Security Work Gets Done: Models Help Find Vulnerabilities, but Spending Is Concentrated in Remediation, Response and Governance

Subscribed

Enterprise security work spans vulnerability discovery, triage and prioritization, remediation, alert response and governance. AI labs are strongest in the first two areas. The later stages depend on data from customer systems and on tools that can block threats or take action. Models can help analyze events, but they do not replace those systems.

For vulnerability discovery, LLMs can read code in context and follow logic across files. This can help them find flaws that rule-based scanners miss. They can also link separate vulnerabilities into a potential attack path and assess how easily it could be exploited.

They can also work across large codebases in parallel. In June 2026, OpenAI’s GPT-5.5-Cyber worked with Trail of Bits and identified hundreds of vulnerabilities in more than 30 open-source projects.

The Linux kernel review covered 30 million lines of code and found 24 local privilege-escalation vulnerabilities. This work takes place at the code and binary level; it does not require access to the customer’s live operating environment.

Industry Expert 1: “AI was not as powerful back then as it is today, so it could not construct a complete attack path. Generally, a kernel-level vulnerability without a complete attack path is not considered a real threat and is unlikely to be prioritized.”

Anthropic September 2026 Threat Intelligence Report: “more than a dozen possible zero-day findings in a single month … Sophisticated attacks no longer require sophisticated attackers.”

Even so, vulnerability discovery is still a relatively small part of customer security budgets. It is also the area where AI labs overlap most directly with existing security tools.

North American Channel Partner 1: “I personally feel that those lab vendors have a good platform to gain market share purely from a VM perspective. Just scanning and identifying the vulnerabilities from the outside looking inside … The area where I think the market is at a crossroads of the transformation is who is going to patch and how you are going to patch.”

North American Channel Partner 3: “Vulnerability and code-scanning tools such as Burp Suite are also expected to be incorporated into OpenAI’s or Anthropic’s capabilities at no additional charge. Code scanning is a small category for customers, accounting for about 1% of their budgets.”

Industry Expert 1: “AI is highly capable in penetration testing within cybersecurity, but it still faces considerable challenges in protection and practical deployment.”

North American Channel Partner 5: “When resources are limited, scanning strategies should suppress low-risk findings and focus on active attack paths and high-severity vulnerabilities.”

Why AI Labs May Struggle to Sell Security Products Directly to Customers

Security Vendors Already Have the Data and Tools to Act

To tell whether activity on a system is malicious, a security team needs telemetry from the customer’s environment and an understanding of the business context. Responding to a threat also means taking action across networks, endpoints, identity systems and cloud infrastructure. Endpoint agents, firewalls and SOC platforms already collect much of this data. AI labs do not have a comparable footprint inside customer environments.

Customers are beginning to use models to correlate security events and help with analysis. But the data stays in their existing tools, where teams still prioritize alerts and manage the response.

North American Channel Partner 2: “For AI features already integrated into security platforms (such as vulnerability prioritization and security copilots), we usually buy directly from the security vendor … The advantage is that these tools already connect asset, vulnerability, identity and alert data, making them a better fit with existing security workflows.”

North American Channel Partner 4: “Agents identify events in the environment based on specific tasks. Human staff then review those events, set priorities and take the appropriate action.”

North American Channel Partner 3: “They may be good at vulnerability scanning or an AI SOC, but entering mature endpoint or email security markets will be difficult.”

Remediation Still Needs People, Leaving Security Vendors to Cover the Gap

Models can find vulnerabilities faster than customers can fix them. Most customers are not prepared to let agents deploy patches on their own, so remediation still involves people. The result is a growing backlog of vulnerabilities awaiting fixes. In the meantime, security vendors can help reduce exposure with virtual patching, isolation and runtime detection.

North American Channel Partner 1: “These lab vendors can say, we’re going to have the agents who are going to help you to patch all these vulnerabilities because the speed at which these lab vendors are identifying the vulnerabilities humans are not able to patch them … but will we trust agents for that the answer is absolutely no, but you need to have a human in the loop.”

North American Channel Partner 3: “Security vendors can combine their own technology with AI while also providing product support. Scanning can identify issues, but customers may still need help understanding the results and fixing them.”

North American Channel Partner 5: “Customers can upgrade to managed detection and response services, using external analysts and automated workflows to triage, investigate and proactively remediate around the clock. They can also use automation such as CrowdStrike Charlotte AI or Falcon Fusion to carry out pre-approved containment actions based on risk scores, rather than having engineers manually patch or isolate every endpoint.”

Customers Are Wary of Sending Sensitive Data Directly to AI Labs

In July 2026, around 1,200 OpenAI evaluation agents coordinated an attack test, with roughly 700 involved in an attack on Hugging Face’s production cluster. OpenAI did not flag the activity in its internal logs before Hugging Face disclosed it publicly on July 16. Anthropic has also reported evaluation incidents that exceeded their intended scope. These cases have added to CISOs’ concerns about trusting AI labs.

Enterprise customers are reluctant to send sensitive source code or production logs straight to AI labs. Many prefer to access models through a cloud service provider (CSP) or a security vendor, with requests routed through controlled gateways.

North American Channel Partner 1: “If I’m a CISO, I’m like, do I really want to trust OpenAI when their agents are really going rogue and penetrating some other organizations without any permission? How can I trust that vendor when they haven’t even fixed their own house? … And it will take a long time for the lab vendors to really get that trust, you know, from the CSOs and the buyers to really, you know, gain some sort of a significant market share.”

North American Channel Partner 1: “We haven’t seen any response in a positive way from any client that, hey, tomorrow I’m going to buy, you know, vulnerability management or SOC solutions from Anthropic or OpenAI.”

North American Channel Partner 2: “When sensitive code or production logs are involved, customers use model gateways such as AWS Bedrock to obtain data isolation, access controls and audit capabilities.” The expert estimates that 50–60% of AI security spending goes through controlled channels such as AWS and Azure, 20–35% through security vendors, and 10–20% directly to OpenAI or Anthropic. The expert expects the direct-purchase share to decline to 5–15%.

North American Channel Partner 5: “Customers typically use CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks or other security vendors that may use these models in the background. I have not seen customers use Anthropic directly for this. Direct use requires more programming work and lacks the integrated interface provided by a security platform. Data residency is also a major concern, as customers do not want external model providers scanning their networks or transferring sensitive data to their servers.”

Customers Mostly Reach LLM Security Tools Through CSPs and Established Security Vendors

In practice, enterprise customers reach LLM security capabilities through three main routes: cloud-provider model gateways, products from established security vendors, and bundled offerings from suites such as Microsoft’s. Direct purchases from AI labs remain a smaller part of the market.

Cloud Providers Are the Main Gateway to Models

Customers generally start with the cloud providers they already use. Enterprise identity, centralized controls, data isolation and audit functions are often built into those arrangements, making it easier to manage model usage and costs alongside other cloud services. Customers typically buy AI features embedded in security products from the security vendor itself. Direct purchases from AI labs are more often reserved for general-purpose code analysis or specific tasks that require access to the latest models.

Source: FUNDA

Industry Expert 2: “For large enterprises, I tend to start with existing cloud providers, especially Microsoft Azure and Amazon Web Services, because of enterprise identity and integration, centralized governance and security controls, and the ability to manage usage and costs under the cloud agreement.”

North American Channel Partner 2: “The reduction will mainly come from two areas. First, the share of direct calls to cloud APIs such as Anthropic’s may decline from 10–20% to 5–15%, as we place greater emphasis on data isolation, access controls and auditing …”

Industry Expert 2: “For strategic use cases, if I need the latest models or specific capabilities that are not yet available in the cloud marketplace, I would also buy directly from AI providers such as OpenAI or Anthropic.”

North American Channel Partner 2: “Purchases of standalone AI security products with narrow, closed functionality may decline, as some of these functions could be incorporated into existing cloud platforms such as AWS.”

Established Security Vendors Are Adding Models to Their Products, and New Budgets Often Go to Them First

Security vendors are building models into products customers already use. In most cases, customers pay through an existing platform license or product agreement rather than signing a separate contract with an AI lab.

The CISO, security architects and procurement team typically make purchasing decisions together. They also look at total cost of ownership over a period of at least three years.

To get onto an approved vendor list, an AI lab needs to offer something existing suppliers cannot, or come in at a meaningfully lower price. Token-based pricing can also make the long-term cost harder for customers to estimate.

The leading security models are not generally available to every customer on an unrestricted basis. Anthropic’s Mythos is available to around 200 vetted organizations through Project Glasswing, while Claude has been integrated into 28 security platforms. OpenAI limits access to its offensive capabilities through the Daybreak defense coalition. In September, Cloudflare launched a vulnerability discovery and remediation service built on the Daybreak model.

For AI labs, working through existing security products also offers a more direct way to reach customers and generate revenue.

North American Channel Partner 3: “I would switch from a large vendor only if the new tool can do something my existing vendor cannot, or is much cheaper, perhaps half the price.” “Token-based pricing could get expensive, and I don’t want to buy a security product I can’t keep renewing over the long term.”

North American Channel Partner 5: “They try to make use of the existing ecosystem. Palo Alto Networks customers will look to add more Palo Alto Networks products, while Cisco customers will look for options within their existing Cisco licenses. Budgets are usually set about six months in advance, so a new incident requires funds to be reallocated or pulled forward from future-year projects. Incumbent vendors and straightforward integration can significantly speed up emergency deployments.”

CrowdStrike Management: “Anthropic, for example, all of them basically saying we’re their number one partner in the cybersecurity space. Anthropic was saying that we are necessary for them to be able to scale so quickly, and we are now listed on the OpenAI marketplace.”

North American Channel Partner 4: “We have a close relationship with Anthropic. What Anthropic mentions most often is that many of its capabilities are designed to run on top of cybersecurity products such as Palo Alto and CrowdStrike.”

North American Channel Partner 3: “Other security vendors already offer similar features and say they plan to integrate model providers’ capabilities without charging extra for tokens. I prefer this approach because it allows me to keep my existing tools and vendor support while adding new technology.”

Industry Expert 1: “On the protection side, AI has a limited role. I think it will be difficult for companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic to enter cybersecurity, particularly the B2B market, and the return on investment would be low.”

North American Channel Partner 5: “Strong AI integration, innovative products or partnerships with companies such as Anthropic could significantly improve its position.” The expert was referring to Cloudflare.

Microsoft and Other Suite Vendors Can Bundle Model Features

Microsoft already has a broad enterprise customer base and access to large volumes of customer data. Customers may use OpenAI models through Copilot, Defender and the E5/E7 suites rather than contract with OpenAI directly. This creates the most pressure for independent vendors whose products overlap with Microsoft’s, although the impact differs by product category.

North American Channel Partner 3: “Many customers may use the same models through Microsoft Copilot or other established vendors rather than signing directly with OpenAI or Anthropic.”

North American Channel Partner 5: “Microsoft may account for 20–22% of total enterprise spending because customers use Windows Server, Azure, Microsoft Office and Entra, and receive bundled discounts.” The expert separately estimates that PANW accounts for 12–15% of security spending and CRWD for 8–10%.

North American Channel Partner 5: “In the mid-market, Microsoft creates pricing pressure because customers question why they should pay another vendor separately when Defender is already bundled.”

Competitive Developments to Watch

Customers May Become More Comfortable with Agent-Led Remediation

If customers become comfortable letting agents write and deploy patches, AI labs could capture more of the remediation budget, while demand for virtual patching may fall. Channel partners expect adoption to start with routine, lower-risk patches.

North American Channel Partner 1: “As and when the market matures and learns more and more about this thing, agents will take over some of the generic patching and the human oversight will do other patching.”

AI Labs Could Buy Vendors with Systems Already Deployed in Customer Environments

Trail of Bits brings security research and auditing expertise, particularly in vulnerability discovery and human review. A different kind of acquisition would matter more for the competitive balance: buying a vendor whose endpoint agents, network appliances or SIEM systems are already deployed at customer sites. That would give an AI lab a foothold in customer telemetry and enforcement—two areas where established security vendors have an advantage.

North American Channel Partner 1: “If you see the job opening on OpenAI and everything about the security architect and whatnot that tells you that they’re looking to build their own security solutions and go to market.”

Enterprise Agents Could Become Concentrated on AI Lab Platforms

If more enterprise agents are built and run on Claude Enterprise or ChatGPT Enterprise, those platforms may be able to provide runtime protection and access controls themselves. That could affect demand for products such as Prisma AIRS and Falcon AI Gateway. Some customers already charge Claude usage to their security budgets.

North American Channel Partner 4: “We use Claude tokens to understand observability and events inside the enterprise and to correlate incidents … It currently accounts for about 3–5% of the end-to-end enterprise cybersecurity budget. As we recruit the right people and build enterprise agents across the organization, I expect this share could rise to 8% in the future.”
