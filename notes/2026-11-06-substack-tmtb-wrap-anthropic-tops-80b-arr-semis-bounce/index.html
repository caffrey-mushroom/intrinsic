Bull vs. Bear Debate

The bull thesis is that NET has become one of the rare scaled infrastructure platforms where several independent growth drivers are compounding at once, on top of an improving GTM engine. Application services re-accelerated as publishers and enterprises moved to control AI crawlers from late 2025. Workers is becoming a default runtime for AI-built apps and agents from mid-2026, with Workers revenue estimated to be compounding 30–40% q/q this year. Security now has an outside catalyst, as AI-discovered vulnerabilities push even non-technical buyers onto a network that sits in front of 20%+ of the web. Bulls see these drivers as additive rather than sequential, and see PoF as what converts them into revenue. A customer signs one commitment, its developers find Workers inside it, usage burns through the commitment, and the customer renews early into a bigger one; that is the pattern that took NRR from 118% in Q1 to 122% now. Bulls see the advantages as structural. NET runs one programmable network across 340+ cities with every service on every server, so new products ride existing capex. Its isolate architecture is, in bulls’ view, inherently cheaper than containers for bursty agent workloads. Its free tier converted a record ~158K paying customers this quarter. And its trusted-intermediary position with frontier labs, researchers and publishers gives it early warning on threats and a seat at the table in setting the economic rules for agents.

This quarter checked nearly every box. The beat was the largest in company history and broad: long tail, every large cohort at a record, no AI concentration. Mgmt built that run-rate into the Q4 guide instead of discounting it, and cRPO and implied cRPO bookings accelerated. OM hit a record despite GM pressure, the clearest evidence yet that Workers dilutes GM but not unit economics, given its far lower cost to book. GAAP profitability arriving ~2 years early, an operator who scaled Splunk’s sales org succeeding Mark Anderson, and the first Act 4 dollars (with OpenAI paying) all lowered perceived risk. Bulls see three paths to higher estimates and a higher multiple. First, a normal Q4 beat delivers a 40%+ exit rate and pushes CY27 Street from ~$3.9B toward $4B+. Second, NRR has room into the mid-120s as PoF goes from a low-double-digit share of new ACV to a standard part of every deal. Third, security and Act 4 extend the duration of 30%+ growth, which is what a premium multiple actually pays for. Bulls also argue a slower data-center build-out helps rather than hurts, since scarce capacity raises the value of a network that is already built, dense and efficient. And in a software market where 30%+ growth at $3B+ scale is nearly extinct, NET’s scarcity premium should widen.

The bear thesis is that NET’s acceleration is increasingly a consumption story built on catalysts with limited shelf lives, but priced as if it were durable subscription growth. Variable revenue (est. mid-teens % of revenue) drove the beat, and customers burning through PoF commitments faster pulls renewals and revenue forward. Total RPO growth slowed to 34% from 38% (implied RPO bookings ~27% vs. ~42%) even as revenue accelerated, so less of the growth is contracted. Two of the three accelerants are time-bound by mgmt’s own description. Mgmt expects the vulnerability wave to be bad “for, I think, two years”, and the developer surge leans on AI app builders and vibe-coding platforms that mgmt concedes may be transient. Many of the 158K new paying customers are small developer accounts; mgmt’s own example was a ~$5/month app. Structurally, bears point to margins and competition. GM is down ~290bps y/y to 72.4% and did not stabilize as the CFO suggested in August, and the long-term GM floor was already lowered to 70% in June. Capex is at the top of its range and will rise with compute, and FCF margin is ~10% vs. 13% a year ago. Every workload is contested. In compute, NET faces hyperscalers (Lambda, CloudFront and Shield; Azure Front Door; Cloud Run and Cloud Armor) and developer platforms like Vercel. In CDN/WAF it faces Akamai and Fastly, and in SASE it faces Zscaler, Palo Alto and Netskope. In Act 4, the AI labs NET hopes to charge have the scale to build their own crawling and payment rails.

This quarter gave bears specific ammunition behind the headline. Mgmt said it is now guiding closer to the expected outcome, so the ~5–6% beats that have powered the stock are unlikely to repeat at that size. PoF seasonality (bigger Q4s, smaller Q1s; Q1’26 added only ~$25M of net new revenue) sets up a potentially awkward Q1’27, just as CY27 expectations reset from ~29% to ~32%+ growth. Quality-of-earnings flags are building. SBC grew 45% y/y to 22% of revenue, and GAAP EPS of -$0.10 missed Street (-$0.04). Q4 “GAAP profitability” will come at the net income line, supported by ~$45M a quarter of interest income on cash raised largely through 0% converts, while GAAP operating income likely stays negative. The President of Revenue transition also lands just as mgmt plans to change the sales model (more forward-deployed engineers, new SE:AE ratios and technical overlays), a classic source of first-half execution slippage. The multiple would compress on any sign that consumption normalizes (NRR back below 120%), a pause in the AI-app and agent build-out, or a broader rotation out of high-beta AI winners. The stock already fell ~34% from its October high into the print on data-center and AI-trade worries. At ~28x CY27E EV/sales and ~155x CY27E EPS, bears point to NET’s ~80% peak-to-trough drawdown in 2021–22 as the template for what happens when a peak multiple meets slowing growth.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bulls see TEAM as the system of record for how technical and business teams plan, track and document work, and argue AI makes that position more valuable, not less. Models are becoming interchangeable; what agents lack is organizational context (goals, decisions, history, ownership), and the Teamwork Graph (250B+ objects, up ~70% in six months) plus Jira and Confluence workflows give agents a governed place to receive work, act and be audited. In that framing, agentic coding multiplies the volume of work and therefore the need for coordination, which shows up as seat growth among developers and the business users who make up roughly two-thirds of Jira and Confluence users. The durable advantages are switching costs and data gravity across 350K+ customers and 85% of the Fortune 500, a low-cost product-led motion (Jira lists at roughly $7 per user), a Marketplace of 2,000+ partners with $7B+ of lifetime sales, and multi-model AI routing that has let gross margin rise while AI usage scales. The runway comes from five levers: migrating the largest remaining DC customers before DC expansions end for existing customers in March 2028 and DC reaches end of life in March 2029, with migrated cohorts spending ~1.75x more after three years; Collections upsell, with Teamwork Collection in <1% of customers at a ~15–20% uplift; Service Collection share gains in ITSM; enterprise penetration, where the Fortune 500 is only ~10% of revenue; and annual pricing, with consumption pricing as optional upside.

This quarter’s forward indicators support that view. RPO grew 44% for a second straight quarter, cRPO accelerated to 28%, $10K+ cloud customer growth re-accelerated to 13% on more than double last year’s net adds, the $1M+, $3M+ and $5M+ cohorts grew >30%, >50% and ~70%, Teamwork Collection roughly tripled since May and MCP users doubled in a quarter. Bulls read the hybrid deals as the largest customers locking in multi-year cloud paths (visible in RPO) rather than delaying them, with revenue parked in DC that should move to cloud as users cut over. They also see a deliberately conservative guide: Q2’s ~27% cloud guide absorbs ~1 pt of DX lap, a Q1-like cushion would put cloud near 29%, and the 18.5% ARR guide implies net new ARR flat y/y ex-DX despite accelerating leading indicators. Estimates could move higher on UBP overage billing from Dec 3 (none in guide), the Oct 13 price increase, migrations pulled forward ahead of the March 2028 expansion cutoff and a FY28 revenue re-acceleration toward the mid-to-high teens as DC EOL comps lap. Margins could surprise as the one-time cash-comp shift is absorbed, SBC keeps falling (~20% of revenue vs. ~24% a year ago) and AI unit costs keep declining, and the multiple can re-rate as GAAP profitability builds and the AI-disruption discount fades: after hours the stock trades at ~20x CY27 EV/FCF vs. ~28x for infrastructure-software comps, and at ~16x 2028 FCF vs. ~33x for high-growth peers.

Bears see TEAM as a seat-based application vendor whose core product monetizes human coordination of software work, the category most exposed to AI agents, and note that the slide from ~$200 in mid-2025 to a ~$56 low earlier this year priced exactly that fear. As coding and ticket work shift to agents acting through MCP and APIs, value could accrue to model vendors and agent platforms (Microsoft and GitHub, OpenAI, ServiceNow, newer tools such as Linear, Notion and monday.com)), while TEAM’s seat growth slows and consumption is either given away in generous credit bundles or captured by others. The structural risks are that migration-driven growth is finite and cloud must eventually stand on seats, upsell and price alone; that hybrid deployments let the largest, most complex DC customers stretch migrations over several years, with government certifications landing in 2028–29 suggesting part of the base runs up to or past the March 2029 EOL; and that AI monetization remains indirect and small (3K Teamwork Collection customers, no meaningful UBP revenue in FY27) while rising Rovo usage already weighs on the FY27 GM guide. Execution risk rises as the enterprise push (85% of the Fortune 500, ~10% of revenue) requires more direct sales and FDE spend that dilutes the low-touch model. Financial quality is the other leg: SBC is still ~19% of FY27 revenue (GAAP OM ~5% vs. ~25.5% non-GAAP), the shift to cash comp moves cost into non-GAAP margins, FCF less SBC is only ~$0.5B at the FY27 guide, and net cash is down to ~$0.1B after buybacks and M&A.

Bulls may be too optimistic in treating the Dec-Q shortfall as purely deferred revenue. Some of the backlog build reflects longer lead times rather than new demand. The inventory MCHP did build (184 days, heavy in finished goods) is evidently the wrong mix. The remedies, requalifying OSATs and moving assembly in-house at higher cost (~6 months per move), add cost and push relief toward mid-2027. This quarter supplied specific evidence:

  • DC growth decelerated sharply (+55.7% y/y vs. +97.8%; ~+7% q/q), and the CEO called his fastest-growing end market a bubble while conceding Gen 6 volumes “could be slightly lower.”
  • A&D slowed to ~+5% q/q, with mgmt calling it “not a hockey stick.”
  • The core MCU line grew only ~5% q/q and missed the Street by ~3%. Mgmt acknowledged its in-house MCUs lacked the hardware AI acceleration that STM, NXPI and TXN already ship in NPU-equipped MCUs.
  • The EPS beat leaned on a 5.7% tax rate and $53M of one-timers.

If AI capex slows, the second-order exposures Sanghi downplayed could matter more than the ~8% direct catalog exposure implies: DC-driven industrial and power demand, the industry-wide pricing that AI tightness has enabled, and A&D budget risk. Estimates could fall further if Mar-Q doesn’t snap back because capacity slips, if GM drifts below 65% as January cost increases hit, or if restocking never materializes. The multiple could compress if investors treat CY27 as peak earnings and the group keeps de-rating after Nov 5’s semis drop.
