SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion evaluated intravitreal IL-6 therapies for uveitic macular edema and the broader category of macular edema secondary to inflammation, including postoperative cases. The expert viewed the latter as encompassing all uveitic cases plus additional inflammatory conditions.

Current treatment relies heavily on topical therapy and intravitreal steroids. Steroids are highly effective but create cataract and intraocular-pressure risks, particularly for younger patients and those with glaucoma. IL-6 inhibitors could provide an important steroid-sparing alternative by addressing blood-retinal-barrier dysfunction as well as inflammation.

Key Insights

Macular edema is especially common and vision-threatening in intermediate, posterior, and panuveitis. Conventional immunosuppressants primarily control inflammation but may not adequately restore the blood-retinal barrier. The expert considers steroids and IL-6 inhibition better suited to this mechanism. The unmet need for steroid-sparing treatment is substantial, including among postoperative patients who would not otherwise require chronic systemic therapy. The expert characterized the IL-6 efficacy findings as highly encouraging, emphasizing retinal drying and durability over visual- acuity changes because vision is affected by multiple structural factors. Faster improvement is clinically valuable because it limits retinal damage and quickly identifies whether steroid-sparing therapy is working. If approved, IL-6 agents could potentially replace more than half of the expert’s intravitreal steroid injections. Systemic brepocitinib was not viewed as a meaningful threat because it occupies the systemic immunosuppression segment rather than directly replacing local edema therapy.
