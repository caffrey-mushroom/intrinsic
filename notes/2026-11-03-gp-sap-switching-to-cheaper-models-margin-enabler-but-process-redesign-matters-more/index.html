SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examines SAP’s reported shift toward less expensive AI models and whether it could improve margins. The expert argues that model choice is only one part of the opportunity: SAP’s business-process knowledge, integrated software, data context, and governance may matter more to customers and create stronger long-term advantages.

Potential early productivity gains are concentrated in rule-based workflows such as finance, procurement, operations, and HR. Broader benefits may take longer, as companies redesign processes across departments rather than simply adding AI to isolated pilots.

Key Insights

Cheaper models are an enabler, not the main source of savings. Model substitution could reduce costs, but the expert sees process redesign and wider deployment as more consequential over time. SAP’s differentiation lies primarily beyond the model. Its enterprise-process expertise, connected business data, and understanding of how transactions relate may help it deliver more relevant results than standalone competitors. Scaling into production is the central challenge. The expert says pilots often fail to expand because they are too narrowly scoped, not simply because AI is too expensive. Pricing is likely to evolve. The expert expects a progression from AI included in core products toward usage allowances, task- based charges, and, selectively, outcome-based pricing. European sovereignty requirements offer SAP an advantage. European models, locally hosted data, and operational control may help address regulated customers’ needs, though the expert says no single factor dominates.
