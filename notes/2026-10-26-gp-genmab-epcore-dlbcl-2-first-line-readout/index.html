SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed top-line data for epcoritamab plus R-CHOP in first-line diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, including efficacy expectations, safety, adoption barriers, and positioning against Pola-R-CHP, tafasitamab-based therapy, glofitamab combinations, and an investigational ROR1 antibody-drug conjugate.

The expert viewed the reported progression-free survival hazard ratio as unusually strong and potentially practice-changing, particularly for patients with International Prognostic Index scores of 2–5.

Key Insights

The expert expects epcoritamab plus R-CHOP to improve cure rates meaningfully and potentially become the preferred regimen for most patients with IPI scores of at least 2. A 10% absolute PFS improvement would be sufficient to change practice, while the expert projects a possible 20%–25% improvement over R-CHOP. Broad benefit across germinal-center B-cell and activated B-cell subtypes would be a major advantage over Pola-R-CHP. This remains an expectation rather than confirmed subgroup evidence. Mature overall survival data are not considered necessary for adoption if 24-month PFS demonstrates a compelling benefit. The largest practical obstacle may be community access to bispecific therapy, including ramp-up dosing and monitoring for cytokine release syndrome and neurologic toxicity. The expert expects first-line use in roughly two-thirds of patients if detailed data confirm efficacy and tolerability.
