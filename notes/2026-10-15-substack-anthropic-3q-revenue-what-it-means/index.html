原标题：Column|Anthropic: What the New 3Q Numbers Mean

#llm #ai-demand

Substack · 2026-10-15

Anthropic disclosed 3Q26 revenue of $16.8Bn during its just-completed pre-IPO investor day. The company had previously disclosed 2Q26 revenue of $11.5Bn and 1Q26 revenue of $4.7Bn.

There is speculation that the company guided to $100Bn of ARR by year-end, but we don’t believe it has given explicit guidance.

Based on the disclosures and our own tracking over time, we built a full-year ARR and revenue model for Anthropic.

Equities went through major volatility over the past week. The reasons included

  1. OpenAI’s ARR is $50Bn, not $70Bn. There is a net vs. gross difference, but the comparison itself shows the market has

started tracking ARR much more closely. A big Personal Agent narrative used to be enough. Not anymore.

  1. Personal Agent DAU has entered a short-term plateau. Since our earlier Muse report, more and more research has

started discussing Personal Agent DAU and retention. The growing focus on numbers is also making the big narrative more short-term.

  1. There were a lot of concerns ahead of the Anthropic listing. After Anthropic published its anti-distillation report

in particular, the market worried that cutting distillation revenue would hurt 3Q26 revenue and future revenue guidance.

  1. Yields. For a while, rising yields signaled that people saw AI demand as strong; it was AI demand driving yields

higher. Now yields and AI demand are gradually decoupling. Yields have become a headwind, and the market is paying more attention to moves in CDS.

After the Anthropic pre-IPO investor day, a few things changed

Subscribed

  1. Backing out from the revenue data, Anthropic’s September ARR comes to about $70Bn. That is below the $80Bn bogey and

below the FUNDA AI Tracker estimate of $80Bn.

  1. According to The Information, the company said that distillation revenue made up 5–7% of ARR, and that it gave up

most of that revenue in September. This is consistent with what investors were worried about on September ARR before the pre-IPO investor day. Adding distillation revenue back, September ARR would likely have been $72–73Bn.

  1. There are many assumptions about how July and September ARR were measured. We think the $65Bn July ARR was on a

trailing-28-day basis. On the same basis, we estimate September T28 ARR at $69Bn. Exit run-rate ARR is about $70Bn. Given how much growth slowed in 3Q, the two measures shouldn’t differ much.

  1. We think the market’s concern mainly comes from not trusting the growth path from $69Bn of ARR in September to $100Bn

by end-December. That would require net new ARR of roughly $9Bn every month from here, while ex-distillation monthly net new ARR over the past quarter was about $5Bn.

  1. From a fundamental angle, one could of course explain the acceleration with a potential pickup in Anthropic’s growth

after the Opus 5.5 release at the end of September. But as we have seen over the past few months, OpenAI and Anthropic growth works like a seesaw. When OpenAI’s monthly net adds started to accelerate, Anthropic began to slow. Once Opus drives an acceleration at Anthropic, the market will worry about whether OpenAI slows down, and the debate falls further into a competitive narrative.

Looking ahead from here

  1. After all this focus on short-term data, whether $100Bn of ARR can be proven becomes the key. Stocks have fallen, but

proving $100Bn of ARR is still hard. From a guidance standpoint, giving long-term guidance and emphasizing 4Q acceleration without giving a year-end ARR figure would be the better strategy, in our opinion.

  1. Whether year-end ARR meets expectations has become the only core question for the AI trade. That means more of a

guessing game and more attention on short-term changes. If that expectation drops because of some unexpected event, there may be a better opportunity. We are also not sure where expectations stand now, after the pre-roadshow worry that anti-distillation would hit ARR. If the company really does reach $100Bn of ARR later on, that could beat market expectations.

  1. Everyone wants to find a new direction, and everyone is anxious about which use cases will drive ARR growth in the

future. The new use cases Anthropic has mentioned could become a more closely watched side thread for a while. These include drug discovery, advanced disease detection and diagnosis, education, financial and legal advice, supply chain automation, and autonomous cyber defense.

  1. This leaves me with a feeling. The closer we get to AGI, the worse the investing experience may become. The closer we

are to the tipping point, the more compute has to go to training, which makes ROI harder to prove. But when that tipping point comes is a very big bet. If we’re lucky, we will find the next big use case before the tipping point. That is what FUNDA hopes to do.
