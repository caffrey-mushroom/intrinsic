原标题：Anthropic S-1. Fuel For the Fire?

#llm #markets

Substack · 2026-10-27

I think this is good for the AI trade. The market agrees. At least for today.

Anthropic made an operating profit in Q2 even after stock-based compensation, and growth is spreading well beyond its largest customers.

The October OpenRouter data suggest demand has continued to pick up.

Q2 revenue was $11.6B, up 144% QoQ. Customers generating at least $100,000 of trailing 12-month revenue increased from approximately 1,500 at the end of 2025 to more than 11,900 by September 30.

The falling concentration is great. The top five customers went from approximately 36% of revenue in Q1 to 21% in Q3, even though every one of the Q3 top five generated record revenue that quarter.

Net revenue retention was approximately 900%, with Q3 revenue from the measured customer cohort roughly nine times its revenue a year earlier. The cohort includes customers who spent as little as $1 in that earlier quarter. Claude Pro and Max subscribers are excluded. A business moving from a tiny trial into deployment can produce a huge percentage increase.

That’s a lot of existing customers choosing to spend much more. The willingness to pay for AI is becoming very clear.

Pricing

Anthropic says Opus 5.5 reduced customers’ cost per task by approximately 40% compared with Opus 5, through lower pricing and better efficiency. Lower costs make AI economical for more work. Customers may also let an agent work longer. So their total bill can rise even as they pay less for each completed task.

After Opus 4.5 launched with better capabilities and a lower cost per task, QoQ revenue growth accelerated from 48% in Q4 to 132% in Q1. Anthropic attributes the acceleration to several factors, including new products and broader adoption.

Reports from the October investor meeting put Q3 revenue at $16.8B, up roughly 45% QoQ. That added more than $5B of quarterly revenue, with percentage growth slowing from Q2’s 144%.

Opus 5.5 launched late in September. OpenRouter’s head of insights reported that the seven-day average of Anthropic token volume on the platform had reached 3.6 times its level at the release. Asked about spending, he confirmed that was up too.

The October pickup is encouraging because both usage and spending are increasing. These data track activity through OpenRouter, including cached tokens with different economics. Walker said caching was already high before the increase.

Gavin Baker has raised the possibility of a $100B+ year-end revenue run rate. That’s his assessment, with the October run-rate field in the filing still blank. An image he shared estimates an October exit annualized spending pace through OpenRouter of roughly $1.9B to $3.3B, depending on caching assumptions.

Infrastructure

I still think the market puts too much weight on OpenAI and Anthropic as the scorecard for the whole AI opportunity.

For the companies supplying the labs, more end-customer revenue makes those spending commitments more credible. It can make the labs less dependent on the next financing round and give them more money to buy capacity.

Gavin also warned that a model market dominated by Anthropic and OpenAI could be bad for AI infrastructure. I think having a few labs doing most of the buying could give them more power over suppliers. A customer placing enormous orders can also ask for better terms.

If capacity is scarce or your product is hard to replace, you have more room to defend your pricing. If several suppliers can provide the same thing, revenue could grow while margins fall.

Profit

Q2 reported operating profit was $974M, including SBC. Adjusted operating profit was $2.24B.

Revenue grew faster than compute and infrastructure expense. Part of that came from earning more revenue from capacity they had already committed to. The chart below measures revenue per dollar of compute expense. Better use of the capacity and more revenue from it can improve that ratio alongside technical efficiency.

Using the separately disclosed cost of revenue, I calculate first-half gross margin of roughly 85.4%. Approximately 48% of first-half revenue came through cloud marketplaces, and channel-partner fees sit in sales and marketing. Model development and research, including internal usage, sit in R&D, which was $9.7B, or roughly 60% of revenue. Those are substantial costs below gross profit.

Models released in the preceding six months generated more than 80% of Q3 revenue. The pace of new releases helps explain why research is such a big part of the cost base. Anthropic has to keep producing models customers want to move to.

Most of the $80.7B first-half net loss came from changes in the value of financial instruments. First-half operating cash flow was positive $3.9B, helped by working-capital timing. Cash and short-term investments totaled $131.7B at June 30, after $110.4B of net preferred-stock proceeds during the half. That’s substantial liquidity, largely supplied by investors.

Cash

They only spent $482M on property and equipment in the first half, but a lot of their compute spending runs through contracts and leases. So that capex number leaves out a lot of what they’re committing to spend.

At June 30, leases due to start between 2026 and 2028 carried approximately $321.4B of estimated undiscounted obligations, some subject to contractual conditions. Their terms extend as long as 20 years. The filing separately lists $265.6B of expected fixed or minimum purchase commitments, primarily for cloud hosting and other infrastructure services. Finance-lease principal payments sit in financing cash flow, so the full infrastructure cash bill includes those payments alongside cash capex as the leases ramp up.

Approximately 80% of first-half revenue was consumption-based. Customers can change their usage much faster than Anthropic can change its long-term capacity commitments. If usage falls short, some of those bills will keep coming anyway.

The optional $42B Broadcom facility was undrawn at June and can only finance specified lease-rent payments. New borrowing can become more expensive and more dilutive if revenue-performance conditions are missed. Anthropic could also lose access to the facility if it keeps failing to meet the required conditions. Slower growth could leave it with less cash and worse borrowing terms at the same time.

Anthropic expects greater use of higher-cost, short-term compute to reduce operating margins in the near term. Its SpaceXAI arrangements reach approximately $3.1B per month once all capacity is delivered, expected in December. These allow termination on 90 days’ notice after initial non-cancelable periods ending in early 2027.

Overall, it’s nice to get this out of the way. Hopefully they show some positive October exit rates and keep the $100B dream alive.
