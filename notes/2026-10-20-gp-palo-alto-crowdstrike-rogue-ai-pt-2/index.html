SUMMARY

Overview

A cybersecurity executive discussed how artificial intelligence is accelerating vulnerability discovery, lateral movement, social engineering, and malicious code generation. Attackers increasingly use AI to create convincing emails, imitate legitimate IT activity, and exploit enterprise AI tools that have broad access to applications and data.

Enterprise priorities remain centered on discovering sanctioned and unauthorized AI across endpoints, cloud systems, SaaS applications, email, identities, and third parties. The next stage will involve monitoring AI actions, containing risky agents, and auditing their activity. No established vendor currently offers complete coverage.

Key Insights

Attack speed has shifted from days or months toward minutes, weakening security strategies that depend primarily on detection followed by human response. AI-generated scripts can closely resemble legitimate administration, making behavioral detection more difficult. Guardrails embedded within a model are insufficient. External isolation, restricted internet access, sandboxing, and automated containment are essential. Mid-sized organizations are generally still identifying AI usage, while larger enterprises are beginning to evaluate containment. The expert advised against long-term vendor commitments because technology and market leadership are changing rapidly. OpenAI and Anthropic could disrupt application-security and vulnerability-testing providers. However, major security platforms should benefit from rising demand for governance, telemetry, orchestration, and enforcement. Experienced security talent may become scarcer because AI magnifies expert productivity while newer practitioners may rely on model outputs without developing equivalent underlying expertise.
