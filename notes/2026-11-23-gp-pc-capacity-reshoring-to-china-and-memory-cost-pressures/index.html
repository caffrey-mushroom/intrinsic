SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a former employee of Taiwanese electronics manufacturers, discussed PC production moving from Southeast Asia to mainland China, the effects on contract manufacturers, and how major brands are managing rising component costs. The discussion also covered market competition, production timelines, and memory sourcing.

The expert expects low- and mid-range notebook production to increasingly move to mainland Chinese manufacturers, where lower costs and established supply chains are advantages. Trade policy and customer flexibility will influence how durable these shifts are.

Key Insights

The expert expects the shift to substantially reshape notebook manufacturing. Taiwanese ODMs may retain about 40% of orders, while mainland Chinese ODMs could capture the rest. Mainland Chinese ODMs compete aggressively on price, including by reducing or waiving tooling and engineering fees. The expert expects this to pressure Taiwanese manufacturers’ margins and accelerate their move toward servers and AI-related products. Capacity decisions are not necessarily permanent. The expert expects brands to preserve flexibility across manufacturers and locations, with trade relations a major determinant. Lenovo benefits from its mainland-based supply chain, purchasing scale, and previously secured memory inventory. However, the expert said its existing memory-price advantage may weaken when agreements expire. The moderator asked whether Lenovo’s margin stability could persist after those agreements end. The expert said this would depend partly on whether Lenovo adopts mainland-produced memory.
