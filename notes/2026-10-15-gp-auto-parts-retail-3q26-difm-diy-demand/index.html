SUMMARY

Overview

A privately held auto parts distributor with roughly 80 locations across five Midwestern states discussed 3Q26 aftermarket demand and the 4Q26 outlook. The business primarily serves professional repair shops, with less exposure to do-it-yourself customers than larger national competitors.

Demand accelerated sharply after a weak August. September performed particularly well despite unfavorable weather, while early October strengthened further. The expert believes both industry expansion and modest market-share gains contributed.

Key Insights

3Q26 exceeded expectations because of a strong finish, with sales, units, and transactions all growing. Professional and DIY demand followed similar trends, although professional demand remained slightly stronger. Consumers are moving from entry-level products toward mid-tier offerings, particularly in brakes. Deferred maintenance may be returning, as wear-related categories are growing faster than vehicle miles traveled. Private label is gaining acceptance, especially among professional installers, without necessarily implying lower quality or price. Availability and rapid delivery are viewed as the industry’s primary competitive advantages; price and brand matter less. The expert expects a strong 4Q26, supported by agricultural activity and core repair categories. Tools and equipment remain the principal risk. Gross profit is stable. Healthcare and wages remain cost pressures, but broader operating expenses appear controlled.
