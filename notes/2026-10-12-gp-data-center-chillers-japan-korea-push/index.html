SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed US data center cooling demand, the continued role of chillers, and the ability of Japanese and Korean HVAC manufacturers to compete with established US OEMs and integrated cooling specialists.

The expert expects data center construction and cooling demand to remain strong despite permitting, power-grid, and political concerns. Near-term regulatory pressure is viewed largely as temporary noise, although certain states may impose meaningful electrical-infrastructure constraints. Projects may shift toward more accommodating regions rather than disappear.

Water-cooled chillers are expected to remain central to hyperscale facilities because of efficiency, reliability, long service life, and rising rack densities. Air-cooled systems retain a role in smaller facilities, backup applications, lower-density environments, and non- rack spaces.

Key Insights

The expert strongly rejected the idea that chillers will become unnecessary. Chillers were characterized as the central and most technically sophisticated component of the cooling system. Reliability is the leading purchasing criterion, followed by aftermarket support, energy efficiency, and cost. Product bundling carries comparatively little weight. Large data center operators increasingly use internal technical teams to influence specifications and approved suppliers. OEMs must therefore act as trusted technical advisers rather than rely on conventional bidding channels. Asian OEMs can compete effectively, but uneven regional service capabilities are a major obstacle. National specialist teams capable of traveling between sites could help close this gap. Heat recovery may become an important design frontier, especially if data centers generate electricity onsite. Absorption chillers could regain relevance by converting waste heat into cooling. Future innovation will emphasize variable-speed systems, oil-free compressors, fewer moving parts, improved efficiency, heat reuse, and compliance with changing refrigerant rules.
