Agenda

  • Adoption trends for AI voice agents across healthcare front-office and patient access operations
  • Customer segmentation and the core sales pitch, including call capture vs cost-efficiency use cases
  • Competitive positioning of major players, including Assort Health, EliseAI and Hello Patient, highlighting key areas of differentiation
  • Unit economics of enterprise contracts – pricing model evolution, implementation scoping and miscellaneous operating costs
  • Susceptibility to EMR (electronic medical record) displacement across end markets and share defence strategies

Key Insights

  • c30% of patients immediately request a human agent, and after accounting for complexity and frustration drop-offs, only c50% of inbound calls can realistically be fully automated today
  • EliseAI's prior experience in real estate voice AI gives it a meaningful edge in patient engagement tactics, such as knowing when to escalate calls earlier to reduce frustration, which will aid its healthcare expansion effort
  • Electronic health record application programming interface costs are the stickiest expense in delivering AI-handled calls, as vendors are charged per electronic health record query on a usage basis, making comprehensive scheduling workflows increasingly expensive to operate
  • Solution expansion beyond inbound scheduling into outbound billing resolution, referrals and prior authorisations can roughly double the initial contract value, with outbound billing alone adds c25-35% uplift
  • Regarding consolidation trends, market will likely mirror ambient scribing dynamics, settling around five dominant players, as implementation credibility and long-term customer relationships become primary differentiators
