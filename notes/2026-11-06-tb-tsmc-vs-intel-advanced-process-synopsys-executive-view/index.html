原标题：Advanced Process Foundry Industry – TSMC & Intel – Senior Executive, Product at Synopsys Inc (Conducted in Mandarin)

#台积电 #英特尔 #先进制程

Third Bridge · 2026-11-06

核心摘要

  1. 专家称英特尔前道工艺长期落后台积电，但2025年下半年开始快速追赶，部分因为一名台积电副总加入英特尔。
  2. 18A已更名为18A-P，采用GAA和背面供电，整体性能优于台积电3nm。
  3. 受晶体管密度限制，18A-P达不到台积电2nm水平。
  4. 14A更名为14A-E，对标台积电2nm，凭SRAM密度和背面供电至少不逊色。
  5. 访谈还讨论了两家客户关系、人员变化以及英特尔新管理层的竞争举措。

Client-led • 26 Oct 2026

Companies: INTC US, 2330 TW, AAPL US, QCOM US, NVDA US, 4062 JP, IBM US

Expert Senior Executive, Product at Synopsys Inc

Agenda

  • Around 10 years' experience in the chip design industry, with a strong knowledge of EDA use in advanced process fields
  • Deep insights into the gap between TSMC and Intel in front-end and back-end technical processes, as well as the impact of personnel changes at both companies
  • Well-placed to discuss the relationship between TSMC and Intel’s clients, clients’ technology advancement in advanced process technology iterations and measures taken by Intel’s new management to enhance competitiveness

Questions

00:00:05] Has the gap between TSMC and Intel in advanced process widened or narrowed over the past 3-5 years or has it remained relatively stable?

Expert [00:00:05]: In terms of process, Intel had lagged behind TSMC in front-end process for a long time, but it began to catch up quickly in H2 2025. This was partly because a vice president of TSMC went to Intel and brought some know-how with him. From the perspective of actual operation, Intel’s semiconductor technical process, especially 18A-P. It was originally called 18A and now it is called 18A-P. 14A has become 14A-E. The progress of these two technical processes started from H2 last year, especially 18A-P. 18A-P is one of Intel’s most advanced semiconductor technical processes at present. Its benchmark process nodes are TSMC’s 3nm, but 18A-P is a FinFET, a GAA transistor configuration.

It also provides special wiring capabilities such as back power supply. At this time, its overall performance is better than TSMC’s 3nm. However, due to the gap in transistor density and special size, it cannot reach TSMC’s 2nm level, so it can be considered better than TSMC’s 3nm. 14A-E is benchmarked against TSMC’s 2nm chip. However, due to some characteristics of 14A-E, such as better SRAM density and back power supply capability, it is at least not inferior to TSMC. Its overall capabilities are a bit similar to TSMC’s A16 but between 16A and TSMC’s 2nm chip. Therefore, its overall semiconductor technical process level is now basically on par with TSMC’s capabilities, but there is still a gap between it and TSMC’s most advanced platforms starting from A14, 13 and 12.

However, this is currently planned on Intel’s roadmap. After all, TSMC has announced A14, so Intel is almost half a generation behind TSMC. However, there is little difference in market performance. There are no tape-out projects for advanced technical process equipment such as A14 in the world at present and relevant clients have just started designing. Therefore, although Intel doesn’t have it, it does not mean that it will suffer a loss in terms of supply capacity. From the perspective of technical process options available on the market, 3nm and 2nm are currently the most mainstream advanced nodes. For example, TSMC’s A16 won’t have the first batch of clients until 2028. The tape-out of A14 may not be mass-produced until a little later in 2028.

00:04:26] You said that TSMC’s current lead is about half a generation. What is its historical lead? Because I feel that historically, especially Intel has been stuck with 10nm for several years. Historically, TSMC’s lead should be greater than this, right?

Expert [00:04:26]: That’s right. Over the past 8-9 years, TSMC has iterated its technical process from 12nm to 7, 6, 5, 4 and 3nm every two years. From the perspective of technical process benchmarking, Intel’s 10nm is close to TSMC’s 12nm. That was when Intel just started to develop the FinFET process and encountered some manufacturing problems. However, Intel didn’t delay for that long because it took nearly four years to develop 10nm chips. It was very difficult but mass-produced. It is a mainstream mass production node. The next generation of Intel is Intel 7. Intel 7 is a very successful technical process, which can roughly benchmark against TSMC’s first-generation 7nm.

Intel 4 and 3 are similar to TSMC’s 5nm or 4nm process nodes. Of course, Intel’s technical process characteristics are a little different from those of TSMC. That is to say, the density of Intel transistors and SRAM is higher, but some line spacing distances are slightly rougher than that of TSMC. Intel is a bit inferior in terms of some special sizes such as pitch. However, Intel has a higher pure density of transistors. Anyway, they have different focuses on this aspect. Intel’s technical process serves its own CPU, which is a chip with high transistor density and relatively controllable wiring complexity.

However, TSMC has a large number of consumer-grade chips, especially those for mobile phones. Therefore, its technical process tends to be low power consumption and small area. Therefore, it has better wiring resources and parameters such as line spacing. Intel has always been inclined to HPC, but there is a big gap. Its front-end yield rate can only be stuck at the level of small silicon wafers with an area of no more than 150 square millimetres or 100 square millimetres in process nodes from 10 to 3.

The yield rate of each generation of TSMC’s technical process can reach about 90% or even higher when it is put into mass production. Later, Intel developed a back-end process called EMIB, which is built-in silicon bridge technology. It stitches together many small computing dies or I/O dies in the front end through small silicon bridges and then splices them into a relatively large chip. This makes up for the shortcoming of the front-end silicon wafer size with the back-end process. So this is a long-standing problem.

00:08:59] Before Intel accelerated its catch-up recently, what was the lead of TSMC? Was it one generation or one and a half generations?

Expert [00:08:59]: TSMC has been one and a half generations ahead of other players in the past 5-8 years. For example, when Intel was at the 12nm level, TSMC had already evolved to the 5nm range, which is basically about one and a half generations. During the most difficult period of 10nm, TSMC was basically two generations ahead and then gradually caught up with one and a half generations. By the time of Intel 3, it was still about one and a half generations. The 18A technical process basically only reduced its size to one generation. However, after the emergence of the 18A technical process, it evolved very hard from 2024 to 2025, so there was still a generation gap and it returned to one and a half generations. The backwardness of 18A has increased a little, but it made rapid progress last year and basically caught up.

Client [00:10:37]: You just said that in the same generation of technical process, Intel’s SRAM and transistor density are higher. I always thought TSMC’s transistor density would be higher.

Expert [00:10:37]: The transistor density we are talking about refers to the number of transistors that can be integrated per square millimetre. Although the theoretical transistor density of TSMC is not low, in fact, because TSMC’s design is more inclined to support logic itself, its wiring consumption is relatively large. It has a relatively high content of standard cells, so it requires more wiring resources. Intel’s design is pure CPU, which contains a large number of SRAM. SRAM can be customised to achieve high-density size. Therefore, strictly speaking, the density of transistors and logic units used in computing is not as high as that of TSMC, so its wiring resources are saved. After saving wiring resources, it can improve the utilisation rate. Therefore, the area utilisation rate of Intel chips is very high.

However, the wiring consumption of TSMC is relatively large. Therefore, TSMC can provide 15 or even 17 layers of high-metal layers at 5nm because it consumes a lot of wiring resources. Therefore, TSMC does better than others in this regard. It is not easy to improve the MET because it will increase the complexity and cost of exposure and etching. To put it bluntly, there is a difference between general technical process and special technical process. Strictly speaking, Intel is a kind of foundry that specialises in technical processes. Of course, it can be regarded as a general technical process in the era of 18A-P and 14A-E. However, it is still struggling to increase its yield rate in different application directions, but it has basically approached this goal. Previously, general technical processes were not considered special technical processes.

00:13:09] What do you think is the reason why Intel has been catching up quickly from last year to this year? Apart from some former employees of TSMC who have joined Intel, have you observed any obvious changes in other companies?

Expert [00:13:09]: I think there are several changes. The first is the management direction and strategy of foundries. When Pat was leading Intel, OEM business was a relatively low priority, but Pat was unwilling to divide it into other businesses. Pat believes that Intel should strictly adhere to the IDM system because it has been doing so for many years. Serving itself well and doing a good job in its own projects is a high priority for Intel.

Later, Lip-Bu came and made some drastic cuts to Intel, especially in the foundry sector. This frees up the constraints of foundries, because basically all the optimisation of the original departments of foundries needs to be done towards Intel’s own internal CPU optimisation direction. For example, they want to reduce power consumption or optimise some areas. They want to design special products for consumer-grade, high reliability and automotive grade applications. Now with the traction of external clients, they can start to try this.

Therefore, this is an important starting point for its current technical process progress because it has more external clients. Since the end of last year, Apple, Nvidia and Broadcom have been involved in helping Intel introduce external projects to quickly iterate its front-end support. This is an important reason. In addition, there is a VP named Luo from TSMC who plays a key role.

Another important change is that before 18A, High-NA EUV was required to solve the exposure accuracy problems of some key layers. Now 18A-P does not need high numerical aperture EUV and can be produced with traditional standard numerical aperture EUV. Based on this point, they are not the same technical process. The difference is huge. At present, 14A-E still retains the EUV exposure option for individual layers, but it is limited to some high-performance scenarios and application formulas. In consumer-grade formulas, 14A-E does not need EUV, that is, standard numerical aperture EUV can solve the problem, so this is an important progress. In addition, TSMC’s factories in the US are not far away from Intel’s factories. Therefore, they can learn from each other in terms of logistics. Intel has learned from TSMC and gradually caught up with it step by step.

00:20:52] You just mentioned that it has been improved because there are many external clients to help iterate the technical process. Do clients such as Nvidia, Apple and Broadcom cooperate with Intel because they want to be secondary suppliers themselves or is it a government requirement or both?

Expert [00:20:52]: I think it has both. In 2024, it started from the perspective of secondary supply. After the arrival of H2 Lip-Bu in 2025, it was a process of restoring both technical and confidence. Lip-Bu later reached a consensus with Donald Trump that the US government had invested in Intel’s shares. At this time, the US government began to promote cooperation between leading clients in the US and Intel from a political perspective, such as the revival of the US “MAGA”, the semiconductor industry and manufacturing industry. First of all, there are two companies that fully support this matter, one is Nvidia and the other is Apple. They have invested real money in their own projects.

The US government provides subsidies to every production order, which means that the US government pays a single part for you. In this way, it provides some pricing better than TSMC. This is to encourage companies to try this technical process. I think this is also correct. The US has realised the return of semiconductor manufacturing and learned from some industrial policies in China. This is a model, so it is the result of two-pronged approach.

Client [00:25:45]: However, I am confused about this. As far as I know, the development of advanced process nodes does not rely on a few people to separate OEM from products or even with the support of clients. I don’t think Intel can catch up with TSMC in this regard. Especially considering that Intel is a US-funded enterprise, there seems to be a big gap between it and TSMC in terms of corporate culture and employee competition.

Expert [00:25:45]: That’s right. However, one thing needs to be corrected. People are still very important. You can imagine that Liang Mongsong went to Samsung and brought an immediate improvement to Samsung. Later, he went to SMIC and made breakthroughs in N+1 and N+2 technologies immediately. It was within half a year. Semiconductors involve with parallel trial and error. For example, after a transistor is configured, there may be hundreds of possibilities or even thousands of combinations in terms of manufacturing technical process parameters. However, it takes a long time to verify it on the actual machine. Because transistors only have one layer and there are more than ten layers of metal on them. If all these are completed, it may take less than 2-3 months. This is the fastest process because you can’t get samples. This is just for verifying a formula. If there are dozens or hundreds of formulations, do you have so many EUV and machines that can be used only for R&D but not for production? Therefore, R&D and production are highly contradictory. If more investment is made in R&D, the production capacity will be affected. This has always been a contradiction for Intel.

But why does TSMC solve it well? TSMC adopts advanced virtual design and manufacturing technologies. It realises these complex trial-and-error processes in the virtual wafer foundry. The target configuration it can screen out are highly recommended before they are finally put into practice in the physical world. Therefore, its semiconductor evolution speed is very stable. However, Intel’s technical process development is very old-fashioned and still uses the previous physical iteration method. Therefore, it has been moving slowly. It is equivalent to some trial and error, but it just can’t find out. After the VP Luo took over, he was in charge of TSMC’s advanced technical process. Of course, he knew where to step on this point, so Intel soon made a breakthrough. This is indeed very important. However, there is no doubt that there are still some gaps in culture and efficiency.

00:37:35] How big is the gap between Intel and TSMC’s 2nm PDK [process design kit] in terms of ease of use and richness of IP classes?

Expert [00:37:35]: 18A-P is benchmarked against 3nm. At present, I think it can completely compete with TSMC in terms of IP richness. Both TSMC and Intel have their own IPs. Therefore, their IPs are highly comparable with each other and there is not much difference between them. However, their maturity is still a little bit behind in terms of mass production records. After all, its technical process has not yet fully entered the state of mass production, so it will take some time to verify its maturity.

Client [00:38:58]: How is the experience of clients using its PDK in EDA compared with that of TSMC? Is there a gap or is it almost the same?

Expert [00:38:58]: At present, the correlation of PDK has basically met the standard since H1 this year. There is no difference between it and TSMC. However, the overall comprehensive yield rate is still low due to the small number of iterations. It takes some time for them to achieve this goal.

Client [00:39:54]: Therefore, there is no gap between TSMC and Intel in the technical selection process of fabless clients at least in EDA and IP.

Expert [00:39:54]: Yes, there is almost no gap. Some clients even design their own IPs, such as Nvidia’s NVLink and other key PHYs, including its HBM high-speed interfaces. It is equivalent to porting these IPs from the version of TSMC to Intel with its PDK. Anyway, everyone is working on it. Basically, I don’t think there are any problems with the performance of IPs now. The porting is good. Yield mainly occurs when the size of silicon wafer is large and its body yield rate is low.

00:46:36] What other measures has Lip-Bu Tan taken to improve Intel’s OEM competitiveness after taking office? He separates OEM from products. In addition, what other measures have he taken in terms of corporate culture, employee management and operations?

Expert [00:46:36]: Another important thing that I didn’t mention is its back-end. Intel has always been weak in the front end, so after Lip-Bu came, it made full use of its strengths and avoided its weaknesses or even made up for its shortcomings, but now it is accelerating to realise its strengths. Intel has developed a back-end process called EMIB, which is similar to the CoWoS process. However, its overall tendency is to rely on multiple small dies to spell out a large chip. You can understand that there is also a yield rate loss because you rely on the back-end process to increase transistor density and complete Moore's law. However, if the yield rate of the back-end process is not good, it will actually pull down the already pitiful yield rate of the front-end process by one level, which is a big trouble. Therefore, Intel has spent much more energy on the EMIB-T process in the past 5-8 years than it did before.

If you look at Intel’s history over the past few decades, you will find that its real strength lies in the back-end process. Intel has always been a global leader in product packaging capabilities because CPU packaging itself is very advanced and complex. With the support of IBM, many advanced semiconductor product packaging technologies and testing technologies come from the joint cooperation between IBM and Intel. Therefore, EMIB-T has moved towards silicon bridge technology at an early stage, which happens to be a key technology for enabling large radical size and high multiple radical size panels.

TSMC chose organic high-density board solutions such as CoPoS and CoWoP instead of silicon bridge solution, let alone glass substrate solution. Intel has long been moving towards a hybrid solution of high-density HDI organic tandem and glass-based new board technology. It started with passive silicon bridges and later entered the era of active silicon bridges. Therefore, EMIB is now called -T, which is an active silicon bridge solution. This solution can enable high-speed signal interconnection between dies on a large scale. At present, this technology is absolutely leading in the world.

00:57:40] Is Intel’s back-end development helpful to its front-end development? Or does it depend more on the yield rate ramp-up in the front end?

Expert [00:57:40]: I think it is helpful, but everyone is realistic. A good back-end doesn’t mean that they must use a front-end one because the back-end and front-end are decoupled now. In essence, Amkor and ASE Technology have always provided pure back-end services similar to CoWoS. This is decoupled in nature, but it takes real skills for the front end to attract clients. The first is yield rate, which means good and comparable performance. Secondly, the yield rate should be high and the price should be appropriate. These are not achieved in a day or two. It needs long-term trial and error. It’s even a chicken-and-egg problem. The less people use your technical process, the lower its yield rate and no one uses it. This is a vicious circle.

00:59:05] Do you think TSMC has accelerated in any aspect recently? Steady progress is made in the front-end process, mainly including 3D stacking in the back-end process and exploration of silicon bridges. Is that right?

Expert [00:59:05]: That’s right. It is exploring silicon bridge now, but Intel has spent more than 5-6 years on it. Even if you factor in the relentless work ethic across Asia and how Chinese engineers push especially hard, you're still looking at a minimum three-year lead time. That's a realistic window. You don't just close the semiconductor gap overnight, so projecting that we'll roughly match Intel's current performance benchmark by 2028 is a grounded call, not baseless hype. However, I think 3D stacking is indeed a change. Why did TSMC start to pay attention to 3D stacking this year? The back-end process is to increase the logic density. If I can improve the logic density through the front-end semi-process such as 3D stacking, will it solve the problem? For example, if I/O dies and logic dies are bonded together, there is no need to expand the size of radical after vertical rise. In essence, this can also alleviate the problem.

Therefore, TSMC is quite smart. It thinks that 3D stacking is a shortcut. However, this is also difficult. 3D stacking needs to be verified by a large number of clients. In essence, the wafer-on-wafer process of 3D stacking has natural yield rate problems. The yield rate distribution on a wafer is random or may vary from one wafer to another. When two wafers are bonded, the upper part is good and the lower part is bad or vice versa. You can’t put all the bad parts together unless you separate them into die-on-wafer. However, the technical process of die on wafer is relatively expensive, even more expensive than the general CoWoS process in the back-end, so it is not an easy task. In addition, Intel also has the foveros 3D process. It originally had a 3D overlay process and this process is not behind TSMC but even relatively ahead of it. Intel has long achieved the mass production record of fovers 3D in the technical process of large-scale multi-die integrated CPU. Therefore, this is only a temporary solution to the problem and it needs to make up for what it should do.

01:02:07] Does TSMC feel the urgency to accelerate competition at present?

Expert [01:02:07]: As far as we can see, TSMC has not accelerated its pace in back-end R&D because the technical roadmap of TSMC’s back-end R&D is inclined to partners. Take Ibiden as an example. Therefore, the yield rate of CoPoS cannot be solved by TSMC itself because the back-end process is naturally a multi-party cooperation process. For example, Intel cooperates with Ibiden and Corning. It is a multi-party cooperation. Unlike the front-end process that can be solved by overtime alone, what’s the point of working overtime for the back-end process? The Japanese side doesn’t work overtime or follow the order, so you don’t have to worry about it. Therefore, TSMC has been looking for some local suppliers and material manufacturers such as Innolux to explore glass substrates. However, I think it will take at least one year before they can obtain preliminary qualification results.

Client [01:04:10]: You don’t think there is any acceleration in the front end, right?

Expert [01:04:10]: TSMC is a little too far ahead in the front end. However, the technical process is very expensive. At present, only leading clients in the world dare to use such an advanced technical process to produce chips, namely Apple, Nvidia and AMD. Other clients are taking a wait-and-see attitude. This is embarrassing. You are ahead in the front end, but there are fewer users because the cost is very high. Especially the mobile phone market, on which TSMC relies most for survival. At present, its shipment volume of semiconductor technical process equipment is the largest. It also cooperates with Qualcomm, Apple and Google. Although its shipment volume is low and it used to have some clients in China such as Unisoc and Xiaomi, the global mobile phone market has been very bad this year, with a decline of at least 20-30%. Therefore, the tape-out and wafer orders in the whole mobile phone market are being cut. In other words, although there is still demand for a new generation of semiconductor technical process in name, its iteration speed will definitely be much slower.

Apple won’t go to A16 or A14 easily, it may wait a little longer to absorb the existing costs. Therefore, TSMC’s leading position in the front-end market has become less important from the perspective of cost and global demand. Of course, HPC may still have demand for A16. Nowadays, all players are diversifying their business. It is hard to say what will happen in TSMC at least at this time of year.
