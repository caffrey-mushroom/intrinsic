SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined how federal and state regulation is shaping the PJM power market amid rapid data center demand, limited generation capacity, interconnection delays, and affordability concerns. The expert focused on the federal regulator’s rejection of key elements of PJM’s large-load interconnection and reliability procurement proposals, creating uncertainty over capacity procurement, curtailment obligations, and cost allocation.

Key Insights

PJM is expected to revise its reliability procurement proposal, but the process will likely be delayed. The procurement target may also decline because some forecast load has already exited the queue. Bilateral capacity contracts could offset most of the centralized procurement. Additional time may allow large-load customers to negotiate more agreements. The large-load interconnection framework faces a deeper problem because the federal regulator questioned PJM’s jurisdiction over retail customer curtailment. States may therefore assume a leading role. The expert did not view persistently rising capacity prices as inevitable. Interconnection reform, new transmission, state capacity requirements, and market redesign could reduce scarcity. Speed to power is the primary concern for data center developers. Projects unable to secure timely grid access may relocate, use temporary on-site generation, or operate as islanded facilities. Grid access and local permitting opposition were identified as the two most important constraints on future data center development. The expert considered a permanent capacity-price collar unsustainable, although a temporary extension may be politically possible.

Position and implications

PJM

Faces capacity shortages, generation queue bottlenecks, and uncertainty over its authority to impose curtailment requirements.

Federal regulator

Accepted the general need for reliability action but rejected aspects of cost allocation and questioned jurisdiction over retail loads.

State governments

Increasingly pursuing large-load tariffs, customer-funded infrastructure, capacity commitments, curtailment rules, and stricter permitting.

Data center developers

Prioritize rapid, reliable power access and generally resist inflexible curtailment. They may fund infrastructure or secure bilateral capacity.

Utilities

Can use specialized tariffs to reduce speculative interconnection requests and protect existing customers from large-load costs.

Other power markets

May attract projects displaced by PJM delays or restrictive local requirements.
