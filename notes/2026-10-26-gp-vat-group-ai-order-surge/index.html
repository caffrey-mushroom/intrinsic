SUMMARY

Overview

A former product manager at a leading vacuum-valve supplier discussed semiconductor demand, AI investment, manufacturing expansion, and pricing power. The supplier holds a near-monopoly position in critical vacuum valves and has increased its exposure to semiconductor-equipment customers.

The expert views the current recovery as healthy and expects favorable demand through 2027 and potentially 2028. However, greater semiconductor concentration could increase sensitivity to future industry cycles, customer concentration, regulation, and changes in data-center spending.

Key Insights

AI is broadening semiconductor applications and supporting continued investment in advanced logic and memory capacity. The expert sees no evidence of a major downturn through 2027, although growth may be approaching a plateau. Semiconductor cyclicality could become less predictable. Broader AI use may diversify demand, while inexperienced investors could amplify reactions to negative developments. Political and ethical rules governing AI in medicine, transport, law, and other areas may increasingly influence chip demand across regions. The supplier appears well equipped for downturns due to its strong market position, flexible cost structure, customer relationships, and history of protecting margins. Capacity expansion is more constrained in Switzerland by limited space, expensive labor, and competition for technical workers. Malaysia and Romania offer more room and lower costs. Pricing power remains strong because customers have few qualified alternatives and generally prioritize product reliability over price.
