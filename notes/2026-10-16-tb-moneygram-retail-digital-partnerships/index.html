Companies: WU US, WMT US, RELY US, IMXI US

Armando J Cruz (AJC) Co-head, Corporate Sales at Viamericas Corp

Jonathan King (JK) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • MoneyGram's retail footprint, digital migration opportunity and economics of shifting customers towards higher-margin digital transactions
  • Competition across key Mexico and Central America corridors, including pricing, exchange rates and revenue per transaction
  • How MoneyGram can win and expand partnerships through competitive pricing, reliable service and broad corridor coverage
  • Emerging digital channels such as mobile links and WhatsApp, and MoneyGram's ability to adapt to changing customer behaviour

Key Insights

  • MoneyGram's shift to >70% digital transactions risks masking retail distribution weakness, as major contract losses, including Canada Post, threaten transaction volumes, revenue projections and remaining network economics
  • Smaller providers such as Viamericas and Intermex adjust FX rates multiple times daily, while MoneyGram and Western Union face slower internal decision-making, risking margin erosion without meaningful customer retention gains
  • Agent-branded WhatsApp transfers using QR codes, pursued by Viamericas and Ria, offer an omnichannel model that preserves agent attribution, enabling digital convenience, potentially sustaining retail distribution over the next 2-3 years
  • MoneyGram's independent recovery could become increasingly difficult over the next 3-5 years as agent losses and FX-driven margin compression persist, making acquisition a more viable path to stabilising the business than standalone operations
  • Western Union's multi-brand strategy, spanning Orlandi Valuta and Vigo alongside Intermex, could strengthen distribution reach and customer segmentation, enabling competition across retail networks through multiple consumer-facing brands
