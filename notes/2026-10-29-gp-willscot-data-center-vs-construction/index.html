SUMMARY

Overview

The modular-building market is experiencing strong demand despite weakness in general construction. Data centers, renewable- energy projects, and larger construction sites are absorbing most available multi-unit buildings. Supply is constrained by long factory backlogs and manufacturing consolidation, supporting higher rental rates and utilization.

The expert expects the leading national lessor to benefit from its scale and available fleet, although its large inventory of lower- demand single-wide units and sales-force weaknesses may keep performance below peers.

Key Insights

Recent demand exceeded the expert’s expectations, with strong quoting, leasing activity, and advance planning. Data centers represent a relatively small portion of installed rentals but approximately 30% of current industry activations. This could rise to 35%. The expert believes the data-center expansion cycle may continue for another five to seven years. Larger complexes are approaching historically high utilization, while single-wide units remain flat and weigh on industry averages. New units are frequently assigned to customers immediately after production. Some suppliers have turned customers away because suitable inventory is unavailable. The leading lessor should report solid near-term results, with stronger financial momentum expected from the following quarter onward. Operational improvements at the leading lessor are progressing, but its sales organization, retention, and compensation structure remain significant weaknesses. Pricing discipline is strong, with sufficient demand limiting aggressive competition.
