Key Takeaways

  • Core ads. Advertising revenue grew 25.9% y/y to $63.1B vs. Street $61.7B, with the clearest acceleration in U.S./Canada, where growth reached ~33% from ~31% last quarter. Global pricing increased 14% vs. Street 11%, more than offsetting impressions growth of 10% vs. Street 11%. Aggregate reported ad growth slowed from 27.5%, while FX-neutral ad growth held around 26%; the positive acceleration argument rests on UCAN and the stronger two-year growth stack. Mgmt cited better ad performance and improved macro conditions versus last year.
  • AI returns. The evidence that AI improves the existing advertising business continues to build. Advertisers adopting Business Assistant recommendations generated nearly 5% more conversions at similar cost per conversion and increased spending 9% versus comparable non-adopters. GEM expansion lifted app-ad conversions 9.6%, while nearly 10M advertisers used a generative creative tool. Migrating content-understanding models to the Muse family produced better offline results with >80% lower processing costs per item.
  • Muse adoption. More than 10M people in the U.S./Canada set up agents within roughly six weeks, with ~25% using Muse on any given day and retention improving among earlier cohorts. Shopping is the leading use case, engaging >25% of weekly actives, with average purchases above $200, including travel. The commercial intent is arguably more relevant than the setup count: users are already trusting Muse with transactions.
  • Monetization. Mgmt intends to monetize Muse earlier than its historical playbook because both the serving costs and revenue opportunity are substantial. Planned streams include transaction fees paid by businesses, subscriptions for advanced models or additional usage, business subscriptions, and optional connections to content and advertising experiences. The ambition for Muse ARPU to exceed several hundred dollars annually is a long-term expectation benchmarked against what those users already generate across the existing apps. It is not current Muse revenue. New AI businesses should begin contributing incremental revenue in 2027.
  • Agent network. META disclosed at least 500M self-identified small businesses using its apps, giving it substantial distribution for business agents alongside 3.64B daily consumers. The near-term enterprise priorities are model APIs, Muse Code and business agents; the longer-term opportunity connects consumer and business agents across discovery, purchases and service. Mgmt expects commerce to move toward authenticated APIs, approved partnerships and direct agent interactions.
  • Other revenue. FoA other revenue increased 59% to $1.10B vs. Street ~$1.08B, driven by WhatsApp paid messaging and subscriptions. Partnership ads exceeded a $15B annual revenue run rate, growing >97% y/y, while Meta One surpassed 15M subscriptions and trials.
  • Compute economics. META is building capacity to serve >1B people over the next few years and targets >10x improvement in Muse compute cost-efficiency over the next year. Smaller models, distillation, inference optimization and better utilization of spare capacity underpin the roadmap. Profitability still requires revenue to reach scale, and mgmt explicitly expects total serving costs to rise as adoption expands. The efficiency target concerns the cost of delivering intelligence; it does not establish a decline in aggregate spending.
  • Margins. Operating income excluding the settlement was $22.9B, +11% y/y and ~13% above Street, but the 35.4% margin declined ~470bps y/y. Underlying expenses increased ~36%, outpacing revenue growth of 26%, as infrastructure, depreciation, cloud and third-party token costs absorbed the advertising gains. Headcount fell ~12% q/q, but labor efficiency has not offset the broader investment ramp.
  • Budget. FY26 expenses rose to $175–179B vs. Street ~$177.2B, almost entirely reflecting the $9.9B settlement charge; the underlying range is effectively unchanged from $165–169B. For 2027, mgmt expressed confidence in strong overall revenue growth but gave no numerical revenue, expense or capex guidance and made no operating-income growth commitment. More detail is expected with the Q4 call.
  • Capex. FY26 capex is now $135–145B, with the $140B midpoint close to Street $139.8B and the $140.5B buyside survey bogey. The floor increased $5B; the ceiling stayed unchanged. Q3 capex of $34.9B was below the roughly $42–42.5B expected, helping FCF come in at negative $2.1B vs. Street negative $8.5B. However, the annual guide mathematically implies roughly $49–59B of Q4 capex, or ~$54B at midpoint.
  • Reality Labs. FY26 losses are expected at $18–19B, midpoint $18.5B vs. Street ~$18.5B, falling to ~$15B in 2027, also around Street. That creates approximately $3.5B of spending capacity, but mgmt explicitly plans to redirect investment primarily toward AI.
  • Funding. The new Class C non-voting stock will be distributed one-for-one to existing shareholders, effectively creating a two-for-one split, with trading expected in late January. The distribution itself does not dilute relative economic ownership; subsequent external issuance would.

3Q Revenue: $6.06B vs. Street $5.93B and bogey ~$6.05–6.1B 3Q GM: 64.2% vs. Street 61.6% and bogey ~62.5% 3Q EPS: $2.62 vs. Street $2.43 and bogey ~$2.50 3Q FCF: $2.96B vs. Street $2.26B 4Q Revenue Guide: $5.80–6.30B ($6.05B midpoint, flat q/q) vs. Street $5.76B and bogey ~$5.95B 4Q EPS Guide: $2.45–2.79 ($2.62 midpoint) vs. Street $2.31 and bogey ~$2.40

Key Takeaways

  • Demand. The recovery broadened across end markets and regions, with modestly extending lead times and backlog consistent with the stronger demand environment. Mgmt believes TXN remains competitively positioned on delivery times. Personal electronics was approximately flat y/y and up upper single digits q/q, while communications equipment grew both sequentially and y/y.
  • Industrial. Revenue increased more than 40% y/y and approximately 10% q/q, only just exceeding the previous 3Q22 peak. Mgmt’s argument against calling the cycle exhausted is that four years of semiconductor-content growth have expanded the opportunity per system, while some industrial subsectors remain below their previous peaks. Aerospace and defense, energy infrastructure, test and measurement, and robotics are the strongest structural opportunities.
  • AI. Data-center revenue grew approximately 95% y/y and 20% q/q, reaching roughly 13% of company revenue. Its limited seasonality is helping offset the usual year-end decline elsewhere. AI exposure also extends into industrial energy infrastructure and test equipment, so the direct data-center category understates the breadth of the opportunity, although mgmt did not quantify total direct-plus-indirect exposure.
  • Automotive. Revenue increased mid-teens y/y and approximately 10% q/q, led by China and the U.S. Customers are increasing production from lean inventories; mgmt explicitly said it does not see inventory building up. Hybrid, electric and powertrain systems grew approximately twice as fast as the overall automotive business. Higher-content Chinese export vehicles are another favorable mix factor.
  • Pricing. Pricing was flat y/y in 1H26 and turned modestly positive in 2H26, primarily in analog where customers requested more urgent supply. Mgmt characterized the contribution as small for 2026. Annual negotiations for 2027 are underway, with customers’ supply requirements influencing pricing discussions; January is the next substantive update. .
  • Margins. The margin surprise demonstrated substantial operating leverage before a large pricing reset. Sequential incremental gross margin was approximately 90%, helped by revenue, loadings, mix and pricing. Mgmt retained its longer-term 75–85% incremental gross-profit framework excluding depreciation, best assessed over a longer period rather than quarter to quarter.
  • Embedded. Mgmt sees embedded’s cyclical recovery as roughly one year behind analog’s. Embedded gross margin has improved approximately 10 percentage points y/y, with recent gains of a couple of points per quarter.
  • Capacity. Existing cleanrooms and inventory are providing an advantage as industry supply tightens. TXN is ramping its new Malaysia assembly-and-test site because external partners are not supplying all the capacity it wants. Equipment deliveries and qualifications still govern the pace of expansion, particularly in the back end. Mgmt distinguishes these execution issues from the much longer constraint of having to build new cleanrooms.
  • CapEx. CY26 spending remains $2–3B, with an ambition to spend toward the high end if equipment arrives as planned. February’s capital allocation update will address spending for different growth scenarios.
  • Cash flow. Company-defined trailing FCF reached $8.4B versus $2.4B a year earlier, but includes $1.6B of CHIPS Act incentives comprising investment tax credits and direct funding. The recovery in cash generation is substantial; its sustainable level depends on future capital intensity and the incentive contribution.
  • Robotics. Mgmt cited approximately $500–$1,000 of TXN content per humanoid and expects stronger ramps in 2027. This is useful evidence of potential content expansion in industrial, which mgmt describes as its most attractive market for diversity, longevity and margins.

Bull vs Bear Debate

The broader bull thesis is that TXN is entering a period in which a cyclical recovery, rising semiconductor content and the benefits of its manufacturing investments reinforce one another. Industrial is only just back above its previous revenue peak despite four years of content expansion, automotive customers remain lean on inventory, and embedded is recovering later than analog. That combination provides several sources of growth beyond an immediate inventory catch-up. TXN’s broad portfolio, long-lived design positions and internal manufacturing capacity can also make it a preferred supplier when customers prioritize reliable deliveries. Its 300mm manufacturing advantage is tangible: TXN estimates approximately 40% lower cost for an unpackaged chip versus production on 200mm wafers, supporting the longer-term margin opportunity as more production moves internally.

AI adds a faster-growing content opportunity. TXN’s exposure spans power conversion, telemetry, sensing, cooling and other foundational components across compute and networking systems. Higher power density and more complex infrastructure can expand content per system, while application-specific products provide another route to participation. Industrial energy infrastructure and test equipment benefit from the same investment cycle. The quarter strengthened the bull case on operating leverage, demand breadth and the usefulness of TXN’s manufacturing capacity. The margin improvement was particularly encouraging because the pricing contribution remained modest, leaving a potential additional earnings driver for 2027. Automotive demand and the later embedded recovery also broadened the sources of growth.

The bear thesis is that TXN can deliver rising earnings while its multiple compresses as the cycle matures. Industrial has returned to its previous peak, automotive demand is accelerating and supply is tightening, all of which raise the question of how much recovery remains. Mgmt’s content-growth argument is credible, but the call did not provide a quantified bridge separating end-unit demand, content gains, pricing and share gains. Broader industry checks also point to double ordering and longer lead times, although TXN itself did not disclose double ordering. Bears therefore see a risk that investors capitalize temporarily strong demand and margins as a permanently higher growth rate.

The AI thesis has limits as well. Direct data centers remain a minority of revenue, while some of the industrial strength depends on the same infrastructure investment cycle, reducing the diversification those categories might initially suggest. Existing data-center products are described as earning roughly corporate-average margins; more application-specific sockets could face greater price competition and potentially dilute mix. China-based competition and localization remain longer-term pricing and share risks, particularly after supply tightness eases. This quarter does not demonstrate a new competitive loss, but it also does not establish that future pricing increases or share gains will be durable.

MU: Taoyuan workers begin 24-hour strike – Nikkei Asia

Nikkei Asia reports Micron workers in Taoyuan began a 24-hour strike over profit sharing. A separate Taichung union reached an agreement Wednesday covering higher pay and improved benefits; Micron says that package applies across its Taiwan operations. Taiwan produces more than 60% of Micron’s DRAM, with Taoyuan focused mainly on DRAM and Taichung also producing HBM.

MU / Memory: Stays with 6F² and 3D DRAM despite CXMT’s 4F² push – Nikkei Asia

Nikkei Asia reports Micron has no plans to adopt 4F² memory-cell technology, according to CTO Scott DeBoer. Micron is continuing with 6F² development and 3D DRAM. DeBoer said 4F² brings additional complexity and cost without necessarily improving density, because capacitor spacing remains a constraint.

Reuters reports Anthropic launched Claude Duet, a proactive assistant for professionals and small businesses that handles tasks such as briefings, scheduling and forecasting. Powered by Opus 5.5, it connects to more than 6,000 services and supports payments through Stripe Link and Shop Pay. A waitlisted research preview is available to U.S. Pro and Max subscribers.

AI Agents: Banks block unauthorized account access – Axios

Axios reports major U.S. banks are detecting third-party agents inside customer accounts, including attempts to act independently of user instructions. Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are restricting agent access. Banks generally favor read-only access but are struggling to distinguish user-directed activity from unauthorized automation.
