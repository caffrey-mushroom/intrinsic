Agenda

  • PCB (printed circuit board) demand trends, capacity expansion plans and progress across suppliers and supply tightness changes
  • Raw material and imported equipment shortages and Nvidia supply chain landscape
  • PCB market competitive landscape shifts, plus PTFE orthogonal backplane solutions

Key Insights

  • Expert believes that although PCB manufacturers are making huge CAPEX, material and equipment bottlenecks mean effective capacity additions will lag behind paper plans, and the supply-demand gap will not close quickly in 2027. The limited upstream supply of fine glass fibre, high-end copper foil and Mitsubishi laser drilling machines is the core bottleneck constraining supply
  • Expert expects the PCB price rally to move in tandem across high-end and low-end segments, with price increases likely to persist at least until the end of 2027. The current round of price increases stems from a structural shift in CCL capacity. High-end speciality materials such as PTFE and low-Df glass fibre have long capacity expansion cycles, and raw material shortages keep board prices rising. Wus Printed Circuit's high-end product mix provides stronger gross margin resilience
  • Nvidia's PTFE solution for orthogonal backplanes has a very high probability of being implemented, and Shengyi Technology is the key beneficiary of PTFE boards. Among the three technical solutions, “PTFE core board plus glass-fibre-reinforced PP” is most likely to become mainstream. Shengyi has a complete range of material solutions and significantly leads its Taiwanese competitors in production capacity. Shengyi Electronics is expected to enter Nvidia's supply chain by leveraging its parent company's board material resources

00:54:21] What is Shengyi Technology's PTFE [polytetrafluoroethylene] supply solution? That is, some PTFE layers are embedded in high-layer-count boards. Is this understanding correct?

Expert [00:54:31]: Yes. Finally, I will talk about the PTFE solution. In fact, the customer is currently testing a total of three PTFE solution options, all of which use glass-fibre-free PTFE. The first option is PTFE plus hydrocarbon prepreg. PTFE acts as the core board, and the hydrocarbon PP is used for bonding. The PP contains glass cloth, specifically second-generation glass cloth. The second option is PTFE plus hydrocarbon. The hydrocarbon PP has no glass-fibre cloth. The third option is PTFE plus PTFE PP. The first two options both use hydrocarbon prepregs, while the third option uses a PTFE core board plus PTFE prepreg. In total, three options are being tested. In fact, the first option, PTFE with cloth, has performed better in testing so far. The difference between having cloth and not having cloth is that, with cloth, dimensional stability is slightly better.

The PTFE solution is mainly intended to solve two problems: one is the signal problem. PTFE's Dk and Df values are superior to those of both M9 and M10. It has the lowest Df loss and a Dk value of 2.1, which is also the best. From a performance perspective, PTFE has significant advantages. However, PTFE also has obvious disadvantages: because it contains no glass fibre, its thermal expansion and contraction, CTE value, and dimensional change will be relatively large. During PCB processing, if the deformation is large, the board will be difficult to fabricate.

Currently, these three solutions have their own advantages and disadvantages. For example, the first PTFE solution, hydrocarbon PP with cloth, has the best processability and relatively good dimensional stability. The second solution has the best performance because it contains no cloth, which can affect the signal. The Dk and Df values of glass cloth are much higher than those of PTFE, so mixing it in will affect the signal. The signal performance of the third solution is also relatively good, but it contains no glass fibre. The dimensional stability of PTFE plus PTFE is also relatively poor, but its performance is the best.

Currently, it may ultimately be up to the customer to decide which of the three solutions is used in which scenario. For example, GPU boards and ASIC chip boards have very large chips and require high dimensional stability. Therefore, it would be better to use cloth-based materials, as they offer good dimensional stability. For boards without chips, such as mid-boards, dimensional stability requirements are not particularly high, but their signal transmission performance is relatively good. Ultimately, different solutions may be chosen based on different application scenarios.

However, as of now, the PTFE solution is very likely to be adopted and will definitely be used. As for how much to use and in which scenarios, it may be necessary to choose from among the three solutions.
