SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed industrial robots and collaborative robots in US consumer packaged goods and food and beverage production. Automation demand is expected to increase, but adoption depends on investment, suitable integration partners, and the challenge of engineering equipment for food-processing conditions. Older facilities often lack space, making compact systems appealing, while high-throughput and washdown applications often require industrial robots or extensive customization.

Key Insights

The expert expects broader automation adoption over the next few years. Palletizing, packing, and warehouse material handling are likely starting points because they are comparatively straightforward to deploy. Cobots can fit confined spaces and support human-robot work, but their lower throughput can limit suitability for demanding production. High payload alone does not guarantee a fence-free application; safety assessments remain necessary. Standardized, pre-engineered cells appeal to customers seeking predictable cost, delivery, and performance. They work best for repeatable tasks, but adapting them beyond their defined scope can add cost and uncertainty. Food-grade coatings and ingress protection may suit light overspray, but the expert cautioned that they are not a durable substitute for stainless steel in frequent, chemically aggressive washdowns. Reliability and longevity outweigh a lower initial price. AI tools may save time on basic picking and packing, but the expert expects production use to require human validation. Complex food-processing tasks still depend on specialist engineering, and the competitive effect of multiple AI partnerships remains uncertain.
