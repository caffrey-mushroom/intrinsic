SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined how tighter insurance practices are affecting residential roof claims, contractor activity, and shingle volumes. The expert works in roofing and independent claim appraisal, helping determine repair scope and costs.

Appraisal applies mainly to disputes over the value or required scope of repairs, not disagreements over policy coverage. Each side appoints an independent appraiser and jointly selects an experienced umpire to resolve any deadlock.

The expert described a broad shift toward partial repairs, higher deductibles, slower payments, stricter documentation, and policies that limit reimbursement based on roof age. These trends make full roof replacements harder to secure and reduce insurance- funded roofing demand.

Key Insights

The expert cited industry training indicating that more than 90% of adjuster-prepared roofing estimates may be inaccurate and undervalued. Insurers increasingly use field photographers rather than licensed adjusters. Remote personnel may then make decisions without visiting the property or having construction expertise. Carriers often begin with limited shingle repairs even when roof age, discontinued materials, or repair damage may justify replacement. Appraisal generally produces a fairer loss valuation but can add substantial delays, sometimes reaching six months before work begins. Higher deductibles discourage claims when expected repair costs are near or below the homeowner contribution. Financial pressure compounds the issue: borrowers increasingly need financing, while credit requirements have tightened. The expert expects fewer claims and replacements under comparable storm conditions as insurers continue tightening processes and policy terms. Weather variability prevents a reliable market-wide volume estimate.
