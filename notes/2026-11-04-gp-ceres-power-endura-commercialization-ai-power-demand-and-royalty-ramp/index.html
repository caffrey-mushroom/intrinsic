SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed prospects for licensee-built solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) systems, particularly technology licensed by Ceres, for data centers and other power users. Permitting and community opposition may remain obstacles in the US and Europe for at least another year, while grid interconnection queues have no clear near-term remedy. Behind-the-meter and islanded power systems may help buyers bypass those delays.

The expert expects the US to ease permitting constraints before Europe, citing a stronger commercial focus and urgency around AI infrastructure. However, early licensee orders are more likely in Asia, especially South Korea and Taiwan.

Key Insights

Commercial and industrial buyers are expected to make the earliest smaller volume commitments, followed by neoclouds and hyperscalers. Defense is a less predictable but potentially fast-moving buyer. The expert believes meaningful Korean orders have likely already occurred, based on a licensee’s reported manufacturing scale-up; Taiwan may follow. US and European licensee demand appears less certain. Delta is viewed as best positioned to reach high annual sales, given its 800-volt power architecture and potential alignment with emerging data-center designs. Access to a major AI-computing ecosystem could be crucial to turning that capability into orders. Licensee system pricing is expected to initially be comparable to Bloom’s. The expert does not anticipate a price war, given the size of the potential market. Bloom’s established product availability and field history are major advantages. Licensee technology still needs to demonstrate performance in commercial operation. Time to power can outweigh equipment cost: the expert believes even a several-month delivery advantage may justify substantial additional capital expenditure. Factory-built modular systems are considered the biggest route to improved project economics, by reducing on-site labor needs, construction costs, and execution risk. Rapid cost reductions by 2028 are uncertain without substantial volume. The expert points to the long periods established fuel- cell manufacturers needed to improve margins.
