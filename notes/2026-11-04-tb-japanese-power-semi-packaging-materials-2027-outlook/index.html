核心摘要

  1. 受访者是贺利氏前业务发展经理，讨论电动车功率半导体封装材料。
  2. 专家预计2026年材料需求同比增长20%，主要由电动车等车辆增产带动，2027年起每年增长10%至20%。
  3. 碳化硅芯片价格大幅下跌至原来的约10%，封装材料降价压力随之加大，银价上涨只能部分通过成本联动定价传导。

  7. 日本仍以焊料为主流贴片方式，欧洲和中国普遍用银烧结，银价飙升正推动中国加速转向低成本铜烧结；加压烧结材料McDermid约占60%、贺利氏约40%。

Agenda

  • Japanese power semiconductor materials demand outlook
  • Competitive landscape and market share trends in the metal-ceramic substrate segment – Ferrotec (TYO: 6890), Denka (TYO: 4061), NGK (TYO: 5333) and Mitsubishi Materials (TYO: 5711) vs global competitors
  • Raw material and powder procurement conditions and supply chain trends – Mitsubishi Materials, Sumitomo Metal Mining (TYO: 5713) and Resonac (TYO: 4004)

Key Insights

  • Materials demand to rise 20% YoY in 2026, driven by increased production of EVs and other vehicles; 10-20% annually from 2027. With silicon carbide chip prices plummeting to 10%, pressure to reduce packaging material prices intensifying. Some of the silver price increase is passed on through cost-linked pricing
  • Niterra monopolises silicon nitride substrates in Japan; in China, Maruwa and local firms. Active metal brazing substrate market is 40% Ferrotec, 20-25% Rogers and 15% Niterra. Chinese players are a decade behind, but making headway in replacing Japanese products with low prices
  • Wiring materials market split between Tanaka Kikinzoku and Heraeus; Heraeus stronger due to China sales network and the copper wire shift
  • Solder remains the mainstream die attachment method in Japan due to costs, while silver sintering is typical in Europe and China. Shift towards low-cost copper sintering is accelerating, driven by China, which faces fierce price competition due to soaring silver prices. McDermid holds 60% of pressurised sintered materials market and Heraeus 40%

00:01:26] How has demand for power semiconductor packaging materials for EVs – such as printed circuit boards, wires, bonding materials and thermal management materials – trended over the past one year?

Expert [00:01:26]: Over the past year, 2025-26, the EV market has been in a state of stagnation – 'stagnation' is the best way to put it. Whilst the EV market had been growing very strongly since around 2023, the stagnation in the European EV market in particular has become increasingly apparent. This is because the European EV market had been expanding rapidly with the aim of targeting markets such as China and the United States, but this expansion has now come to a sudden halt. Furthermore, the European market itself has come to a screeching halt – a point frequently highlighted in industry circles. Companies such as the Volkswagen Group and the Mercedes Group have rapidly scaled back their EV-related manufacturing and development activities. Meanwhile, in the US market, the Trump administration's reversal of the shift towards EVs – essentially based on the view that, whilst there is no protection for fossil fuels, they will continue to be sufficient – has placed a significant brake on EVs, particularly those centred around Tesla.

As for the Chinese market, the proportion of EVs has risen significantly. However, a closer look at the figures reveals that a considerable number of these are plug-in hybrid electric vehicles, centred on manufacturers such as BYD; in terms of pure electric vehicles, there is still room for growth, although the number of EVs is now very high. However, given the overall market sentiment and economic conditions in China, which are extremely unfavourable, the expected growth has not materialised. Against this backdrop, as the EV market is not developing as initially anticipated, I believe there is a significant sense of oversupply.

00:04:32] Is demand for other components, such as wires and bonding materials, holding steady at a similar level to that of printed circuit boards?

Expert [00:04:32]: There are various factors at play here, so it's difficult to generalise. For example, looking at specific items: regarding wire materials and bonding wires, there is aluminium, as well as clad materials – that is, aluminium coated with copper. Then there is pure copper. In addition to these, there are lead-frame-type materials, such as block materials. These are the types available. One trend is certainly a shift from aluminium to copper wherever possible.

This is because current density is increasing; when considering temperature ranges, there is a prevailing view that copper is preferable to aluminium, so there is a drive to move towards copper as much as possible. Furthermore, if we wish to increase current density even further, there is a tendency to move away from wires towards lead frames or block-type materials; broadly speaking, the trend is towards increasingly thicker and heavier components.

However, cost is another factor to consider. As the price of copper rises to this extent, there is a countertrend favouring the use of aluminium rather than copper; I believe this is an area where the overall picture is not yet entirely clear. Therefore, broadly speaking, whilst performance considerations favour the use of copper, cost considerations suggest a desire to reduce its use; I believe this is the reality facing many power module manufacturers at present.

00:12:14] With the price of 6-inch silicon wafers plummeting due to increased production by Chinese manufacturers, the cost ratio of packaging materials has become more prominent as chips have become cheaper. Are there any requests for price reductions?

Expert [00:12:14]: Yes. It's not a matter of a specific percent, but as you mentioned, this applies to both Si and SiC. Over the past year or so, the price of SiC in particular has plummeted – I believe it has fallen to about one-seventh or one-eighth of its previous level, on a wafer basis. Consequently, in a structure where, for example, there is a heat sink at the bottom, followed by thermal interface material, a heat-dissipating substrate, then the mounting adhesive – such as solder or sintered materials – on top of that, with the chip mounted on it, and finally a lead frame or bonding wires on top, the price of SiC was by far the largest cost factor within this structure three or four years ago. I imagine it probably accounted for about half. If this has fallen to one-tenth, other components naturally come to the fore, so there is undoubtedly a demand to keep the overall cost as low as possible and to use cheaper materials.

KM [00:12:14]: When the price of SiC fell to one-seventh or one-eighth of what it was, to what extent did this affect the materials?

Expert [00:12:14]: Well, it's not simply a case of prices falling across the board. Take silicon nitride substrates, for example, or alumina substrates. The prices for these materials have stabilised to a certain extent. It's not a matter of saying, "Since the price of Si or SiC has fallen to this level, please reduce this price by this much." Instead, various suppliers – for instance, those dealing in SiN, or silicon nitride – are actively promoting their products. Consequently, when comparing prices across the board – for instance, if a product is currently selling at 100, the question becomes: "Would you accept 90? Or 95?" – that's the general trend. However, as things stand – and this applies to any company – there are cases where the modules are already fully manufactured. To be honest, even if you have a supplier, say Supplier A, and Supplier B offered a 20% discount, it wouldn't be possible to switch suppliers.

This is due to the issue of certification; approval must be obtained. As this process involves the car manufacturers as well, it inevitably takes one, two or even three years. Consequently, switching suppliers purely on the basis of price – to put it bluntly – is impossible, even if the new supplier were to offer the modules for free. What happens, then, is that the discussion shifts to the next generation – for example, committing to supply at a certain cost for designs three years down the line – so it no longer becomes a matter of direct cost comparison.

00:16:35] I understand that silver prices are rising. Given the balance of power in price negotiations, to what extent do you think the increase is being passed on to the selling price of power semiconductor materials?
