Agenda

  • Extent to which engine OEMs are over-earning with unsustainable margins
  • Airlines' ability to push back on MRO (maintenance, repair and overhaul) price increases
  • Outlook for MRO work scope and engine mix impact on margin

Key Insights

  • Engine MRO margins are "overinflated", but unlikely to reverse over the long term. OEMs have effectively monopolised the market, leaving airlines with little negotiating power regardless of dissatisfaction with pricing
  • New-generation engines such as the geared turbofan and LEAP are fundamentally less robust than legacy CFM56s and V2500s, suggesting recurring reliability issues throughout their operational lives, not just teething problems
  • Legacy independent MROs such as SR Technics face a slow decline as new engine contracts flow exclusively to OEMs – survival window is c4-5 years before legacy fleet retirements accelerate. OEMs now strategically gatekeep the aftermarket, unlike the 1990s, where simpler engines allowed independents to thrive
  • Annual MRO price hikes are expected to normalise back to 5-6% from double-digits, as supply chains stabilise post-pandemic and airlines resist repeated above-inflation increases
  • Ryanair's announced engine MRO facilities are likely negotiating a bluff – operational complexity of OEM dependency and work stoppages awaiting approvals make genuine in-house engine overhaul implausible
