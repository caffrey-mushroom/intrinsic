SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a long-time data center cooling industry participant, discussed equipment supply, manufacturing strategies, and evolving cooling technologies. The market remains busy, with demand spanning air cooling, chillers, cooling distribution units (CDUs), and liquid cooling. The discussion also covered public concerns about data centers’ energy, water, and noise impacts.

Key Insights

Supply conditions have remained broadly unchanged. Long-term forecasts and firm commitments are increasingly important, while allocations for critical components can disadvantage customers with weaker supplier relationships. Fans, especially electronically commutated fans, remain a key bottleneck. Concerns about ownership changes at a major supplier have prompted customers to seek alternatives. The expert expects more design changes over the coming 12–18 months, driven by higher rack densities and pressure to improve energy efficiency. Liquid cooling is expected to dominate AI workloads, but air cooling will remain in use where it can meet requirements. The expert estimated an 80% liquid and 20% air split for AI-oriented deployments. Two-phase cooling may become more compelling as densities rise, though adoption is unlikely to shift rapidly until performance and cost advantages are demonstrated at scale. European supply could support further growth, but permitting and public opposition may constrain data center expansion. The expert did not expect major changes to the broader US growth outlook. Standardized designs and increased factory assembly could broaden supplier choice, lower costs, and shorten installation and commissioning. Liquid cooling can require substantially greater maintenance effort than air cooling and demands specialized labor.
