The growth across its businesses, how long it could last and what I’m paying for it today

I originally got interested in CAT through the Physical AI angle.

Autonomous machines doing useful work in the real world are exactly the sort of thing I want to follow over the coming months/years.

But increasingly, I also wanted exposure to a business that has been compounding for years. Something a little more under the radar than the AI names that we are all familiar with. I have a starter in CAT now, and that broader appeal is a big part of why.

CAT has equipment working across construction sites and mines, supported by a dealer network that keeps those machines running.

Power generation adds another growth opportunity, with engines and turbines going into data centers and other energy applications.

Each part has its own demand cycle, and the original equipment sale can lead to years of parts and service work.

Below, I work through how those businesses are growing and what could sustain the earnings.

Then I get into the valuation, my entry zone and what I want to see before adding more size.

Earnings are next week (the 29th) so that might present a nice opportunity for an add to my port. Or it might make me push back my decision to add based on the results/guidance.

Right now I have my position average right around $700.

We are in a zone right now that has provided meaningful support throughout 2026. I am interested in building a position if we find support here and break above again.

What I am buying into

For me, the appeal starts with equipment that customers need to earn money. A contractor needs productive machines to finish a job and a miner needs to move material efficiently. When equipment is expensive to replace and downtime costs money, there is a reason to keep spending on maintenance and upgrades.

That is the foundation underneath the newer opportunities. Autonomy can make the installed fleet more useful, and the power buildout can bring more equipment into that service network.

It still matters where we are in each cycle. CAT’s construction and mining exposure will not behave exactly like its power business, so I want to look at them separately.

Power and Energy

For the next few years, this is where I see the clearest link between expanding capacity and more equipment deliveries. CAT supplies engines and turbines for power generation, oil and gas, and industrial applications.

  • Q2 sales $8.24B, up 17% YoY- Segment profit $2.03B, up 30%- Segment margin 24.6%, up from 22.1%

Within the segment, external Power Generation sales grew 29% to $3.10B. CAT supplies backup equipment for data centers, primary power used in normal operations, and bridge power while sites wait for grid connections. More gas-fired power can also create demand for CAT’s gas-compression equipment upstream.

Management wants Power Generation sales above three times the 2024 level by 2030. CAT is expanding large-engine capacity to nearly three times the 2024 level, with turbine capacity targeted at 2.5 times.

By September, the large-engine expansion was two to three months ahead of plan and turbines were on schedule. Management said 2027 engine and turbine production schedules were already largely filled.

For 2026, management expects Power Generation growth across engines and turbines, with oil and gas growing moderately. CAT has not provided annual segment growth guidance for 2027 and 2028. The longer-term opportunity depends on keeping those delivery slots filled as capacity increases.

Construction Industries

With all the attention on power, I was interested to see how much the familiar machinery business contributed last quarter. Infrastructure spending and dealer rental fleets are supporting demand for excavators, loaders and other construction equipment.

  • Q2 sales $8.35B, up 35% YoY- Segment profit $1.95B, up 57%- Segment margin 23.3%, up from 20.1%

Construction added $703M of segment profit YoY, compared with $473M from Power & Energy. That is meaningful earnings support from another part of CAT.

Some of the strength came from dealers building inventory. Construction inventory increased about $400M in Q2, versus a decline a year earlier, and management expects that comparison to become a headwind in the second half.

Sales to users grew 22%, including equipment added to dealer rental fleets. What would be ideal is those fleets staying busy and earning rental revenue. That would support further purchases after the initial loading.

Management expects construction sales to users to grow in 2026, supported by infrastructure and rental demand. I would expect the growth rate to look less dramatic as the inventory comparison changes. With a margin above 23% last quarter, continued demand and decent pricing can still contribute a lot of profit.

Resource Industries

Mining gives CAT another reason for customers to spend. Management describes high equipment utilization and an aging fleet, with copper and gold supporting investment plans. Machines working hard for longer need rebuilding or replacing.

  • Q2 sales $4.65B, up 20% YoY- Segment profit $693M, up 23%- Segment margin 14.9%, up from 14.5%

The segment includes mining, quarrying and heavy construction, with rail now included too.

For 2026, management expects higher sales to users and a moderate increase in rebuild activity. That gives me a practical way to think about the next few years. An older fleet operating at high utilization can support replacement demand without requiring commodity prices to rise every quarter.

Manufacturing costs absorbed part of the volume benefit in Q2, so profit growth only slightly exceeded sales growth. Better execution could help margins, while weaker customer economics could push purchases further out.

CAT’s autonomous hauling fits the Physical AI opportunity that first got me interested.

Luck Stone is expanding autonomous hauling to two more quarries after using it at Bull Run. An existing customer expanding a deployment gives me something concrete to follow. I want to see more evidence that autonomy helps customers get more work out of the equipment, which could strengthen CAT’s relationships and create further opportunities to sell technology and support.

Financial Products supports equipment purchases through financing. Q2 segment revenue grew 10% to $1.15B and profit grew 32% to $328M. It is a smaller earnings contributor, with credit exposure tied to customers’ ability to pay.

How long could the growth last

CAT finished June with $72.1B of firm backlog, up $9.4B in one quarter. About $29.2B was scheduled beyond the following twelve months, and all three industrial segments contributed to the increase.

That backlog includes construction, mining and other power applications as well as data centers. It gives CAT visibility across several end markets while the factories expand.

In power, large customers also share rolling multiyear plans. Further-out requirements convert into firm orders over time, which can make quarterly bookings uneven.

Then there is the equipment already working. An engine supplying a data center every day accumulates far more operating hours than a backup generator waiting for an emergency. Those hours create maintenance work and demand for parts.

CAT estimates the service-revenue opportunity from primary-power reciprocating generators at more than 40 times that of backup units. Management expects most of the incremental service benefit from the new primary-power fleet after 2030.

The next couple of years are more about delivering equipment, while the growing installed base could keep producing service revenue much later. It is one reason I am interested in CAT beyond the immediate power-order surge.

Construction and mining already have their own replacement and service needs. CAT’s breadth appeals to me, although the businesses are still exposed to investment cycles. Delayed data-center projects could affect both power equipment and some construction demand.

What I am paying at this price

At $687, CAT trades at roughly 21x consensus 2027 EPS of $32.59, with earnings expected to grow about 19% next year. Looking further out, TIKR’s October 22 snapshot puts normalized 2028 EPS at $39.21, bringing the multiple down to roughly 17.5x.

Those earnings estimates have held up while the stock has fallen. Published 2027 EPS consensus was $30.45 ninety days ago, compared with $32.59 now. I’m getting a lower price while the earnings outlook has moved higher.

For late 2027, applying roughly 21–25x to that $39.21 estimate gives me a potential range of about $825–980, or 20–43% above today’s price. The lower end assumes CAT carries roughly today’s 2027 earnings multiple into the following earnings year. Getting toward $980 would require investors to pay a higher multiple as well.

I think there’s a reasonable argument for that premium if the power expansion keeps translating into deliveries and new orders. Management said 2027 engine and turbine production schedules were largely filled. As more equipment enters service, CAT also builds a larger base of machines that will need parts and maintenance for years.

Historically, CAT’s reported forward P/E was around 15–18x at the 2021–2024 year-ends. Those are just some reference points but they help frame what happens if investors start valuing CAT more like the older equipment business again.

Applying the low end of the 15x to the same $39.21 earnings estimate gives roughly $590. That’s my multiple-compression scenario. Not a hard floor by any means. It still assumes CAT achieves those earnings. If customers delay purchases and earnings estimates fall too, the downside would be greater.

I like the risk/reward enough to have a starter. For additional size, I’m watching the $668–702 chart area alongside October 29 earnings, particularly delivery schedules and whether new power orders continue supporting the expansion.
