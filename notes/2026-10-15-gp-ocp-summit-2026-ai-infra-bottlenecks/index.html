SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion covered rapid changes in AI data-center architecture, including rack standards, liquid cooling, optical connectivity, shared memory, networking, and processor competition. Attendance at the summit reached capacity, reflecting broader interest beyond chips and servers in connectors, power delivery, cooling, and physical infrastructure.

Key Insights

The expert expects OCP Rack v3 adoption to accelerate as high-density AI systems outgrow traditional 19-inch racks. New facilities should lead adoption, although selected existing sites may require substantial retrofits. Copper should remain dominant within racks over the next one to two years, but co-packaged optics could begin entering AI clusters sooner than some industry roadmaps suggest and become strategically important within two to three years. CXL-based memory pooling is moving from concept toward commercial deployment. It can repurpose older DDR4 and DDR5 memory as a slower tier, but it will not replace HBM or resolve near-term memory shortages. AI infrastructure demand remains exceptionally strong because agentic applications are increasing the economic usefulness of compute. Supply availability may matter more than benchmark leadership. The expert views AMD as strongly positioned due to earlier product availability, broader system support, and an expanding processor portfolio. Networking is becoming a central source of value. Competitive advantage increasingly requires control of NICs, DPUs, switches, software, and security across the full stack. Copper availability emerged as a possible raw-material constraint, although the observation was anecdotal.
