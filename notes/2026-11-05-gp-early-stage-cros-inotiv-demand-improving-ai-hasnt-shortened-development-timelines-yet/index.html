SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a preclinical drug-discovery specialist, described improving demand for contract research organizations (CROs) after a slow start to the year. Sponsors are returning to postponed projects and increasingly seek strong evidence of target engagement, in vivo activity, and clinical relevance before pursuing investment or costly safety studies.

The discussion covered industry growth, biotech financing, AI’s role in discovery, non-animal methods (NAMs), animal-model capacity, pricing, and competition from lower-cost overseas providers. The expert expects gradual industry growth, with safety testing remaining an important source of demand.

Key Insights

Demand strengthened through the later quarters, and the expert expects the final quarter to be slightly better than the preceding one. A possible slowdown early next year remains a concern. AI has not yet shortened overall development timelines or reduced mandatory safety testing. Its current benefit is clearest in target identification and early discovery. The expert sees AI as an opportunity for CROs to offer more integrated scientific support, including model validation, omics, and translational analysis—not merely individual studies. NAM adoption remains limited by uncertain regulatory acceptance. Sponsors generally use these methods alongside conventional animal studies rather than replacing them. The expert’s central caveat is that AI-generated candidates must demonstrate improved clinical efficacy, not just faster discovery. Meaningful evidence may emerge from clinical results in the coming years.

Competitive Landscape

Provider or segment

Positioning and competitive factors

Large established CROs

Investing in AI, data capabilities, and NAMs. Scale and integrated study packages can help retain work, though building usable data foundations takes time.

Overseas CROs

Can offer early-stage work at substantially lower prices. Domestic providers aim to retain later- stage studies through specialized models and broader development packages.

CRO market overall

Capacity constraints, particularly for non-human primates, support demand but can delay study starts and data delivery. Pricing has stabilized; the expert did not expect providers to raise prices simply because schedules are full.

Metric

Expert’s assessment

Industry growth

Approximately 2%–5% annually over the next several years; safety work could be somewhat faster, but growth near 10% appears unlikely.

Demand mix

Roughly half of incoming work is previously delayed; the remainder is new activity. About 30%–40% of new opportunities may take two to three months to finalize.

AI timeline and cost potential

Current target-to-validation work takes about 15–18 months. A future goal is 9–12 months; the expert estimated potential cost savings of 35%–40% if fewer candidates need screening.

Candidate screening

AI could narrow evaluation from roughly 10–15 molecules to three to five, while improving selection quality.

Overseas pricing

Providers in lower-cost regions may be 30%–40% cheaper.

In-silico use

Estimated at about 20% of relevant activity, with adoption increasing.

Study delays

Full CRO schedules may push study starts back six to eight weeks.

Non-human primates

Demand and prices have increased; supply remains constrained despite some improvement.

Clinical Data

The expert said some AI-associated drug candidates have shown early safety or pharmacokinetic signals but have not yet established efficacy in later-stage trials. The expert estimated that 30%–40% of AI-labeled targets have not progressed successfully, and emphasized that clinical efficacy evidence is still needed to validate the approach.
