WDC -2%: Beat and Raise above buyside bogeys, pricing Is Still Accelerating, but pushback is lower cost/TB decline and concerns around supply with higher capex

Recap: FQ1 revenue/EPS of $4.38B/$4.64 beat Street $4.14B/$4.10 on $/TB up 28% y/y. The FQ2 guide of $5.1B/$5.80 is ~12%/~20% above Street ($4.57B/$4.82), with GM guided to 60-61% vs. Street ~57.4%. FQ2 guide implies ~52.6% OM (Street ~49%) and ~75% q/q incremental GM. Opex steps up to $400-410M on product qualification costs.

$/TB accelerated to +28% y/y (from ~18% last q), mgmt expects Dec-Q growth to be higher still with pricing mostly locked, and it sees room to keep expanding y/y gains for another 3-4 quarters, which is driving ~80% incremental GMs. Exabytes (+22% y/y, below the >25% target for a second straight quarter) and cost/TB (only ~4-5% lower y/y on DRAM/NAND inflation) were the soft spots.

Overall solid set of numbers, especially given positioning as stock is down 50%+ since June highs and 20%+ since Oct 1st Toshiba news coming in (one of the worst performing stocks in AI semis). Stock was flat initially AH but fell another ~5-6% after hours after mgmt said capex “modestly above” the 4-6% range next to talk of accelerating exabyte supply growth. Buyside numbers going up slightly morning and still sit around $60+ for CY‘28 vs street at $45 so trading at around 5x at sub $350. Bulls will like the accelerating rev % y/y and price increases to roll through the next 3 q’s with incrementals at 80%, which means still room for margins to keep rising. Bears will pushback on industry dynamics changing for the worse, mgmt’s comments on “pricing moderation” after 3-4 qs (although they walked that back on callbacks), and slower cost/TB declines.

On input costs, language was slightly more explicit than at Citi with only a 4-5% decline but kept 10% LT target alive

Key Takeaways

  • Pricing. Blended $/TB rose 28% y/y and ~9% q/q (nearline +26% y/y, +8% q/q), up from ~18% in the June quarter and ~9% in March. Strength showed up in both nearline and client/consumer (client revenue more than doubled y/y). The drivers:
  • contractual escalators as LTAs hit their anniversaries
  • expiring LTAs renewed at higher prices
  • strong spot pricing on non-contracted exabytes
  • mix toward higher-capacity drives

Dec-Q $/TB growth is guided above 28% (implied mid-to-high 30s%). HAMR will be priced at parity with ePMR per TB (“the price is the price”), so WDC won’t be buying HAMR share with introductory discounts. Incremental: pricing is now running well above the high-teens framing the Street had treated as sustainable.

  • Margins. GM of 58.1% (+1,420bps y/y, +370bps q/q) beat Street ~55.6% and the 55-56% guide, with >80% incremental GM both y/y and q/q. OM was 49.1% vs. Street 46.4% as opex fell to 8.9% of revenue. Mgmt laid out three durable GM drivers:
  • pricing (escalators, renewals, spot)
  • up-mix to higher-capacity drives, which carry a higher ASP per drive
  • more exabytes through areal density without adding fixed unit cost

In callbacks, mgmt said HAMR margins should be at least comparable to ePMR once at volume.

  • Exabytes. Shipments were 248 EB (+22% y/y, +7% q/q), with nearline at 223 EB (+22%, ~90% of EB). Both came in a touch below Street (~249-250 total, ~227 nearline). Growth was stuck at 22% for a second quarter, below the >25% LT target and well behind STX’s mid-30s. Mgmt called Sept-Q “the floor.” It expects growth to accelerate through FY27-28 as 40TB ePMR reaches ~30% of nearline bits in Dec-Q and ~50% in Mar-Q, followed by HAMR. Mgmt called 40TB ePMR the “fastest transition of any ePMR platform.” The 50% target is unchanged from August; the 30% is new. Asked whether ~35% EB growth in six quarters is the message, the CFO said “you’re thinking about it in the right direction.” Bears will say if WDC grows supply ~35%, STX ~30% and Toshiba doubles capacity, industry supply could outrun demand and the 28%+ price increases could reverse. In callbacks, mgmt said 35% isn’t guidance. The long-term target is still >25%; above 30% is possible but not guided, and the growth is meant to fill orders customers have already signaled.
  • Capex. Capex roughly doubled q/q to $227M (5.2% of revenue vs. ~3% in FY26).
  • New wording: the script now has capex “modestly above” the 4-6% range for the next two years, with the five-year

average still 4-6%. In callbacks, mgmt said up to ~2pts above, i.e., ~7-8%, which is 3x+ FY26’s $418M in dollars.

  • Where it goes: head wafers (HAMR heads need more process steps and longer cycle times, which cuts throughput),

media and glass substrates, and factory automation. It also covers more heads and media per drive as platter counts go from 11 to 12 and eventually 14. It is not for unit capacity: total platter counts are growing low-teens % y/y on minimal unit growth.

  • Costs. Cost/TB fell only ~4.5% y/y and was roughly flat q/q, vs. ~8% in June and ~10% in the Dec and Mar quarters. That is well short of the ~10% LT target. Mgmt blamed DRAM/NAND inflation inside the drives in a tight supply chain, plus being between product transitions. Value-engineering fixes are gaining traction but take several quarters to implement and qualify. Near-term declines will stay under 10%, with no timeline back to ~10%. Incremental: input costs were first flagged at the September conference, but this is the first time the slippage is this visible. This is where WDC’s ~2-year HAMR lag vs. STX shows up in the P&L. Pricing is more than covering it for now.
  • Competition. On Toshiba’s capacity plans and chatter about industry consolidation, mgmt said long-term industry fundamentals haven’t changed (25%+ demand CAGR, tight supply). It reiterated it will not add unit capacity. Mgmt argued the bottleneck is head and media capacity, not drive assembly. Players that rely on third-party heads and media may have to add units “just to maintain their share” and “will have a harder time” growing exabytes. WDC buys some external heads and media to benchmark its own, but only for lower-capacity drives; all leading-edge platforms use internal heads. That matters given reports of Toshiba’s interest in TDK’s HDD head business. If a competitor meaningfully adds units, WDC’s response “is going to be the same”: areal density, not units.
  • HAMR. The roadmap:
  • qualifications with two hyperscalers finish in CQ1’27
  • volume shipments start in 1H CY27
  • two more customers begin qualification in 1H CY27
  • 2nd-gen HAMR (5TB/platter, ~55TB on 11 platters) begins qualification in 2H CY27

Mgmt says customers like its failure rates vs. what they’ve seen from other players. The “two hyperscalers” wording read as a step back from September, when mgmt said it was “in qualification with four large hyperscale customers” (see callback below). High-bandwidth drives are sampling with multiple customers for KV cache in AI inference and for video streaming. Mgmt is now working out how to price them, with impact from late CY27 into CY28.

  • Demand. Mgmt says revenue is limited by supply, not demand. All of its top seven customers provide visibility through 2030-31, the demand signal is “increasing” quarter after quarter, and the supply-demand gap isn’t going away near term. Mgmt framed four demand legs:
  • cloud, accelerating on video and AI-enabled services
  • training and RL
  • inference and agentic AI, with “tremendous” growth in logs, checkpoints and outputs
  • physical AI, led by AV customers in Asia and robotics

It argued softness in any one leg, including neoclouds, would be absorbed by the others. Incremental: in September mgmt said customers were asking to sign LTAs through 2030-31. Now it cites visibility through 2030-31 across all of its top seven customers.

  • Customers. Cloud was $3.9B (88% of revenue, +54% y/y, +16% q/q). Demand is broadening to enterprise and hybrid-cloud OEMs, neoclouds and frontier AI labs, which mgmt is now building into its forecasts and investment plans. Mgmt is selective on neoclouds, favoring strong balance sheets and quality end customers. Per callbacks, it also structures agreements to limit credit risk.
  • Cash. FCF of $1.38B (31% margin) came in ~8% below Street (~$1.50B). Capex doubled and inventory rose ~18% q/q (+$270M), built in components rather than finished goods ahead of EB acceleration; mgmt says any extra exabytes would ship immediately. WDC returned $1.06B ($1.0B of buybacks, $56M of dividends) and raised the dividend 17% to $0.175.
  • Callback. The most important takeaway: Dec-Q is largely de-risked. The vast majority of the quarter’s revenue is already under contract with pricing locked, and $/TB growth will exceed 28% y/y. Mgmt also walked back the scarier readings of the call:
  • ~35% EB growth is not guidance; >25% remains the LT target, and >30% is possible but not guided. Supply growth is

aimed at existing customer demand, not excess capacity.

  • Four hyperscalers remain in HAMR qualification (two finishing first, with two new customers to follow), so there is

no pushout.

  • The 3-4 quarter pricing comment refers to y/y growth continuing to accelerate, with no set timeline for moderation.

The less comforting detail: LTAs mix fixed pricing, premium pricing on incremental volume and annual escalators. But commercial terms for the 2030-31 visibility years are not finalized, and purchase orders run only ~4-5 quarters out.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bulls this is an AI supercycle and Supply is limited by heads and media, not assembly lines. The two vertically integrated leaders (WDC and STX, ~90% share) are growing exabytes through areal density rather than units. The new pricing is being written into LTAs with escalators and renewals, and customers now plan storage five-plus years out alongside land, power and compute. Bulls argue this looks like a planned, multi-year infrastructure buildout, not the PC-driven cycles that earned HDDs single-digit multiples. They see Toshiba’s capacity ambitions as a prove-it story, given its reliance on third-party heads and media and its history of missed roadmap milestones.

This quarter fed every leg of that argument. Pricing accelerated to +28% with Dec-Q higher and largely locked, and mgmt sees 3-4 more quarters of expanding y/y gains. Pre-print Street CY27 models embedded only ~13-16% average $/TB growth. Every 5pts of CY27 pricing is worth roughly $1B of revenue that falls almost entirely to profit, or ~$2 of EPS.

GM is guided to 60.5%, and bulls see the mid-to-high 60s by CY28. 40TB ePMR and HAMR (priced at parity, with margins at least equal to ePMR) should lift EB growth from the 22% “floor” toward 30% while the industry adds no units. Opex is essentially fixed at ~$400M a quarter, so OM can head toward 60%. With 35-45% FCF margins and 85%+ returned, WDC would retire ~4-6% of its share count a year at today’s price. Bulls read the capex step-up as old news aimed at the real bottleneck (heads and media). Catalysts include STX’s upcoming print, HAMR qualification completions in CQ1’27, the Dec-Q pricing print and LTA renewals.

Bears see a classic commodity up-cycle dressed up as secular growth. $/TB is up 28% and heading toward the mid-30s in an industry where it historically fell 15-20% a year, and pricing like that invites a supply response:

  • Toshiba reportedly plans to double capacity, and has reportedly shown interest in TDK’s HDD head business, which would ease its component bottleneck.
  • STX is already growing EBs in the mid-30s on HAMR.
  • WDC itself is more than tripling capex and talking up accelerating EB supply growth toward 30%.

An exabyte from areal density is still an exabyte. If industry supply grows in the high 30s while demand compounds at the ~25%+ mgmt keeps citing, the shortage closes sometime in 2028. The LTAs bulls lean on won’t stop it. Mgmt conceded that the further out you go, the less volume and pricing is covered; out-year terms aren’t set, and purchase orders run only 4-5 quarters. The incremental demand supporting spot pricing comes from neoclouds and frontier labs, the part of the AI stack most exposed to the financing stress now hitting the complex. Hyperscale customers (cloud is 88% of revenue) have every incentive to second-source and push back at renewals once supply loosens.

The bull case assumes pricing stays strong after supply responds and that WDC closes its technology gap. This quarter cut against both:

  • EB growth was stuck at 22% for a second quarter.
  • Cost/TB declines slowed to ~4-5% as the ~2-year HAMR lag vs. STX shows up in the P&L.
  • HAMR volume still depends on qualifications that don’t finish until CQ1’27.
  • The promised acceleration means running two transitions at once while absorbing HAMR head throughput losses.

Mgmt’s own comment that pricing growth will “eventually slow down” after 3-4 quarters puts the second-derivative peak on the calendar for 2H CY27 although mgmt seemed to walk back the moderation comment in the call back.

BE -4%: Third Beat-and-Raise Clears the Buyside Bar withs some pushback around the unchanged 34% GM guide

Overall, a very solid set of numbers. Q3 revenue $1.24B (+138% y/y) vs. Street $1.08B; non-GAAP EPS $0.94 vs. Street $0.68; FY26 revenue guide raised ~11% at the midpoint to $4.4–4.6B vs. Street $4.12B and a buyside bar of a ~7.5% raise; FCF -$181M.

It was BE’s first $1B+ product quarter (+179% y/y), and product GM rose 120bps q/q to 38.4% despite ramp and expediting costs. Opex grew only 26% y/y, which pushed op margin to 23.1% and the FY op income guide up 17%. Leading indicators improved: backlog is still outgrowing revenue, product/installation RPO rose 49% q/q, ~$2.6B of supplier commitments fall due in 2027, and a second Fremont site is being added as mgmt prepares for capacity beyond 5GW. Top-customer concentration fell to ~43% from ~73%. The nits were OCF of -$27M on a ~$430M contract-asset build, no reiteration of the

$375M OCF guide, and an unchanged ~34% GM guide.

Key Takeaways

  • Guide. The third raise of the year cleared the buyside bar. Revenue rose ~11% at the midpoint vs. hopes for ~7.5%, the range narrowed to $200M, and op income and EPS were raised 17% and 20%. Mgmt built it the same way as prior raises: backlog assessed customer by customer against site readiness, plus a probability-weighted pipeline. It said a small number of year-end deliveries will decide where in the range BE lands. The implied Q4 midpoint (~$1.45B) sits ~18% above Street. The year started at ~60% growth, ~32% GM and ~14% op margin; the guide now ~122%, ~34% and ~22%, respectively.
  • Demand. Mgmt reiterated that backlog is growing faster than revenue but declined to bracket it; the YE figure comes with Q4 in February. The harder datapoints were product and installation RPO (+49% q/q) and ~$5.8B of new supplier commitments, some take-or-pay, with ~$2.6B due in 2027. That reads as BE locking in supply for a much larger 2027.
  • Capacity. BE added a ~158k sq ft Fremont site on a 12.5-year lease, roughly the size of its existing ~164k sq ft cell footprint. It will be built out in parallel with current plants, a path toward ~10GW over time. Q3 capex jumped to $153M from $52M in 2Q (YTD $231M, ~4x FY25), mostly machinery for Fremont and Newark. At ~$150–200M per GW, that’s ~1GW added, putting operational capacity at ~2–2.5GW. Labor isn’t the constraint: mgmt said the stack line runs with the same ~600 people at 5GW as at 1GW.
  • Margins. Product GM’s 120bps q/q gain came in a quarter with new-line ramps and expedited deliveries, which mgmt credited to pricing and cost-downs. Service GM slipped 60bps q/q but held above 20% for a second straight quarter. The disappointment: after climbing from ~32% earlier in the year, the ~34% FY GM guide was held despite the ~$450M revenue raise, implying flat Q4 GM. The CFO cited expediting and startup costs and said engineering, sourcing and manufacturing cost programs take time to reach COGS. He called the outlook “measured,” which makes GM expansion a 2027 story. Opex leverage did the heavy lifting: Q3 op income alone exceeded all of FY25.
  • Pricing. Mgmt said pricing “remains strong,” and math supports it. If the largest customer is Nebius’ ~328MW NJ project, as some assume, ~$1.31B of 2Q–3Q revenue implies ~$4,000/kW vs. ~$3,000/kW previously assumed, with customers absorbing expediting costs for speed. Others still model roughly flat ASPs near ~$3,300/kW. K.R. leaned on NVDA’s framing that a one-quarter delay on a 1GW data center costs ~$10B of revenue (~$1B per 100MW), and cited Oracle Utah powered in under two months.
  • Customers. Concentration improved, with the largest customer at ~43% of revenue vs. ~73% in 2Q. But a related-party customer was another ~26%, so the top two were still ~69%. On Nebius, K.R. said BE won’t be the bottleneck on any of the ~300MW and was ready for the first block, which the customer is only now close to energizing. That is a reminder that deliveries, and revenue, can run ahead of block-by-block site readiness. New fronts: the US Army conditionally awarded partner Indelible long-term leases to build and operate BE-powered generation on Army installations. BE also shipped its first units into Texas, which mgmt pitched as proof it now competes even in the lowest-cost power markets.
  • New Markets. K.R. expects “significant wins” in utility front-of-the-meter deals. He pitched BE servers as rate-baseable distribution assets that let utilities serve load growth rather than lose customers to behind-the-meter; Ameren Missouri’s IRP already includes ~500MW of gas fuel cells by 2030. Internationally, BE is talking with local partners in Asia and Europe and flagged news “soon,” with higher gas prices abroad widening its efficiency edge. AEP disclosed 700MW of CCS-enabled BE servers for a Google project in Wyoming, the first commercial CCS proof point. On 800V DC, BE is in talks with all the hyperscalers and claims >$5B of savings over five years for a 1GW site, with timing set by when 800V racks ship.
  • Cash. OCF swung to -$27M (2Q: +$226M) on a ~$353M working-capital use. Contract assets rose ~$430M (product revenue recognized ahead of billing milestones), inventory ~$170M and receivables ~$175M. That was partly offset by ~$310M of higher deferred revenue/customer deposits and higher payables. With $153M of capex, FCF was -$181M (YTD +$42M). The prior FY OCF guide of >$375M was not reiterated (YTD ~$273M; Street had ~$460–580M). Cash ended at $2.5B, roughly matching recourse debt.
  • New Mexico. Mgmt didn’t address Oracle’s Project Jupiter, where the Street maps ~2.45GW of BE backlog and ~$2B of first-phase product revenue. The process is moving again after months of permitting delays. Technical filings are due Nov 17 and the public hearing starts Dec 7. The Street sees the air permit landing between mid-February and mid-April 2027 (excluding appeals), with gas pipeline approval also expected in 1Q27. It stays a 2027 revenue driver, but the hearing is contested and a final order could tighten the proposed MW cap.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

The big picture bull thesis is that the grid can’t serve concentrated AI load on any useful timeline, and BE has become the default way to bring your own power. Interconnection queues and utility build-outs take years, and large gas turbines are sold out deep into the decade. BE energizes in months, block by block, with a non-combustion system that permits more easily. It uses almost no water: per mgmt, ~1.4M gallons vs. ~25B for a wet-cooled CCGT over the life of a 1GW site. It is also gaining political cover; Virginia’s governor singled out fuel cells in the state’s 2026 energy plan.

When a quarter of delay costs ~$1B of revenue per 100MW, speed is worth far more than the electrons, so BE can price at a premium and still win. Mgmt also argues the metric that matters is power cost per token, not LCOE, and that reliability, power quality and load-following tilt total cost further its way. BE is the only at-scale SOFC producer. Beyond hyperscalers, bulls see the TAM broadening into C&I, utilities, the military and international, with CCS, CHP and 800V DC as options on the same platform.

This quarter checked most of the bull boxes. The raise beat the buyside bar, product GM expanded through a ramp, and opex grew at less than a fifth of revenue’s pace. The forward indicators argue 2027 is a step up: RPO +49% q/q, ~$2.6B of 2027 supplier commitments, ~$310M of higher deferred revenue/deposits, a second Fremont site and backlog outgrowing revenue. Concentration fell, and the AEP-Google CCS project shows BE can win hyperscaler business on more than speed.

The implied Q4 run-rate alone (~$5.8B annualized) is ~85% of pre-print Street FY27 revenue. That is before New Mexico, Wyoming or any new utility or international wins. Mgmt also pointed last quarter to 30–40GW of new AI data-center capacity coming online in 2027. Bulls therefore see February’s FY27 guide and first backlog number since YE25 as the next estimate catalyst. Layer on GM expansion as expediting and startup costs roll off, and capacity that costs ~$150–200M per GW against ~$3B+ of annual revenue per GW (mgmt says new capacity pays back in months). On those numbers BE can self-fund 50%+ growth at 25%+ op margins, which is what would drive estimates and the multiple higher.

The broader bear thesis is that the market is capitalizing a scarcity year as a steady state. BE’s growth and pricing reflect an acute shortage of interconnection and turbine slots. As utilities, turbine OEMs, recip and battery suppliers and new SOFC entrants add supply, the speed premium (~$4,000/kW implied on the largest deal) should compress toward ~$3,000/kW or below, just as BE roughly doubles its footprint.

The model is lumpy and concentrated. The top two customers were ~69% of Q3 revenue. Some estimate a single financing partner, Brookfield (whose financing commitment was expanded by $20B last quarter), could be more than half of FY26 revenue. Mgmt itself says a handful of year-end deliveries decide where FY26 lands. Bears see a project/hardware business priced like a platform. Revenue is recognized at delivery while billing follows milestones, so fast growth consumes working capital. Much of 2027 also depends on things BE doesn’t control: a contested permit in New Mexico, CO2 infrastructure for Wyoming CCS, and customers’ block-by-block site readiness. Add input risks (scandium and other rare earths with China exposure, customer gas-price sensitivity, ITC/safe-harbor policy) and a crowded, high-beta stock at ~45x Street CY27 EPS.

This quarter gave bears ammunition: FCF swung to -$181M on the contract-asset build, the OCF guide wasn’t reiterated, and the CFO signaled contract assets may keep growing. Paired with K.R.’s comment that Nebius is only now energizing its first block, bears argue reported revenue is running ahead of customer readiness and cash. The GM guide stayed at ~34% despite a ~$450M revenue raise, pushing the margin story to 2027. Service revenue missed and was flat q/q, and implied Q4 growth decelerates to ~86% y/y from 138%.

VRSN: Record Domain Registrations and Higher FY26 Growth Outlook, Though Profitability Misses on One-Time Charge

VRSN delivered another strong quarter of domain growth, with record new registrations of 13.1M (+24% y/y) and the .com/.net base accelerating to +6.1% y/y, driven by AI-enabled website creation, registrar marketing programs, and strength in the U.S. and EMEA. FY26 domain growth guidance was raised materially to +6.5–7.0%. Renewal rates moderated to 74.3%, largely reflecting a higher mix of first-time renewals (~mid-40% retention vs. mid-80% for seasoned domains), a headwind likely to persist into 2027. The ~7% .com price increase takes effect November 1, while .web launches its initial registration phases in December/January ahead of broader availability in April 2027; the new Verisign Informed security service targets 1H27. Revenue beat modestly, but EPS and operating income missed due partly to a $7.5M asset-related charge.

RNG: Biggest Revenue Beat Since 2022 as AI Adoption Accelerates, Margins Hit Records and FY26 Guide Raised

RNG delivered a strong beat and raise, with subscription growth accelerating to +6.0% y/y and record profitability supported by resilient core demand, AI cross-selling, and operating leverage. Customers purchasing at least one paid AI product now account for ~15% of ARR (>$400M of customer-cohort ARR, doubling y/y), with higher ARPU and >100% net retention; AIR reached 22.2K customers (+280% y/y), ACE 6.9K (+60%), and CEB 14.3K (+45% q/q), with ~two-thirds of CEB customers also buying AI. Roughly half of new customers now adopt AI products, while enterprise renewal headwinds have yet to fully ease, providing potential incremental upside into 2027. Gross debt fell below $1B ahead of schedule, FCF grew 21%, and management raised FY26 revenue guidance by $13M, exceeding the quarterly beat. AI-specific revenue remains relatively small, leaving meaningful monetization runway.

AI Credit / CRWV: Global spreads reach 17-month high; CoreWeave bonds yield 15% – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports global corporate-bond spreads widened to 129bp, their highest since May 2025, as AI-stock losses and geopolitical tensions drove investors toward safer assets. Its separate financing report shows some CoreWeave corporate bonds trading near 80 cents on the dollar and yielding roughly 15%, while two chip-backed leveraged loans remain near par.

HUBS: BTIG sees limited 3Q upside as partner checks soften, but RIF savings support FY27 margins; PT to $300

BTIG’s partner checks deteriorated q/q, with longer sales cycles, greater pricing scrutiny and new competitors such as Day.AI/Creatio, though Agent Builder feedback remains constructive. BTIG models ~15% cc 3Q revenue growth with only modest upside, roughly consistent with last quarter’s ~100bps beat, and expects HUBS to maintain FY26 guidance. The bigger offset is the recent ~7% RIF, which could add ~260bps to FY27 operating margin and provide an earnings backstop if topline growth stays durable. Buy, PT $300 from $250.

DDOG: Wells survey shows steady share gains as AI/agentic coding drives incremental observability spend

Wells’ large-customer survey (~$89M annual DDOG spend) remains constructive, with most respondents expecting 2026 spend growth of 5%+ and continued DDOG share gains. AI is becoming a meaningful incremental tailwind: 92% say AI initiatives are increasing observability spend, 80% cite agentic coding tools like Claude Code/Cursor, and over half report >10% higher observability spend from AI. Overall spending intentions improved slightly q/q, supporting a healthy demand setup into year-end.

MSFT: BofA raises FY27–29 estimates; Azure + Copilot remain the core upside engines

BofA models F1Q27 Azure at +44.8% cc to ~$32.3B, supported by ~1GW of quarterly DC additions and $678B of commercial RPO, while FY27 CapEx rises to ~$221B (+52% y/y). The bigger bull case is enterprise AI monetization: 76.7% of CIOs use/plan to use Copilot, 88.4% consider MSFT for cloud AI infrastructure and 69.3% expect to raise Microsoft security spend. BofA increasingly frames Copilot as the enterprise operating layer across apps, data, security and governance. Buy/$600.

Software: BofA CIO survey shows AI taking ~40% of 2027 priorities, with MSFT dominating spend intentions

BofA sees 2027 software budgets +5.3%, with AI wallet share rising to 9.4% from 8.3% and ~61% of AI funding coming from net-new budgets. MSFT dominates across cloud AI, infrastructure software and Copilot, while SNOW is the strongest next-tier infrastructure beneficiary and CRWD/PANW benefit from cybersecurity budgets +6.9% and AI security leading incremental spend. The key risk is ROI: only 17.2% of CIOs report measurable enterprise-wide AI returns today, while ~60% expect just 1–25% of agent pilots to reach production.

NOK: JPM says FY27 guide is deeply conservative; NI EBIT could land ~27% above Street

JPM keeps NOK OW and cuts ADR PT to $20.50 from $21 on FX only, arguing the fundamental setup has improved. AI & Cloud orders reached €1.5B in 3Q and €6.2B including longer-dated backlog; current FY27 Optical/IP guidance of +25–30% implies just ~77.5% order conversion vs. ~96% in FY26. If conversion holds near current levels, JPM sees AI & Cloud revenue at ~€5.17B, Optical/IP growth >42% and NI EBIT ~27% above consensus, translating to ~14% company EPS upside. At ~11.7x 2028E EPS, valuation remains well below NOK’s historical multiple.

Ecommerce: Wedbush stays positive into 3Q; EBAY Top Pick, SHOP also favored

Wedbush expects solid 3Q ecommerce results as traffic/app MAU trends remain positive, with AI agents/bots contributing to web traffic growth. EBAY is the Top Pick, with GMV expected above guidance and strength in eBay Live, collectibles and other focus categories supporting durable growth; PT $137. SHOP is also favored on agentic-commerce positioning and merchant additions, though valuation at ~55x 2027 EV/EBITDA limits upside; OP/$176. ETSY remains Neutral/$88 as product improvements help GMS but comps tighten, while W stays Neutral/$125 given housing/macro pressure despite improving share trends.

BIDU: Barclays says AI infra growth near +100% can’t offset >20% ad decline + margin pressure; PT cut to $80

Barclays sees core advertising deteriorating further, with 3Q potentially down >20% y/y as chatbot usage increasingly displaces traditional search and BIDU’s own AI features have yet to monetize meaningfully. AI infrastructure remains the bright spot, with revenue likely near +100% y/y as BIDU rents scarce GPU capacity into strong demand. But the mix shift from high-margin ads to lower-margin AI revenue, combined with elevated AI investment, could push 3Q non-GAAP operating margins to recent lows with little near-term recovery. Estimates cut; EW, PT $80.
