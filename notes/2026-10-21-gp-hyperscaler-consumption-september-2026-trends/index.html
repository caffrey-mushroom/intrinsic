SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, who works at an AI, data, and application-development consultancy, discussed demand across Azure, AWS, and Google Cloud. Azure gained share within the consultancy’s business, while Google Cloud maintained faster momentum and AWS experienced slower contract approvals.

Azure growth was driven by Fabric, business intelligence, and AI-enabled applications. Customer service and IT were the fastest- expanding functions, while companies increasingly embedded AI into customer-facing digital products. Capacity constraints reported in earlier periods appear to have eased.

Key Insights

Azure performed below the expert’s expectations because several large, budget-sensitive projects were delayed rather than cancelled. Google Cloud met expectations and continued gaining workloads through infrastructure migrations, multicloud deployments, discounts, and migration funding. AWS remained strategically important to existing customers, but slower contract signatures caused a meaningful reduction in the next-quarter outlook. Existing cloud relationships determine more than 80% of platform selections. Pricing and model availability are usually secondary unless differences are substantial. Copilot adoption increased, but weak governance has created excessive numbers of independently developed agents. Customers are now considering consolidation and orchestration. Demand delays reflect organizational readiness rather than infrastructure supply. Business processes and governance are struggling to keep pace with technology adoption. The expert expects embedded engineering and business-oriented AI specialists to generate additional AI development projects in a future first quarter.
