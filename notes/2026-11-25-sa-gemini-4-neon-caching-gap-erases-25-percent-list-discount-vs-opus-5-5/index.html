核心摘要

  1. SemiAnalysis在GDPx基准测试（平均输入输出比121:1）下实测发现，尽管Gemini 4 Neon标价（$3/$15每百万token）比Claude Opus 5.5（$4/$20）低25%，但在Agent多轮循环工作负载下两者实际总成本基本持平甚至Neon反贵4%（$220K对比$213K）。
  2. 造成价差反转的核心原因是Google弱势的提示词缓存命中率：Neon在GDPx上的缓存命中率仅88.0%（Hydrogen仅86.5%），显著低于Opus 5.5的95.2%、GPT-6.2 Sol的98.8%及DeepSeek V4.1 Flash。
  3. 在5%缓存读取定价下（20:1命中与未命中价差），从99%缓存每掉1个百分点即推高输入账单16%，Neon未命中的12.0%输入token贡献了总账单的58.4%（$129K/$220K），而Hydrogen在86.5%命中率下比GPT-6.2 Sol反贵56%（$118K对比$76K）。
  4. 由于Google不收取缓存写入费（Anthropic与OpenAI收1.25倍），若Neon命中率追平Opus 5.5的95.2%，成本本可降至$147K（便宜31%），但目前其需要达到88.8%才刚打平，Hydrogen需94.8%才打平。
  5. 在Google自家的Vertex平台上，Claude（91.9%–94.9%）与DeepSeek V4.1 Flash（90.7%）的缓存命中率也高于Gemini 4（70.8%–85.2%），Priority档位甚至跌至60.7%，证明瓶颈出在Gemini自身推理服务栈与KV内存容量受限。
  6. Google在Gemini 4取消了显式缓存改用尽力而为的隐式保留（4,096 token最小前缀且受负载驱逐影响），并计划于2027年1月12日在Vertex重新推出带1.25倍写入费的付费TTL缓存控制。
  7. 计入单任务token效率后，OpenAI GPT-6.2 Astra（$8/$40）在FrontierCode 2.0（$3.75对比$8.65）与Ramp SWE-Bench v2（$3.31对比$4.38）上得分与总费用双赢，而长程编程中Neon High成本更是Opus High的近3倍（$4.38对比$1.53）。

Author: Adam Holter, Max Kan

Date: Wednesday, November 25, 2026 (published 2026/11/25 22:23:48 Taipei)

Executive Summary

Neon costs about the same as Opus 5.5 on agentic workloads despite a 25% lower list price. This

is due to Google’s weak prompt caching. Based on our GDPx testing, even if you assume the

exact same token usage and workload shape, it costs $220K to run Neon with an 88.0% cache hit

rate, and $213K to run Opus 5.5 with a 95.2% hit rate.

Google should have a caching advantage because it charges no cache-write fee, while Anthropic

charges 25% more for cache writes than regular input. If Neon had the same hit rate as Opus 5.5, it

would be 31% cheaper. Neon must clear 88.8% cache hits just to break even, but it averaged

88.0% on GDPx.

Every Gemini 4 model we tested is weak on caching. Neon, Hydrogen, and Argon cached 86.5%

to 90.3% of input tokens on GDPx, versus 95.2% to 98.8% for Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, GPT-6.2 Sol,

GPT-6.2 Astra, and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. At 86.5% against 98.8% for GPT-6.2 Sol, Hydrogen

costs 56% more than Sol on the same workload despite a 25% lower list price. Google dropped

explicit caching in Gemini 4 for best-effort implicit retention that degrades under load, and Vertex

will not launch paid TTL cache control until January 12, 2027.

On cost per task, OpenAI leads, but Anthropic wins long-horizon coding. Even at $8 / $40 (2.67x

Neon’s list price), GPT-6.2 Astra beats Neon on both score and total spend across major coding

and reasoning benchmarks, while Opus 5.5 ties or undercuts Neon on cost in long-horizon SWE

workloads, because cache misses hurt more in long threads.

Low cache hit rates are almost always an engineering skill issue. The theoretical maximum cache

hit rate for agentic workloads is north of 95%, and doing worse than this directly translates to a

higher total cost for your users. Fortunately for Google, improving an inference serving stack is

one of the skills LLMs are getting extremely good at. We therefore believe Google should be able

to achieve much higher cache hit rates when serving Gemini in the near future.

Why a 25% cheaper model loses its discount on real agent workloads

On paper, Google priced Gemini 4 to undercut both rivals: Neon lists at $3 / $15 per million tokens

(25% below Claude Opus 5.5 at $4 / $20), and Hydrogen lists at $1.50 / $7.50 (25% below GPT-6.2 Sol

at $2 / $10). Almost nobody checks how prompt caching changes that math once you run multi-turn

agents.

Cache hit rate is the single most important factor in determining the true cost of agentic workloads. An

agent runs in a loop: the model reads the conversation, calls a tool like opening a file or running a

command, and the result gets added to the conversation for the next step. Every step sends the whole

conversation again, so the model reads the same text over and over. On GDPx, our benchmark

measuring the 11 domains most important to frontier intelligence, agents average a 121:1 input:output

ratio. Prompt caching lets you pay the much cheaper cache-read price for the majority of your input

tokens, and your hit rate mostly comes down to how well the provider runs its servers. When the

provider drops the cache, it must redo that work, and you pay full price for it. This can cause you to pay

up to 20x more for the exact same workload.

All three frontier labs generally price cache reads at 5% of input (2.5% on Fable 5.2), creating a 20:1

spread between hits and misses. Starting from 99% caching, every percentage point you lose adds 16%

to your input bill. Dropping from 99% to 89% multiplies input cost by 2.6x.

To show what observed cache hit rates do to real API bills, we priced GDPx’s actual token usage across

10 models (357.8B input tokens and 3.0B output tokens) using the cache hit rate we observed for each

individual model. Misses on Anthropic and OpenAI are billed at their 1.25x cache-write rate, while

Google charges no write fee:

Source: SemiAnalysis GDPx

On GDPx, where each model ran in its own lab’s harness (Antigravity for Gemini, Claude Code for

Claude, and Codex for GPT), Neon read 88.0% of its input tokens from cache and Hydrogen 86.5%,

versus 95.2% for Opus 5.5 and 98.8% for GPT-6.2 Sol. At 88.0%, Neon’s effective input price comes to

$0.491/M, 15% above Opus 5.5’s $0.429/M even though Neon lists 25% lower. Assuming the same

workload shape as GDPx, which is representative of general agentic use, that turns a 25% list discount

into a 4% cost penalty: $220K vs. $213K.

Flip it around to see the discount Google is leaving on the table. Because Google charges no cache-write

fee, matching Opus 5.5 at 95.2% caching would cut Neon’s modeled bill to $147K, making Neon 31%

cheaper than Opus 5.5.

Hydrogen fares worse against GPT-6.2 Sol. At 86.5% cache hits, Hydrogen models at $118K versus

$76K for Sol at 98.8% (with 1.2% cache writes), or 56% more despite a 25% lower list price. Matching

Sol’s hit rate would bring Hydrogen down to $55K (-27%), and Hydrogen needs 94.8% cache hits just to

break even.

Because cache reads are so cheap, a few percentage points of cache hits decide the entire pricing battle:

Source: SemiAnalysis GDPx

Against the $213K Opus 5.5 costs at its measured hit rate, Neon must hit 88.8% just to break even, less

than a point above the 88.0% it averaged on GDPx. At 88.0%, the 12.0% of tokens that miss the cache

generate 58.4% of the entire bill ($129K of $220K), while output accounts for only 20.1%.

Why Google’s cache is falling behind

To be fair, Gemini 4 is a clear step up from Gemini 3.1 Pro, which caches at 34% to 44% on

OpenRouter, and launch bugs do get fixed (Argon launched with zero cache hits; Google fixed it 16 days

later, and it now caches at 87%). Still, GPT-6.2 Sol launched a day before Neon and cached 98.8% on

GDPx.

Even on Google’s own hosting, its competitors’ models have better caching than Gemini 4:

Source: OpenRouter, SemiAnalysis

On Vertex standard routing on November 23, Claude cached between 91.9% and 94.9%, and DeepSeek

V4.1 Flash hit 90.7%, well above Gemini 4 at 70.8% to 85.2%. Across 54 OpenRouter endpoints serving

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (median 92.2%), 45 beat Google’s best standard Gemini 4 endpoint, including

smaller hosts like NovitaAI, Venice, and Cloudflare. Paying 1.8x list for Neon Priority on Vertex makes

retention worse, dropping hits to 60.7% and pushing modeled cost to $2,421 per 1B input tokens (3.7x

the $647 for standard Vertex Neon).

That tells us the bottleneck is Gemini’s serving stack rather than Google Cloud. Unlike Anthropic and

OpenAI, Google dropped explicit caching completely with Gemini 4. Retention is implicit and best-

effort (“varies with backend load and capacity” per Google staff), with no TTL controls, a 4,096-token

minimum prefix (vs. 512 on Anthropic and 1,024 on OpenAI), and full-price re-billing of the last 2,050

tokens on short turns. Google expanded Neon’s cache pool in its first-party Antigravity client on

November 21 (claiming 22% more work per quota), yet public APIs on AI Studio and Vertex still lag

rivals.

How that carries over to real cost per task

Caching is only half the bill. The other half is how many tokens a model uses to finish a task (i.e., token

efficiency), and once you factor that in, list prices tell you even less:

Source: FrontierCode 2.0, Ramp

OpenAI beats Google on both caching and token efficiency. Even though GPT-6.2 Astra lists at $8 / $40

(2.67x Neon), it beats Neon on both score and total spend across FrontierCode 2.0 (at $3.75 vs. $8.65),

Ramp SWE-Bench v2 (at $3.31 vs. $4.38), the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (at $2.52 vs. $6.44),

and Vals Terminal-Bench 4 (at $4.69 vs. $6.42). Sol lands close on score at 3x to 6x lower cost.

Against Anthropic, task length decides the winner. Neon is still cheaper than Opus 5.5 Max on shorter

evals like Artificial Analysis (at $3.74 to $6.44 vs. $10.04) and Terminal-Bench 4 (at $6.42 vs. $12.04).

On long-horizon coding, where cache misses pile up, that lead disappears: FrontierSWE v2 ties at

$96.14 vs. $98.87, and Ramp SWE-Bench v2 costs nearly 3x more on Neon High than Opus High (at

$4.38 vs. $1.53), though Neon scores 8 points higher there (76.2% vs. 67.8%).

What Google must fix

Google has a real cost advantage at the chip level with TPUs, and Gemini 4 is a strong model. Today,

though, API customers are not getting that 25% discount on their bills. Recomputing evicted prefixes

also burns Google’s own compute and points to real KV-memory constraints.

To be clear, low cache hit rates are almost always an engineering skill issue, as most providers today are

serving similarly shaped agentic workloads. Fortunately for Google, improving an inference serving

stack is one of the skills LLMs are getting extremely good at. We therefore believe Google should be

able to achieve much higher cache hit rates when serving Gemini in the near future.

Google’s roadmap suggests it knows best-effort caching is not enough for agents. On January 12, 2027,

Vertex will launch paid TTL cache control with a 1.25x write fee, adopting Anthropic’s pricing model.

Until deterministic caching holds up on public APIs under load and Google trims how many tokens

Gemini burns per task, Neon’s lower list price will keep disappearing on real agent bills.
