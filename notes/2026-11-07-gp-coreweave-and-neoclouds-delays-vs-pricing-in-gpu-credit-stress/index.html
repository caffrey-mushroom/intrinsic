SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a data center infrastructure consultant and former cloud-capacity forecaster, described a bottom-up model tracking data center equipment, power, construction, permits, and supply-chain constraints. The discussion focused on U.S. AI data center expansion, policy-related delays, GPU pricing and collateral, and the credit exposure of CoreWeave and other NeoCloud providers.

The expert expects near-term projects to remain largely on schedule, while later projects face greater uncertainty. Power and local permitting were identified as the most difficult constraints to overcome.

Key Insights

The expert characterized broad market concerns about election-related delays as excessive: projects under construction or with permits, particularly through the first half of 2028, are comparatively secure. Delays are more likely for projects planned beyond that period. Power and local permitting are the decisive constraints. Financing, labor, and equipment can sometimes be managed around, but projects without power access or local approval may not proceed. NeoCloud providers face greater exposure than established operators because their growth depends on continued investment and timely delivery. CoreWeave is especially vulnerable due to debt-funded expansion, rented facilities, concentrated customers, and the cost of carrying GPUs or leases before revenue begins. The expert expects GPU rental rates to remain firm, supported by constrained supply and robust demand. A meaningful decline would most likely require demand to weaken; substantial supply relief is considered unlikely in the near term. The expert described current credit stress as a useful correction that could discourage speculative projects and improve diligence, while creating short-term pressure for developers and lenders.
