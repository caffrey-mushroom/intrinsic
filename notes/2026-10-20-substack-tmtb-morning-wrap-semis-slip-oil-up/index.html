AAPL: Loop downgrades to Hold as iPhone cuts increasingly look demand-driven, not just supply

Loop cuts AAPL to Hold from Buy and PT to $330 from $370, lowering estimates after concluding the ~10% Sep/Dec iPhone shipment reductions are unlikely to be fully recovered in 2027 as previously expected. The firm now sees the issue as increasingly demand-related rather than purely supply-driven, while rising memory costs add gross-margin risk. After the stock’s recent appreciation, Loop says AAPL needs a new fundamental catalyst to re-rate.

AKAM: Raymond James upgrades to Strong Buy/$170, says Anthropic financing fears are overdone

RJ upgrades AKAM from OP to Strong Buy after the pullback, arguing the market is over-penalizing its ~$5.5B capital commitment supporting >$11B of expected Anthropic revenue. AKAM represents <3% of Anthropic’s estimated ~$520B of infrastructure obligations over the next 7–10 years, making it a relatively small but differentiated piece of the buildout given Akamai’s networking footprint. While higher rates and Anthropic funding remain risks, RJ sees favorable risk/reward as AI sentiment recovers and contracted inference revenue ramps; PT $170.

CRM: To buy BRZE for $47.50 in cash / $5.6 EV (58% premium; ~5x CY27 sales)

Some comps:

NVDA: China orders exceed $35B; supply falls short – Reuters

Reuters reports ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent ordered more than 3 million export-compliant RTX PRO 5500 workstation GPUs, worth over $35B at list prices, mainly for inference. One source said Nvidia warned partners they might receive only 5–10% of requested quantities. Total Chinese orders exceed 6 million, versus indicated global production of no more than 3 million in 2027.

Anthropic / Nscale / NVDA: Second $45B compute deal in talks – Bloomberg / FT

Bloomberg reports Anthropic is discussing another roughly $45B compute agreement with Nscale, covering a second 460MW building at its West Virginia campus using Nvidia Vera Rubin chips. That would roughly double its existing commitment. The FT reports the first phase targets completion in H1 2028; financing for the first contract and key site permits remain outstanding.

OpenAI: Accelerates $30B raise ahead of Anthropic IPO – Reuters

Reuters reports OpenAI is accelerating a proposed $30B financing at a valuation above $1.4T, seeking commitments before Anthropic’s expected mid-November IPO. SoftBank, Abu Dhabi’s MGX and other existing investors are discussing participation. OpenAI met long-only and sovereign wealth funds last week as the two AI companies compete for many of the same investors.

META: Hearing Edge said macro softer at the margin near end of quarter, but q finished in upper end of the range.

META: Raymond James sees neutral setup as strong ads/Muse collide with higher 2027 expense + capex bogeys

RJ sees 3Q revenue at ~$64B vs. Street $63.3B and a $72–75B 4Q guide vs. $73.6B, but says the bigger swing factor is 2027 spending after META’s >20% rally since late August. Buyside is closer to ~$210–220B of 2027 expenses vs. Street $203B and ~$225–250B CapEx vs. Street $198B; RJ itself is at ~$250B CapEx. Muse adds meaningful optionality, with RJ’s mid-case at $50B+ annualized revenue/$10B+ costs and upside scenarios at $150B+ revenue, but the print likely hinges on whether spending commentary clears the higher buyside bar.

META: Bernstein stays positive on Muse-driven re-rating, but 2027 spend is the key risk

Bernstein remains OP/$800 and expects META to beat 3Q revenue guidance, helped by strong ad checks and AI-driven targeting/creative, but sees a tougher 4Q comp and potential FX pressure. The bigger debate is 2027 spending: Muse’s early traction gives META more license to invest, but OpEx/CapEx could move above current expectations as product ramps, cloud deals and compute needs scale. Bernstein sees Muse as strategically important enough to reorient META around a broader AI OS opportunity, while cautioning that early adoption/TAM enthusiasm still needs to prove durable monetization.

AI Datacenter: TD Cowen sees record leasing as the bottleneck shifts from demand to delivery

TD Cowen sees ~15GW of U.S. data-center leasing in 3Q26, +70% q/q and ~7.5x 2Q25, led by ~10GW from AI labs including ~8GW from OpenAI; the active global pipeline remains near-record at ~12.4GW. META is seeking another ~6.5GW by YE28, ORCL has resumed leasing with NVDA credit support which Cowen views as an indication of future RPO growth, and GOOGL/MSFT remain active. The bigger issue is execution: 2027 equipment slots are already oversold, build costs are rising, and Texas/Ohio permitting could delay capacity and revenue recognition. Cowen estimates U.S. capacity contracted since 1Q25 implies ~$2.25T of building + IT capex, with tightening equipment, substrates and fiber increasingly the constraint.

ORCL: JPM expects FY30 target raise, but funding + peak CapEx visibility matter more for the stock

JPM expects Oracle to raise its FY30 revenue/OCI targets as stronger compute pricing and prior guidance already leave room for upside, with JPM’s model ~$12B above the current FY30 revenue framework and ~$15B above OCI. The bigger investor debate is funding: after completing the $20B ATM, Oracle still needs roughly another $20B of capital in FY27, with gross CapEx potentially $90–95B; JPM expects more clarity on prepayments, supplier/partner financing and the path from gross to net CapEx. Confirmation that CapEx peaks in FY27/28 would be the cleanest catalyst for easing leverage concerns. OW.

ORCL: Citi sees AI World/Analyst Day driving higher LT targets as backlog strength shifts focus from funding risk to earnings power

Citi expects ORCL’s 10/28 event to support upward revisions to long-term OCI revenue/EPS targets, with $200B+ of net new RPO added over the past year and ~50% expected to convert within three years; it sees scope to raise the current $166B FY30 OCI target. Key debates are backlog conversion, GPU vs. Core infrastructure mix, CapEx/financing beyond FY27 and project delays, while take-or-pay contracts, power-cost pass-throughs and premium pricing should support ROIC despite near-term FCF pressure.

AVGO: Hearing Clev positive seeing upside to Oct q driven by XPU shipments and px increases.

AMD: Hearing Clev positive saying CPU demand remains strong with mid 1M’s mi4xx GPU shipments in CY27.

CRWV: JPM says bank exposure is well secured/managed despite HY stress

JPM sees limited systemic bank risk from CoreWeave: financing exposure is concentrated in secured SPVs/DDTLs (~$24B commitments, ~$15B drawn) rather than parent unsecured debt, with capital released against project milestones. Most participating banks are estimated at only ~$0.5B exposure, while larger U.S. banks/MUFG may be closer to ~$1–3B, largely in DDTLs. The bigger watch is whether CRWV’s 15%+ HY yields force tighter financing terms across AI infrastructure, with JPM expecting more equity support as lenders focus on counterparty risk, front-loaded capex vs. ~$130B RPO and broader AI-credit spillover.

Memory: JPM says stocks price a 2027 downcycle that current fundamentals do not support

JPM says memory shares are already trading near historical downcycle trough valuations despite spot DRAM pricing holding flat-to-up, HBM ASP negotiations biased higher and order discussions extending into 2028/29. It sees the most underappreciated demand driver as agentic CPU-related memory and views stronger capital returns as the cleanest near-term catalyst. Preference is SK Hynix over Samsung, with Kioxia and NYT also favored.

Memory: Samsung expected to have 80% of 2027 output contracted – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports Samsung is expected to enter 2027 with approximately 80% of planned memory production secured by long-term contracts. The report also cites growing customer interest in CXMT as an alternative DRAM supplier.

MU: Taiwan union schedules October 22 strike – Reuters

Reuters reports Micron’s Taoyuan union plans to strike on October 22 over employee bonuses, escalating the dispute from authorization to scheduled action. The walkout will coincide with mediation involving the separate Taichung union, which has not voted to strike. Taiwan is a major Micron manufacturing hub for DRAM and HBM.

NVDA / MU / Memory: Rubin Ultra memory configuration under review – Commercial Times

Commercial Times reports that industry analysts say Nvidia is evaluating eight-layer HBM4e or HBM4 for Rubin Ultra alongside its planned 12-layer HBM4e configuration, potentially enabling more GPU shipments from a fixed supply of DRAM dies. The analysts forecast Q4 NAND prices rising 30% sequentially and DRAM contract prices about 20%, versus a roughly 40% DRAM contract-price increase in Q3.

STX/WDC: MS says 8x FY28 already prices in an HDD peak; supply shortfall + pricing still moving higher

MS sees current ~8x FY28 EPS valuations as too punitive, effectively assuming the HDD cycle peaks by FY28 and SSD prices collapse ~80% over 2–3 years. Checks remain bullish: spot nearline pricing is ~4–7c/GB vs. blended contracts ~1.5c/GB, the CY27/28 supply shortfall has widened to ~400EB from 300EB in CY26, and non-nearline pricing is rising 15–30%+ q/q. MS Dec-Q EPS sits ~12% above Street for STX and ~10% above for WDC, with further upside possible; WDC has the cleaner tactical setup into earnings given a lower buyside bar. PTs: STX $1,200, WDC $643.

AMAT: MS more constructive on DRAM/process control, but still prefers suppliers AEIS/MKSI

MS came away from meetings more positive on AMAT’s DRAM share resilience and front-end process-control positioning, but logic share losses from 3nm to 2nm were worse than expected and back-end process control looks less attractive given smaller TAM and fragmented competition. EPIC remains strategically credible but unproven. At ~16x CY28, MS sees better upside in AEIS and MKSI than in AMAT itself.

DeepSeek: Open-Sources DeepInfer, Its High-Performance Inference Framework

DeepSeek released DeepInfer, its in-house inference stack powering V4.1-Flash, designed to boost throughput, reduce latency, and lower compute and energy costs.

TXN: Evercore ISI expects beat/raise but says valuation likely caps near-term upside

Evercore ISI sees above-seasonal demand and positive pricing supporting a 3Q beat-and-raise, but cautions that TXN already trades at ~29x NTM P/E after a ~60% YTD run, with revenue growth expected to peak y/y in 1Q27 before slowing. Longer term, TXN remains its top analog pick, with Data Center revenue modeled at ~$900M in 3Q26, +100% y/y, and FCF/share rising from $3.2 in 2025 to $16.4 in 2028 on 80–90% in-house manufacturing. OP/$330.

SPOT: MS sees AI + new monetization layers supporting ~25% EBITDA CAGR; reiterates OW/$640

EBAY: MS makes Top Pick, says Live Commerce is an incremental GMV driver rather than a trading-card fad

META/AAPL: Stratechery sees Muse Spark Open tie-up as strategic validation of local AI, but not a cloud killer

Stratechery sees the Apple/META collaboration as notable strategically: Apple optimized MLX for Muse Spark Open, enabling large local models on high-memory Macs and use cases like coding/video where effectively “free” local tokens can unlock new workflows. But the near-term cloud read-through is limited: Muse Spark Open still trails frontier models, local hardware is slower and expensive, and Stratechery still expects most workloads to remain in the cloud unless local models become “good enough” and exploit fundamentally new workflows. \
