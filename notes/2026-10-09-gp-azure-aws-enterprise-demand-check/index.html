SUMMARY

Overview

An enterprise cloud sales expert discussed third-quarter momentum across Azure, AWS, Google Cloud, and Oracle Cloud. The conversation covered cloud migration, Microsoft licensing, early AI adoption, infrastructure constraints, and upcoming enterprise budgets. The expert primarily serves mid-sized manufacturing, healthcare, and financial-services organizations.

Azure is benefiting from Microsoft 365 integration and continued migration away from on-premises infrastructure. AWS remains strongest for established DevOps and security-focused development workloads. AI adoption is still preliminary, with most customers testing Copilot and simple automation rather than deploying sophisticated production agents.

Key Insights

Azure is the clear demand leader within the expert’s customer base because most clients already use Microsoft 365 and related workloads. Traditional cloud migration currently contributes more demand than AI. Manufacturing organizations are finally retiring aging on-premises systems. AWS retains a meaningful advantage in DevOps and DevSecOps because developers are familiar with its environment and resist unnecessary platform changes. Healthcare customers show strong AI interest but remain constrained by privacy, governance, and regulatory requirements. Microsoft E7 adoption is limited. Most E5 customers currently prefer renewing existing plans, although bundled identity, security, and AI capabilities could improve E7’s appeal. Rapidly changing cloud prices and short quote-validity periods are damaging customer trust and complicating procurement. Some customers are returning backup and cold-storage workloads to on-premises infrastructure because public-cloud economics have become less predictable. The moderator pursued the expert’s views on pricing instability, Copilot consumption, E7 adoption, and cloud workload repatriation.
