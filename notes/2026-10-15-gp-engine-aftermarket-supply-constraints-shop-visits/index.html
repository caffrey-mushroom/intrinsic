SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined demand, leasing, maintenance and used-serviceable-material trends across legacy commercial engines. The expert argued that supply constraints and unresolved technical issues affecting newer engines continue to extend the service lives of older fleets. Ground-power conversions, alternative replacement parts and vertically integrated leasing and maintenance models were also reviewed.

Key Insights

CFM56-7B demand remains exceptionally strong and has strengthened recently because newer replacement aircraft and engines remain scarce. CFM56-5B demand is softer and market supply appears abundant, although the platform may retain longer-term potential. V2500 engines are difficult and costly to repair, while available used material may exceed demand. MRO capacity is tight as carriers retain legacy fleets and reactivate stored engines. Hot-section component shortages reflect manufacturing-capacity constraints more than raw-material scarcity. The expert expects aggressive engine valuations to create future balance-sheet problems for lessors. The moderator repeatedly explored this risk. The expert anticipates continued 7B strength over the next few years but would avoid long-term exposure around 2030. Short-life, modular engine builds could become increasingly attractive as carriers bridge toward newer aircraft. PMA parts offer faster shop turnaround and lower dependence on OEM supply, but resale acceptance and residual valuation remain obstacles. Ground-power conversion demand is raising values for otherwise unusable engines, although the expert remains skeptical about the sector’s durability.
