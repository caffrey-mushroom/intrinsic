  1. Total revenue grew 96% year over year. The Rule of 40 score rose to 158%, marking its 13th consecutive quarterly

increase. 26Q4 guidance implies year-over-year growth of approximately 88%, above market expectations of 82%–86%.

  1. U.S. Commercial growth did not materially decelerate, but remained below our internal bogey. 26Q3 revenue was $984

million, up 148% year over year, versus our internal estimate of $1.00–1.02 billion, or 152%–157% growth. Full-year revenue guidance of more than $3.559 billion, representing growth of at least 143%, was slightly below our internal estimate of $3.57–3.63 billion, or 144%–148% growth.

  1. U.S. Government growth accelerated to 96% year over year in 26Q3, above our internal bogey of 80%.
  2. Several leading indicators point to slower bookings, which may have contributed to the share-price pressure following

the earnings release. Total contract value (TCV) was $2.918 billion, up 6% year over year versus 49% in 26Q2. U.S. Commercial remaining deal value (RDV) was $6.517 billion, up 4% sequentially versus 27% in 26Q2. Total RDV increased 2% sequentially. Total remaining performance obligations (RPO) were $4.88 billion, slightly below $4.90 billion in 26Q2; on the company’s rounded reporting basis, both were approximately $4.9 billion, broadly flat sequentially.

Shorter Contracts May Weigh on Bookings Metrics Without Signaling Weaker Underlying Demand

  • Based on management’s earnings call commentary and the expansion in NDR and ACV, I favor the first explanation in the table below: Palantir may be deliberately shortening contract duration to preserve repricing flexibility at renewal. If a customer’s ACV can increase 5–15x within one or two quarters, as management indicated, signing a three-year fixed-price contract could effectively give away future pricing upside. Shorter contracts give Palantir the opportunity to adjust pricing annually based on customers’ subsequent usage depth and platform dependence.
  • Another possible explanation relates to customer approval thresholds and budget cycles. At many companies, an annual commitment of $10–30 million may fall within the CFO’s or CEO’s approval authority, whereas a three-year contract worth approximately $100 million would typically require board approval. In the second half of the year, some large projects may need to wait until the following year’s budget is finalized. Karp’s comments that the “contracting timeline has completely changed” and that the period from initial contact to establishing a business relationship has shortened materially may indicate that shorter initial contracts help lower customers’ internal approval barriers.
  • In 2026, some enterprise AI spending still comes from annual innovation or discretionary budgets and has not yet been incorporated into core IT budgets. Customers seeking to sign multiyear contracts may need to wait until the next budget cycle to move this spending into their regular budgets. On the earnings call, management cited a five-month, $15 million contract signed in 26Q2. This may mean the customer initially committed through year-end and would renew after the new budget was finalized. This type of contracting structure affects metrics such as TCV.

Business Highlights

Subscribed

  • The quarter’s results are consistent with our earlier assessment: vendors that can use forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) to identify high-ROI use cases and implement them through Foundry remain in short supply relative to demand.
  • Management highlighted several customer examples in 26Q3 to illustrate ROI. L3Harris fine-tuned an open-source model on its proprietary data and, within 48 hours, achieved better performance than the frontier model it had previously used while cutting costs by 95%. McCarthy saves 30,000 hours annually. Tampa General improved model performance by 7% and reduced latency by 22x. At DevCon, a customer engineer also built a production-grade model within 48 hours that outperformed frontier models. In these cases, customers used business data in the Ontology to fine-tune models for specific tasks, improving performance at lower cost.
  • Customers are adopting the platform quickly. Ryan described the pace of adoption as “extremely fast” and said customers act as soon as they see the product. Karp said the timeline from initial engagement to purchasing a pilot or moving into production had “completely changed”; prospective customers at conferences may also sign contracts quickly. U.S. Commercial revenue grew 29% sequentially and 148% year over year in 26Q3, surpassing U.S. Government revenue for the first time. U.S. Commercial TCV over the trailing 12 months reached $6.1 billion, up 62% year over year. Management said customers are placing greater weight on measurable results.
  • Deployment capacity continues to lag demand. Karp said repeatedly that Palantir cannot meet all current demand and that the addressable market is far larger than its delivery capacity. He added that the company needs to reallocate resources toward customers who have clearly expressed an intention to buy, while the current team is too small to cover all potential demand. My read is that FDE capacity is currently focused on customers with clear buying intent.
  • Adjusted expenses increased 20% sequentially and 53% year over year, which Glazer attributed to technical hiring and the arrival of a new graduate cohort. Adjusted operating margin remained at 62%, meaning operating leverage did not improve this quarter for the first time. Identifying and implementing high-ROI use cases still depends on people, combining product capabilities with hands-on experience, as Karp noted. The pace of hiring could therefore affect how quickly Palantir can grow.

Implications of Open-Source Models

  • The improvement in open-source model performance is broadly positive for Palantir, but may put pressure on some model providers and service vendors.
  • Palantir disclosed that a fine-tuned NVIDIA Nemotron 3 Nano model with only 4 billion parameters outperformed substantially larger frontier models on a real customer task.
  • A 95% reduction in model costs makes more use cases economically viable, while NDR has reached 165%. Lower model costs may also reduce Palantir’s reliance on frontier model providers. At the same time, the business data customers accumulate in the Ontology can make it harder for them to move to another platform.
  • For frontier model providers, this suggests that many enterprise tasks do not require the most capable or expensive models. The premium for closed-source models may therefore be concentrated in complex reasoning and long-tail tasks, putting pressure on enterprise token revenue and pricing power. This trend may also affect Anthropic’s direct enterprise business model, as described in its S-1 filing.
  • For API and cloud model service providers, a shift toward running more inference workloads on local GPUs could send a larger share of revenue to hardware and data-platform providers, putting pressure on gross margins in the intermediary service layer.
  • SaaS vendors that mainly resell model tokens have limited differentiation in their AI offerings. Palantir’s revenue does not depend on token markups; lower model costs instead make more applications economically viable.
  • Not all enterprises will benefit equally. Their ability to capture these benefits may depend on whether they have proprietary operational data that can be used to fine-tune models. Karp described this gap as the sharpest divide between the “resource-rich” and the “resource-poor.”

Growth Drivers

U.S. Commercial revenue surpassed U.S. Government revenue for the first time this quarter, narrowing the revenue gap between the two businesses.

Existing Customers Continue to Expand Spending

Over the past three quarters, NDR rose from 150% in 26Q1 to 157% in 26Q2 and 165% in 26Q3, increasing by 7 and 8 percentage points in the last two quarters, respectively.

Average TTM revenue per top-20 customer also continued to rise, pointing to further spending growth among Palantir’s largest existing customers.

Source: FUNDA

New Customer Conversion and Industry Expansion

Palantir continues to convert new customers into contracts. Examples disclosed over the past three quarters span a wider range of industries, including manufacturing, logistics, banking, insurance, healthcare and consumer goods.

How Commercial Customer Examples Have Evolved

The focus of customer examples changed over the three quarters: operating results in 26Q1, contract value and duration in 26Q2, and the speed of conversion from pilots to ACV in 26Q3.

26Q1 Operating Outcomes

The examples this quarter focused on operating results, including production output, labor hours and utilization.

Customers came from insurance, aerospace and the defense supply chain, as well as major institutions such as the Navy and the Department of Agriculture. Management described these customers as “load-bearing institutions.” Palantir’s products were used to bring AI into day-to-day workflows through operational AI, the Ontology and agents. Management also stressed the need to avoid low-quality AI output in the workplace.

Management emphasized model neutrality while competing with closed-source models from AI labs; product discussions centered on benchmarking and the model factory.

26Q3 Conversion From Pilots to ACV
