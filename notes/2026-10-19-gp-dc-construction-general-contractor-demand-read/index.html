SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined hyperscale data center construction, including demand, financing, permitting, equipment availability, skilled labor, contracting models, and site preparation. The expert, an experienced industrial contractor, described accelerating demand but emphasized that project viability depends on signed tenant commitments, secured financing, power access, permits, equipment delivery, and qualified trade capacity.

Key Insights

Demand continues to rise, with more inquiries converting into pipeline and backlog. Contractors remain selective because many prospective projects may not proceed. Skilled electrical and mechanical labor is the industry’s greatest constraint. Even projects receiving numerous bids may have only one or two contractors capable of meeting the required scale and schedule. Permitting restrictions generally redirect projects toward more supportive jurisdictions rather than eliminating demand. New regulatory reviews can postpone mobilization by several quarters. Long-lead electrical, generation, cooling, and fuel equipment remains constrained but has not materially worsened or improved. Completed projects discussed by the expert generally met operational handover commitments, although this required overtime, multiple shifts, larger crews, and close coordination. Prefabrication, shared rental programs, and more efficient power and cooling designs are reducing on-site labor requirements. However, these gains are insufficient to eliminate labor scarcity. Signed leases with large technology tenants substantially improve access to project financing. Site suitability can materially affect budgets. Poor soil, steep grades, and excavation requirements can make preparation costs several times higher.
