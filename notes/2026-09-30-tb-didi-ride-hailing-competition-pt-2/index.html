Key Insights

  • Didi's net margin still has room for improvement, but the slope will continue to slow down. Commission rectification and capacity clearance do not constitute substantial pressure. Dual-pricing only accounts for 1-2% of industry orders, and comprehensive rectification has little impact on revenue per order. The capacity clearance brought by dual-certificate compliance will only manifest after 5-6 years, with a magnitude of about 10%, posing low short-term risk
  • Amap's driver-side subsidy amid its commission-free season only rose from below 5% normally to below 7%, and commission-free cards are mostly purchased by drivers themselves, making the campaign cost lower than order-rushing rewards. The platform's daily average completed orders in July-August were about 11.5 million, an increase of about 10% MoM
  • The per-vehicle profitability metrics publicly disclosed by robotaxi companies such as Pony.ai exclude vehicle-purchase capital costs and vehicle idling. The industry breakeven point is about 15 orders per day, while Pony.ai, WeRide and Baidu overall still average under 10, and Caocao is estimated at about 7-8 in Hangzhou

00:00:35] In our last Interview, you mentioned that the ride-hailing industry has a 27% commission cap. Is this commission cap currently calculated from the platform's perspective or the fulfilling party's perspective?

Expert [00:00:47]: The requirement states that it should be based on both ends—displaying how much the passenger originally paid and how much the driver received on the fulfilling driver's interface, and then calculating the ratio. Therefore, looking at it from this angle, it must be the fulfilling party. However, many instances where the platform side exceeds 27% involve "yin-yang bills" and multi-platform reselling, where the lower-level segments cannot access the actual revenue of the upper-level segments. Thus, under normal circumstances, it should be based on the fulfilling party's perspective. According to the regulatory authorities' requirements, it should involve both ends: if a passenger places an order on Platform A, and Platform A passes it down a chain to Platform B and others, until Platform C or Platform D finally fulfils it, the calculation should bridge both ends. However, these intermediate platforms only know their immediate upstream and downstream data, and cannot access figures across multiple tiers.

ES [00:02:06]: In practical terms, there may still be instances where commissions exceed the cap. Which categories usually absorb the excess portion? How is it calculated?

Expert [00:02:18]: Didi certainly has quite a few of these currently, but the exact number is truly unclear. Didi’s usual practice is to inform you that this specific order has indeed exceeded the cap, but it promises that once you complete a certain order target, it will be refunded next month. Furthermore, they promise that the monthly average should be around 25%; they have a strategy regarding this aspect, which is the first point. Secondly, the "Sunshine Initiative" rule required by the Ministry of Transport is that the passenger's actual payment over the driver's income. Of course, there is a deduction beforehand: the passenger's discount amount should not be borne by the driver. For example, if a RMB 100 fare is discounted by RMB 20, the passenger's actual payment under normal circumstances is RMB 80, which is used as the denominator, so the calculated result is highly likely not to exceed the cap. The usual practice in this scenario is to directly use the amount after the passenger's discount as the denominator, and if there are incentives, they will be added to the numerator, which lowers the commission rate to a certain extent. However, this somewhat contradicts the principle that drivers should not bear the passenger's discount portion.

Thirdly, there are "dual-pricing bills" (yin-yang bills), and every tier will likely try its best to avoid exceeding the cap. However, if it does exceed the limit in this regard, different platforms may handle it quite differently. Some do not embellish it at all; Didi uses the first method, the majority use the second method, and some do not really care at all. Of course, enforcement is quite strict now, and all parties will keep it below 27% at the level where they can be caught.
