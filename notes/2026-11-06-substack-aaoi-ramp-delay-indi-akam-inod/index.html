原标题：AAOI Delays Big Ramp. Now What? Also, INDI, AKAM & INOD Thoughts

#optical #cloud #robotics

Substack · 2026-11-06

核心摘要

  1. AOI将Q4营收指引下调至3.85亿至4.2亿美元，低于此前超过5亿美元产能和约11亿美元年收入的说法，每月4.71亿美元数据中心收入目标也从2027年中推迟至2027年底。
  2. AOI三季度营收创纪录达2.699亿美元、环比增长40.6%，数据中心收入1.556亿美元、同比增长254%，但400G占比偏高令非GAAP毛利率降至28.7%，主要客户加速转向1.6T导致DSP和材料短缺。
  3. AOI手握约4亿至4.5亿美元1.6T订单，已签LTA约定客户2026年6月至2029年5月至少10%的1.6T需求向其采购、预计收入10亿至15亿美元，第二份LTA规模可能是首份的五倍以上但尚未签署。
  4. AOI三季度资本投入5.01亿美元并完成6亿美元ATM增发，2026年底设备产能目标从每月65万只降至50万只以上，作者认为约39亿美元市值下仍难过度苛责。
  5. Indie Semiconductor营收7,020万美元、同比增长31%，雷达已量产出货，管理层估计每年5,000万至7,000万颗、单价5至6美元，对应2.5亿至4.2亿美元年收入潜力。
  6. Indie出售无锡子公司仍待中国监管审批，约1.35亿美元款项未到账，管理层预计无需该款项即可在2027年Q2实现非GAAP盈利、Q3现金流转正。
  7. Akamai今年签订超过140亿美元的多年期CIS承诺，其中Anthropic贡献116亿美元，预计2027年CIS收入11亿至13亿美元，四笔大单完全爬坡后年收入约22亿美元、前期投入约68亿美元。
  8. Akamai Q4资本开支预计24.45亿至26.45亿美元，三季度调整后营业利润率从31%降至25%，管理层预计利润率在2027年上半年见底。
  9. Innodata营收9,560万美元、同比增长53%并将全年增速下限上调至42%，但历史最大客户一个项目10月中止将影响Q4约1,200万美元收入，前两大客户占比升至74%，调整后毛利率降至40%。

Four calls to get through.

There was some big news here. Also some answers in the Q&A that change some things. Especially on AOI’s ramp and Innodata’s customer situation.

Let’s start with Indie.

INDI

Radar is in high-volume production. Nice to see some volume behind the design wins.

Revenue was $70.2M, up 31% year over year.

The core business did $40.7M, up 14% sequentially, and management guides to about $47M in Q4. That’s a $188M annualized pace.

The ams OSRAM acquisition is expected to contribute little this quarter, so the improvement is substantially organic.

Radar has completed automotive production approval and is shipping to the Tier 1 partner. A top-three North American automaker also selected it, although we don’t have a production date for that program.

Then management put some numbers around where this could eventually go with 50M–70M radar units a year at $5–$6 each. That works out to $250M–$420M in annual revenue. Just from radar. No year attached to it, but that is a big opportunity relative to the business today.

The memory shortage is helping the DRAM-less vision processor too. Some customers are going from first contact to shipment in two or three months. That’s quick. Fewer than 20% of the vision wins have even started ramping. Keeping those customers after the shortage eases would give this a much longer life than a short-term shortage.

On robotics, AgiBot, Unitree and Figure AI remain disclosed wins. There are more than 40 total vision wins this year, including Physical AI, plus a LiDAR win for a warehouse robot at a major logistics company.

Management plans to move a substantial part of 2027 R&D toward Physical AI. There’s a lot of automotive technology to reuse here. There’s also still an automotive ramp to deliver.

None of those Physical AI wins is in the $6.2B strategic backlog yet. They can’t reliably estimate the volumes and timing. That backlog is a rolling ten-year estimate and also excludes Wuxi.

Now Wuxi. The sale is still under Chinese regulatory review, so the roughly $135M gross proceeds haven’t arrived. Q4 guidance of $73M–$81M still includes about $30M from Wuxi alongside $47M from the core business.

Indie owns 34.38% of Wuxi but consolidates all its revenue through voting control. So selling it removes $29.5M of quarterly reported sales, far more than indie’s share of the economics. Management expects organic radar and vision growth to more than replace that revenue in 2027. Removing Wuxi also helps lift the remaining business’s margin into the low 50s.

The funding side is less exciting. Cash, including restricted cash, fell just $4.7M to $144.3M. But debt’s carrying value rose $13.9M, and receivables and inventory grew faster than revenue.

And $24.3M of stock compensation on $70.2M of revenue. About 35%. That is a lot. The adjusted operating loss improved while the GAAP operating loss slightly widened.

The ams acquisition has slipped to year-end. Even after paying for it, management says it can reach Q2 2027 non-GAAP profitability and Q3 positive cash flow without the Wuxi proceeds. That’s great.

They currently have around $430m of debt and $134M of unrestricted cash.

Overall I am very pleased with this call. I would like to add more soon as this is a Physical AI play with (practically) no data center exposure and that is attractive to me in this environment.

AAOI

Well, this wasn’t ideal.

In the past we have talked a lot about putting a ‘haircut’ on the ambitious numbers laid out by management.

Today we get a taste of what that could look like.

Management had talked about more than $500M of Q4 production capability and roughly $1.1B of annual revenue. The Q4 revenue guide is now $385M–$420M.

And the $471M monthly data center revenue goal that we were so excited about has moved from mid-2027 toward the end of 2027.

Those are substantial changes.

There was still plenty of growth in Q3.

Record revenue of $269.9M.

Up 40.6% QoQ….

And they are guiding to another ~49% QoQ increase at the midpoint.

That is extremely strong.

Data center did $155.6M, up 254% year over year, and CATV hit a record $110.9M. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.17.

GAAP net income was positive at $6.3M, although operating income was still a $1.4M loss. Other income helped get the bottom line over zero.

800G revenue was $47.5M and 400G was $93.4M. The heavier 400G mix pushed non-GAAP gross margin to 28.7%, below guidance. Management said a major customer moved toward 1.6T faster than AOI expected, leaving it short of the required DSPs and materials.

Meanwhile, they have roughly $400M–$450M of 1.6T orders in hand. Huge demand. They didn’t give the full delivery schedule for that book, but expect DSP availability to improve substantially in Q1 and are qualifying another supplier.

The signed LTA helps and was nice to see. The customer agreed to buy at least 10% of its 1.6T requirements from AOI from June 2026 through May 2029, subject to conditions including AOI’s ability to supply. Management estimates $1B–$1.5B of revenue. There are agreed price reductions, and the customer isn’t contributing capital.

So AOI has to build the capacity itself, and the construction timetable is slipping too.

Phase 1 is still expected to start operating in December, with less year-end readiness than previously expected.

Phase 2 is delayed by power and construction issues. The year-end 2026 equipment-capacity target falls from 650,000 monthly 800G/1.6T units to above 500,000. The end-2027 target rises to more than 1M.

Look at the amount being spent along the way. Q3 capital investments were $501M, including $142.8M of equipment prepayments.

AOI also completed a $600M ATM equity offering. Q4 guidance assumes 96M diluted shares versus 87.8M in Q3.

There’s a new $200M bank commitment, split between a term facility and revolver.

Nine-month operating cash flow was negative $76.7M, against $928.8M of equipment and construction commitments at quarter-end.

The 2027 capex answer was messy. Prepared remarks said spending would increase further. In Q&A, Lin said it could be lower as construction delays push payments out.

And three customers accounted for 41%, 28% and 23% of Q3 revenue, so a few deployment decisions have an enormous impact.

The CPO details were interesting too.

They are qualifying lasers with four or five customers, and management said preliminary testing has gone well. Lin said customer feedback indicated AOI had the best power efficiency among the suppliers.

He also talked about gross margins above 60% for the lasers.

The lasers are being qualified first, with the external laser modules to follow. Management described an 18–24 month process from equipment investment to volume production. Some of that investment is already underway.

They also said customer funding is more likely for CPO/NPO than for conventional transceivers, although nothing was announced.

Management also said a second LTA is in its final stages and could be completed within weeks. Lin said it could be five times the size of the first agreement, or even more. Using management’s $1B–$1.5B estimate for the first deal, that would imply $5B–$7.5B or more. That would be huge. But it hasn’t been signed, and that dollar range is an estimate based on his comparison, not a disclosed contract value. We also don’t know the duration or minimum purchase terms.

Now here’s the thing. People love to crap on AAOI’s management for over promising and under delivering. And I get it and we are seeing it play out. But the thesis never relied on them hitting 100% of their timelines or numbers.

If the stock was trading >$10B, this would be really rough because you are pricing in significant growth ahead.

But at around $3.9B after hours? A company that just grew rev 40.6% QoQ and is guiding to another ~49% increase, with roughly $1B of revenue this year?

It’s hard for me to be that critical at these prices.

I wish we had more clarity around what the first couple quarters of the next fiscal year would look like. It sounds like DSP supply will be more readily available by Q1.

I know that a lot of people were burned by AAOI because the share price ran so aggressively and has since round tripped all it’s gains.

But if we take a step back and just look at how the company is executing (regardless of their promises) and the price that it’s at today, I think that is much more productive.

AKAM

More than $14B of multi-year CIS commitments signed this year, including $11.6B from Anthropic. For a business that just did $109M of quarterly revenue.

That gives you an idea of the change coming through Akamai.

Management expects CIS revenue of $1.1B–$1.3B in 2027, with 70%–75% arriving in the second half. Total company growth is expected to reach 17%–22% in constant currency, then revenue above $7B in 2028. The extra $9B Anthropic option is excluded.

Two of the first three big CIS deals have started generating revenue.

About $30M slipped from Q4 into 2027 because of supply-chain and third-party data-center delays. Management says the issues are resolved and the contract value hasn’t changed.

Now look at this slide.

Once fully ramped, Akamai expects these four deals to bring in roughly $2.2B of annual revenue. It will cost about $6.8B upfront to build the infrastructure.

Management expects around a 30% operating margin once the deals are fully running. That gives us a better idea of the economics after accounting for the equipment than the 75% EBITDA margin on the slide.

And remember, these are the largest deals at Akamai’s lowest pricing. Smaller customers could bring better returns on the same platform.

For Anthropic, Akamai has contracted all the necessary space and power and secured about 70% of the hardware and components. Pricing adjustments help protect against increases on components not yet ordered. Billing starts after installation, testing and availability, and the commitments carry service and termination conditions.

More than 90% of the servers being deployed are CPU-based. Akamai is building around virtual machines, storage and networking, and can reuse equipment across compute, security and delivery. That’s useful flexibility compared with a GPU-heavy training buildout.

On the political pushback around data centers, the CEO said they already have the permits needed through 2028 for the announced deals and more.

That’s good to hear. Akamai also uses many smaller locations, which management believes leaves it less exposed to the opposition facing giant campuses. Having permits, space and power already contracted helps. Delivery by its data-center partners, future expansion and any new costs outside those contracts can still be affected.

These anchor contracts have “the lowest prices you are going to see on a unit economic basis.” Smaller, shorter deals carry better returns. If this first build brings in more of those customers, there’s more to the opportunity than collecting the revenue from the initial four contracts.

Security did $622M in Q3, up 9%, while delivery and other cloud applications fell 3% to $391M. Total revenue was $1.122B, up 6%. Non-GAAP EPS fell 5% to $1.77, and adjusted operating margin dropped from 31% to 25%.

So the investment is already taking a bite. Management expects margins to bottom in the first half of 2027.

Q4 revenue guidance is $1.145B–$1.170B, with non-GAAP EPS of $1.50–$1.70. Against that, Q4 capex is expected to be $2.445B–$2.645B on the company’s accrual basis. That is a massive spending quarter.

Cash and marketable securities were $4.24B at quarter-end, with $7.57B of convertible debt.

Management plans to use existing cash and operating cash flow, with further financing possible.

Q3 operating cash flow of $433M was already below the $605M cash spent on equipment and capitalized software, leaving roughly negative $172M before acquisitions.

INOD

INOD put up $95.6M in revenue, up 53% year over year, and raised its full-year growth outlook to at least 42%.

Thirteen consecutive quarters of year-over-year growth now. And the annual growth floor has moved from 35% at the start of the year to 42% today.

The new largest customer signed a $29M program in Q3 and another approximately $18M program in Q4. Another hyperscaler has awarded five programs that management expects to take that customer from $3.1M of trailing revenue to more than $20M in 2027.

Those are meaningful wins. The business is still bringing in a lot of new work.

This was also Rahul Singhal’s first earnings call as CEO. The succession was announced in August and took effect September 30. Jack Abuhoff remains executive chairman and is still involved in research and the federal and enterprise opportunities. This was a planned handover.

The historically largest customer ended a program in mid-October. Management expects that to cost about $12M of Q4 revenue, with different programs at the same customer replacing it by Q1. This was one program ending, not an announcement that the entire customer had left.

An analyst asked directly whether the replacement work had been won.

Rahul said they were in late-stage discussions on several programs and anticipated filling the gap. No award was confirmed. The CFO also declined to say how the replacement work’s margins would compare.

So they’re still relying on those discussions turning into work. The timing and profitability of that replacement matter a lot here.

Then there are the employee layoffs.

Reports said roughly 550 people in India were let go in late October, with additional cuts reported in the Philippines. Employees described abruptly losing access, and a termination email cited a ramp-down in an assignment. Innodata had not confirmed the reported headcount.

Management didn’t directly address those reports on the call. An analyst did ask whether workers could be moved to other programs. Rahul said that sometimes they can and sometimes they can’t, depending on the skills and training needed. He didn’t give a headcount or address the reported employee experience.

We don’t know that all of those layoffs came from the program discussed on the call. But this is still something management needs to address more directly. Saying replacement work could come in Q1 doesn’t explain what happened to the people affected.

The annual guidance raise is encouraging, but it still allows for Q4 revenue to fall to roughly $79.5M at the implied floor, around 17% below Q3. They can raise the full-year number because of the revenue already delivered while still having a weaker quarter ahead.

Then there’s the diversification commentary. Back in Q2, management said the diversification they had planned for had been delivered. They have made progress reducing dependence on the old largest customer. The newer customer becoming a similar-sized account is a real achievement.

But the two largest customers now represent 74% of revenue, up from 71% in Q2. That still leaves an enormous amount of the business dependent on two companies changing their priorities and moving projects around.

Adjusted gross margin came down to 40%, from about 49% in Q2. To be fair, 40% was management’s stated target. They had already said the higher margin came from favorable mix, so this wasn’t a missed margin guide.

But revenue grew 53% year over year while GAAP operating profit fell about 26% to $8.7M. Adjusted EBITDA only grew 9% to $17.7M. A tax benefit helped the reported $0.29 EPS.

That is a very different picture from just looking at the revenue beat.

The 10-Q explains a lot of it. About $21M of the $23.1M year-over-year increase in direct costs came from buying content for AI projects. Labor-related costs increased only about $0.6M.

Management also said it deliberately takes some lower-margin work to build capabilities and future growth. That may be the right investment, but we need to see the profit follow. This margin decline happened in Q3, before the October program ended.

There is still a lot to like in the opportunity itself.

Physical AI needs data from the real world. Innodata is collecting human-perspective video, using motion capture and labeling robot training data. It reported more than 620,000 robot-executable labeling jobs this year. Agent training and safety testing are expanding too.

The release says one robotics pilot could generate about $7M in 2027 if it scales successfully. The call described that as up to $7M of run-rate revenue. The federal opportunity is another $5M a year for five years, but was still a verbal win awaiting a signature.

There’s plenty in the pipeline. The signed $29M and $18M programs show they can turn some of it into substantial contracts. The other opportunities still have to get there.

Cash is a clear positive. About $164M, or $153.7M excluding customer prepayments, up from $134.4M in Q2 on that same basis. No debt, no ATM shares sold in Q3, and roughly $10M of capex expected over the next twelve months.

They have room to invest without needing the kind of funding we’re talking about with AOI or Akamai.
