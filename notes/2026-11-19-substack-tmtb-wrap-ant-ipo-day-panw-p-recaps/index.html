PANW +10%: NNARR Blowout and a Raise ~2x the Beat Outweigh Applied Compute’s FCF Reset

Despite elevated positioning/sentiment going in, just a great quarter overall which should embolden cyber bulls after all the hacking headlines we’ve seen in the press. Main debate centers around the AC acquisition and what that means for margin/fcf profile and what it means vis-a-vis competition vs. the labs.

First the numbers: NNARR of $630M crushed the Street (~$450M) and buyside bogeys (~$520-600M), revenue posted its biggest beat since F4Q21, and all three segments accelerated. I have Organic NGS ARR growth ticking up to ~30%+. FY27 NGS ARR was raised $375M to $11.475-11.525B, which beat Street ($11.11B) and the ~$11.24B bogey: a raise 2.1x the $180M F1Q beat on the guide mid (the bogey assumed ~0.8x). We covered the full #s here last night.

Mgmt tied demand to an AI-driven vulnerability wave (critical/high CVEs at ~7x last year’s monthly pace) that is speeding up consolidation, firewall buying and virtual patching.

The main debate coming out is the Applied Compute acquisition impact to margins/FCF: $4.5B in stock for ~$100M of annualized revenue, targeting $1B of NGS ARR by FY29. Asked what led to it, the CEO said PANW must “get in the training business” to supervise AI: “That’s our bet.” Mgmt confirmed in callbacks it was excluded from the guide, and the deal takes up to 100bps off FY27 op margin and 200bps off FY28’s, back to 29-30% in FY29. That’s as low as ~27.5% in FY28 vs. Street’s 30.5%, up to ~$0.5B less OI. The FY28 FCF target drops to 38%+ from 40%+, now before data-center capex (<$3B through FY30): ~$0.4B under Street. Entering the training/inference biz comes with higher capex/lower margins, we know that and we’ve seen investors react differently and different points in time to shifts like this so it will be interesting to see how investor feedback evolves as time goes on. But so far, #s and Cyber tailwinds outweighing any debate.

Despite the margin/fcf hit, most investors I spoke to liked the strategic rationale for it although heard some debate around this potentially being a defensive move. Here’s a good take from TMTB Slack:

On model capabilities, Nikesh said he doesn’t expect model capabilities to “plateau for another 6 to 9 months”, so customers need “persistent frontier AI testing” for “12 to 18 months” before platform vendors absorb it (“I think that is two years out”). Intg as you imagine he has some insight int Labs pipeline.

Key Takeaways:

  • Callback. The key clarification was on cash: mgmt said 38%+ is not a ceiling for FY28+ adj. FCF margins (39-40% is possible as OM recovers in FY29), and the under-$3B capex is back-weighted to FY29-FY30 and incremental to the ~2% of revenue PANW spends today. Near term, Applied Compute runs on pay-as-you-go compute, its existing 3-year contracts and its ~$500M of cash and prepaid commitments, with owned capacity an ROI call once demand is visible. Mgmt added that $1B of ARR needs far less than $3B of capex, since the build includes room for future growth, and that it is optimizing for FCF dollars rather than margin; fully loaded FY28 FCF margin likely lands in the mid-30s
  • AI. Mgmt argues frontier models (it cited Mythos) have industrialized vulnerability discovery for attackers and defenders alike: critical/high CVEs are running ~7x the 2025 monthly average, recent outages were partly blamed on rushed patching, and Frontier AI Defense budgets are coming out of “emergency funds.” That is monetizing through Advanced Virtual Patching, one of the biggest sources of Q1 upside, sold as ATP+ at a ~50% uplift to an ATP subscription already attached to 80%+ of ~60K firewall customers (Gen 4+ boxes just switch it on). Prisma AIRS reached ~$170M of ARR (+~45% q/q, >1,100 customers), the fastest ramp in PANW’s history, and its AI gateway processed up to 3.8T tokens a day in October. Strategically, Nikesh said PANW is “pivoting to ensure all of our businesses are aligned towards token flow,” sizing security at 2-5% of token spend, a theme he previewed in September and has now put capital behind with Applied Compute.
  • Segments. All three segments accelerated under the new disclosure. Network & AI Security grew 20% to $2.28B (~17% pro forma vs. ~14% in FY26), well ahead of the low-double-digit FY27 framework from September, a gap callbacks attributed partly to upfront vs. ratable timing. Cortex grew 53% (+40% pro forma vs. a ~30% FY27 framework), with XSIAM ARR at ~$800M (+65%+, a touch slower than F4Q’s +70%) and observability ARR up ~4x off a ~$160M base, including a nine-figure frontier-lab migration that is now complete; the Prisma Cloud to Cortex Cloud migration is past its nadir. Idira grew 23% pro forma (FY27 framework: high teens to 20%), with underlying CyberArk ARR growing in the high 20s, Secrets & AI bookings >2x and 100+ cross-sell logos from the PANW base. Callbacks set F2Q q/q guardrails of LSD for Network & AI Security, MSD-HSD for Cortex and HSD for Idira.
  • Competition. PANW is taking share across form factors, with competitive displacements in SASE and firewall building for 12-15 months and accelerating as customers consolidate vendors to patch faster. SASE ARR grew 30% (~2x the market) with >$100M of net new NGS ARR, and software firewall ARR accelerated for a third straight quarter to +34% (from +29%), largely on Prisma AIRS. PANW is also going after FTNT’s enterprise installed base more directly rather than moving downmarket, and hired FTNT’s former North America GTM head, Trevor Pagliara, to run Network Security GTM. On pricing, SASE bandwidth tiers are now enforced, and with agentic SASE traffic up ~9x y/y, mgmt expects customers to blow past licensed capacity and traffic monetization to start in 6-9 months; none of that is in the guide.
  • Margins. GM of 75.2% fell 170bps y/y (but rose 40bps q/q), slightly below the Street’s ~75.5%, on hardware component costs and a heavier mix of early-stage SaaS; Cortex GMs stay below the company average over the medium term before returning to 70%+, and callbacks pointed to a q/q GM decline in F2Q. Opex leverage more than offset it: OM of 30.7% (+50bps y/y, a record) beat the Street’s 29.3%, with S&M down to 25.7% of revenue from 28.3% and AI efficiency work in quoting and procurement, while Idira’s margin is converging with the core in about half the 18-24 months originally targeted. The FY27 OM guide of 29.5% was held, implying ~29.1% for F2Q-F4Q, and EPS grew only 13% vs. 42% growth in op income because diluted shares rose 18% to 837M post-CyberArk.
  • Observability. the ~4x ARR growth includes nine figures from one frontier-lab customer whose migration finished in Q1, so Chronosphere growth now tracks that customer’s spend and its quarterly NNARR likely normalizes to ~$20-30M. Applied Compute adds more AI-native exposure, with some customer commitments approaching nine figures.

Bull vs Bear Debate

Bulls see PANW as the scaled consolidator in a market that AI has just made urgent. Frontier models have industrialized vulnerability discovery for attackers and defenders, turning cyber into a CEO-level, emergency-funded priority, and when attacks move at machine speed, stacks of 30-40 point vendors break down and buyers consolidate onto platforms with a shared data layer. PANW has the broadest one (network, SASE, SecOps, cloud, identity, observability and AI security), a ~70K-customer network security base to upsell with virtual patching and Gen 5 refreshes, and ~2,670 platformizations that already account for 2/3 of NGS ARR at >120% net retention. The long-term drivers are XSIAM displacing legacy SIEMs, SASE share gains from the #2 position, Idira cross-sell into the PANW base, and Prisma AIRS becoming the control point for enterprise AI. Bulls also like that mgmt is steering the model toward consumption, with AIRS, observability, SASE traffic metering and now Applied Compute all scaling with tokens rather than seats, the opposite of the AI seat-compression risk hanging over most of software.

This quarter directly addressed the main worry after F4Q, that growth would flatten once acquisitions lapped: organic NGS ARR growth ticked up, every segment accelerated, and mgmt raised the year by roughly twice the beat, which it rarely does in Q1. Bulls see more room. The FY27 guide still embeds ~29.1% OM for the rest of the year after a 30.7% Q1, implies 2H NNARR only ~5% above the September plan and includes nothing for SASE traffic metering, while virtual patching attach across the installed base is just starting and mgmt expects hardware strength to last at least another 12 months. Applied Compute is cheap optionality at under 2% of market cap from a team with a strong M&A record: XSIAM went from a similar scale to ~$800M of ARR in ~2.5 years, observability ARR quadrupled under PANW, and mgmt says AC’s planned capacity can support multi-billion ARR. If PANW proves it is a durable ~20% grower with consumption-linked AI exposure and 38-40% FCF margins, bulls argue the multiple should move toward the scaled 20%+ growth cohort and away from PANW’s own 33-36x history, especially with CRWD still trading at roughly twice PANW’s FCF multiple.

Bears think the quarter flatters a business that is getting lower quality and more capital intensive. Much of the upside came from sources that may not repeat: a nine-figure frontier-lab migration in observability that is now complete, a hardware surge mgmt partly attributes to customers buying ahead of price increases, and emergency Frontier AI Defense budgets that mgmt itself expects to be absorbed into platforms within about two years. Underneath, GM fell 170bps y/y, product GM dropped 6.6 points in a single quarter, Cortex SaaS margins sit below the company average, SBC rose to 18% of revenue (GAAP OM was ~3%), and FY27 EPS growth of ~12% trails ~27% revenue growth because the CyberArk deal lifted the share count 18%. Applied Compute compounds the problem: PANW is paying ~45x ARR, about 1.4x a private valuation set two weeks earlier, for a compute- and services-heavy business with neocloud-like GMs, taking up to 200bps off FY28 OM and redefining its FCF target to exclude up to $3B of data-center capex. It is the third large deal in a year after CyberArk (~$25B) and Chronosphere (~$3.35B) and the furthest yet from security, and Nikesh’s own rationale, that securing AI requires frontier-level models, arguably hands the long-term edge to the labs that train those models.

Bears also think several bull assumptions are too optimistic. Mgmt’s own timeline implies 12-18 months of elevated frontier testing and patching, which sets up difficult FY28 comps if budgets normalize; buy-ahead hardware demand can reverse, as firewall peers have learned; and AC’s $1B ARR target assumes enterprises adopt sovereign open-weight models at scale and buy training and inference from a security vendor rather than hyperscalers, neoclouds or specialized serving platforms, in a market where inference is commoditizing. The quarter carried its own warning signs: the FY27 raise is front-loaded and mgmt did not reiterate its 2H weighting, the FY RPO guide rose just $100M against a $375M ARR raise, XSIAM growth eased to 65%+ from 70%, TTM adj. FCF margin slipped to 37.3%, and FY27 OM is guided to ~29.1% for the rest of the year. The bigger risk is the multiple: PANW trades at ~44x CY28 FCF after the move vs. a 33-36x average over the past one to three years, and if investors value AC like a compute business and shift to fully loaded FCF, both the FCF base and the multiple compress.

P +5%: Core-Led Beat and raise, Surprise Price Cuts, but an Unchanged FY28 Outlook

Solid enough print for a consensus long. F3Q27 revenue of $1.46B (+51% y/y) and EPS of $0.86 beat Street $1.33B/$0.78, OM hit a record 23.6%, F4Q was guided above Street and FY27 was raised for a third straight quarter, but the $7.0–7.3B FY28 outlook was only reiterated.

The beat was core-led: the vast majority of the ~$495M y/y revenue increase came from the core, revenue accelerated with and without hyperscale, and the first meaningful hyperscale quarter (royalties at 75–85% GM) lifted product GM 110bps q/q and drove a ~300bps OM beat. The incremental surprise is that P cut average product prices for most models entering F4Q, funded by value engineering and explicitly aimed at share while peers are still raising. The pushback this morning is that the callback conceded there was probably no terabyte growth outside hyperscale, so core growth has been price and mix just as NAND spot prices roll over, and mgmt left FY28 untouched (now ~37% growth at the midpoint). Net, should be good enough for a consensus long as the the print de-risks FY27 and supports the Street moving to the top of the FY28 range, but still open questions around how FY28 actually ends up as

Key Takeaways

  • Callback. The key disclosure: it is hard to split core growth between price and terabytes, but there was “probably no terabyte growth outside of hyperscaler”; customers are spending everything their budgets allow and volume hasn’t kept pace with price, which confirms core growth has been price and mix and frames the F4Q cuts as an attempt to unlock elasticity. On FY28, mgmt said it isn’t walking away from the Analyst Day momentum and didn’t push back on delivering the same growth rate off the higher FY27 base, which would imply roughly $7.2–7.5B vs. the $7.0–7.3B range. The second customer is a top-five hyperscaler on the same deal structure and economics now being discussed with others; P is <1% of hyperscaler storage purchases and sells into data centers that already exist, so mgmt argued even a 50% cut in hyperscaler spend wouldn’t hurt it. HDD replacement remains a multi-year theme (at today’s prices the flash/disk price gap needs to narrow to ~3–4x, with new disk deals at 5–7 cents/GB), subscription GM should run low-to-mid 70s near term with bonus-accrual pressure easing into FY28, and neoclouds and sovereigns have been by far the biggest Scale AI opportunities.
  • Demand. Revenue accelerated to $1.460B, +51% y/y (last q +38% y/y) vs. Street $1.333B, +~38%, and product revenue grew 77% to $948M vs. Street ~$807M (+~51%); mgmt stressed that the vast majority of incremental dollars came from the core, with the new businesses growing faster than average off a small base. Drivers were more “franchise” deals where customers standardize on P across workloads, an unusually high number of large customers transacting, and a mix shift to higher-performance, lower-latency (“hotter”) workloads that carry higher ASPs; pipeline visibility for the next two quarters is “solid.” Mgmt called demand “solid overall” given component costs per terabyte at decade highs. US revenue grew 42% (+19% in F2Q) and international 73% (33% of revenue vs. a record 42% in F2Q).
  • Pricing. After repeated increases in 1H (starting with ~20% in February) and holding prices from F2Q through F3Q, P cut average product prices for most models at the start of F4Q, funded by value engineering (less memory, compute and component content for the same performance) and better data reduction; the cuts have gone to resellers and are reaching customers now. Mgmt reviews pricing monthly, and because discounting was standardized at the start of the year, list cuts flow straight through to street prices and ASPs. This runs opposite to peers (NetApp just announced an increase) and sets P up to take share from incumbents such as Dell, but it also starts unwinding the ASP tailwind that powered FY27 just as NAND spot prices fall.
  • Margins. GM of 69.2% (−490bps y/y, −70bps q/q) missed Street ~69.7% as subscription GM fell 230bps q/q to 72.6% (bonus accruals, cost of spares) vs. Street ~75.4%, while product GM rose 110bps q/q to 67.3% vs. Street ~66.5% as hyperscale royalties (75–85% GM) began to mix in. OM reached a record 23.6% (+330bps y/y) vs. Street 20.5%, with opex down to 45.5% of revenue. Core product GM ex-hyperscale stays at the low end of the 65–70% range while semiconductor prices stay high, the CFO wouldn’t call F3Q the trough, and the F4Q OI guide implies ~23.5% OM, flat q/q despite more hyperscale mix, so the price cuts are absorbing the mix benefit. The offset bulls will cite: mgmt said that when component prices fall it can “easily get to the higher end of the range.”
  • Hyperscale. Hyperscale sales “increased substantially” in the first quarter of meaningful contribution, booked as royalties within product revenue and landing exactly on plan; mgmt won’t disclose the figure until fiscal year-end, and Street estimates for F3Q run ~$100–150M. Further growth is expected in F4Q and FY28, and the second top-five hyperscaler should begin shipping around the beginning-to-middle of FY28 (de minimis this year). Mgmt flatly rejected the data-center build-out fears behind the November selloff, arguing that its small share and superior cost, power and performance let it take share even in a down market, and that falling NAND prices reopen HDD substitution while rising prices sharpen its edge vs. SSDs. Concentration stays high until the second customer ramps.
  • FY28. The preliminary FY28 outlook (revenue $7.0–7.3B, OI ~$1.7–1.9B, 24–26% OM) was reiterated but not raised, so implied growth slips to ~34–40%, or about 37% at the midpoint, off the higher FY27 base vs. 39–45% when introduced in September. The CEO was emphatic that it “was not a guide. It was flavor,” and that confidence in next year’s growth is “just as high as it ever was.” Two nuances matter: the Analyst Day outlook assumed “pricing stays reasonably near where it is today,” and P is now cutting prices into falling NAND; and post-print Street FY28 estimates of roughly $7.27B revenue, $1.86B OI and $4.33 EPS already sit in the upper half of the ranges, so the Street is underwriting the top end of a non-guide.
  • AI. Mgmt sees a “significant upturn” in mainstream enterprises (regular banks and insurers rather than HPC or quant shops) building on-prem GPU “AI factories” to run open-weight models at a fraction of cloud cost, a shift it ties to open-weight model gains (especially in coding) since early 2026; products directly attached to GPU environments (core AI) had a strong quarter. Wins and launches included Databento standardizing on FlashBlade//S, PureKVA (up to 20x faster time to first token), and FlashBlade//EXA topping every checkpointing and KV-cache metric in MLPerf Storage v3.0. The tone is more emphatic than September’s “pilot to production” framing, with mgmt now expecting this to “grow substantially over the next year,” though it says the trend was already in its Analyst Day CAGRs.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bulls see P as a structural share gainer that has become a multi-engine storage and data platform whose growth no longer hinges on the enterprise storage cycle. The core has taken share for more than 12 straight years, and its software-defined DirectFlash architecture (Purity managing raw NAND rather than off-the-shelf SSDs) lets P engineer cost out of its systems, which is exactly why it could cut prices entering F4Q while incumbents such as NetApp are still raising. Evergreen subscriptions (ARR $2.2B, +20%; RPO $4.2B, +44%) add a recurring, low-churn base, and Data Intelligence and Portworx extend P into a fragmented data-management layer that mgmt sees as a consolidation opportunity. On top sits hyperscale: royalties at 75–85% GM with little opex attached, now validated by two top-five hyperscalers on the same deal structure, with order commitments for tens of exabytes running into calendar 2028 and a pipeline across tech titans and neoclouds. Because P is still <1% of hyperscaler storage purchases in a hyperscale TAM mgmt sizes at $97B by 2030, bulls frame hyperscale as a share story rather than a capex story and see the November de-rating on AI-capex and data-center regulation fears as mis-pricing P as a memory derivative.

The quarter gave bulls plenty to point to. Growth accelerated to 51% with the core, not hyperscale, supplying most of the incremental dollars, and the first meaningful hyperscale quarter immediately showed up as a 110bps sequential product GM lift and a ~300bps OM beat, early evidence that hyperscale mix makes the 24–26% FY28 margin outlook conservative. FY27 has now been raised three times (from $4.41–4.51B in May to $5.20–5.21B), F4Q embeds only ~3% q/q growth after two quarters of 8–10% beats vs. the guide midpoint, and the callback suggested mgmt is comfortable repeating a ~40% growth rate off the higher base. Bulls read the price cut as offense: allocation risk has eased, P can cut while holding core product GM at the low end of its range, and mgmt argues that pent-up demand (spare capacity shrinking while data keeps growing) is released as prices fall, while cheaper NAND also reopens HDD replacement at hyperscalers. The enterprise on-prem AI factory trend adds a performance-driven demand vector that barely registered a year ago. Estimates can move higher on the second hyperscaler ramp from mid-FY28, any third hyperscaler or tech-titan win, volume elasticity as NAND deflates and product GM drifting toward the top of the 65–70% range once component costs fall, while the multiple can expand if investors re-classify P from memory proxy to a recurring- and royalty-weighted growth platform.

Bears see FY27’s growth as largely a NAND-inflation artifact that reverses in FY28. P raised prices repeatedly in 1H (starting with ~20% in February) and has been operating at pricing levels mgmt itself called a 10-year high; in F2Q system units were down and customers were paying more for less capacity, and the callback conceded there was probably no terabyte growth outside hyperscale in F3Q. That leaves core revenue sitting on a large ASP tailwind that is now flipping: P is cutting prices in F4Q, NAND wafer spot prices fell more than 20% in the three weeks to early November, and industry trackers now expect contract prices to start falling in 2Q27 and drop faster in 2H27, squarely inside P’s FY28. The FY28 outlook was built assuming pricing stays near September levels, and the CEO conceded that if prices suddenly drop P will have “several different quarters.” The bears’ precedent is 2019, when rapid NAND deflation pressured customer budgets and industry pricing, P missed its revenue guide and the multiple compressed hard. Beyond pricing, bears point to concentration (hyperscale is effectively one customer until the second ramps, with revenue timed to its deployments), an HDD-replacement leg mgmt itself places years out, and incumbents (Dell, NetApp, HPE) that can bundle and discount once falling NAND lowers everyone’s costs.

Several details in the print fit confirmed that view The beat came with a GM miss, a 230bps drop in subscription GM, core product GM pinned at the low end of the range and a CFO unwilling to call the trough, while the F4Q OI guide implies flat sequential OM despite more hyperscale mix, so price cuts are already absorbing the royalty benefit. E//One TCV fell to $188M from $277M and mgmt’s language slipped from “has accelerated to” a >$1B run rate to “accelerating towards” it, RPO growth is set to slow, capex jumped to ~12% of revenue, and the FCF guide didn’t move despite a $138M OI raise, so cash conversion is weaker than the EPS line suggests. Inventory more than tripled to $379M at peak component costs, a risk if NAND keeps falling, and mgmt’s own comments that many customers have spent their budgets for the year and that visibility runs about two quarters hardly underwrite ~40% FY28 growth. The bull case’s most optimistic assumptions are that elasticity arrives fast enough to offset ASP declines, that hyperscale ramps on time through data-center and permitting delays, and that investors will pay a premium multiple for a business still trading on memory-cycle sentiment. If FY28 core growth decelerates toward low double digits, Street estimates in the upper half of the $7.0–7.3B range (and ~$4.33 of FY28 EPS) are too high, and P’s P/E premium to NetApp, already down to ~60% from ~150% at the Sep-25 peak, can keep compressing toward pre-hyperscale levels.

ANT targets FY27/28 rev of $135B/$280B; Wolfe’s $135 PT implies ~$2.23T EV, or ~19x CY27E rev, with compute availability, competition, switching costs and AI commoditization the key risks.

Buy/$450.
