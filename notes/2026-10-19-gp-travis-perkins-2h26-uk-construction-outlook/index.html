SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed weak UK construction demand, Travis Perkins’ merchanting strategy, pricing durability, Toolstation’s market-share gains, and competitive pressures. The market remains in a low-growth phase as contractors preserve cash, purchase materials by project stage, and compare prices aggressively.

Private residential construction is the largest drag, while essential maintenance, civils, roofing, plumbing, and heating remain more resilient. The expert expects subdued conditions through the remainder of 2026, with a possible recovery beginning in spring 2027.

Key Insights

The expert attributed roughly three-quarters of current weakness to cyclical factors such as interest rates and housing turnover. The remainder reflects structural shifts toward direct manufacturer sourcing and specialist retailers. Current housing starts indicate stabilization rather than recovery. Sustained improvement would require consecutive quarters of materially higher starts plus renewed activity among smaller housebuilders. Travis Perkins has successfully protected margins by abandoning low-margin bulk orders, but this strategy caps volume and leaves fixed costs underutilized. The expert expects a need to pursue volume again during 2027. Further price increases risk pushing trade customers toward independent merchants. Recent margin improvement appears driven more by sales mix than shelf-price increases. Toolstation is benefiting from trade-customer acquisition, digital engagement, repeat purchases, and demand for essential repairs. The moderator explored these market-share gains and expansion opportunities extensively. Wage, employment, fuel, and distribution costs are rising faster than productivity, increasing pressure for additional overhead reductions. The expert favored investment in Toolstation Benelux rather than divestment, including more locations, broader ranges, and faster delivery.
