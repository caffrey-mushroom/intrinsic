原标题：Strong Earnings To Start The Week. LITE, FN, AEHR, TSEM, LSCC, ON

#optical #semis #npo

Substack · 2026-11-03

核心摘要

  1. Fabrinet季度收入14.74亿美元，比指引上限高约4,900万美元，同比+51%，12月季度指引约16.25亿美元、EPS 4.85美元。
  2. Fabrinet数据中心收入7.77亿美元、同比+78%，多个收发器客户在爬坡，NPO本财年晚些时候开始贡献，管理层认为OCS和NPO各自可成为每年数亿美元的机会。
  3. Fabrinet预计1月有约100亿美元年制造产能，Building 11在2028年中再加30亿美元；本季capex 1.59亿美元，经营现金流仅2,100万美元。
  4. 作者还点评了Lumentum在业绩加速时同意与Marvell合并，以及Tower的强劲数字。
  5. 文章覆盖AEHR、ON和LSCC的财报。
  6. 作者整体认为本周光通信和半导体财报开局强劲。

Alright, we’ve got a lot to get through.

On Sunday we went over what these companies needed to show.

Now we have FN and TSEM putting up some really strong numbers, and Lumentum agreeing to combine with Marvell while its earnings are ramping this quickly.

Let’s get into it.

I am going to cover FN, LITE, TSEM, AEHR, ON, LSCC.

FN

$1.474bn in revenue, about $49m above the TOP of the guide.

Adjusted EPS came in at $4.34, and December’s guide takes us to about $1.625bn and $4.85 at the midpoints.

So another 10% QoQ in revenue and almost 12% in EPS after growing revenue 51% YoY this quarter. Really strong.

Data center was $777m, up 78% YoY and 16% QoQ.

And they have multiple transceiver customers ramping now, alongside the direct hyperscaler business.

We have spent a lot of time talking about individual programs moving around. Having more customers contributing makes a big difference as these ramps get larger.

Now think about what is still ahead. NPO starts contributing later this fiscal year. CPO is further out. Management sees OCS and NPO each becoming opportunities worth hundreds of millions annually, and they have barely shipped anything for either yet.

FN is already growing like this before those get going. And as these products get harder to put together, FN thinks it can make more on the work too. Management expects operating margins to improve through FY2027, with newer programs helping gross margins over time.

They expect roughly $10bn of annual manufacturing capacity by January. December’s revenue guide annualizes to about $6.5bn. Then Building 11 adds another $3bn of capacity around mid-2028.

A lot of room to keep growing if these customer ramps come through.

We are paying for that space already. $159m of capex this quarter against $21m of operating cash flow, although $77m of the spend was a property purchase.

So the earnings look great, with quite a bit of cash still going back into the expansion.

Also, LITE and MRVL are both FN customers. Management said previous customer combinations had brought them more business. The CEO hadn’t spoken with either company about this deal yet, so we will have to hear how much of the combined manufacturing ends up with FN.

Overall very happy with these earnings. But the stock is at the mercy of the market of course. At least it was nice to see it not drop 10% on release (in fact it went up 14% at one time).

TSEM

SiPho revenue was about $240m this quarter.

By year-end, they expect twice the wafer-start capacity that supported those shipments. The fuller revenue benefit comes through in Q2 2027.

That is a pretty significant step up coming, and we already got $526m of total revenue this quarter, up 33% YoY. Gross profit grew 92% and adjusted EPS more than doubled to $1.15.

Look at how much of the extra revenue reached gross profit. Roughly $66m of sequential sales growth brought nearly $41m of extra gross profit. Management expects 55–58 cents of gross profit per additional dollar of sales even with more depreciation coming. Filling these fabs at those margins gets very powerful very quickly.

December is guided to $600m, another 14% QoQ. And they expect to grow again in Q1, which is usually the seasonally softer quarter.

Then Track-1 starts adding capacity in Q3 2027. Eventually that would give them room for roughly $720m of quarterly SiPho revenue. Three times what they shipped this quarter. And management says the capacity is spoken for, with signed agreements and contracts in advanced negotiations extending into 2029. That gives us a better sense of how long this ramp could last.

And with NPO, there is more silicon content per optical unit. More lanes, larger chips, a higher starting wafer price. The customers already buying their pluggable wafers are also the customers working on these new designs, with meaningful NPO shipments expected in H2 2027.

So as those customers move forward, Tower has a way to sell more into each connection while continuing to supply the existing products.

Remember the $3.6bn revenue and $1.2bn net-profit model for 2028 they gave us in August. It assumes 85% utilization once the $920m expansion is installed and qualified.

Today’s numbers help us work toward that model. The next few quarters need to keep showing the capacity coming online and customers taking the wafers.

Customers have also put up $305m of advances through September to help fund all of this.

Q3 operating cash flow was $185m, including $23m of advances, against $191m going into equipment. They are getting some help paying for the ramp.

LITE

Before getting into the deal, look at LITE’s December guide.

$5.90–6.20 in adjusted EPS, about 32% QoQ growth at the midpoint.

Revenue of $1.575–1.625bn, another 27% QoQ at the midpoint.

September revenue was $1.263bn, within their prior guide.

EPS of $4.57 was above the $4.05–4.35 range.

Adjusted operating margin reached 42.3%, and they are guiding 45–46% next quarter.

About 65% of the extra sequential revenue became additional operating income. Opex barely moved.

Pump lasers and OCS were important contributors here too, on top of the transceiver ramp.

And then another NPO/CPO award with more than $5bn of purchase commitments. They didn’t attach a delivery schedule, so we can’t drop that into next year’s numbers.

But that is a substantial commitment to the next generation while the current products are already producing these results.

Which makes the timing of the Marvell deal even more interesting.

Why combine with Marvell now?

Why would Hurlston give up control with the business growing like this?

He said they had looked at buying semiconductor businesses themselves. That tells us something about what he thought Lumentum needed. Marvell already has those capabilities, and combining gets them there faster. Murphy will run the company, with Hurlston joining the board.

As optics moves closer to the switching and compute silicon, more of the electronics and optical connection have to be designed together.

Lumentum gets a much larger role in that combined design. In exchange, its shareholders own about 33% of the new company.

I get why people are looking at this and wondering if Hurlston sees something coming for optical margins or oversupply or whatever.

We have shortages today, with a lot of money going toward adding supply.

At some point that supply catches up.

Meanwhile, customers are changing how they connect the chips, and that can change who gets paid the most.

Maybe this is him using a very strong period for LITE to get ahead of that.

The earnings and purchase commitments still point to growing optical demand.

Where those profits end up a few years from now, and what the market will be willing to pay for these stocks is the harder question.

4.5 MRVL shares for each LITE share. The $1,132 headline value used October 28 prices, so what LITE holders receive moves with MRVL.

Closing is expected in H1 2028, subject to approvals. There is a long way to go.

They are targeting more than $1bn in annual cost savings, mostly in production. That is a big part of how they make the combined earnings work.

For COHR, customers may value having a large independent supplier even more. But COHR also has a stronger competitor that can offer more of the whole connection.

Both LITE and MRVL said they would keep supplying components to other manufacturers, so we need to see whether customers actually shift orders.

Overall this is rough for LITE shareholders.

But MRVL is going to be an absolute powerhouse if/when this closes. Who knows what the stock price will be at that time, but I know it’s a company I would want to own eventually.

LSCC

$291m of revenue against a $245–265m guide. That is a big beat.

AMI (new acquisition) contributed $39m, but the existing FPGA business did $252m, up about 90% YoY. So there was plenty happening in the business they already had.

Adjusted EPS was $0.72. December’s guide is $0.82–0.86, with a $335m revenue midpoint and 72% adjusted gross margin.

Another 15% QoQ in sales, with margins moving higher too.

For 2027, management expects at least 60% revenue growth from roughly $1bn this year, including a full year of AMI.

That already assumes growth slows in the second half.

If the first-half pace holds up longer, there could be room for that number to move higher.

They see server shipments going from about 20m to 25m. At the same time, Lattice’s content has gone from one to three FPGAs per server to four-plus.

More servers, with more Lattice chips in each. You can see how that starts to add up.

As the racks get more complicated, they need more of the control, security and power-management work these chips handle.

Some of the upside this quarter came from finally shipping delayed orders.

But they also have backlog extending into H1 2028, and AMI’s security retrofit product reached its first production customer this quarter.

Broader adoption is expected in 2027, with the combined Lattice and AMI products contributing more meaningfully in 2028.

$77.6m of quarterly FCF also gives them money to start working down the acquisition debt as those products ramp.

ON

AI revenue reached $180m, up 39% QoQ.

They now expect about $620m this year and another doubling in 2027.

Google volume shipments started in Q3, so that relationship is already contributing.

Across the company, revenue was $1.72bn, up 11% YoY, with $0.90 in adjusted EPS. December’s guide only adds about 2.5% sequential revenue growth at the midpoint, but EPS goes to $1.01.

Gross margin is expected to improve from 41.2% to 42.5%.

That is what happens as more volume goes through the factories. There is about a two-quarter lag before the fuller utilization reaches margins, so some of the improvement is still ahead.

Auto is expected to fall high-single digits QoQ in Q4. Management says they are directing constrained supply elsewhere, with auto customer replenishment pushed into Q1.

We should see those shipments come back early next year if that is how it plays out.

They are also buying Ferric for $600m upfront and up to $175m in milestones, adding power regulation closer to the processor. It adds $500m to their 2030 AI revenue target, taking it above $3bn, with little near-term contribution.

So the doubling they expect next year comes from the existing AI ramp.

Capex moves from roughly 2% of revenue this year to 5% next year as they put more money behind it.

AEHR

Multiple SiPho customers placed more production orders. That is useful alongside what we just heard from TSEM and FN. Customers are buying the test equipment to support those optical volumes.

Revenue tripled YoY to $33m, with $46.7m in bookings.

Some deliveries moved forward into Q1, and management put the full-year outlook at $150m, the top of the previous $130–150m range.

They are shipping more than a dozen Sonoma systems a month to the lead hyperscaler.

A second processor is qualified for a planned 2027 ramp, and another potentially large customer starts evaluation in Taiwan early next year.

Getting those into production would give AEHR more customers to grow with.
