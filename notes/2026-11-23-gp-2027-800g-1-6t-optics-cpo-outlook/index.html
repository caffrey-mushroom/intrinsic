SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed demand for high-speed optical modules, the transition from copper to optical connections, competing interconnect architectures, and supply constraints across the AI data-center supply chain. The discussion covered scale-up connections within computing clusters, scale-out networking within data centers, and links between campuses. The expert expects optical interconnect demand to rise, while different technologies continue to coexist.

Key Insights

The expert expects inference growth to support, rather than weaken, optical demand: 800G modules should continue expanding, while training workloads drive faster adoption of 1.6T. In scale-up networks, CPO and NPO are positioned to displace some copper links as bandwidth and distance requirements increase. Nvidia’s integrated CPO approach may strengthen its control of scale-up, while white-box networking could broaden supplier choice in scale-out. The expert views NPO and CPO as competing options, not simply sequential stages. Adoption will vary by region: domestic Chinese systems are more likely to favor NPO, while Nvidia’s position could support CPO in North America. Silicon photonics can ease shortages of high-speed EMLs, but dependence on indium phosphide and lower yields remain constraints. The expert expects it to coexist with EML-based solutions. OIO was characterized as a longer-term technology, following broader CPO adoption.
