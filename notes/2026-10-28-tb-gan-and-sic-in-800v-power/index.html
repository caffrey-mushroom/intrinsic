Agenda

  • 800V adoption in AI data centres and implications for WBG (wide-bandgap) semiconductor demand
  • GaN (gallium nitride) and SiC (silicon carbide) technology characteristics, voltage ranges and application fit across AI infrastructure, EV and industrial power
  • Competition among Infineon Technologies (FSE: IFX), STMicroelectronics (NYSE: STM) and Innoscience (HKEX: 2577), focusing on differentiation and customer requirements
  • 2027-28 market size

Key Insights

  • Native 800V architectures could see system-level demonstrations and early deployments in H2 2027, followed by broader adoption in 2028 and meaningful GaN ramps in H2 2028 to early 2029
  • The GaN market is estimated at USD 550m-600m in 2026. Expert projects a c35% CAGR over the next 4-5 years, driven increasingly by AI infrastructure
  • Direct 800V-to-12V/6V conversion could represent USD 30-35 per kilowatt of GaN-addressable content, while predominantly SiC-based sidecar power supplies carry cUSD 40-50 per kilowatt
  • Scaling GaN from 6- to 8-inch wafers may deliver limited cost savings, as epitaxy remains the primary cost driver, and yield losses can offset larger-wafer benefits
  • STMicroelectronics has "very little in terms of GaN internally", making its Innoscience partnership its only viable catch-up path. However, geopolitical risks and Innoscience's IP lawsuits create uncertainties
