Agenda

  • Volume trends and key factors impacting propane margin per gallon
  • CAPEX blowout risk and EDP's (Energy Distribution Partners') ability to pass through heightened asset costs to customers
  • Operating constraints and unit cost drivers, including upside from network density gains

Key Insights

  • EDP faces potential customer churn post acquisition of bolt-ons. Expert notes one experience with immediate driver losses post takeover leading to service failures, resulting in c30% customer attrition in the first winter. Customer attrition can be particularly impactful, given the net loss on tank pick-ups
  • Regarding EDP unit economics, bobtail trucks cost cUSD 250,000-300,000 and can be run for 10 years contingent on predictive maintenance, with typical annual maintenance of cUSD 30,000. On-premises propane tanks cost cUSD 1,000-1,500, and 80-90% are customer-leased. Drivers and service technicians make USD 24-27 and USD 30-32 per hour, respectively
  • Expert believes EDP's ageing fleet could lead to maintenance costs per truck spiking 10-20% annually, with service truck crane repairs already up much higher YoY
  • Residential propane margins are highest, while agriculture and industrial spreads can be razor-thin – sometimes cents per gallon
