SUMMARY

Overview

The expert described how a large pharmaceutical company is investing in AI and digital tools across research, clinical development, manufacturing, and commercial operations. Priorities include improving existing systems, automating document workflows, and building a flexible AI infrastructure that can connect company data with internal and external tools. The discussion also covered vendor offerings, investment returns, and future budget pressures.

Key Insights

The company prefers commercial products when they fit, but will build internally when available tools do not meet specific needs. Its AI architecture is intended to remain adaptable rather than depend on a single provider. The expert sees AI as becoming a foundational capability, though its value is difficult to prove until AI-supported therapies reach approval. The company assesses potential returns using workflow savings and changes in a drug candidate’s likelihood of success. The expert expects AI spending to remain relatively protected over the next few years, since productivity improvements can help offset budget cuts. Broader scrutiny may intensify around 2029. The expert was critical of Veeva’s document-generation capabilities, while describing its document-management platform as strong. The expert also noted that Salesforce’s commercial AI tools face pressure to demonstrate measurable revenue gains.

A major platform provider with strong clinical document management. Its document-generation tools have not met the company’s needs for many specialized workflows, though simpler documents may be a fit. Its AI-supported medical, legal, and regulatory review has been useful.
