SUMMARY

Overview

A pharmaceutical development expert discussed clinical research organization demand across oncology and hematology. Sponsors are advancing more programs but remain selective, prioritizing assets with differentiated mechanisms, persuasive clinical evidence, clear regulatory pathways, and strong commercial potential.

Complex oncology programs involving antibody-drug conjugates, bispecifics, trispecifics, biomarkers, and novel combinations increasingly require specialist external support. Patent expirations are also pressuring large pharmaceutical companies to accelerate pipeline replacement. Outsourcing demand should strengthen gradually, although continued portfolio pruning makes a sharp industry boom unlikely.

Key Insights

CRO demand is recovering as sponsors commit resources to pivotal studies and increasingly global development programs. Large pharmaceutical companies want internal ownership of data, analytics, forecasting, and AI-enabled decision tools while continuing to outsource execution, monitoring, recruitment, and site management. AI is expected to improve trial speed, quality, and cost efficiency before materially increasing trial volume. CROs will initially retain much of the productivity benefit from AI. Sponsors should later seek lower pricing as procurement teams gain evidence of reduced labor requirements. China-based CROs offer lower costs, recruitment access, and strong connections to local biotechnology ecosystems. However, the expert remains cautious about using them as lead partners for global registrational studies due to regulatory, geopolitical, and execution concerns. SMOs can deliver predictable recruitment and strong retention at defined site networks, while CROs remain better suited to managing multinational portfolios. The outlook is cautiously positive, with bookings likely to improve before revenue fully reflects stronger demand.
