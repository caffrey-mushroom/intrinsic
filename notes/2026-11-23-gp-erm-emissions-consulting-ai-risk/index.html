SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a former digital-products leader at ERM, described the firm’s emissions-related consulting and software activities, competitive position, client concentration, and response to regulatory and technological changes. Their experience centered on emissions, carbon trading rules, predictive monitoring, and related software.

Key Insights

ERM’s strength in emissions work comes from specialist knowledge and regulatory experience. The expert said this can favor ERM over engineering-focused competitors when regulatory interpretation matters; detailed engineering scopes may favor those competitors. Client relationships and technical credibility are important in winning work, particularly with risk-averse industrial customers. However, the expert described some ERM rates as excessive, saying price could end discussions with prospective clients. Revenue is concentrated among a small group of major clients. This brings focus but leaves the firm exposed if a key customer takes work elsewhere. The expert estimated that relationship-led and competitively tendered work were roughly balanced, with limited forecasting certainty beyond a year. The expert sees AI as a major strategic risk, particularly for regulation-heavy advisory work that depends on interpreting large volumes of complex material. They believe ERM has been slow to adapt its traditional, hours-based consulting model. Fieldwork and technical work requiring people on site were viewed as less exposed. European compliance requirements, including CBAM and UK participation in the EU emissions trading scheme, were identified as potential demand supports. US policy changes were said to have weakened demand for emissions-reduction initiatives, while routine compliance and permitting work held up better. The expert could not reliably assess ERM’s overall future financial performance or margins.
