SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed vocacapsaicin, a locally administered capsaicin prodrug being developed for postoperative pain. The expert specializes in regional anesthesia and acute pain medicine and treats approximately 100–200 surgical patients monthly across several noncardiac specialties.

Vocacapsaicin could complement multimodal analgesia through a non-local-anesthetic mechanism. Its potential advantages include prolonged pain control, reduced opioid use, compatibility with nerve blocks, and avoidance of motor weakness. The expert estimated that more than half of surgical cases could benefit from an additional analgesic mechanism.

Key Insights

Opioid-free recovery was considered the most meaningful endpoint, followed by total opioid consumption and then pain scores. The bunionectomy pain reduction was viewed as modest, although the opioid-free result was more compelling. In knee replacement, the pain benefit was not considered impressive, but the reduction in opioid consumption was clinically meaningful. The lack of comparable efficacy at 60 mg versus 36 mg was considered unusual and raises dose-response questions. Smaller, localized incisions appear better suited to surgical-site infiltration than extensive procedures such as abdominoplasty. Lack of motor weakness could provide a major advantage over local anesthetics, particularly for extremity surgery. Adoption will depend primarily on efficacy and local safety, especially wound healing, tissue injury, and infection risk. A price below $500 per procedure was viewed as important for hospital adoption.

Clinical Data

Surgical model

Findings

Expert assessment

Bunionectomy

A 147-patient Phase 2 trial improved 96-hour resting pain and reduced opioid use. Opioid-free rates were 26% versus 5%, while consumption fell by 50%.

Pain improvement was modest; opioid freedom was the strongest result.

Knee replacement

An initial 54-patient study at 5–15 mg showed no pain benefit. A later 187-patient study succeeded only at 36 mg, not 60 mg.

The inconsistent dose response requires clarification.

Abdominoplasty

A 54-patient Phase 2 study at 10–15 mg failed.

Large treatment areas may dilute effectiveness.

Ventral hernia repair

A 24-patient study produced an early efficacy signal.

Evidence remains preliminary.
