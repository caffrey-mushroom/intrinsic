Anthony DeRuijter (AD) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • Centrus Energy's (NYSE: LEU) key demand risks and implications for long-term commercial viability
  • Scaling and project execution bottlenecks for key growth projects, including Oak ridge and Piketon
  • Feasibility of HALEU (high-assay low-enriched uranium) production, considering infrastructure limitations and technological hurdles

Key Insights

  • On Russian import ban, Tenex's current oversupply implies that western separative work units prices would be lower without ban, creating supply-demand imbalance that could benefit Centrus post-2027
  • Expert considers Centrus's USD 900m in government backing as its greatest competitive advantage, allowing freedom to absorb mistakes during scaling without direct capital drawdowns, unlike competitors still developing pilot technology
  • If Centrus's physical infrastructure isn't visibly under construction by year-end 2027, its stated 2029 commercial delivery target could become potentially unachievable, regardless of parallel technological progress
  • Key operational bottlenecks are unlikely to be centrifuge technology itself, but adjacent areas such as cylinder availability, electrical infrastructure lead times and workforce experience gaps from United States Enrichment Corp's retired operators
  • On HALEU oversupply risk, combining Centrus's 12 metric tonnes with General Matter's potential 10 metric tonnes could create significant early-market oversupply if advanced reactor demand develops slower than projected
