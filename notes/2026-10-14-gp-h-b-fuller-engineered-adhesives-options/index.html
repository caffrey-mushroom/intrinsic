SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed a global adhesive manufacturer’s engineered adhesives business, including growth prospects in electric vehicles, space systems, robotics, semiconductors, electronics, and medical applications. It also examined whether selling the building adhesives division would affect operations or strengthen the company’s shift toward higher-value, technology-intensive markets.

Key Insights

The expert expects engineered adhesives to outgrow the company’s other divisions because its technology platform has not reached its full commercial potential. Electric vehicles offer opportunities across batteries, electric motors, electronics, and premium interior materials. The company appears particularly well positioned in vehicle interiors and selected battery applications. Space-related demand is attractive because of the company’s capabilities in electronics, energy systems, and solar-panel materials. China’s electronics weakness was attributed mainly to longer smartphone replacement cycles and stronger local competitors, rather than semiconductor shortages. The expert viewed the slowdown as temporary. Selling building adhesives would probably have little direct effect on engineered adhesives. Technology, manufacturing, purchasing, and customer overlap are limited, although shared chemistry and production facilities could complicate separation. Medical and surgical adhesives support the strategic transition from high-volume products toward smaller-volume, higher- margin solutions. Geographic expansion into Asia and developing healthcare markets could create substantial growth, subject to successful integration. Customer selection varies by market. Consumer electronics emphasizes innovation and rapid problem-solving, while automotive, medical, and semiconductor markets prioritize reliability, approvals, and long qualification histories. Acquisitions are often the fastest route into highly regulated or entrenched applications.
