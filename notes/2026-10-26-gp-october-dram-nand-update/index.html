SUMMARY

Overview

A memory-industry expert discussed DRAM and NAND conditions across consumer electronics, enterprise systems, and AI infrastructure. Consumer demand has weakened as prices rise, while enterprise and hyperscale demand remains robust. DRAM is materially tighter than NAND, supporting stronger pricing.

The expert expects disciplined supply growth to sustain favorable market conditions through 2027. However, additional capacity and softer consumer demand could bring the market closer to balance or modest oversupply in 2028.

Key Insights

Fourth-quarter contract pricing matched expectations, with DRAM increasing more sharply than NAND. DRAM shortages are unlikely to ease in the next quarter, and pricing should continue rising throughout 2027. Consumer customers are delaying purchases and reducing inventory because module vendors are discounting products to generate cash and protect revenue. Enterprise buyers have fewer substitution options because of stricter quality requirements. They are instead adopting more QLC NAND and modestly broadening approved specifications. Consumer manufacturers are evaluating refurbished components, lower capacities, and downgraded NAND. Broad adoption depends on competitors standardizing similar configurations. PCB availability has emerged as a short-term enterprise bottleneck, although inventory building could alleviate it in a subsequent quarter. The expert was more cautious than suppliers projecting shortages through 2030. Long-term agreements provide protection, but new capacity and weaker consumer demand could alter market balance. NAND-based cache architectures could strengthen future NAND demand, but meaningful adoption is unlikely in early 2027 and was not central to the forecast.
