Zia Ahmed (ZA) Independent Consultant

Garrett Waters (GW) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • MaxLinear's (NASDAQ: MXL) shift towards higher-growth infrastructure and implications for revenue mix
  • Competitive positioning across optical interconnects, focusing on Keystone 800G and Rushmore 1.6T
  • Customer qualifications, market share gains and foundry strategy, including Samsung (KRX: 005930) diversification
  • Medium-to-long-term outlook

Key Insights

  • MaxLinear's 26-28 week optical lead times largely reflect standard manufacturing and logistics cycles, rather than necessarily indicating sustained customer demand
  • TSMC's capacity constraints could limit Keystone's volume ramp even as customer demand grows, while MaxLinear's longstanding Samsung relationship should provide better foundry support for Rushmore
  • Rushmore's 2027 ramp benefits from Keystone algorithms and the same design team, but faces execution risks around jitter, power efficiency and Samsung's manufacturing yields
  • MaxLinear's analog front-end quality and power efficiency could differentiate Rushmore at 1.6T, although outsourced packaging may put its thermal performance behind Broadcom
