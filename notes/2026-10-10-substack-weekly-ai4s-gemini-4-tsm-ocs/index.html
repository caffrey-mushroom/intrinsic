Substack · 2026-10-10

Google is back in the frontier race, and we think a new training cycle is beginning. As we argue in this week’s column, Gemini 4 is evidence that scaling still works; the next test is how much post-training improves coding and long tasks in Gemini 4.1. With OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and xAI all investing to defend or improve their positions, we remain bullish on the networking and optical interconnects needed to keep larger clusters productive.

AI for science gives us another reason to be optimistic about where that compute can go. OpenAI’s latest mathematics release still needs independent scrutiny, but progress in problems with verifiable answers could help AI take on more research and engineering work. Drug discovery, materials and energy research offer much broader potential benefits. Physical experiments and clinical trials will still take time; our optimism is about expanding the ideas scientists can test and the connections they can make across disciplines.

The supply chain is preparing for that investment. We see TSMC’s growth increasingly governed by how quickly capacity comes online, and have raised our ASML EUV shipment forecasts again. Marvell’s Investor Day targets are moving closer to our forecasts, backed by longer-term supplier planning. OCS adds another source of optical demand as content per accelerator rises, although deployment beyond Google remains an important assumption in our longer-term model.

On the application side, we are watching retention and cost together. Muse’s improving retention gives Meta a reason to expand acquisition once the economics support it, while computer use opens a much larger pool of work to automation. Our expert interviews also underline the obstacles: high token consumption can limit enterprise adoption, and physical capacity still depends on manufacturing yields, equipment and power. Better models and more infrastructure need to translate into reliable, affordable work.

Deep | CRWV: Capacity Expansion and Further Upside to Revenue per GW

We initiate with a constructive view of CoreWeave’s capacity expansion and long-term customer agreements. Our forecasts put active power at 2.10/3.60/5.40GW at year-end 2026/27/28, with annualized revenue per GW rising to $10.5b/$12b/$13b; execution and realized pricing remain central to the outcome.

Deep | TWLO: How Much Can Twilio Earn When Agents Make Calls?

Personal agents calling businesses could create incremental demand for Twilio’s voice APIs and carrier connections. A scenario of 300mn monthly active users making two calls per month implies $126m of annualized revenue, about 2.1% of Twilio’s 26Q2 annualized revenue, before allowing for displaced existing communications and changes in share or pricing.

Weekly Expert Interviews Summary

A snapshot of the expert interviews we conducted during the past week is below; full transcripts and takeaways are available on the FUNDA platform.

Anthropic: Computer-Use Economics and Enterprise Strategy

Executive, Leading AI research & deployment company

The expert sees high token consumption as a constraint on enterprise computer-use adoption and discusses whether protocol-based automation can improve the economics.

FTAI Power: Execution Risks Behind the Expansion Target (FTAI)

Manager, Leading Aviation Services Provider

The expert questions the feasibility of 100 sets in 2027, citing Montreal space, dedicated staffing and supply-chain constraints that could push delivery into 2029–30.

Memory: Supply Constraints and Customer Negotiations

Former Procurement Manager, Global Cloud Technology Company

The expert sees tight HBM and server DRAM supply supporting pricing, with phased negotiations and long-term agreements moderating increases rather than eliminating them.

HVLP Copper Foil: High-End Capacity Remains Hard to Add

Executive, Regional China Copper Foil Manufacturer

The expert highlights finishing-equipment constraints and yield losses at higher grades and thinner gauges, leaving high-end supply tight even as lower-end HVLP faces potential oversupply.

Trainium: Manufacturing Yields and Capacity Diversification (AMZN)

Executive, a major hyperscaler

The expert reports liquid-cooled rack first-pass yields of roughly 50% in Mexico, with alternative capacity also facing equipment lead times, licensing and power constraints.

Enterprise AI: Spending Shifts Toward Core Platforms (OKTA, MSFT, CRM, NOW, MDB, SNOW)

Strategy Executive, Top US telecom provider

The expert sees AI spending favoring core security, cloud, database and workflow platforms while reducing overlapping point tools, with additional infrastructure spending following model adoption.

Diagnostics: AI Security and Data Modernization (PANW, OKTA, CRWD, SNOW, TEAM)

AI Executive, Mid-sized diagnostics firm

The expert describes a sharp rise in AI security spending from a small base, alongside platform consolidation and Snowflake investment tied to data modernization and agent workloads.
