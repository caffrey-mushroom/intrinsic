TSLA: Barclays upgrades to OW/$450 as SPCX takeout optionality joins improving core + Robotaxi inflection

Barclays upgrades TSLA to OW and raises PT to $450, arguing upside is no longer just about a potential SPCX combination: core auto trends are improving after two years of declines, Energy is nearly sold out for 2027 with >50% shipment growth potential on data-center demand, and Robotaxi miles have inflected sharply alongside FSD uptake >75% on newly purchased North American Teslas in 3Q. Barclays still sees a potential SPCX/TSLA merger and associated premium as an important catalyst, but now views improving fundamentals and Robotaxi momentum as standalone support for the stock.

TSLA: DB says SpaceX merger could become the main stock driver; Robotaxi progress tangible, Optimus still early

DB keeps Buy/$420 and says a potential SpaceX combination may increasingly anchor TSLA’s valuation, potentially creating a floor as investors handicap the premium needed to secure shareholder approval. Robotaxi is the clearest operating positive: unsupervised miles reached 3.1M, ~9x in three months, Cybercab is already in paid Austin service, and broader 24/7/geographic expansion is planned into 2027. Optimus remains much earlier, with production-quality units starting but management still describing a difficult S-curve; DB does raise energy-storage expectations as AI data-center demand improves pricing

SPCX: DB raises PT to $250 as compute pricing + capacity economics look better than expected

DB says near-term estimates remain too low after 3Q, with Customer B already generating ~$1.68B/month vs. its prior ~$1.25B assumption, suggesting an earlier renewal and/or pricing above the original ~$31/GW. DB also sees SpaceX’s vertically integrated build model sustaining ~$35–36B/GW capex, with a 440K Vera Rubin GPU training cluster (~1.3–1.4GW) due by 2Q27 and spot-market economics implying ~8–9 month paybacks. Funding looks manageable despite a ~$175B gap under a 10GW 2027 build scenario, with DB expecting investment-grade/GPU-backed debt; Buy, PT $250 from $235.

SPCX: Bernstein raises PT to $268 as terrestrial AI capacity/pricing materially expands the revenue path

Bernstein raises PT to $268 from $248, primarily on a much larger terrestrial AI build: SPCX now assumes ~12.8GW of terrestrial compute by YE27 and ~20.1GW by YE28, with average compute pricing raised to ~$26/W in 2027 versus recent deals above $30–50/W. Enterprise/Government Connectivity also strengthens, with Bernstein seeing the segment scaling from ~$500B toward ~$1T of revenue exiting 2027. Orbital AI remains the LT thesis, with first data-center launches targeted for late 2027/mid-2028 operations, but the nearer-term upside now comes from terrestrial compute economics. OP/$268

TikTok’s U.S. joint venture is exploring several gigawatts of computing capacity, with one source putting its target at 4GW by the early 2030s. The company confirmed Oracle will remain its primary cloud provider. The report adds a sizable potential demand source, but identifies no signed capacity contract or newly selected supplier.

MU / Memory: New capacity may take until 2028 to lift supply – Chosun Biz

Samsung and SK Hynix’s record investment is initially funding cleanrooms and HBM capacity, with industry forecasts cited by Chosun Biz pointing to more meaningful memory-supply growth from 2028. NVDA: Barclays says cash flow fully covers AI ecosystem commitments; financing/circularity fears look overdone

Barclays estimates NVDA has only ~$250B of customer-related off-balance-sheet commitments despite headlines around ~$500B of strategic partnerships, since NVDA’s support is generally capped at ~25% and structured as residual-value backstops rather than full financing. Against that, Barclays models cash rising from ~$99B today to ~$230B by Apr-27 and FCF reaching ~$100B/quarter by Jul-27, implying current cash + NTM FCF covers commitments by ~125% even after buybacks/dividends. The takeaway is that NVDA can keep using its balance sheet to accelerate AI infrastructure adoption without stressing its own liquidity, making current financing/circularity concerns look manageable.

AMZN: UBS raises PT to $348, sees AWS margin estimates too low despite heavier 2027 CapEx

UBS argues the key debate has shifted from AWS growth to profitability: while AI workloads are lower-margin and 2027 CapEx could rise to ~$250–300B, investors may be underestimating operating leverage as AWS adds ~$80B of incremental revenue against only ~$20B of D&A, limiting the SG&A increase needed to hold margins. UBS sees AWS operating margin ~46% vs. Street ~38% in 2027, which should force upward EPS/FCF revisions even as spending stays elevated. Buy, PT $348 from $318.

CART / DASH / UBER: Instacart explores a sale – Bloomberg (out late Friday)

Instacart has hired an adviser and explored a sale, including preliminary discussions with DoorDash and Uber. Bloomberg independently reported that Uber walked away and is no longer in talks; other discussions remain early and may not produce a transaction. Instacart shares gained 9.2% Friday, making deal probability and potential delivery-market consolidation the next focus.

CART: Wedbush sees M&A optionality as Uber/DASH grocery ambitions converge with Instacart’s assets

Wedbush sees clear strategic rationale for CART to combine with Uber or DoorDash: Instacart brings grocery aggregation, enterprise capabilities and a strong advertising business, while either buyer adds restaurant delivery, loyalty and broader local-commerce distribution. Uber could cross-sell across ~150M members, while DASH is already scaling Grocery/Retail rapidly; regulatory risk looks manageable given Walmart/Amazon’s strength. CART still captures ~60% of grocery intermediary e-commerce sales, supporting standalone value as well. OP/$63.

MSFT: Oppenheimer raises PT to $625, sees Azure accelerating toward 50% as capacity constraints ease

Oppenheimer sees healthy enterprise demand supporting sustained Azure/M365 acceleration, modeling F1Q Azure +46% cc vs. buyside ~47% and F2Q approaching 50% as incremental capacity comes online. MSFT plans to double data-center capacity over two years, while Maia 300 deployments begin in 2027 with ~40% more tokens/MW than leading GPUs, supporting better AI economics. Copilot is also evolving from an Office upsell toward a broader enterprise execution platform with monetization tied to users/workloads. OP, PT $625 from $570.

MSFT: Truist raises PT to $600, sees durable high-40s Azure growth + balance-sheet advantage as AI spend scrutiny rises

Truist expects Azure to outperform the ~45% F1Q27 cc guide and sustain high-40s growth into 2QFY27/H2 as capacity unlocks, efficiency improves and Copilot/agentic workloads scale. The incremental bull case is financial durability: MSFT has the tightest CDS spreads among hyperscalers/AI infra peers and is the only Big Six name Truist expects to remain FCF positive in CY27 despite elevated AI capex, giving it more flexibility if AI monetization takes longer. Buy, PT $600 from $575.

MSFT: Truist raises PT to $600 as Azure durability + FCF resilience differentiate amid AI funding concerns

Truist expects Azure to outperform the ~45% F1Q27 cc guide and sustain at least mid-40s growth into F2Q/H2 as capacity unlocks, enterprise AI broadens beyond frontier labs and Microsoft increasingly becomes the orchestration/control plane for 1P/3P agents. The more differentiated angle is financial: MSFT has the tightest CDS among hyperscalers/AI infra and is the only Big Six name Truist expects to remain FCF positive in CY27 despite elevated capex, giving it materially more flexibility if AI monetization takes longer. Truist lifts LT revenue CAGR to 14.5% from 12%; Buy, PT $600 from $575

AAPL: BofA says Duo demand remains exceptionally tight while iPhone 18 Pro availability normalizes

BofA’s Week 7 tracker shows iPhone Duo global lead times still ~79 days, with U.S. carrier waits widening to 57 days from 50 and Apple.com at ~91 days; Duo also remains largely unavailable for store pickup. By contrast, iPhone 18 Pro lead times fell to 4 days from 8 globally and just 1 day in China, while Pro Max stays tighter at ~15 days. The read remains strong for Duo demand/mix but increasingly normal for the standard Pro cycle. BofA reiterates Buy.

ORCL: Baird says Investor Day needs to de-risk funding + customer concentration more than raise growth targets

Baird stays OP and sees AI World as primarily about restoring confidence in Oracle’s funding path and reducing concentration concerns, with OpenAI still just under 50% of RPO. A further FY27 raise would help, but the bigger swing factors are future capital needs, funding sources and margin evolution; GPU renewals clearing at ~20% premiums suggest useful-life assumptions may be conservative. Power execution remains mixed, with Project Jupiter’s El Paso Electric agreement rejected while Wisconsin’s Project Lighthouse advances.

ORCL: Wells cuts PT to $250 as funding/CapEx visibility becomes the key Investor Day debate

Wells remains OW but cuts PT to $250 from $280, arguing ORCL’s AI opportunity is intact while investors increasingly need clarity on how the FY30 buildout is financed. RPO has risen to ~$664B from ~$455B, but ~$105B of contracts now involve BYOH/customer prepayments, which carry very different revenue economics than traditional OCI deals; GPU/component inflation also raises questions around returns on older contracts.

LITE: StoneX initiates Buy/$1,200 as optical capacity stays sold out into 2029

StoneX says qualified optical capacity is the core scarcity asset, with AI-related optoelectronic supply effectively committed into early 2029 and demand still exceeding supply despite >50% EML capacity growth through YE26. OCS is the clearest bridge to FY28 earnings, while 1.6T ramps through FY27 and NPO/CPO/ELS create additional architecture upside; NPO timing has also pulled forward into late CY27/early CY28. StoneX models FY27/28 revenue of $6.38B/$10.66B and EPS of $22.03/$38.01, still ~5% below management’s FY28 ~$40 framework. Buy/$1,200.

COHR: StoneX initiates Buy/$425 as 6-inch InP scale-up unlocks AI optical growth

StoneX sees qualified InP output as the main constraint and earnings lever, with capacity targeted to more than double by YE27 as Sherman/Durham ramp and Zurich comes online. 1.6T and Photolink should push above ~$3B of annualized revenue by FY27-end, while CPO/NPO production ramps in 2HCY27 add further upside. StoneX models FY27/28 revenue of $10.63B/$14.42B and EPS of $9.40/$13.71, with margins expanding as larger wafers, automation and internal laser content improve yields/cost. Buy/$425.

NTAP: Guggenheim initiates Neutral as AI/storage demand supports FY27 upside, but pricing-driven growth looks hard to sustain

Guggenheim sees a strong near-term setup, with F1Q27 revenue growth accelerating to ~30% and FY27 potentially +27% vs. Street +18%, helped by AI infrastructure modernization, AFX/Novus/AIDE and resilient all-flash demand despite large price increases. The concern is durability: capacity shipments are still declining high-single digits y/y, implying much of the current acceleration is pricing rather than volume, while evidence of sustained share gains remains limited. Guggenheim sees FY28 EPS ~$13 vs. Street ~$11.50 but expects growth to fade back toward MSD beyond FY28. Neutral, no PT.

NTNX: Guggenheim initiates Buy/$100 as VMware displacement + AI/Kubernetes create path to 20% growth

Guggenheim sees Nutanix as the clearest enterprise-wide VMware replacement, with share gains and forced bundle pricing supporting a durable replacement cycle; it models revenue growth accelerating to ~17% in FY27 and ~20% by FY29 alongside 20% New ARR growth. Storage partnerships and public cloud broaden the opportunity, while Agentic AI/Kubernetes products provide upside beyond the core HCI thesis, including potential Portfolio Solutions ACV expansion to ~34% by FY29. At ~17x FY28 FCF on Guggenheim’s estimates, valuation looks undemanding; Buy/$100.

BE: Evercore ISI says 2028 earnings power is the bigger story; capacity + operating leverage can overwhelm near-term FCF noise

Evercore argues the market is over-focusing on quarterly FCF after 3Q, while BE’s bigger setup is a path to at least 5GW of capacity and sharply higher incremental margins as manufacturing scales. House estimates have adj. EBITDA margin rising from ~24% in 2026 to ~38% in 2028, with incremental margins reaching ~46% in 2027 and ~58% in 2028 as utilization, automation, sourcing and overhead absorption improve. FY26 revenue guidance at ~$4.5B implies +122% y/y, while capacity expansion beyond 5GW is already being prepared incrementally rather than through one large factory bet. OP/$350

ARM: StoneX says AGI CPU demand is strong but foundry supply caps near-term upside; Hold

StoneX sees strong demand for ARM’s AGI CPUs, but foundry capacity remains the binding constraint, limiting FY28 CPU revenue to ~$8.0B vs. Street ~$8.3B and leaving META as the only signed customer until ARM secures more advanced-node supply. Licensing remains healthy at +25–30% in FY27 as customers convert to subscriptions, while royalties should reaccelerate in FY28 as Cloud AI grows from ~14% of royalty mix today toward ~35–40% by FY31. StoneX likes the LT setup but sees limited NT upside at >100x NTM non-GAAP EPS; Hold, no PT.

ARM: Wells sees another tough print setup, but early AGI guide update could be the catalyst

Wells expects limited near-term estimate revisions, with F2Q27 royalties ~+17% and licensing +27% y/y as data-center strength is partly offset by smartphone weakness. The more important debate is AGI CPUs: management has become incrementally confident in >$2B of backlog vs. the prior >$1B revenue framework, creating potential for an early guide update; Wells currently embeds ~$100M of AGI revenue in F4Q27. Valuation remains demanding, but a higher AGI outlook could reset the debate. OW/$280.

ASML: TD Cowen CEO meetings reinforce sold-out CY27/28 capacity + margin upside from productivity pricing

TD Cowen says ASML’s semi supply/demand imbalance should persist through CY28, with CY27/28 capacity effectively sold out and order activity for 2028 still building; Cowen models 90/122 EUV units in 2027/28. The mix also improves as 3800E ramps in 2027 and 3800F in 2028, while higher-throughput tools and upgrade pricing support ~150–200bps of GM expansion from the mid-50s over two years, with LT potential toward 60%. CY27 DUV should benefit from immersion mix and CY28 shifts more toward memory, while ASML continues expanding manufacturing flexibility and the Southeast Asia supplier base.

DDOG: Oppenheimer raises PT to $330, sees ~4% 3Q beat as AI + multi-product expansion sustain 20%+ growth

Oppenheimer expects 3Q revenue of ~$1.20B, +35% y/y and ~4% above Street, with NRR holding 120%+ on strong enterprise/large-customer momentum. Checks point to accelerating software development and AI adoption, with Bits AI plus GPU/LLM/agent observability and AI Guard broadening DDOG’s AI opportunity beyond its largest customers; 52% of customers now use 4+ products and 13% use 10+, leaving further cross-sell runway. Security (~$100M ARR), Flex Logs, federal and international add additional FY27 levers. OP, PT $330 from $300.

DOCN: UBS more constructive into print, but funding remains the gating factor; PT to $105

UBS sees a favorable 3Q setup, with high-30s revenue growth supported by Kansas City utilization and Memphis coming online; a FY27 guide raise to 60%+ could be enough despite buyside expectations closer to ~75–80%. Checks remain strong, with AI demand described as “absolutely insatiable” and pre-lease lead times stretching beyond a year. The key debate remains financing, with UBS estimating ~$4B of incremental capital needed through mid-2029 to fund ~300MW of expansion. Neutral, PT $105 from $125.

GLW: JPM sees stable display pricing supporting 4Q, but 2027 visibility remains limited

JPM says October LCD TV panel pricing was flat m/m vs. a typical ~1% decline and should remain flat in November, supporting GLW’s 4Q Display performance despite weak smartphone/IT demand and component-cost inflation. Chinese TV makers are cutting 4Q26 panel purchases ~8–10%+, concentrated in low-margin small displays, while larger-screen mix and potential December price increases provide offsets. Near-term read is modestly positive, but JPM says 2027 panel demand/pricing visibility remains limited.

SE: Morgan Stanley cuts PT to $128 as Sea Cloud capex/funding concerns outweigh Monee IPO value unlock

MS stays OW but cuts PT to $128 from $153, arguing Sea Cloud introduces a new capex/FCF overhang just as investors were rewarding Shopee’s improving profitability and operating leverage. The simultaneous Monee IPO, while positive for valuation transparency and independent funding, has heightened concern that proceeds could effectively subsidize a capital-intensive cloud build. MS sees Sea Cloud’s software-heavy, ~500-engineer positioning as potentially less capex-intensive than feared, but says the key debate is whether SE can monetize its existing stack without restarting a major investment cycle.

RBLX: JPM sees modest engagement improvement for a second straight week as tail activity broadens

JPM’s weekly tracker shows peak platform CCUs +1% w/w to 26.9M, with October MTD y/y declines improving to -12% from -17% in September. Steal An Egg continues to fade, but broader tail engagement rose ~5% w/w and Murder Mystery 2 spiked to 1.7M peak CCUs on its Halloween update, helping offset viral-title normalization. JPM expects the Hollow Halloween event to provide an additional engagement tailwind through Oct. 31.

FIG: Piper sees AI Credit monetization running well ahead of plan; Design Agent adds a new upside leg

Piper says Figma’s AI monetization is already exceeding expectations: 2Q AI Credit revenue of ~$42M beat its prior ~$28M estimate by ~51%, lifting FY26 Make AI Credit revenue to ~$123M from ~$104M despite August’s effectively lower credit pricing. Piper models Make AI Credit growth of ~50% in FY27 and total AI Credit revenue +~90% as Design Agent and Weave begin contributing, with ~$55M combined FY27 revenue and Design Agent alone potentially scaling to a $200M+ run-rate product. OW/$30.

NOW: BNP partner checks reinforce AI governance + Control Tower upside; adoption broadening beyond core IT

BNP’s ServiceNow World Forum checks were broadly positive, with customers/partners highlighting NOW’s role in governing and automating AI workflows and one customer citing ~30–40% IT help-desk savings. Control Tower adoption remains very early but interest is accelerating sharply, while Armis/security assets plus CMDB strengthen NOW’s positioning as an enterprise AI orchestration/governance layer. Partners are also hiring aggressively into their NOW practices and seeing adoption expand beyond IT into employee and customer workflows; premium pricing remains the main friction.

NOW: MS sees attractive Q3 setup, with Federal the cleanest positive and cRPO upside possible

MS keeps OW/$180 as channel checks come in-line to ahead, with strong enterprise deals and Federal demand the healthiest in 2+ years. Headline cRPO guide is +20% cc, but ~17% organically after M&A, making buyside’s ~22% bar dependent on a meaningful acceleration; AI Control Tower adoption is also building, reaching >25% penetration at one partner. Hybrid/token pricing remains a watch, though MS views it as sector-wide rather than NOW-specific.

EBAY/ETSY: Needham sees differentiated marketplaces as key winners from agentic shopping

Needham argues agentic commerce should benefit marketplaces with unique inventory, strong trust/services and non-routine purchases, with EBAY and ETSY best positioned as agents improve search/discovery rather than disintermediate them. EBAY’s authenticity/fraud/KYC infrastructure and increasingly unique inventory via Depop provide defenses, while ETSY’s 5.7M sellers and differentiated goods make its inventory valuable to agent platforms; both are also working to make listings more agent-accessible. Commodity marketplaces face more pricing/take-rate risk, while ride-share looks particularly exposed as agents make cross-app comparison frictionless. EBAY Buy/$135; ETSY Buy/$100.

INTU: Oppenheimer raises PT to $406 as Big Bets + AI support high-teens growth

Oppenheimer comes away more comfortable with INTU’s updated multi-year strategy, centered on scaling its “Big Bets” (~30% of FY26 revenue, growing >30%), accelerating customer growth and monetizing AI-driven product innovation. TurboTax Live remains the largest upside lever, with FY26 revenue +37% y/y and a $99 assisted “Sign & File” offering expected to drive DIY-to-Live migration, while Business/Consumer Money and mid-market products add further growth. With high-teens+ adjusted EPS growth still intact and valuation near historical lows, Oppenheimer raises PT to $406 from $380; OP.

FTNT: Baird sees sector-best checks into 3Q, with 2027 durability the bigger catalyst

Baird’s channel checks are the strongest in recent quarters, with partner net score improving to +33% and refresh activity broadening into wireless/SD-WAN and integrated security. It models 3Q billings of ~$2.30B, +27.5% y/y, with 28.8% OpM and 36% FCF margin; AI data center, OT and sovereign SASE add incremental demand beyond the core refresh cycle. After the stock’s ~124% YTD run, the key catalyst is management reinforcing durable 2027 growth rather than the magnitude of the 3Q beat.
