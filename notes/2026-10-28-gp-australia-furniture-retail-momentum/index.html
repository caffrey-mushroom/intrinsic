SUMMARY

Overview

An Australian furniture retail expert discussed weak consumer sentiment, holiday trading expectations, pricing strategies and competition across Temple & Webster, Nick Scali and other major retailers. Higher interest rates are causing shoppers to delay purchases, choose cheaper products or switch to lower-priced retailers. Premium consumers remain more resilient but are increasingly waiting for promotions.

Lower-ticket homewares, including rugs, mirrors, cushions and decorative products, are outperforming larger furniture categories. Retailers use these products to increase store visits and create opportunities to sell higher-value furniture.

Key Insights

Black Friday has overtaken Christmas and Boxing Day as the most important promotional period for Australian furniture retail. The expert expects Black Friday trading to improve meaningfully from recent weak months but remain below the prior comparable period. Retailers are protecting profitability through deep discounts on selected promotional or discontinued products while limiting reductions on core ranges. Product availability will be decisive. Retailers offering immediate delivery should outperform those with long lead times. Temple & Webster faces a critical test as it shifts from aggressive customer acquisition toward higher margins. Its recent sales improvement does not yet prove that customers accept higher prices. Nick Scali remains one of the sector’s strongest operators despite weakening sales. Its pressures appear primarily related to consumer demand and competition rather than its systems disruption. Higher Asian freight costs threaten margins and may eventually increase retail prices. Business-to-business demand is becoming more cautious, particularly for new construction, while renovation activity is holding up better.
