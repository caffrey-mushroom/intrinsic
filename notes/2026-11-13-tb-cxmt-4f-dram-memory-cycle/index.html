2026.11.13 | TB | Memory | CXMT 4F² DRAM & Memory Cycle

原标题：China's Memory Chip Market – Former Senior R&D Executive at Wuhan Chuxing Technology Co Ltd (Conducted in Mandarin)

Third Bridge · 2026-11-13

核心摘要

  1. 这位前武汉存储芯片研发高管表示，长鑫存储（CXMT）的4F²技术采用垂直环栅晶体管，将CMOS外围电路和存储阵列分别做在两片晶圆上，再通过混合键合互连；今年才开始流片，最早要到2027年才会有外部可见的出货，真正量产预计在2028年。
  2. 三星和SK海力士同样走4F²路线（三星引入IGZO材料抑制漏电），三星计划约在2028年量产；美光因电容间距瓶颈以及ALD、外延工艺成本高，选择从6F²直接跳到3D DRAM，时间预计在2028年之后；长鑫因拿不到EUV、在3D DRAM材料上没有优势，只能走4F²路线。
  3. 从6F²到4F²，理论上单元面积缩小可带来约50%的产出提升，但受电容尺寸极限、双晶圆键合的良率损失以及键合材料的限制，实际提升可能只有20-30%。
  4. 这20-30%的产出增量叠加各大厂的激进扩产，若2028年后AI需求降温，行业可能在2028-2030年由上行周期转为供过于求；专家认为供需明显转向最早可能出现在2029年。
  5. 长鑫的第五代DDR5平台已实现量产，但量不大，有源区半间距11.95nm（约12nm节点），引入了高K金属栅，相当于三大厂的1b/1c代；由于DUV需要四重曝光，成本更高，但延迟和读写带宽与SK海力士差距不大，6F²路线已接近极限。
  6. 对于英伟达在Rubin Ultra上从12Hi降到8Hi HBM的传闻，专家认为在3D DRAM成熟之前，没有能替代HBM或DRAM的产品，AI硬件对带宽和容量的需求只会继续上升，根本解决之道是技术突破或扩产。
  7. 预计四季度DRAM合约价环比涨幅将从约40%放缓至约20%，原因包括季节性、长约锁定产能以及渠道库存偏高；2027年现有晶圆厂已接近满产，新产能要到2028年才会放量，长鑫新产线主要满足国内需求。
  8. 专家认为在2028年前国内长约违约的可能性很小，除非出现战争等黑天鹅事件，DRAM价格难以回落到2025年水平；地缘冲突的主要风险在于设备、材料和化学品供应中断，以及终端产品销往中东和东南亚受阻。

Expert [00:02:10]: 4F Square, which was only disclosed this year, mainly uses its own vertical surrounding-gate transistors. In addition, it fabricates CMOS logic and memory cell arrays on two separate wafers and interconnects them through hybrid bonding. From a global perspective, the other three companies have long had some research in this area. In terms of progress and the technology gap, CXMT will basically carry out tape-out this year and it has not yet reached the mass production stage. If it wants to reach mass production, it must pass customer verification and improve yield before it can officially start mass production and then ramp up. Therefore, there is still a long way to go before its true commercialisation. Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron started researching this a long time ago. There are some differences in the design of memory cells. For example, in SGT, the channel was originally on the same plane as the gate but it is now made into a three-dimensional vertical structure, with the gate wrapping around it from the periphery to control the short-channel effect and leakage current.

For Samsung, it will also make the transistor channels vertical but its approach will not use the surrounding structure adopted by CXMT. Instead, the channel and gate will be arranged vertically on two planes. Of course, it will later also adopt the approach of splitting memory cells and peripheral circuits across two wafers and then interconnecting them by bonding. It is just that the materials used for bonding and the bonding dimensions will also be somewhat different. In this regard, Samsung will introduce the replacement of some traditional silicon-based materials with IGZO, possibly drawing more on advanced materials in 3D DRAM to suppress leakage current. SK Hynix is also not much different. It essentially transitions the conventional planar structure into a three-dimensional vertical architecture to shrink its memory cell footprint.

However, Micron has made it public that it will not take this route. The main reason is that, for 4F Square, there is a relatively large bottleneck in truly increasing its density: the spacing between capacitor plates cannot be reduced indefinitely. This bottleneck will still weaken the density advantage gained from moving from 6F Square to 4F Square. In addition, it has also been noticed that, in terms of processes such as ALD and epitaxy, these processes have high process requirements and relatively high costs in size miniaturisation, which will also weaken the benefits brought by the area reduction from 6F Square to 4F Square. Therefore, Micron intends to move directly from 6F Square to 3D DRAM.

Therefore, the three companies have made different choices. For Samsung and SK Hynix, when choosing between 4F Square and 3D DRAM, they still want to continue with 4F Square, mainly because 3D DRAM is not particularly mature yet. Secondly, they still have their unique advantages in 4F Square. For example, on the materials front, they can introduce IGZO to mitigate process challenges. Micron Technology gave up this path mainly because, from its perspective, the gap between cost and benefit was not as large as advertised and the process had some stringent requirements. As a result, the yield could not easily ramp up to mass production as traditional DRAM could, so it abandoned this path based on cost and benefit considerations. In fact, this is also a result of different strategic choices made by each company.

The biggest reason why CXMT chooses this path is that it has no advantage in 3D DRAM materials. In addition, it is unable to obtain advanced EUV lithography machines. Therefore, it is bound to continue to move to advanced nodes by converting from 6F Square to 4F Square, which may also bring some cost increase. However, if it can narrow the technology gap with the top three and capture market share by doing so, it regards this as an acceptable cost. As a result, all four companies will make different choices.

Rationally speaking, the approach of Samsung and SK Hynix should be the safest. Micron's approach is relatively aggressive, as it aims to leapfrog directly into 3D DRAM to capture early market leadership. For CXMT, it is similarly forced to choose this path due to some restrictions. Looking at all four companies as a whole, this is the case for 4F Square technology. There are subtle differences in technology among them. In terms of mass production pace, CXMT will not achieve meaningful shipment volume until 2027 at the earliest that is visible to the external market.

For Samsung, its current plan is to mass-produce 4F Square technology around 2028. It is expected that CXMT will actually launch products in 2028. For Micron, if it goes directly to 3D DRAM, the timing will also be after 2028 based on current estimates. Broadly speaking, for these four companies, these are the main points regarding 4F Square. Each company has its own different considerations in making its choice.

00:15:36] Regarding the 4F Square and 6F Square cell architectures, is the primary goal to increase die output per wafer by reducing the memory cell area by 33%? If this technology is realised by 2028, will it significantly expand industry capacity?

Expert [00:15:36]: Yes, transitioning from 6F Square to 4F Square shrinks the footprint per memory cell. In theory, this miniaturisation can indeed have a relatively noticeable effect but in actual practice, it will be affected by many factors. As just mentioned, if capacitors are used for memory, capacitor devices are definitely required. The size of this device cannot be infinitely reduced. Its area determines its memory capacity and the spacing affects its memory stability. Normally, the capacitor dielectric must remain non-conductive; however, if the spacing is reduced indefinitely, issues such as quantum electron tunnelling, unintended leakage conduction or dielectric breakdown will occur. Therefore, this is the limiting condition for its size reduction.

The second factor is that under the conventional horizontal 6F Square structure, peripheral CMOS circuits and memory cells could be fabricated on the same wafer. Now that it has been changed to this vertical structure, it can no longer be manufactured on the same wafer, because there will be some leakage problems as just mentioned. Therefore, this solution can only fabricate the peripheral CMOS circuits and the memory cell array (transistors and capacitors) on two separate wafers for production. This will lead to a problem: when manufactured on a single wafer, its main circuit is directly conductive, so there is no need to couple the two wafers together through hybrid bonding.

Once there are two wafers, a problem arises. Previously, it was relatively easy to improve the yield on a single wafer but now, with two wafers, you need to improve the yield of each wafer separately and then couple them together. In this coupling process, the individual yields of the two wafers will be reduced to some extent, meaning there is a yield penalty. Therefore, the yield improvement will differ from that of the previous single wafer and the yield will also affect the actual number of known good memory dies that can ultimately be yielded.

In addition, hybrid bonding itself is subject to many constraints. The choice of bonding materials and bonding dimensions is limited by the technology and these constraints also restrict the electrical performance of the device, as well as its heat dissipation and energy consumption. Therefore, theoretically speaking, switching from 6F Square to 4F Square will indeed reduce the area by about 50%. Theoretically, production output should increase by 50%. However, due to the factors just mentioned, the expected increase may only reach 20-30%.

Whether this increase is sufficient to offset the cost concerns introduced by this technology is also a key consideration for Micron. It abandoned this technology because, after calculating the costs and benefits, it concluded that it would be better to move directly to 3D DRAM. To answer your earlier question, size reduction does indeed bring about an increase in output. However, due to the factors just mentioned, this increase will be somewhat discounted, so it will not be as large as 50%, and it may only be 20% or 30%.
