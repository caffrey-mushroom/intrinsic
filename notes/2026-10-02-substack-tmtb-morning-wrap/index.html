HDDs/WDC/STX: Toshiba to invest 60 billion yen to double HDD supply; demand expands due to difficulties in semiconductor memory procurement - Trendforce

AI data centers are driving a new wave of demand for high-capacity storage, prompting Toshiba to significantly expand its HDD production. According to Nikkei, the Japanese technology group plans to double production capacity for hard disk drives used in AI data centers within fiscal 2027. Toshiba will invest roughly JPY 60 billion (US$380 million) to expand its facilities in the Philippines.

The expansion marks Toshiba’s first major HDD investment in around five years. In addition to adding production lines at its Philippine plant, the company will begin handling new products that increase storage capacity per drive by as much as 40%, the report notes. Looking further ahead, Toshiba plans to work toward mass production of 65TB-class HDDs in 2030, followed eventually by 100TB-class models.

Samsung / HBM: Proposes 2027 HBM4 prices above 3× current HBM3E – Maeil Business

Maeil Business reports Samsung has proposed 2027 HBM4 prices in the mid-to-high $4 range per gigabit, versus about $1.50 for HBM3E, as annual supply negotiations approach their final stage this month. Samsung is seeking higher prices for HBM4’s performance, with the report citing stable 11.7Gbps transfer speeds and a peak of 13Gbps. Maeil also cites TrendForce forecasting industry HBM average selling prices up 121% in 2027, driven by a greater HBM4 sales mix and constrained supply.

AVGO: UBS NDR with CEO/CFO points to upside to FY27/28 AI guide, with XPU + networking momentum intact

UBS says after a virtual NDR with AVGO’s CEO/CFO, management framed FY27/FY28 AI revenue guidance as conservative, with v8i ramping in 2027 and its flagship XPU customer committed to the next two generations shipping in 2028/29; AVGO also confirmed ~4–5GW/year of XPU shipments in CY27. UBS sees rising content from SRAM, ARM CPU and advanced packaging, while networking is already ~30% of AI revenue and could grow faster as Tomahawk 7 doubles throughput and switch/interconnect counts rise. Powered-shell constraints are viewed as a timing issue rather than structural; UBS stays Buy/$470.

AVGO / Anthropic: Banks assemble $60B AI chip financing package – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Broadcom’s banking syndicate is preparing a $60B financing package to fund AI chips and infrastructure for Anthropic and other customers. Banks are preparing to syndicate a $42B senior-secured tranche, while Blackstone is leading $18B of junior debt, committing $9B from its funds and planning to distribute the remainder. NVDA: Lenders seek stronger guarantees on GPU-backed loans – Reuters

Reuters reports lenders are pushing for stronger guarantees under Nvidia’s $500B financing initiative, questioning how much GPUs will be worth as long-term collateral. Banks typically underwrite chips over three to four years, versus Nvidia’s argument that they can generate revenue for a decade. Tens of billions of dollars of prospective loans are expected to carry stronger customer contracts and Nvidia guarantees; bankers say some structures may require investment-grade customer revenue rather than relying primarily on chip resale values.

AMZN / NVDA: Amazon explores $8B GPU sale and leaseback – FT

FT reports Amazon is in talks to place roughly $8B of Nvidia Grace Blackwell chips into a special-purpose vehicle funded by outside investors, then lease the equipment back. The chips are already deployed across more than a dozen US data centers in five states. Investors expect the vehicle to obtain an investment-grade rating supported by Amazon’s credit quality. The proposal comes as Amazon expects $220B of capital expenditure this year, primarily for AWS.

EFX / TRU / FICO: FHFA plans to cut mortgage credit-report requirement to two bureaus – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports FHFA plans to require lenders selling mortgages to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to obtain credit data from two bureaus rather than all three of Equifax, Experian and TransUnion. An announcement could come as soon as October 12, with implementation one to three months later. The move follows FHFA’s decision to use the same mortgage pricing grid for VantageScore 4.0 and Classic FICO scores, eliminating the previous 20-point difference.

ON / SYNA: Revises Synaptics deal to $123 cash per share – onsemi

onsemi says it will acquire Synaptics for $123 per share in cash, or approximately $5.7B, replacing the original stock agreement valued at about $7B when announced. The revision follows an unsolicited competing proposal. onsemi now expects immediate non-GAAP EPS accretion and has identified additional revenue and manufacturing synergies beyond the previously announced $200M annual target. Morgan Stanley has committed debt financing, and closing remains expected by mid-2027.

SPCX / xAI: Compute rentals expand as customer commitments outgrow capacity – The Information

Reuters cites IDC estimating Huawei’s China shipments rose 13% in January–August despite a 7% decline in the overall market.

Samsung / Smartphones: Raises US Galaxy S26 prices by $100 to $200 – The Verge

The Verge reports Samsung has increased US prices by $100 across most Galaxy S26 configurations, lifting the base model to $999, while the 1TB S26 Ultra rises $200 to $1,999. The S26 and S26 Plus had already launched $100 above their predecessors, with Samsung partly attributing those earlier increases to higher memory costs. Apple and Google have also raised phone prices this year.

NVDA / China: State-backed firm disclosed restricted Blackwell chip financing – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Semi-Tech Leasing, a Chinese state-backed financier, funded servers containing restricted Nvidia chips for AI infrastructure developer Glory View Technology. Investing.com’s account of the report says one disclosed transaction covered 32 Asus servers using B300 Blackwell chips, within financing for more than 700 servers. The report cites over RMB3B of sale-and-leaseback funding for Glory View since mid-2025, with much of the equipment intended for a China Mobile computing hub in Ningxia.

NVDA / China: US charges exporter over more than $300M of restricted servers – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports US prosecutors arrested the owner of Earthmade Computer over an alleged scheme to send more than $300M of export-controlled servers containing Nvidia GPUs to China. One cited purchase involved 27 servers containing H100 GPUs for about $7.6M.

PSKY: Warner acquisition debt falls sharply in initial trading – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports investors suffered more than $100M of losses shortly after Paramount Skydance issued $52B of debt to fund its Warner acquisition. OpenAI: Notifies more than 100 organizations of unauthorized agent activity – Reuters

Reuters reports OpenAI has alerted more than 100 organizations about unauthorized activity by its AI agents as it reviews roughly 50 petabytes of records following the Hugging Face breach. The review is expected to take months. OpenAI says it is adding technical and operational controls to address unintended internet use and inadequate restrictions on agent activity.

DIS: Plans TV consolidation and hundreds of additional job cuts – WSJ

WSJ reports Disney is preparing a television restructuring expected to consolidate divisions and eliminate hundreds of jobs. The plan would organize programming around streaming customers instead of separate legacy television brands and could affect executives across its production units, with further reductions expected at ABC News. Details may not be finalized until year-end. The changes follow more than 300 cuts this week, largely in human resources and IT.

CMCSA: NBCUniversal cuts hundreds of streaming technology jobs – Business Insider

Business Insider reports NBCUniversal is cutting a few hundred employees in its global streaming technology organization, including engineering and quality assurance roles. Most cuts affect Sky in the UK, with some US positions also eliminated.
