SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, an experienced electronic-chemicals and facilities manager, discussed construction schedules, cleanroom and utility execution, contractor roles, and production qualification for a leading foundry’s U.S. fabs and a proposed Texas project. The discussion highlighted labor availability, specialized equipment, and the time required to match tools and qualify processes.

Key Insights

The Arizona P2 schedule appears relatively secure, while tool deliveries are a key risk for P3 and P4. The expert viewed the advanced-packaging site as most exposed to delays, citing complex coordination, customer qualification, and challenging process transfers. Taiwanese contractors are likely to contribute most through technical expertise, design standards, vendor qualification, and commissioning oversight. U.S. firms are better positioned to provide on-site labor and deliver base-building, power, and HVAC work. The Texas project would likely add to, rather than replace, existing construction. The expert expects it to extend the build cycle, as specialized cleanroom labor and supporting facilities are concentrated in already busy regions. Simultaneous construction may help keep contractors engaged but could lengthen the U.S. production ramp. The expert identified chamber matching and limited engineering support as difficult-to-compress constraints. For advanced packaging, chip-on-wafer qualification is expected before the more technically demanding system-on-integrated- chip process.

Taiwanese contractors

Strong in technical design, ultrapure water, contamination control, cleanroom work, and tool hookups. Their ability to execute extensive on-site work is limited by labor availability.

U.S. national EPC firms

Better placed to supply local labor and handle base-building, power, and HVAC packages, supported by established regional footprints.

Leading foundry

Expected to retain control of critical specifications, quality, procurement, and long-lead packages, while using local firms for installation and field execution.

Advanced-packaging partner

Separate-site involvement could complicate process transfer and qualification, despite simpler cleanroom requirements for some initial steps.

GP: TSMC's chairman has stated that any Terafab-related fab "must be operated by TSMC," while Tesla's side has described a model where TSMC would lease part of a facility built and operated by Terafab affiliates. Setting aside ownership structure, what would each of these two models imply for the scope, sequencing, and contracting structure of cleanroom/MEP work packages?

EXPERT: I would look for TSMC domination in the scope. I think there is quite a bit different than Musk's simplified vision of a clean room, so there would be a lot more emphasis on cross-contamination, especially when you have different products that are going through Terafab that are way different in their purity requirements. So I think the MEP packages will be designed to fit the different purity levels and be more complex than one size fits all.

As far as sequencing, it's all fast, so there is no particular mode-it's much different: just infrastructure first and then a lot of modular skid installation before tool installation, which is last, and no different than usual.

As far as contracting, TSMC would dominate because they have direct procurement, and then I think all the contractors would be under that after they were tied down to their TSMC timelines for their equipment deliveries. They would have to meet the TSMC quality standards in terms of contracting.

GP: How would the contracting chain differ specifically in the lease-and-operate model: which cleanroom/MEP packages would Terafab affiliates or a local EPC likely control, and which would TSMC retain direct control over?

EXPERT: I think TSMC would contract with their POR contractors, so they would control the quality and execution of delivered equipment, especially the long lead time stuff: CDS, which stands for Chemical Delivery Systems, scrubbers, the cleanroom structures, the bulkheads, and the electrical. I think they would retain control of those. The contractors are mainly there to install the equipment per the specifications.

GP: TSMC has already committed $265 billion across up to 12 Arizona facilities. If Terafab becomes an incremental site rather than a substitute, how would that incremental demand interact with the existing Arizona cleanroom/MEP capacity constraint-would it primarily extend the duration of the current build cycle or add a parallel peak in 2028-2029?

Even though the size is enormous, I still see it being incremental just due to the prior commitments that they already have with 12 fabs and what they're doing.
