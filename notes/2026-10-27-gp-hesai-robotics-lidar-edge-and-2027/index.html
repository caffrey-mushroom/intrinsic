SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined humanoid-robot lidar demand, pricing, applications, competition, and Hesai Technology’s growth prospects. The expert expects market expansion to depend primarily on factory logistics, mobile assembly, and other industrial uses requiring three-dimensional perception. Research, education, and data collection currently dominate humanoid-robot applications, while scaled industrial deployment remains limited.

Lidar is not essential for every robot because cameras, depth vision, and algorithms can address some tasks. Sensor configurations have not standardized and may range from no lidar to several units per robot.

Key Insights

The expert views Hesai and RoboSense as the two leading Chinese robotic-lidar suppliers. Hesai’s strongest barriers are its proprietary chips, systems-engineering expertise, automotive-grade production experience, quality control, cost-reduction capability, and broad customer relationships. Hesai’s compact JT128 was considered particularly competitive for humanoid robots because of its wide field of view, low weight, small size, and suitability for embedded installation. Current humanoid-robot demand remains too small to fully demonstrate Hesai’s manufacturing advantages. Most announced customers are still testing, validating prototypes, or placing small orders. Industrial deployment is essential for sustainable market growth. Scientific research and demonstration demand alone cannot support large-scale adoption. Pricing pressure is already severe and could intensify sharply as volumes rise. Suppliers are accepting aggressive pricing to secure design positions because integrated lidar systems are difficult to replace after adoption. Overseas expansion could materially improve Hesai’s outlook, although market-access and geopolitical restrictions create uncertainty.
