Agenda

  • SMID (small- and mid-cap) biotech macro spending priorities and R&D software budget allocation trends
  • Customer evaluation criteria when choosing among Schrödinger (NASDAQ: SDGR), competitors and open-source solutions
  • Impacts from compute-token pricing models on virtual screening volume and workflows
  • Software simulation tool limitations in eliminating physical wet-lab testing for downstream tasks, such as toxicology and safety validation
  • Software spending outlook and willingness to adopt new AI platform modules

Key Insights

  • SMID biotechs insulate core molecular-modelling software budgets from economic downturns, protecting platforms such as Schrödinger during capital pullbacks, while directing initial expenditure cuts towards contract research organisation staff and lab testing
  • Developing machine learning models drives physical synthesis and lab assay spending, because computational teams must synthesise inactive compounds to generate training data required for model training
  • High token costs for Schrödinger's Glide docking tool push computational discovery teams to run large-scale virtual compound screening on open-source software, saving expensive Glide tokens for final, high-precision binding predictions
  • Established computational drug-discovery suites face unbundling risks unless vendors offer enterprise informatics platforms such as LiveDesign to streamline cross-team data distribution and integrate specialised external open-source tools such as AlphaFold
  • High-precision free-energy perturbation software, FEP+, works reliably only on rigid target proteins with reliable co-crystal structures, causing model accuracy and predictive confidence to drop significantly across flexible protein families or poorly defined targets
