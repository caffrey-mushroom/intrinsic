SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a regional reseller of a major CAD and building-design software provider, described a difficult business environment. The software company changed reseller terms, reducing renewal margins, while its new purchasing process has prompted customers to review usage and cut unused licenses. High interest rates have also delayed some construction projects, although the expert sees early signs that postponed work is moving ahead.

The discussion focused on reseller economics, competition in cloud services, rehabilitation work, and AI’s potential to automate technical design. The expert expects resellers to rely more on consulting, specialized add-ons, and services beyond core software sales.

Key Insights

The expert said the reseller’s year-to-date performance was behind budget and that matching the prior year’s revenue would be difficult. Competitors were also facing challenges, with some reducing staff. Rehabilitation of buildings and infrastructure was identified as a major growth opportunity. The expert believes CAD, scanning, and cloud tools will be used more widely to document existing assets and plan upgrades. AI-enabled design tools are advancing quickly. They could allow smaller engineering teams to produce more models and may eventually reduce demand for software seats, though engineers will still need to check outputs. AI does not eliminate the need for skilled resellers. Customers will still require help configuring software, integrating data, and adapting tools to specific disciplines and projects.

Resellers and specialist developers

Face lower renewal margins but can expand through consulting, integrations, and specialized applications. Local knowledge can help address regulations and customer-specific workflows.

AI-focused entrants

Emerging providers are combining AI with standard design software and add-ons. Their tools could speed up routine technical drawing and modeling.
