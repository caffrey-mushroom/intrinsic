Agenda

  • Outlook for Bread Financial (NYSE: BFH) and the key macroeconomic, technology and fraud-related drivers shaping performance over the next 12-24 months
  • Evolution of Bread's fraud strategy, including its shift to Alloy's orchestration platform and greater use of third-party fraud, identity and authentication tools
  • Trade-off between fraud prevention, approval rates and customer friction, including how Bread calibrates controls across different risk tiers
  • Use of AI and automation across fraud analytics and broader operations, including the potential impact on productivity, decision-making and loss prevention

Key Insights

  • Bread's credit trajectory is likely to remain primarily mix-driven. Expert attributed c85% of recent improvement to higher-quality borrowers, leaving underwriting and fraud optimisation as secondary contributors
  • Through 2027, the principal untapped Alloy opportunity appears to be analytical rather than technological, with c35% of potential benefit remaining through more granular segmentation of authentication and fraud risk
  • Synthetic identity risk should increase as co-brand penetration rises, given higher credit limits, while targeted eCBSV deployment – estimated at cUSD 750,000 annually – could provide definitive identity validation before accounts are booked
  • Approval-rate expansion represents the more material Alloy opportunity, with improved strategy development potentially increasing approvals by up to 8% among credit-approved applicants, vs 3-4% across total applications
  • Achieving 6% net loss rate depends on unemployment remaining contained and wage growth improving relative to inflation, while partner concentration, buy now, pay later competition and late-fee regulation remain material medium-term constraints
