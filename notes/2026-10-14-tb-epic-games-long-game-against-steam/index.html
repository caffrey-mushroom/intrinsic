Agenda

  • Epic Games' publisher adoption, content acquisition and release strategy
  • Free games, customer retention and purchasing behaviour
  • Store economics and path to sustainability
  • Mobile expansion and Epic's broader platform strategy

Key Insights

  • Epic Games Store's decision to restrict its 20% rewards programme to Fortnite could reduce annual third-party spending by USD 50m-75m, with spending potentially returning to USD 250m-300m over the next few years
  • With c8-10% PC storefront market share vs Steam's c75%, Epic may need to at least double its USD 400m annual third-party spending to sustainably cover operating costs
  • Epic's offer of 100% net revenue for six months is unlikely to justify exclusivity for most publishers, as Steam's significantly larger audience outweighs Epic's more favourable revenue-sharing terms
  • Epic's cross-play requirements can impose significant integration costs on publishers using legacy Steamworks infrastructure, while its USD 1m annual revenue exemption limits Epic's ability to monetise smaller titles
  • Epic's USD 20m annual free games budget implies cUSD 2.11 acquisition cost per user, but only 16-18% convert into paying customers, underscoring the challenge of monetising its large user base
