AI FUNDING

ORCL: Files automatic mixed securities shelf (6 part $10B Debt)

ORCL: Stephens upgrades to OW/$213 as Investor Day resolves cloud funding/FCF concerns

Stephens upgrades ORCL from Equal Weight and raises PT to $213 from $185, saying Investor Day materially de-risked OCI by capping incremental cloud infrastructure funding at ~$20B while pointing to lower capital intensity and much higher FCF. With the funding overhang reduced, Stephens says perceived OCI risk has come out of the stock and feels materially better about the setup.

OpenAI / AI Funding: Fundraising target cut to about $20B – FT

OpenAI has reduced its fundraising target to about $20B from $30B and its proposed valuation to $1.25T from $1.4T. The initial close could fall below $20B after investors reduced allocations, with a subsequent offering to wealthy individuals potentially filling the gap. WSJ said has only raised $11B out of the $20 so far.

CRWV / NBIS / Neoclouds: High-yield funding becomes prohibitive – WSJ

The WSJ reports that neocloud bond issuance has effectively stalled as borrowing costs surge. CoreWeave bonds issued at 9.75% now yield over 15%; Nebius dropped a high-yield offering and is using customer prepayments. The constraint is material for AI deployment, although Nebius says asset-backed financing remains available. Capital access, rather than demand alone, is increasingly separating operators.

AVGO / Anthropic: Blackstone expects junk rating after IPO – FT

Blackstone now expects Anthropic to carry a high speculative-grade rating after its IPO, versus its earlier investment-grade expectation. The revised assumption affects a $63B TPU financing package: over $42B of senior debt has Broadcom guarantees, while $18B of junior debt relies on Anthropic’s lease payments. Major agencies have not issued an official rating.

META: S&P cuts credit outlook to negative – S&P

S&P affirmed Meta’s AA− rating but cut its outlook to negative. It forecasts capex of $140B in 2026, over $225B in 2027 and $252B in 2028, with free operating cash-flow deficits of $22B, $71B and $39B, respectively. Data-center leases could push leverage above 1x in 2027; the forecast excludes Meta’s planned $60B at-the-market equity program.

GOOGL / AI Debt: Euro bond sale tests investor appetite – Bloomberg

Alphabet opened a seven-part euro bond offering with maturities from two to 19 years, following its May issuance. Initial pricing implied an average concession of about 45bp to outstanding bonds, subject to tightening. Its existing euro spreads have widened by as much as 60bp since May as investors reassess AI spending and borrowing

CRWD: DB upgrades to Buy/$300 as AI expands cyber TAM and strengthens Falcon’s consolidation edge

DB upgrades from Hold and raises PT to $300 from $240, arguing AI expands cyber TAM from >$200B today to ~$260B/$565B by CY29/CY34 while reinforcing CRWD’s endpoint leadership, trusted-consolidator role and proximity to agent/token activity. Checks are DB’s strongest yet, with endpoint momentum accelerating for four straight quarters and Falcon Flex/single-agent architecture driving share gains; Guardian, SafeMind and Nvidia/CoreWeave partnerships add AI upside. DB sees CRWD comfortably ahead of its $10B FY30 ARR target and sustaining >20% compounded growth for years.

COIN: MS downgrades to Underweight/$120 as weaker trading + crypto uncertainty undermine the earnings recovery

MS cuts COIN from Equal-weight and PT to $120 from $258, lowering 2027/28 revenue 28%/18% to $6.4B/$6.6B and EBITDA 47%/25% to $1.9B/$2.4B; retail trading revenue falls 40%/21% to $2.6B/$2.8B, still ~40% of revenue and the key earnings swing factor. The Binance hack adds uncertainty around crypto confidence/regulation/adoption, while lower normalized earnings and a reduced 12x target multiple vs. 20x drive the sharply lower PT.

SPCX: DB sees compute ramp reaching ~2.7GW by YE26, with ~$77B annualized neocloud revenue already contracted

DB estimates SPCX has ~1.8–1.9GW online today, rising to ~2.3GW by November-end and ~2.7GW exiting December as another ~220K GB300s arrive in November and ~200K in December, broadly supporting management’s 2.5–3.0GW target. Five signed neocloud customers represent roughly 1.6GW and ~$77B of annualized revenue, implying ~$45–50B/GW pricing, with additional large deals/add-ons likely given industry capacity constraints.

SPCX / SATS / Telecom: SpaceX explores Boost Mobile deal – Washington Post / Reuters

After major U.S. carriers rebuffed its network-access requests, SpaceX is discussing buying EchoStar’s Boost Mobile or using its network arrangements, the Washington Post reports. A deal could accelerate Starlink Mobile’s expansion, but AT&T and T-Mobile may need to approve transfer of Boost’s existing access contracts. Talks remain preliminary; no deal has been announced.

TSM / SPCX / Terafab: Musk reportedly softens stance on TSMC role – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports that Musk has become more pragmatic about TSMC’s role in Terafab, after initially insisting on building and operating the fab himself while offering TSMC only leased space. Supply-chain sources say the two sides plan to revisit cooperation. The report describes potential renegotiation, not a finalized partnership.

RIVN: UBS upgrades to Buy/$17 as R2 ramp + margin inflection create better risk/reward

RDDT: Reddit downgraded to Hold from Buy at Loop Capital

GOOGL: Wells says AI Overviews, not demand, are driving Search deceleration; PT to $365

Wells keeps OW but cuts PT to $365 from $371, arguing Search softness reflects AI Overview penetration, now ~82% of queries, not weaker engagement or agent displacement; Search/Gemini sessions still grew 36% y/y. Paid clicks/session is highly inversely correlated with AI Overview adoption, so Wells cuts Search growth to +9% in 4Q26 and +4% in 1Q27 before +6% in 2Q27, with FY27/28 EPS to $14.60/$17.79.

GOOGL: BNP says Search engagement is strengthening despite 3Q monetization miss, with AI Mode potentially larger than ChatGPT

BNP keeps OP/$410 after Search revenue missed Street by ~2% in 3Q, arguing the issue is monetization rather than user loss: Google app DAUs have risen from ~1.8B to >2.3B since early 2025, time spent nearly doubled to ~9.2 minutes/day, and Google still has ~3x the DAUs and ~15x the web traffic of its closest AI peers. BNP estimates ~41% of Search users are using AI Mode, ~33% more than ChatGPT’s ~710M users, excluding Gemini; multi-turn ads in AI Mode and more ads in AI Overviews could begin helping as soon as 4Q. Near-term Search growth may stay lumpy as AI Mode cannibalizes some traditional queries.

AMZN: Phillip upgrades to Buy/$330 as strong demand visibility supports aggressive AI capex

Phillip raises AMZN to Buy from Accumulate and PT to $330 from $320 after Q3, arguing strong demand visibility justifies elevated capital spending. It sees Amazon well positioned in AI through a model-agnostic strategy, custom silicon and full-stack infrastructure combined with partnerships across leading LLM providers.

AI Cloud: Jefferies sees a healthy AI “pit stop,” not a bubble, as demand/backlog stay strong but capex/FCF pressure drives rotation

Jefferies says AI demand remains intact: hyperscaler + ORCL backlog reached ~$2.5T, +106% y/y, while aggregate cloud growth accelerated to 57% from 48% last quarter. The debate has shifted from demand to ROI/financing as CY27 capex estimates rise toward ~$1.5T including SPCX/CRWV, with combined hyperscaler + META + ORCL FCF not expected to turn positive again until CY29; that has driven rotation into high-FCF software names. Jefferies’ bottom line is a capacity/capex pause rather than an end to the AI cycle

CRDO: Mizuho management meeting highlights Positron ASIC as a meaningful new memory-wall opportunity

Following meetings with Positron management, Mizuho highlights its first custom AI ASIC, Asimov, targeting the memory wall with a 3nm design, Titan server platform and CRDO’s 112G OmniConnect; first ~$55M OCI deployment starts production in 4Q26 and Positron says it has ~$750M of interest from Tier-2 hyperscalers/neoclouds. Asimov uses lower-cost LPDDR5X instead of HBM and disaggregated memory to raise available DRAM, improve XPU utilization and reduce HBM/stacking constraints, with CRDO positioned to supply the memory interconnect and potentially earn revenue in FY28. Positron plans roughly one new ASIC per year, making this a positive longer-term read-through for CRDO.

OTHER NEWS

AAPL / BABA: Qwen integration sets China AI rollout – EBN

Alibaba says its Qwen models will power Apple Intelligence features in China, including Siri, writing tools and visual intelligence. Developer testing begins November 4, with general availability planned alongside iOS 27.3 in December. Users can choose whether to enable Qwen, and their questions and answers will not be used to train Alibaba’s models.

Data Centers / Policy: Election raises risk of construction pauses – The Information

Candidates backing some form of data-center moratorium could win as many as 15 governorships, The Information estimates. At least one major-party nominee supports a pause in 21 of 36 races, including states hosting large AI campuses. Proposals differ in scope and still require implementation, but could complicate project approvals and deployment schedules.

NVDA / Components: Murata doubles investment as capacitors tighten – Nikkei Asia / Commercial Times

Murata plans ¥1T, about $6.3B, of MLCC investment over five years, roughly doubling annual spending as factory utilization stays near 95%. It targets more than doubling MLCC sales by March 2031. Separately, Commercial Times reports lead times above 52 weeks for 16V/47µF capacitors used in AI systems, highlighting supply constraints despite expansion plans.

DeepSeek / AI Agents: Open-source coding agent targets Claude Code – Bloomberg

DeepSeek released a prototype called Super-Agent that can monitor developer group chats, identify coding problems and suggest fixes without being explicitly prompted. The open-source release extends competition with Anthropic’s Claude Code into more autonomous workflows. It remains a prototype; the announcement does not establish performance parity or commercial adoption.

Data Center Power / PJM: March auction would fund new generation – Bloomberg

PJM proposed a March auction for generating capacity needed to meet data-center growth, subject to FERC approval. Contracts could run 15 years at up to $555/MW-day. Procuring its previously projected 6.8GW shortfall at that ceiling would imply about $20.7B of payments; revised rules allocate costs according to each utility zone’s demand growth.

Semis / Silicon Wafers: Incremental 2027 supply faces higher prices – Commercial Times

Commercial Times reports that Shin-Etsu’s existing long-term contracts remain unchanged through 2027, while demand above contracted volumes could face higher spot pricing from January. Analysts expect double-digit increases for some 12-inch wafer sales outside existing agreements. Capacity growth of less than 10% next year limits the supply response; this is not a blanket contract-price increase.

Foundry / Samsung: Customers told 8-inch production ends in 2030 – The Elec

Samsung has notified customers that it plans to end all 8-inch foundry production in 2030, The Elec reports, giving its shift toward advanced 12-inch manufacturing a firm deadline. Affected products include power-management, display-driver, microcontroller and image-sensor chips. Migration could be difficult because alternative domestic foundry capacity is already heavily utilized.

AI Infrastructure / Financing: Brookfield arranges $8B Korean GPU loan – Bloomberg

Brookfield selected KB Kookmin to lead a 10.7T won, roughly $8B, syndicated loan supporting AI infrastructure leased long term to Naver Cloud. The financing forms part of a 12.6T won project and could carry interest in the high-5% range. Discussions remain ongoing, with terms subject to change.

OpenAI / Memory: Altman meets Samsung and SK executives – DigiTimes

Sam Altman met Samsung and SK leadership in South Korea on October 31, accompanied at SK by OpenAI hardware chief Richard Ho. The visits came as memory suppliers allocated future capacity to large AI customers. Neither side announced a new supply contract, pricing agreement or investment commitment from the meetings.
