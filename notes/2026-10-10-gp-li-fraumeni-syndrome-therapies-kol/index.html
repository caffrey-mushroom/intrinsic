SUMMARY

Overview

Li-Fraumeni syndrome is an autosomal dominant cancer-predisposition disorder caused by germline TP53 alterations. It creates exceptionally high lifetime cancer risk, including early breast cancer, sarcomas, central nervous system tumors, and adrenocortical cancer. Diagnosis relies on personal and family cancer patterns, broad genetic testing, and confirmation that blood-based findings are not age- or treatment-related clonal hematopoiesis.

The expert favors intensive standardized surveillance for all confirmed carriers because current risk models are not sufficiently reliable for reducing screening. Treatment generally seeks to limit radiation and genotoxic chemotherapy due to secondary malignancy risks.

Key Insights

Variant biology materially affects disease onset. Dominant-negative missense variants are associated with earlier and more pediatric cancers, while loss-of-function variants generally present later. Blood-detected TP53 variants should be confirmed using cultured skin fibroblasts because 20%–40% may reflect clonal hematopoiesis rather than inherited disease. Annual whole-body and brain MRI remain central to surveillance. Liquid biopsy may detect disease earlier but should complement, not replace, imaging. The largest unmet need is chemoprevention. Metformin is the leading practical candidate because of its established safety, low cost, and biological rationale. Mutant-p53 reactivators may require combination therapy, while ATR inhibition appears more promising than earlier WEE1 inhibitors because of lower marrow toxicity.
