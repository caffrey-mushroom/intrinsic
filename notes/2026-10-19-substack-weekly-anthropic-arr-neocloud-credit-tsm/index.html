The AI trade has become much more sensitive to near-term numbers. As we argued in our latest column, investors are watching ARR, personal-agent DAU and retention more closely, while higher yields have become a headwind. A compelling long-term story now has to contend with monthly growth data, and the recent plateau in personal-agent usage has made that shift more uncomfortable.

Credit markets added another source of pressure this week. CoreWeave’s bond yields climbed to around 15%, and its five-year CDS reached roughly 1,195bp on Thursday, as investors questioned the funding behind the AI buildout; Nebius and IREN also sold off. For leveraged neoclouds, slower revenue growth becomes more damaging when fresh capital is expensive or harder to obtain. That can delay data-center expansion and chip purchases, spreading the concern across the AI supply chain. We see the financing squeeze as an important amplifier of the selloff: the market is now testing both the revenue trajectory and the balance sheets needed to fund it.

We flagged a related demand-side risk in our September 2 Weekly Call with Premium Subscribers. Newly funded AI labs, or Neolabs, had become important marginal buyers of short-term GPU capacity, paying high rental rates despite uncertain business models. We noted that their funding could support roughly six to twelve months of spending, after which continued purchases would depend on raising more capital. We are watching this closely: if risk appetite weakens and the next funding round becomes harder, these labs could cut compute purchases, putting pressure on neoclouds’ short-term rental rates. Financing conditions can therefore affect both neoclouds’ cost of capital and their customers’ ability to pay.

Anthropic’s $16.8b of 3Q26 revenue puts September exit ARR at about $70b in our model, below the $80b expectation we had been tracking. Giving up most distillation revenue explains part of the shortfall, but the harder question is the path to $100b by year-end, a market expectation that we do not believe the company has explicitly guided to. Our model puts September trailing-28-day ARR at about $69b; reaching $100b would require roughly $9b of monthly net new ARR, against about $5b excluding distillation over the past quarter. Opus 5.5 could help growth accelerate, but gains at Anthropic may then renew concerns about OpenAI’s growth as the competitive debate intensifies.

We think year-end revenue delivery will keep driving the AI trade, even after the pullback. Expectations have become harder to judge, and actually reaching $100b could still surprise the market. We are looking for the next use cases that can broaden revenue growth, from drug discovery and diagnosis to supply-chain automation and cyber defense. Getting closer to AGI may make the investment experience harder along the way: more compute goes into training before the return is easy to demonstrate. Finding the next large application before that turning point remains central to our research.
