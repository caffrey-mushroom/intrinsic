原标题：Preview|NOW 3Q26: Solid Checks and a Healthy 2027 Outlook, AICT Adoption Starts to Take Shape

#software #agents

Substack · 2026-10-21

Summary

We revisited three ServiceNow channel experts this quarter and spoke with customers in insurance and healthcare. Feedback remains solid, with two channels modestly ahead of their 3Q plans and the third tracking within expectations. Renewals, AI tier upgrades and module expansion support a healthy 4Q and 2027 outlook, extending our view from last quarter. On AI, AICT is moving beyond discussions into discovery, governance workflows and live-environment pilots. The strategic opportunity is becoming more tangible and could become a more meaningful revenue driver in 2027, despite its limited near-term contribution. NOW is not necessarily the outright governance winner, but success with AICT could make its role in the AI era more critical. Now Assist sales and deployment are improving; incremental consumption and customer ROI remain more uneven. We expect a 150-200bps beat on 3Q cRPO growth is reasonable, while 4Q cRPO growth guidance is likely to be in line with buyside expectations at around 20%.

3Q26 Checks Modestly Ahead Overall with a Healthy 4Q and 2027 Outlook

Last quarter, we expected new pricing, early renewals and Prime upgrades to support 2H growth. This quarter’s follow-up largely supports that view. Two channels beat their earlier growth expectations, while the North American channel remained steady. The drivers are broadening from pricing and renewal timing to module expansion and implementation of purchased AI capabilities. Our checks continue to support a solid 3Q with room for a modest beat, followed by healthy demand into 4Q and 2027.

North America Channel Expert A

Subscribed

  • 3Q26 growth: preliminary growth of 21.8–22% yoy, slightly above 2Q’s 21.6–21.8%. The expert described the result as within its own plan, rather than a beat.
  • Demand mix: ITOM, ITAM, security and risk remain healthy. AI tier upgrades also create work around CMDB quality, asset data and implementation of functionality customers have already purchased. SecOps demand is still led by vulnerability management, incident response and compliance, with AI governance adding another use case.
  • 4Q26 outlook: ~22.2% growth, a modest improvement from 3Q. Pricing and tier upgrades support both software and implementation, but the expert remains cautious on the pace of near-term acceleration.

Global Channel Expert B

  • 3Q26 beat: growth close to 28% or higher, versus the prior 26–27% expectation and ~25% in 2Q. Migration to new tiers, demand for agentic functionality and additional modules drove the improvement.
  • Renewal upgrades: ~65–70% of customers renewing in 3Q moved to the new tiers. Advanced remains the most common choice, with Prime concentrated in larger accounts. Negotiated uplifts were ~20–25% for Pro Plus to Advanced and ~15–20% for Enterprise Plus to Prime.
  • 4Q and 2027: 4Q growth expectations rose from ~28% to ~30%, including professional services. Year-end renewals, module cross-selling and more complex implementations support the pipeline. The expert sees a 31–35% growth scenario for 2027; the 34–35% upper end depends on broader production adoption of AICT and agentic applications.

Europe Channel Expert C

  • 3Q26 beat: growth reached 17–18%, versus an expectation of ~15%, the same pace as 2Q. Multi-product purchases, longer contracts and AI upgrades contributed; some deals expected to run for one year were signed for two.
  • AI purchases coming earlier: some customers initially waiting for 2027 budgets upgraded after completing POCs. Closed migrations from legacy Pro to AI tiers produced ~20% contract uplifts. Discounts and concern about future price increases also helped close deals, so this is not all usage-driven growth.
  • 4Q and 2027: 4Q expectations increased to ~18%. One proposed November enterprise agreement would consolidate ~US$6m of existing customer spend across ten contracts into a three-year agreement; it is not yet closed, and the US$6m is not all incremental. The initial 2027 outlook is 15–18%, which the expert considers conservative, with a chance of exceeding 20% if enterprise agreements and AI upgrades progress.

Healthcare Customer E

  • Spend and growth: ~US$6–8m of annual direct NOW spend, including licensing and NOW-provided consulting and implementation, but excluding third-party delivery and internal staff. Spend rose 15–20% cumulatively over 2024–2026, equivalent to ~7–10% annualized. The expert initially guided to 12–15% growth in 2027 and later widened this to 12–18%, pointing to acceleration. This is not a pure subscription-spend metric.
  • Expansion drivers: in an illustrative 15% growth bridge, ~10ppts came from new modules, 3ppts from AI and 2ppts from renewal pricing and NOW build services. HR, Field Service Management and clinical device management are the main additions. Nuvolo is a third-party application built on NOW; its entire bill should not be treated as NOW revenue. Armis is under evaluation and is not in the base case.

AICT Adoption Is Becoming More Tangible but Revenue Contribution Is Still Early

Where AICT Sits in the Architecture

AI Control Tower is a common management layer for an enterprise’s AI assets and governance processes. It helps identify which agents, models and AI applications exist, who owns them, what business processes they touch and which risks and approvals apply. In the company’s Analyst Day architecture, AICT spans the model, build and execution layers. AI Agent Orchestrator sits within execution and plans and coordinates tasks. AICT provides the governance context around those tasks; it is not itself the engine that plans every action.

Source: ServiceNow Financial Analyst Day 2026, p.17. Company target architecture; customer coverage depends on deployment and integration.

Discovery is the starting point: authorized connectors, APIs and registration processes establish an inventory and link assets to owners, data access and business use cases. Risk assessment, deployment approval, monitoring and enforcement follow. Registering an external agent is therefore a different level of adoption from reviewing or blocking each action it takes.

Source: FUNDA interpretation of the company architecture and product documentation. Illustrative responsibilities, not a mandatory call path for every deployment.

Agent Fabric connects agents and supports collaboration. Action Fabric exposes NOW business actions to external agents and connects NOW agents to external tools. Orchestrators can plan the work, while workflows, business rules and approvals govern how it is carried out. AICT links the assets, owners, risk decisions and evidence across this architecture. Enforcement still depends on the gateways, identity providers and execution systems connected to it.

For example, an external assistant could request equipment and system access for a new employee. An orchestrator breaks the request into tasks; NOW workflows route the approvals and invoke downstream systems. AICT can connect the requesting agent to its owner, risk status and operating evidence. The actual granting or revoking of access still occurs in the relevant identity or business system. This illustrates the division of responsibilities rather than a fully deployed workflow at every customer.

Why the Position Matters

The logic behind the NOW opportunity is that cheaper coding and more capable models increase the importance of enterprise context, permissions, execution and auditability. An agent still needs to know what an action will affect, who has approved it and how a failure can be traced and remediated. NOW’s installed base of CMDB data, approvals and cross-department workflows gives it a route to remain involved even when employees start from an external AI assistant.

Source: FUNDA analysis. An analytical framework, not evidence of completed customer deployments.

This creates several potential revenue routes. AICT can be purchased directly; governance work can lead to broader GRC, SecOps or Workflow Data Fabric adoption; external agents using NOW’s workflows may generate paid consumption. The thesis does not require Now Assist to become every employee’s front end. It does require NOW to remain part of the actual approval and execution process, rather than simply receiving logs after the work is done.

Source: ServiceNow Financial Analyst Day 2026, p.28. Company commercial strategy, not evidence that all external-agent calls already produce incremental revenue.

Our Checks Show Discovery and Governance Moving into Live Use

  • North America Channel A: the industrial customer discussed last quarter, together with related entities, has completed 8–12 weeks of paid discovery and is negotiating deployment and ongoing support. The channel has more than 12 AICT pilots, POCs or POVs; over one-third of its customers are discussing the product. The 40–45+ year-end pilot count is a target, not an actual. One industrial customer thought it had fewer than 500 agents; discovery found ~6,000 across six platforms. The case supports demand for an inventory, while the commercial rollout is still being worked through.
  • Global Channel B: healthcare and pharmaceutical customers are using AICT in production for inventory, shadow AI discovery and governance workflows across multiple cloud and AI platforms. Finding previously unmanaged agents has led to formal request catalogs, risk assessments and deployment approvals. The most mature value is in discovery, governance and measurement; observability and security controls need deeper integration.
  • Insurance Customer D: AICT is in production for discovery, ownership, risk classification, pre-populating assessment materials and consolidating reports and logs. The customer runs four NOW agents across 59 use cases, with two more agents in POC. It sees less manual risk-assessment work, but third-party agents currently sit mainly in the inventory and risk taxonomy. They are not yet connected to its local NOW instance for runtime governance; that integration is more likely in 2028.
  • Healthcare Customer E: AICT is being piloted in a live operating environment, monitoring ~30 agents: more than 20 within NOW and ~10 that connect with Oracle, Nuvolo or Microsoft Copilot. Connections to external systems do not establish that all ten are externally built agents or that every external action is controlled in real time. Another 75–100 enterprise agents remain outside this coverage, with departmental agents potentially taking the total into the hundreds.
  • A concrete healthcare use case: a NOW-built agent checks network-device configurations across hospital sites. AICT links the use case, owner, risk review, access rights and operating evidence, helping identify behavior outside the authorized scope. This is evidence of access governance and deviation monitoring. The interview did not establish an actual incident in which AICT blocked an external agent or revoked access in real time. AI Gateway is still being piloted separately.

Direct Spending Is Emerging but Expansion Remains Conditional

AICT is still too early to isolate as a material driver of NOW’s company-level results, but it is no longer accurate to describe it as having no direct revenue. Healthcare customer E has committed more than US$400k annually to the current live-environment pilot. The expert discussed licensing and setup together, so this is not a clean subscription ACV figure. Channel A’s industrial customer had purchased ~US$220k before completing discovery this quarter, highlighting the gap between purchase, implementation and realized value.

Healthcare customer E sees a more realistic 2027 AICT spending scenario of ~US$0.8–1.2m, also mentioning US$1.3m at the upper end, versus US$1.25–1.85m under full adoption. These are expansion scenarios rather than signed orders. ROI, functionality, adoption, available funding and negotiated pricing will determine the outcome. The scenarios do not fully reconcile with the overall growth bridge, so we do not use them to calculate a firm AI contribution to next year’s NOW spend.

Indirect monetization remains important. Channel A estimates a potential 2.5–4% increase in NOW spend from related modules at the industrial customer; a 7–12% uplift for deeply engaged Prime customers remains unproven in its own base. Channel B sees ~15–20% spending uplifts at production customers, including tier and module upgrades. These are broader contract effects, not standalone AICT pricing.

NOW Has a Credible Role but Is Not Necessarily the Governance Winner, Yet

NOW’s advantage is its ability to connect AI ownership and risk decisions to existing business processes. That is particularly relevant for enterprises already using its CMDB, approvals and workflows. Governance, however, spans inventory, identity, data protection, monitoring and action-level controls. Customers can use different vendors for each layer, including Microsoft, cloud platforms and specialist security providers.

The insurance customer is still testing a third-party orchestration approach on AWS alongside AICT. A central role for NOW in workflow governance can coexist with other platforms retaining identity, security and execution controls.

Healthcare customer E prefers to consolidate the agent inventory, ownership, risk decisions and evidence in existing NOW workflows, but retains native Microsoft and Databricks management capabilities. It did not run a full competitive evaluation against another cross-platform governance provider. Its criticism of Microsoft reflects its own experience, rather than proof that Microsoft lacks the capabilities. Okta and SailPoint continue to handle identity, security vendors remain involved in detection, and Datadog provides observability; AICT connects the governance process around them.

Pricing also constrains consolidation. The customer has not committed to putting the remaining 75–100 enterprise agents into AICT. Coverage of lower-risk agents depends on negotiating a cheaper way to govern them; otherwise, it may keep multiple systems. External interfaces and access to platform data impose further limits. A preference for a common governance view is not yet a commitment to move the entire budget to NOW.

Overall, we believe AICT is progressing from narrative and discovery into live use and identifiable customer spending. This makes its strategic position more credible, even though the near-term contribution to NOW’s results is still difficult to quantify. The stronger thesis requires more than an inventory: customers need to rely on NOW for approvals, evidence and remediation, and be willing to pay for broader coverage. Cross-platform integration, control depth and pricing remain the main tests. We see a growing role for NOW, but not enough evidence to call it the outright governance winner. If AICT successfully expands to more customers and covers agents beyond NOW’s platform, it would help solidify NOW’s value proposition in the AI era.

Now Assist Sales and Deployment Improve while Consumption Monetization Remains Uneven

Relative to last quarter’s lukewarm AI feedback, we are seeing more customers moving beyond POCs, buying AI tiers and expanding deployment. Global channel B had already seen some Pro Plus customers buying extra Assist packs last quarter. This quarter, the European channel is also seeing broader rollouts. The evidence is stronger for sales and deployment than for recurring consumption above included allowances. We retain a gradually improving sales and usage view, while looking for sustained activity, actual allowance consumption and repeat purchases to establish a broader monetization inflection.

Europe Channel Expert C

  • Sales: several of the latest five to six contracts include Now Assist and broader enterprise rollout. The channel is tracking use at five customers. AI tier migrations have produced ~20% contract uplifts, showing that interest is turning into orders.
  • Usage: the expert sees ~10–15% more ticket creation, handling and resolution activity, with faster handling and response times. Use cases include standard replies, service-request triage and standardized build requests. This is a workload measure, not a reported token-consumption growth rate.
  • Top-ups: broader use should exhaust included credits sooner, but the expert did not provide realized incremental spend after allowances ran out. Sales and rollout are improving; a recurring consumption uplift has not yet been quantified.

Insurance Customer D

  • Production use and ROI: a claims assistant covers ~150 users. A six-month POC indicated ~15 hours saved per user per month through summarization, information extraction and identifying missing documents. Those time savings have not yet translated into cash savings in claims; underwriting headcount reductions remain conditional targets.
  • Consumption outlook: POCs, development and rollout are all driving use, with trial credits not covering every cost. The customer expects NOW AI consumption to double over the next two years and agents to increase from four to 15–20. These are plans, not realized growth, and lower unit costs should prevent spending from rising in direct proportion to calls.
  • Overall NOW spend: direct annual payments to NOW are ~CAD1.8–2.5m, up only ~2% this year, with 8% and 12% growth expected in 2027 and 2028. The broader ~25% increase in capability-building costs includes personnel, consulting and implementation, and should not be treated as software revenue growth.

Healthcare Customer E

  • Deployment: Now Assist has expanded from a 15–20-person test around a year ago to more than 70 production-licensed users, mainly in ticket and case handling. Licensed users are not the same as monthly active users; no comparable task-volume or per-user consumption data were provided.
  • ROI and next purchases: the expert sees summarization as relatively generic and Now Assist’s ROI as less clear than AICT’s. The next budget opportunity is task automation, deeper ticket analysis, recurring-incident handling and problem-management content. Native functionality is attractive because it reduces integration work, but the interview did not confirm credit exhaustion or paid Assist-pack top-ups.
  • Build versus buy: the expert estimates a current ~60% vendor / 40% internal-build mix for AI solutions, with a preference to increase internal development using Microsoft Foundry and Databricks. Native NOW functionality is preferred inside established NOW workflows, while custom agents can connect for specific needs. Knowledge management, analytics, triage and cross-system orchestration remain areas for internal development. This supports the installed workflow base without implying that all AI spending will flow to NOW.
