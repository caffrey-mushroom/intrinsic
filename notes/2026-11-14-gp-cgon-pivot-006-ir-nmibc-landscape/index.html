SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion focused on treatment choices for intermediate-risk non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC), including established procedures and emerging intravesical therapies. The expert described current use of TURBT followed by surveillance, early experience with ZUSDURI, and expectations for cretostimogene’s PIVOT-006 study. Other topics included FGFR-targeted treatments and practical considerations that affect real-world outcomes.

Key Insights

The expert estimates that intermediate-risk patients make up a substantial share of their bladder cancer practice. Despite new options, they do not expect treatment patterns to change substantially without evidence of durable benefit. For low-volume, first-time disease, TURBT and surveillance remain common. Small, consistently low-grade recurrences may also be managed with in-office fulguration, avoiding a course of intravesical therapy. The expert would consider cretostimogene more compelling if it meaningfully reduces recurrence and shows durable results. They identified roughly a 30% recurrence reduction as a personal, approximate threshold for choosing treatment over observation. Three-month response measures alone may not be enough; 12- or 24-month durability would influence adoption. Accurate pathology and careful assessment are essential. Mixed-grade, more extensive, or anatomically higher-risk disease may make a patient unsuitable for approaches studied in intermediate-risk populations.

Competitive Landscape

Option

Positioning and expert perspective

TURBT, surveillance, and fulguration

Current mainstay for many patients. Fulguration is practical for small, low-grade recurrences and can avoid a multi-week treatment course.

ZUSDURI

Being used for higher-volume disease and patients seeking to avoid anesthesia or resection; use is expanding as insurance access improves. Early experience is limited, though treatment has been well tolerated.

Cretostimogene (PIVOT-006)

Potential adjuvant option. The expert is interested in recurrence outcomes, durability, and results among patients with small high-grade tumors. Observation is viewed as an appropriate control.

TAR-210 / localized FGFR approach

The expert is interested in localized delivery and would favor it over systemic therapy if supported by data, partly due to concerns about systemic side effects.

Oral FGFR inhibitors

Some patients have considered these, but reported systemic effects, including skin-related changes, have been a concern.

INLEXZO device

The expert has used it and considers the device workable, while noting catheter rigidity can complicate placement in some patients, including those with prior prostate surgery.
