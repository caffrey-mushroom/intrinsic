The selloff

Well, that was a rough week.

The S&P 500 finished down 2.7%, the Nasdaq fell 3.4%, and the Dow was down 0.9%. Small caps also struggled, with the Russell 2000 down 2.1%.

Friday’s bounce helped, but it didn’t undo the damage from earlier in the week. And depending on what you owned, it probably felt a lot worse than those index numbers suggest.

Semis in particular have taken a beating. Looking at this chart, I’m having a hard time getting too excited about one green day.

There have been plenty of bounces along the way down. For now, I want to see support hold and some higher lows start to form before getting too aggressive.

I’ll get into the levels I’m watching on the indexes later and how they’re influencing my plan for this week.

Anthropic

Since I wrote about Anthropic’s filing, we got another look at how quickly the business is growing.

The Wall Street Journal reported that its annualized revenue run rate topped $80 billion at the end of October, citing a person familiar with the business.

Anthropic generated $17 billion in revenue in Q3, but the cost of serving all that demand increased. For every dollar it spent on compute and infrastructure, it generated $1.72 in revenue. That was down from $2.08 in Q2.

A big part of that was the roughly $5 billion it spent on short-term compute commitments. That capacity is more expensive than what it can secure through multiyear agreements.

So demand is growing fast enough that Anthropic is paying up for capacity it can use now. As more contracted infrastructure comes online, it has an opportunity to move workloads onto cheaper capacity. That’s good for its margins and gives infrastructure providers a clearer picture of the demand they’re building to serve.

Adjusted operating profit rose to $2.5 billion in Q3, but the margin fell from 19% to 15%. On a GAAP basis, it reported a $567 million operating loss, including $1.07 billion in stock compensation and $1.96 billion in noncash donation expense.

We’ve already talked about the growth in its customer base and the drop in concentration. One major thing keeping an eye on now is how quickly Anthropic can bring the cost of serving those customers down as demand keeps growing.

The rotation

I’ve been talking for weeks about broadening my exposure to other industries.

My concern now is that a lot of the strongest non-tech stocks have already had big runs.

If money rotates back into technology, I could end up prioritizing new additions away from a trade as it gets cheaper and buying another after a big move.

Last week showed how uneven the market has become. Technology fell 8.23%, while communication services, real estate, consumer discretionary, financials and consumer staples finished higher.

That’s why I’ve spent so much time looking for stocks that are still building bases or starting early reversals.

I want businesses I’m comfortable owning, with valuations that make sense and charts that give me a reasonable place to start.

I’m not looking to abandon AI obviously. I still have so much exposure. I want to keep the companies I think are best positioned while adding businesses with different sources of demand.

Below, I’ll get into the companies I’m considering and how I’m approaching each one, from power and cooling to non-tech, Physical AI, networking and longer trading hours.

Vertiv (VRT)

After working through the data-center article, I was looking for companies that help satisfy the demands being put forward.

Developers need to use less water, manage increasingly dense power loads and get facilities running on time.

Vertiv has products and services aimed at those specific problems. Let’s look at them.

  1. Power inside the facility. Vertiv supplies power-distribution and backup-power equipment. Higher-density racks make reliable delivery of electricity inside the building more demanding. That creates equipment demand, while the utility connection and available generation remain separate constraints.2. Cooling and water. Liquid cooling addresses the heat created by denser computing equipment. Vertiv also says its PurgeRite NearZero commissioning process can reduce flush-water use by up to 90%. The saving applies to that commissioning step, rather than total ongoing water consumption.3. Getting capacity online. Vertiv’s OneCore prefabricated offering is designed to accelerate deployment, with the company claiming timelines up to 50% faster. Prefabrication can reduce work at the site. Permitting and grid approvals still determine when the facility can operate.4. Keeping it running. Installation, commissioning and maintenance create work after the original equipment sale. Q3 service sales were $675.7 million, up 46.4%. Using disclosed sales and costs, I calculate service gross margin at about 44.1%, compared with roughly 37% for products.

Q3 organic orders grew more than 100%, organic sales grew 55%, and adjusted operating margin reached 25.2%.

Asia-Pacific and EMEA represented roughly one-third of nine-month sales. Its 30 MW liquid-cooling deployment in Mumbai is one example of demand outside the U.S. So their opportunity extends across regions facing different construction and permitting conditions.

The risk is a slowdown in construction large enough to offset the rising equipment content per project. If project volume fell 20%, revenue per project would have to rise 25% to hold new-project sales flat, with everything else unchanged. The existing service business helps, but new-build demand remains important.

The business is the reason I want exposure. I have wanted to own VRT for a long time and just never pulled the trigger.

The chart and the pain in the AI trade is what holds me back right now.

At $152, VRT would trade at about 21.3x the midpoint of its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance and 15.6x the 2027 consensus EPS estimate of $9.74.

So I will be watching that ~$152 level to see what it does there.

O’Reilly (ORLY)

This is an interesting one.

With any brick and mortar investment I want to understand how it doesn’t get cannabalized by online competitors.

What I found is that repair demand gives the business a durable reason to keep growing.

Cars break, owners keep older vehicles on the road, and repair shops need the right part quickly. Its distribution network and local availability help it compete on more than price. A mechanic waiting on a part isn’t only deciding which seller saves a few dollars. They’re deciding when the repair gets finished.

Q3 sales were $5.12 billion, up 9%, with comparable-store sales up 7.2%. GAAP EPS reached $0.97, about 14% higher. The quarter also benefited from a $39 million refund, so the underlying earnings run rate needs to account for that benefit.

Management guided to full-year sales of $19.0–19.3 billion, comparable-store sales growth of 5.5–6.5% and GAAP EPS of $3.25–3.35.

Around $89, the stock is about 27x that midpoint. This is a quality business at a more reasonable price.

The historical multiple gives that some context. Its year-end trailing GAAP P/E was roughly 22–24x in 2021–2023 and around 29–30x in 2024–2025. The current valuation sits between those periods.

A return to the highest multiple isn’t required for a decent outcome, but buying it today does assume the business keeps executing.

When looking at the chart, it’s at quite a pivotal point and what happens over the next few days decides what I will do.

We’ve got a nice potential setup here. We’ve got this descending channel thats been forming since the end of last year. It tried to break above several time and hasn’t sustained it. We are currently in one of those tests.

The 30w is also right here at $89.2. And the RSI is encouraging.

So my plan is that if it can hold this channel + 30w, and close above with volume, I will take a starter. Then add to it on further confirmation.

If money continues to find places outside of tech, hopefully this ticker could get constructive.

Chewy (CHWY)

When screening for stocks I came upon CHWY an remember it going crazy back in 2020.

I really like the chart setup here and I think the fundamental business could support it.

Modest sales growth is starting to produce much stronger operating profit. Pet food and medication are repeat purchases, and Autoship helps make the ordering process habitual.

The opportunity is to earn more from that existing relationship through pharmacy, advertising and other services while making fulfillment more efficient.

Q2 sales grew 7.3%, or 5.7% excluding acquisitions, while fulfillment expense increased only about 1.5%. That reduced fulfillment costs as a share of sales by roughly 62 basis points, or around $21 million relative to keeping the prior-year ratio. Some of that reflects operating leverage and mix, so the improvement reflects more than warehouse automation alone.

First-half GAAP operating income rose about 50%, even after expensing stock compensation.

Q2 adjusted EPS was $0.36 versus $0.33 a year earlier.

On the call, management’s full-year outlook was $13.46–13.57 billion in sales and a 6.7–6.8% adjusted EBITDA margin.

At $21.50–22.20, Chewy would trade at about 11.5–11.9x the $1.87 consensus adjusted EPS estimate for the fiscal year ending January 2028.

For EV/Sales the stock recently traded around 0.74x, near the lower end of its roughly 0.71–2.01x range at the last five fiscal year-ends.

At the same time, fulfillment costs are growing more slowly than sales and GAAP operating income is improving.

Now lets look at the chart.

A few things are encouraging to me here.

We’ve seen a nice double bottom forming after months of a downtrend,

We see a closing above the 30w with nice volume.

We see RSI getting constructive with nice bullish divergence.

What I would like to see is that ~$21.65 area to hold for a starter.

Domino’s (DPZ)

Yeah the pizza place.

Domino’s interests me because the stock gives me exposure to a large franchise system at a much lower earnings multiple than it commanded a few years ago.

Franchisees fund and operate most of the stores.

Domino’s earns royalties tied to their sales and also supplies food and equipment through its supply-chain business.

That gives it two ways to benefit when the system expands.

More stores and stronger store sales increase the royalty base.

They also increase the volume flowing through the supply network.

Q3 operating-income growth was helped by both franchise royalties and higher supply-chain gross-profit dollars.

Q3 revenue was $1.186 billion, up 3.4%, operating income grew 8.3% and GAAP EPS reached $4.60, up 12.7%.

U.S. same-store sales were up only 0.3% and international same-store sales rose 0.2%.

Management also reduced its outlook, with U.S. comparable sales expected at the low end of low-single-digit growth and international comparable sales approximately flat.

The growth in earnings is encouraging, but franchisees need healthy store economics for the system to keep opening locations and investing.

The royalty structure gives the parent exposure to store sales without directly carrying all of each franchisee’s operating costs.

But franchisees still have to earn enough after labor, food and delivery expenses to justify opening the next store. Weak store economics eventually affect expansion.

My case is for an earnings-producing business at a reset valuation, with improving customer demand offering a route to a better multiple.

At $322–327, Domino’s would trade at about 15.6–15.9x the $20.63 consensus adjusted EPS estimate for 2027.

Weak same-store sales and debt leverage of around 4.2x are two things I’m keeping in mind as I look at the valuation.

Similar idea as the last few charts.

Looking for an entry if it can hold that $323 area.

Intuit (INTU)

This one I really went back and forth on. And I still consider it quite speculative and it has earnings this week.

It’s really the valuation and the chart that get me interested.

At $341.50, it trades around 14.8x management’s $23 midpoint for FY2027 adjusted EPS. This includes SBC for Intuit.

Management expects 9–10% revenue growth overall and 13–14% in business solutions.

QuickBooks retention was 83%, flat YoY.

Tax is where I have more concern. TurboTax lost price-sensitive DIY users, and retention fell from 77% to 76%. Some assisted-tax growth came from existing users moving into a more expensive product, which is different from adding new customers to the overall platform.

A major concern with Intuit (and many other SaaS companies) is that they will get cannabilized by advancements in AI.

But I’ve also been thinking about the other side of the AI argument.

I think that AI is creating more small businesses than ever in my lifetime at least. It’s never been easier to build a business.

If these tools make it easier for more people to build businesses, there could be more demand for bookkeeping and tax software.

That is a plausible source of growth for Intuit, but I can’t quantify it yet.

Plenty of new solo businesses may need only a basic tax product rather than a large QuickBooks subscription and payroll package.

I personally have been using TurboTax for years. And even though I am AI pilled and would consider myself on the more advanced side of being able to leverage it, I will continue to file through TurboTax. Even though I am prepping much of my income and everything with ChatGPT.

So I’m keeping INTU in the speculative software bucket.

Stabilizing customer growth and continued QuickBooks expansion could reduce the pressure on the multiple. A less threatening outlook and a better chart could be enough for investors to pay more for the existing earnings.

I also compared it with ServiceNow and Guidewire. Guidewire has a strong case for being harder to disrupt because it runs core insurance systems and often prices around written premiums rather than employee seats.

ServiceNow has stronger growth, although acquisitions contribute to that growth and its chart needs repair.

For this particular recovery setup, I’m still more interested in Intuit’s valuation and developing base. That comes with more uncertainty around the long-term tax business.

Early 2026 has been gnarly for the stock.

But we have put in a nice double bottom over the past few months.

We have closed above the 30W.

And we are now retesting the highs from August and there is also a gap to fill from that earnings in May.

I think I will take a risky starter if we hold $325 going into earnings.

Earnings will decide if I take a larger position or I cut entirely.

Physical AI

My goal here is to increase position size in businesses with limited dependence on AI infrastructure capex.

I like many of these stocks, but I want to try to keep it focused for this article.

CGNX and AMBA can still move with tech for a while. But these are two names at the top of my list.

Cognex (CGNX)

Cognex gives me a profitable business helping factories and logistics operators automate work they can measure.

Finding defects earlier, identifying products correctly and reducing manual inspection can save the customer money. More capable machine vision makes those systems useful across more tasks.

Q3 revenue reached $313 million, up 13%, while operating income rose 74% to $101 million.

Higher volume, pricing and product mix improved gross margin, while operating expenses fell 7%.

Free cash flow was $104 million, up 21%.

The business is converting the sales recovery into considerably more profit.

RealSense adds depth-sensing cameras and robotic perception if the planned acquisition closes.

Combining identification with a better understanding of where an object sits in three-dimensional space can expand the work a machine performs. That gives Cognex an opportunity to sell into more robotic applications alongside its existing inspection business.

Management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $1.68–1.72.

Integration costs and a slowdown in factory investment could affect that progress.

Electronics and semiconductor manufacturing also create indirect links to the broader AI cycle, but the customer applications extend across many industries.

Ambarella (AMBA)

The buyout rumors are getting hard to ignore on AMBA.

First we had reports that NXP was in talks to buy the company. Then Qualcomm and Nvidia’s names came up as potential bidders. One September report even mentioned an unnamed buyer considering $95–105 per share, mostly in cash.

Now, the latest report says Ambarella’s board considered multiple offers and decided to move forward with NXP in exclusive talks.

None of this has been confirmed, and we don’t know what NXP might be offering. But I liked AMBA before all of these rumors, and I like it even more now. It also feels like the possibility of a deal might be providing some support for the stock.

Aside from that, I think they’re well positioned for the years ahead. Their chips help cameras, cars and other devices understand what they’re seeing and process that information on the device itself. As more machines need to see and respond to the world around them, I think that’s going to become a much bigger opportunity.

We’ve got their newer edge-AI chips making their way into products, a standalone AI accelerator, and agreements that could help get their technology in front of more customers.

The bigger inflection might still be a couple of years away as those opportunities ramp.

If we look at the current financials, revenue was $108.1 million last quarter, up 13.2%, and edge-AI revenue hit a record. Management guided for $115–124 million this quarter. They’re still working toward consistent profitability.

Marvell (MRVL)

If Marvell actually buys Lumentum, this is going to be an exceptionally strong company.

And if they don’t, well, I think the stock could recover nicely as some of the concerns around the deal go away. There could be a breakup fee depending on why it falls apart, so that’s something we’d have to look at.

I kind of see it as a win-win though. But the price still has to be right. Maybe I’m getting greedy on this one. We’ll see.

But here’s how I see Marvell and Lumentum.

Marvell makes the chips that help move data around AI systems. Lumentum makes the lasers, optical transceivers and switches that help move that data over fiber.

Put them together and Marvell would have a lot more of the pieces that go into connecting these massive AI clusters. As the systems get bigger, getting those pieces to work together while using less power is going to become increasingly important.

Management also thinks it can take more than $1 billion in annual costs out of the combined business and says the deal should add to adjusted EPS in the first year after it closes.

The big concern is what they’re paying. This is an all-stock deal, and Lumentum shareholders would own roughly a third of the combined company. That’s a lot of new shares.

But even without Lumentum, I really like what Marvell has going on.

They have a five-year agreement with AWS covering custom AI chips and networking products. They also have an agreement with Google covering chips around its TPU systems, including inference, networking, storage and memory.

Last quarter, revenue was up 37% to $2.74 billion, and data-center revenue was up 46%. So there’s already a lot of growth here before we even start thinking about what Lumentum could add.

As for the chart, these are the two levels I’m interested in:

Coherent (COHR)

COHR is the other networking company I’m interested in here. And at this valuation, I think there’s a lot to like.

First, the customer commitments. We’ve got the roughly $20 billion, five-year PhotonLink agreement with an unnamed AI customer, expected to start ramping in 2028. Then there’s NVIDIA’s multibillion-dollar purchase commitment and separate $2 billion investment in the company.

That gives me more confidence in the demand they’re building for. We don’t have all the details on cancellation protections, though, and they still have to get the products out the door.

Last quarter, revenue was $2.37 billion, up about 50% YoY, and adjusted EPS grew roughly 80% to $2.09. Management expects more than 60% pro forma revenue growth in fiscal 2027.

At Friday’s $192.81 close, the stock traded at about 18.6x the $10.37 consensus adjusted EPS estimate for the fiscal year ending in June 2027.

My biggest concerns are still six-inch yields, supply and how much margin expansion they can actually get as they ramp.

Management continues to say yields on its 6-in device-production lines are already better than on its three-inch lines. That’s encouraging, although they haven’t given us actual yield percentages. They also doubled their internal InP capacity a quarter ahead of schedule and are targeting more than another doubling by the end of 2027.

And we just got another development on supply. A subsidiary of Guangzhi Technology disclosed a letter of intent for at least $601 million of six-inch InP substrates for Coherent from 2027 through 2029. I find that very encouraging. Hopefully they are high quality.

Margins are the other part I’m paying attention to. Adjusted gross margin was 40.6% last quarter, up just 37 basis points from the prior quarter. Management is targeting a quarter above 45% before the end of 2028, with more internally produced components expected to help get them there. I don’t love the speed of the improvement relative to other companies. But their rev growth helps offset that for me.

And I think that with Lumentum being acquired it could provide a nice opportunity for Coherent to capture more customer interest.

24/5 Trading

I went down a bit of a rabbit hole on 24/5 trading this weekend.

And the timing is pretty interesting. Nasdaq and NYSE Arca are targeting December 6 to begin trading U.S. stocks 23 hours a day, five days a week.

You can already trade stocks overnight today. IBKR offers overnight access to more than 10,000 eligible U.S. stocks and ETFs, and Robinhood has its own 24 Hour Market for eligible names.

What changes in December is that the exchanges would add their own overnight sessions. So this isn’t starting from zero, but it could make it easier for more brokers and investors to participate.

Think about all the people around the world who want to trade U.S. stocks during their own daytime.

Some of that activity would probably happen during regular market hours anyway, but I have to imagine easier access could bring in people who aren’t trading much today.

So I wanted to find companies that could benefit from that, while already growing enough to make them interesting on their own.

Interactive Brokers (IBKR)

IBKR was the first one that stood out to me.

They already have a huge international customer base. So if someone in another part of the world wants to trade U.S. stocks during their own daytime, IBKR is in a good position to serve them.

And we can already see the interest. Customers placed about 10 million overnight trades last quarter, up from 3.5 million a year earlier.

The rest of the business is growing too. Accounts were up 35% to 5.58 million, and margin loans increased 36%.

What I like here is that a new customer can do a lot more than place an overnight trade.

They can bring assets over, use margin and trade in other markets.

So if longer hours help IBKR bring more people onto the platform, there are multiple ways that relationship can grow.

I like the business. The chart is what I’m having trouble with right now. It’s had a rough move lower, so I’m going to watch how it acts around support before deciding where I want to enter.

Virtu (VIRT)

VIRT is the other one I found interesting, and it benefits in a different way.

Rather than bringing the customer onto a brokerage platform, Virtu helps make trades happen. It provides liquidity by quoting prices and standing ready to buy and sell.

So if more people start trading during overnight hours, Virtu gets more opportunities to provide those services.

They already have the technology and infrastructure in place, which makes me interested in how much additional activity they can handle without adding costs at the same pace.

The business is already putting up strong numbers. Last quarter, adjusted net trading income grew 50.7% to about $704 million, and adjusted EPS rose to $1.77 from $1.05.

There are risks with the overnight opportunity too. Trading can be thinner, hedging can be more difficult, and more competition could bring spreads down.

We also don’t know how much of the activity will be new versus people moving trades they would have placed during regular hours.

Like IBKR, I want to watch the chart and see where it finds support before deciding on an entry.
