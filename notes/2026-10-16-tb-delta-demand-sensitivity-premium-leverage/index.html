Jake Abel (JA) Founder and Principal at Sage North Advisory

Agenda

  • Demand resilience for Delta Air Lines (NYSE: DAL)
  • Continued segmentation against middle-market peers
  • Ancillary revenue addressable market and implementation
  • Premium unbundling impacts to customer base

Key Insights

  • High-net-worth and leisure travellers are showing stronger fare resilience than large corporations, which face pressure to cut discretionary travel. Premium cabin mix between these two segments has largely normalised to 50/50. Overall demand resiliency is strong and not masked by capacity cuts on underperforming routes, and the tailwinds should continue through 2027
  • Expert believes achieving mid-teen operating margins requires premium revenue to remain durable and continued cost discipline, noting labour headwinds and structural changes to selling costs. Keeping non-fuel costs at high-single-digit growth through 2026-27 is feasible, but it may be challenging to lower further
  • Delta's non-airfare partnership approach is culturally distinct, prioritising genuine win-win relationships over transactional contracts. Negotiation leverage should continue, with growth driven largely by card spend, which was up double digits through H1 2026
  • United's strategy of flying more US-origin routes internationally risks lopsided point-of-sale dependency, requiring heavy investment and price competition. Delta is electing for a real asset-based approach with local partners, creating brand relevance slowly but more durably
