Agenda

  • Cargo business strategy and competitive positioning of Japan Airlines (JAL; TYO: 9201) against other Japanese airlines
  • Fuel procurement and margin exposure
  • Operating margin management and capital discipline

Key Insights

  • Japanese carriers control 3-4% of global air cargo – capped near 5% – but dominate intra-Asia with 20-25% market share. Over next 2-3 years, expert anticipates volume-revenue decoupling. Capacity will grow c5% annually, but revenue will lag at c2% due to 2-3% yield erosion
  • JAL's Q1 FY26 volume surge of 11.5% is an optical illusion driven by aircraft, crew, maintenance and insurance partner Kalitta up-gauging to 747-400Fs under JAL air waybills and isn't organic expansion. This asset-light reliance deters top-tier forwarders from signing long-term block space agreements, creating structural growth ceiling by 2029
  • On fuel, surcharges and hedges recover only 50-60% of price spikes. Although government subsidies lift JAL's effective recovery to c80%, remaining 20% directly drags EBIT. Consequently, its FY26 fuel guidance of JPY 417bn is at severe risk. Q1 alone consumed c35% of annual budget at cJPY 150bn, heavily threatening full-year EBIT and cash flow
