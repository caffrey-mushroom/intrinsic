SUMMARY

Overview

The expert described momentum in a Workday-focused services business spanning government, higher education, and commercial customers. Recent growth benefited from previously sold projects moving into delivery and improving new-customer activity in state, local, and education markets. The discussion also covered AI adoption, Workday’s partner strategy, product demand, and competitive dynamics.

Key Insights

The expert said near-term political developments were unlikely to materially change Workday’s broader trajectory, though government buyers may remain cautious. Funding uncertainty was seen as a greater concern for new transformation projects than for renewals. Fourth-quarter pipeline was above the level needed to meet the company’s plan, and the expert expected bookings to exceed the original forecast. Larger deals could make growth look unusually strong. Higher education demand is split between large, broad deployments and lower-cost entry projects that begin with a limited set of functions and may expand later. AI adoption is uneven. Some customers are advancing use cases, while others need help understanding governance, value, and what existing Workday tools can do. The expert cited faster financial close processes and improved system administration as practical AI benefits. The expert also expects AI-enabled deployment tools could reduce implementation effort and support better customer retention. The expert believes Workday has clarified its approach to AI consumption and related commercial terms. The next test is whether customers see enough ongoing value to expand usage. The moderator pursued further detail on AI’s real-world benefits, partner opportunities, and Workday’s competitive position.
