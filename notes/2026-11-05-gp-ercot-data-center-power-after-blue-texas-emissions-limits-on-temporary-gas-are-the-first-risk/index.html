SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed how political changes could affect Texas data center development, power supply, environmental rules, and transmission investment. The discussion covered ERCOT’s large-load review process, competing grid regions, and the challenge of meeting fast-growing demand while maintaining affordability and reliability.

Key Insights

Environmental limits are the most immediate policy risk. The expert expects potential new operating restrictions on less efficient, high-emission generators used by data centers as temporary power. A transition period that lets these units operate initially, then limits them to backup use, was presented as a reasonable approach. Near-term permitting and grid decisions remain uncertain. Election-related pauses may ease, but changes in agency priorities or leadership could affect both environmental approvals and ERCOT’s large-load process. The 765-kV transmission buildout is important but vulnerable to delays. The expert views it as a long-term solution to grid stability and capacity constraints, while citing cost, siting opposition, and political pressure as obstacles. Reliability must remain central. The expert cautioned against adding renewable capacity without dependable storage or other firm resources. Data centers could accelerate investment in alternatives such as fuel cells and small modular nuclear reactors. Large data centers may improve their prospects by contributing to the system. Providing power resources, supporting transmission costs, reducing or shifting demand during stress, and addressing local concerns could help projects gain acceptance.

Position and considerations

Texas / ERCOT

Major destination for data centers, but projects face queue uncertainty, political scrutiny, and transmission constraints.

SPP and MISO

Possible alternatives for developers concerned about ERCOT delays. SPP’s resource-adequacy requirements can add costs, and its network-upgrade studies raise cost-allocation concerns.

Temporary on-site generators

Available sooner than large new plants, but some smaller or older machines may face emissions or operating-hour limits.

Combined-cycle gas

A more efficient longer-term supply option, though delivery timelines are extended.

Wind and solar

Can contribute energy, but the expert sees limited value for peak reliability without sufficient long-duration storage.

Batteries, fuel cells, and small nuclear

Potential solutions that could improve firm supply; cost, maturity, and deployment timelines remain important constraints.
