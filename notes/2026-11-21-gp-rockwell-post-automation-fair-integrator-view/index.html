SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, an automation integrator with experience across manufacturing sectors, discussed Rockwell Automation’s product announcements after its Automation Fair, the adoption of AI and software, customer investment trends, and competitive positioning.

Rockwell introduced a broad range of products and updates. The expert viewed the volume as difficult to absorb, with AI-related offerings—particularly an AI connector—among the most notable. In the broader market, automation demand has improved, with more greenfield projects and larger, end-to-end scopes. However, customer approvals remain constrained by uncertain returns, project complexity, and doubts about incentives.

Key Insights

AI could improve manufacturing decisions, not just automate tasks. The expert sees valuable applications in using quality data to improve product design, production yield, and factory layouts. Current use of manufacturing data remains limited. Simulation can make projects more viable. Emulate3D was used to model factory throughput, and AI-assisted layout work reportedly reduced clean-room space needs enough to save millions of dollars. Rockwell is catching up rather than pulling ahead. The expert sees recent software updates as efforts to make an acquired and fragmented portfolio more complete. Asset IQ appears promising, but its practical performance is still unproven. Customers need integrated solutions, not product brochures. The expert believes Rockwell should work more closely with integrators, who can assess customer needs and deliver implementations. Rockwell’s hardware brand is strong, but awareness and demand for much of its software portfolio appear weaker. Investment is recovering, but not evenly. Automation activity has strengthened, and projects are moving through sales pipelines more decisively. The expert expects the next year to be better, while noting that geopolitical shifts could delay investment. AMR economics remain challenging. Forklift robots can be difficult to justify in variable manufacturing settings; lower costs or highly repetitive warehouse work improve the case.
