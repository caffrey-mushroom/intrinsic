SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed European solar demand, installation trends, project economics, and the growing role of battery storage. The expert expects the market to remain broadly flat in the near term before returning to growth, although policy targets may still be difficult to achieve.

Utility-scale and commercial and industrial projects are leading additions, while residential demand remains weak following subsidy reductions and reduced consumer concern about energy prices. Grid congestion, curtailment, and frequent low or negative daytime power prices are now the principal barriers to development.

Key Insights

The expert considers battery storage essential for most new utility-scale solar projects. Solar-only assets are increasingly difficult to finance because daytime generation often earns little revenue. Grid access has overtaken permitting as the primary development bottleneck. Projects with secured connections command substantially greater value than projects lacking grid capacity. Flexible grid agreements may accelerate connections but expose developers to uncompensated curtailment. Module prices appear close to their economic floor. Any savings are increasingly offset by higher labor, construction, inverter, and storage costs. Conventional solar power purchase agreements based on variable output are losing appeal. Buyers increasingly prefer storage- backed delivery, baseload structures, consumption-matched contracts, or diversified technology portfolios. Residential systems are shifting toward solar-and-battery packages as feed-in tariffs, net metering, and other incentives are reduced.
