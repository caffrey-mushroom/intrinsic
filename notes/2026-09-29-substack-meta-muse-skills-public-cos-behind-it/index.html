Substack · 2026-09-29

25 software products from 24 listcos that Muse is leveraging on

We went under the hood of Muse to see which software services it calls as it carries out a user’s request. We followed the steps from finding information and accessing accounts to making calls, managing bookings and returning a result, then linked those steps to the companies behind the software. This first part covers 25 products from 24 listed companies. It shows where Muse could bring more activity to existing communication, productivity, travel, commerce and connected-device services—and where we can currently confirm only that a connection exists.

Potential Beneficiaries by Category

The table groups the companies by the role their products could play in Muse tasks. These are potential beneficiaries: an integration does not, by itself, show additional revenue, a commercial agreement or how often the service is used.

How to Read This Guide

We looked at the software connections and task instructions available in Muse as of September 28, 2026. For each product, we show what Muse can call, where approval is needed and how it checks the result. The labels describe how much we could confirm, not how much revenue a company might earn.

  • Tested Workflow: We checked the sequence of calls and key results through code testing.
  • Documented Workflow: The available instructions describe the steps, but we have not confirmed the full sequence through testing.
  • Connection Identified: We found a dedicated connection to the product. Its detailed capabilities are not disclosed, so we do not assume them.

Products are linked to their listed parent companies, so Outlook and GitHub both count toward Microsoft. The company count is therefore smaller than the product count.

How a Muse Task Reaches a Software Company

The basic sequence is similar across products: the user asks for something, Muse selects a service, any required approval is collected, and the service returns a result.

Looking up information. Muse usually reads information within the access the user has already granted. A new connection may require the user to sign in first.

Taking action. Sending, publishing, buying, canceling or deleting may require the user to approve the action.

Checking the outcome. Muse needs a returned result—such as a confirmed reservation—to know the task succeeded. Starting a request is not the same as completing it.

Product-by-Product Breakdown

01. Twilio Telephony

Twilio · TWLO · Tested Workflow

From user approval to merchant pickup, AI and human callers ultimately route through Twilio lines

How It Works

  • The merchant’s public number is verified first; UNCLEAR requires supplemental web evidence, and DENIED terminates the flow.
  • After the call, phone_call_judge and recipient_annoyance run in parallel before the call result is delivered.
  • The approval card is a non-bypassable human control point; any change in cost or recipient requires reconfirmation.
  • Product boundaries: U.S. merchants only; no cold calls; cannot receive inbound calls or send text messages.

What We Found

twilio_call_sid; phone.* tool family; CallProvider=retell

Phone tools are not enabled for the current account and remain gated under early access (code is present but not enabled at runtime).

02. OpenTable Restaurant Reservations

Booking Holdings · BKNG · Tested Workflow

Searches availability, locks and books a slot, verifies confirmation, and supports modifications and cancellations

How It Works

  • If the connection fails, only the official link, alternative platform options, or a phone fallback can be returned.
  • Modifications go through modify-reservation-with-lock; cancellations go through cancel-reservation.
  • book-reservation combines the slot lock and booking into a single action, but status must still be re-verified.
  • A reservation may be described as “booked” only when an explicit confirmed status is returned.

What We Found

opentable.sock; book-reservation; modify-reservation-with-lock

03. Spotify Music & Podcasts

Spotify Technology · SPOT · Tested Workflow

After OAuth connection, supports discovery, saving, playback control, and content cleanup

How It Works

  • Exact track searches use the “track by artist” format to avoid matching cover versions.
  • Podcast/show removal is handled by the save-to-spotify CLI and is kept separate from general library operations.
  • Playback and queueing typically require an active Spotify Connect device; explicitly specifying the device is preferred.
  • On a wearable cold start, a music_fulfillment is generated first and then executed by the device.

What We Found

spotify-api.sock; save-to-spotify.sock; Spotify Partner API CLI

04. FlightAware Flight Data

RTX (via Collins Aerospace / FlightAware) · RTX · Documented Workflow

Reads flight status, tracks, airports, and future schedules, with time-window-based monitoring

How It Works

  • Use flight for current and near-term flights; use schedules for dates further out.
  • Scheduled checks are orchestrated by Muse; FlightAware only supplies data for each query.
  • Recommended cadence: daily when more than 48 hours out, hourly within 48 hours, and every 10–15 minutes within 6 hours of departure.

What We Found

flightaware.sock; flightaware CLI; AeroAPI

05. Ticketmaster Event Discovery

Live Nation Entertainment · LYV · Documented Workflow

Searches events and seat recommendations; transactions must be completed via an external Buy-now link

How It Works

  • Muse can retrieve events, event details, and seat and pricing recommendations.
  • Key boundary: purchases are not completed within Muse under this integration; the user executes the final transaction on an external page.
  • seat-view-carousel is display-only and does not change order status.

What We Found

ticketmaster.sock; search-events; top-picks; seat-view-carousel

06. Peloton Classes & Scheduling

Peloton Interactive · PTON · Documented Workflow

From class search to scheduling, with support for rescheduling and deletion

How It Works

  • Standard class reads call the target command directly without a status pre-check; authorization is triggered only when not_connected.
  • On-demand classes use ride-id + scheduled-start-time; live classes use join-token.
  • metadata-mappings is retrieved first to avoid hardcoding instructor or class type IDs.
  • Subsequent changes are managed via reschedule-event or delete-scheduled-event.

What We Found

peloton.sock; Peloton 3P API helper CLI

07. Philips Hue Smart Lighting

Signify · LIGHT.AS · Documented Workflow

Discovers devices and controls lights, rooms, and scenes via Hue Remote API v2

How It Works

  • OAuth connection and Bridge pairing are two separate stages.
  • Color, color temperature, brightness, and dynamic effects are passed as explicit parameters.
  • Control actions support individual lights, room/zone groups, and scene activation.

What We Found

philips-hue.sock; Philips Hue Remote API v2 CLI

08. Microsoft Outlook

Microsoft · MSFT · Documented Workflow

Mail, calendar, and contacts are written to Microsoft Graph through three isolated connectors

How It Works

  • Mail supports sending, replying, flagging, and moving items to Deleted Items.
  • The three connectors are separate: outlook-mail, outlook-calendar, and outlook-contacts.
  • Calendar supports create, update, and delete; Contacts supports create, update, and delete.

What We Found

outlook-mail.sock; outlook-calendar.sock; outlook-contacts.sock

10. Google Workspace

Alphabet · GOOGL · Documented Workflow

Gmail, Calendar, and Drive share an isolated CLI, with reads and writes routed by resource type

How It Works

  • Gmail drafts require approval of the exact text before sending; archiving, labeling, and unsubscribing are also supported.
  • Drive supports upload, update, copy, and share; permanent deletion requires explicit confirmation.
  • Calendar checks availability via freebusy, then inserts, patches, or deletes events.

What We Found

hatch-gws-cli-fully-isolated.sock; Gmail/Calendar/Drive skills Subscribed

11. Apple HealthKit

Apple · AAPL · Documented Workflow

After device sync, queries daily summaries, raw samples, and sleep/workout sessions

How It Works

  • metrics are full-day summaries; samples are unbucketed heart rate, step count, GPS, and sleep-stage data points.
  • delete erases stored records and is a separate destructive action.
  • sessions treat each sleep or workout as a discrete session.

What We Found

healthkit-cli.sock; health-cli --provider healthkit; manifest actions

12. Meta Social Products

Meta Platforms · META · Documented Workflow

Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and Threads read content independently; outbound publishing and message changes are approval-gated

How It Works

  • Facebook Marketplace create/edit/delete/publish actions are write operations.
  • Messenger syncs first, then reads by cid; mutations require approval, and output masks raw IDs.
  • For Instagram reads and writes, the user’s owned account is first selected via accounts.
  • Threads first selects the account via threads-cli accounts; feed is read-only, and every publish-post requires approval.

What We Found

facebook-cli.sock; instagram-cli.sock; messenger.sock; threads-cli.sock; threads-messages-cli.sock

Meta also operates a separate first-party meta-ads-cli.sock (ad platform connector); it is likewise attributable to META but is not charted separately.
