原标题：Review|NOW 26Q3: Below Expectations, Deployment Needs Complicate the Outlook

Q3 beat was light, Q4 cRPO and margins missed bogeys, and AI deployment needs delay monetization.

Oct 29

READ IN APP

Executive summary

NOW’s results fell short of expectations despite healthy channel checks. Q3 cRPO grew 21% cc, beating guidance by 100bps versus an expected beat of 150–200bps. The Q4 cRPO guide of 18.5% implies approximately 19.25% growth after adding back the quantified on-prem and T4C effects, below the ~20% bogey. Operating margin guidance of 32% was also 150bps below consensus of 33.5%. We underestimated the deployment support needed to put AI orders into production and its impact on subscription revenue and delivery costs. Despite healthy demand signals from channels, implied organic growth also fell short of expectations and points to further deceleration. Momentum in AICT and security continues to support our medium-term view. However, monetization may take longer and require more upfront investment than anticipated.

A smaller beat than expected, with several factors affecting the outlook

Q3 delivered a smaller beat than expected

  • cRPO: Up 21% cc, versus 20% guidance and a bogey of 21.5–22%. The 100bps beat was roughly half early renewals and half underlying performance, leaving a 50–100bps shortfall against expectations.- Subscription revenue: Up 20.5% cc, versus 20% guidance. Adding back the approximately 25bps deployment-related drag implies ~20.75% growth, still below Q2’s 23%. This was a modest beat; healthy demand translated into less subscription upside than we expected.- Profitability: non-GAAP operating margin was 32.5%, above the 31% guide.

Q4 adjustments explain part of the shortfall, while organic growth slows

  • cRPO bridge: 18.5% cc guidance + 25bps from a higher on-prem mix + approximately 50bps from termination-for-convenience (T4C) provisions = ~19.25%. That remains ~75bps below the ~20% bogey and ~45bps below the 19.7% consensus estimate.- Several factors complicate the outlook. A higher on-prem mix brings forward some revenue that would otherwise remain in backlog. T4C contracts can contribute revenue but are excluded from RPO because of their cancellation provisions. Management also described a modest deployment-related cRPO impact without quantifying it. The 50bps deployment drag disclosed for Q4 subscription growth cannot simply be added to cRPO. The available quantified adjustments still leave growth below 20%.- Organic cRPO: Excluding acquisitions, Q4 guidance implies approximately 16.75% cc growth, or about 17.25% after the T4C adjustment alone. The underlying trajectory continues to decelerate.- Q4 subscription revenue: Growth of 21.5% cc is broadly in line with consensus and accelerates from Q3’s 20.5%. The slowdown is therefore most evident in forward cRPO, while quarterly subscription growth improves.- FY26 subscription revenue: The midpoint increased by $15m to $15.785b, but approximately $12m comes from FX, leaving only $3m of underlying uplift after the $8m Q3 beat against the high end. As in Q2, the raise falls short of the beat.- Margins: Q 4 non-GAAP operating margin guidance of 32% is 150bps below consensus of 33.5%, while the full-year 31.5% target is unchanged despite the Q3 margin beat. The 100bps increase in full-year free cash flow margin guidance to 36% is positive, but lower capital expenditure contributes to that improvement.

The 2027 outlook depends on deployment and revenue conversion

Management discussed an illustrative FY27 subscription-growth framework of around 18%: starting with 18.5% Q4 cRPO growth, subtracting roughly 100bps for deployment support and adding back about 50bps for T4C. Management has not issued formal FY27 guidance. The framework suggests a more measured starting point for subscription growth, with deployment support offsetting some of the near-term revenue benefit from AI demand. Upside depends on how effectively that support drives adoption, paid usage and expansion.

Management still expects operating-margin expansion, while flagging gross-margin pressure from data-center and hyperscaler investment. These infrastructure costs are separate from the FDE initiative. The investment can support a larger AI business over time, but the pace of revenue conversion and delivery efficiency will determine the near-term margin benefit.

What we underestimated: NOW’s role in AI deployment

Subscribed Our checks captured customer interest in AI, but we underestimated how much hands-on deployment work NOW would need to provide alongside the software. Management is including deployment support in selected large deals to accelerate adoption. A portion of the contract value is consequently allocated to professional services rather than subscription revenue. The company has not disclosed the share of new orders requiring this support, so we cannot conclude that most AI contracts have become services-heavy. The disclosed impact shows that converting AI bookings into subscription revenue requires more work than we assumed.

The FDE bottleneck we identified also applies to NOW

As we wrote in recent Palantir report, meaningful AI projects require business-process redesign, data integration, coordination across departments and engineers who have delivered similar projects before.

The implication for NOW: selling AI functionality and demonstrating customer interest are only the first steps. Getting agents into production still requires integration, permissions, workflow configuration and validation. NOW’s deployment specialists address that implementation bottleneck, similar in purpose to PLTR’s FDE model. PLTR’s accumulated delivery expertise was part of our positive thesis there. We should have given more weight to the same deployment requirement when assessing the pace and economics of NOW’s AI monetization. Similar deployment needs do not imply identical accounting or economics across the two companies.

Deployment changes the near-term mix, with a potential longer-term payoff

  • Near-term revenue mix: The deployment component reduces subscription growth by approximately 25bps in Q3 and 50bps in Q4. Part of this reflects revenue allocation within bundled contracts, rather than lost order value. It reduces the recurring software component recognized in the near term, but can also help customers put the purchased capabilities into production. Strong bookings remain encouraging , even if their conversion into subscription growth is less direct than we expected.- Margin implications: FDEs and other deployment resources incur delivery costs. Greater upfront support makes initial deployments more labor-intensive. Repeatable methods, automation and partner participation could improve efficiency. The Q4 margin miss warrants caution, but management has not quantified how much is attributable to FDE support.- Renewals offer a potential payback: Management expects the contract value allocated to services to shift back toward subscription revenue at renewal, typically in roughly two years, potentially sooner through early renewal. This could improve the subscription mix as deployments mature, provided contract value is retained and customers need less ongoing implementation support. The timing and magnitude remain uncertain.- Early adoption results are encouraging: Management cited supported customers reaching roughly twice the adoption within five weeks, with materially higher usage. This can improve renewal and expansion prospects. The next test is whether those adoption gains translate into enough incremental paid consumption and expansion to justify the deployment investment.- As we argued in the PLTR report, FDE demand is real and essential. In our view, NOW’s own engineers may be well placed to address complex deployment needs on its platform. Successful execution could support faster consumption growth over time. We therefore temper our expectations for the timing of monetization, while retaining the potential for a stronger contribution as deployments mature.

Healthy channel feedback did not fully capture the delivery requirement

Our channel partner checks were broadly positive on demand, renewals, AI upgrades and implementation activity. Management’s comments on bookings above internal expectations and no Q3 deal pushouts are consistent with that demand picture. The checks did not fully capture NOW’s direct implementation role and the resulting software/services mix. Management indicated that partners could take on more of the simpler deployment work as NOW establishes repeatable approaches. That could improve scalability, although the timing and magnitude remain uncertain.

AICT and security continue to support the medium-term case

AICT momentum is becoming more tangible

  • Commercial progress: AI Control Tower NNACV grew more than 6x y/y. Overall AI NNACV grew 65%, and management raised its AI ACV target from $1.5b to $1.7b. These sales indicators support continued AI expansion. Near-term revenue contribution remains harder to assess : ACV and NNACV are distinct from current-period recognized revenue, and AICT’s absolute contribution was not disclosed.- Customer evidence: Management cited Accenture’s approximately 900 ServiceNow-native AI assets and Nestlé’s planned expansion across 60 countries. These examples support the value of common processes for inventory, ownership, approval and monitoring. External-agent coverage and direct runtime control still need to be assessed separately.- Strategic value and limits: As more agents enter production, enterprises need to govern who owns them, what they can access and how exceptions are handled. NOW can connect that governance to existing enterprise workflows. However, identity platforms, cloud providers and security vendors may still enforce the underlying controls. AICT’s improving position supports our constructive view. Responsibilities remain divided across vendors, and leadership in governance is still being established.

Security has a clearer commercial path

  • Sales momentum: Security and Risk appeared in 16 of the top 20 deals, with 13 deals above $1m; AI-related NNACV in the portfolio grew more than 4.5x. This provides broader commercial evidence than AICT’s growth rate alone, although the figures do not isolate organic subscription revenue from each acquired product.- The product proposition: Armis adds asset discovery and exposure context; Veza adds visibility into permissions; SecOps connects findings to investigation and remediation workflows. Management described a more unified offering and sales approach, with further CMDB and operational integration still to come. The opportunity is to expand NOW’s role in security operations and cross-sell into the installed base.
