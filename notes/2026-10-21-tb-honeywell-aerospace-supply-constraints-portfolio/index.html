Agenda

  • Post-IPO rebound for Honeywell Aerospace (NASDAQ: HONA)
  • Source positioning by platform and general risks with supply
  • Area concentration review noting exposure concern
  • Outlining the pace of OE growth and its impacts on future aftermarket business

Key Insights

  • Expert would not expect a meaningful shift in revenue mix in Electronic Solutions from current c45% defence, c40% aftermarket and c15% commercial OE mix. Defence revenue mix will grow faster than commercial with potential opportunities for higher-margin, international defence revenue that should offset margin compression from domestic markets
  • Avionics aftermarket revenue is purely event-driven post-warranty, often 2-5 years out, making demand appear lumpy at the individual product level but forecastable in aggregate across the product suite
  • Expert considers the pull-forward of USD 50m CAPEX for full-year 2026 justified, enabling Honeywell Aerospace to unlock its supply base, re-organise procurement strategies and position itself more favourably in the long term. Throughput is likely to improve high-single- to low-double-digits for the next 18 months
  • Boeing new platform supplier selections are expected around 2027-28, c7-8 years before anticipated entry into service around 2035, creating near-term critical competitive windows for Honeywell
