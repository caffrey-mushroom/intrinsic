SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion covered Yangtze Memory Technologies’ planned Fab 4, its equipment sourcing strategy, and opportunities for domestic semiconductor-equipment suppliers. The expert, who has experience in process engineering and regional equipment marketing, described how export restrictions have pushed Chinese fabs to qualify alternative suppliers and localize equipment. Fab 4 is expected to focus mainly on production, while Fab 3 retains more development work.

Key Insights

The expert expects Fab 4 to repeat equipment already qualified at Fab 3, while also providing opportunities for newly qualified tools and more advanced equipment. The proposed 60–70% localization target is constrained by gaps in lithography, high- performance ion implantation, and some metrology applications. He sees localization as both a response to restrictions and a strategic choice: fabs value dependable supply and are wary of relying on foreign vendors whose ability to provide support could change. Higher NAND layer counts raise equipment requirements and demand. Deeper channel-hole etching becomes much harder, while deposition faces greater thermal and stress-control challenges. Fabs may need more tools and faster process times. The expert expects domestic suppliers to gain share, particularly in etch and deposition, but cautioned that localization does not mean one vendor will supply every process step. Fabs prioritize yield, uptime, and tool performance. He expressed particular confidence in AMEC’s memory etch capability, citing its performance in deep-hole etching and suggesting its share at Fab 4 could exceed its existing baseline. The moderator followed up on the basis for this advantage and its implications for supplier competition. He considered a shift into DRAM possible but unconfirmed, emphasizing that DRAM and 3D NAND require different technical capabilities.
