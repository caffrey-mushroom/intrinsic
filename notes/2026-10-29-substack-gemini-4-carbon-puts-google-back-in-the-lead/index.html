Gemini 4 Carbon tops AA rankings, signaling Google regained AI leadership and faster RSI-driven releases.

Oct 29

Executive Summary

  1. Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon on October 28, about two weeks after Argon became broadly available on October 13. On version 5 of the Artificial Analysis (AA) Intelligence Index, Carbon ranks first with a score of 59, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 58 and GPT-6 Astra at 57; Argon scores 50. Pricing matches Argon: introductory rates of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising to standard rates of $4/$20. All token prices below follow this input/output convention. Carbon is initially being made available to a select group of cyber defenders, with access then expanding to more partners through the Fairwind Program. Google has not announced when developers and consumers will get access.2. Google is back in the top tier. With models from the three leading labs separated by just two points on the AA index, we believe competition in model training has intensified further. Consistent with our earlier view, Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai said on the company’s earnings call the same day that AI is helping Google speed up model training and releases. He expects recursive self-improvement (RSI) to drive further acceleration over the next 12 months. Our understanding is that Google used model soups in this cycle to accelerate model development.

  • Carbon’s strengths are long-horizon tasks, long context and cybersecurity; it does not yet lead in software engineering or computer use. It leads on 15 of the 17 benchmarks in Google’s comparison. The two exceptions are FrontierSWE 2, where it trails Astra and Opus 5.5, and OSWorld-2.1, where it trails Opus 5.5.- Third-party evaluations paint a similar picture. AA says Carbon does not yet lead on coding and knowledge-work evaluations. On its Coding Agent Index, Carbon running in the Antigravity CLI scores 59, below the score of 62 achieved by Opus 5.5 running in Claude Code. On LMArena, Carbon ranks first on the text leaderboard and second in Agent Arena, behind only Opus 5.5.- At standard pricing, Carbon’s weighted average cost per task on the AA Intelligence Index evaluations is $7.76, about 23% lower than the cost for Opus 5.5 at its highest reasoning setting.

Carbon Scores Nine Points Above Argon as Training Competition Intensifies

Subscribed

  • When AA released version 5 of its index on October 27, the top five models all came from Anthropic and OpenAI, while Argon and Meta’s Muse Wonder Full both scored 50. A day later, Carbon took first place.- In our October 1 note on Gemini 4, we said Argon was only an early post-training version. According to internal documents seen by Business Insider, Argon, Barium and Carbon are all versions in the Gemini 4 series, and Pichai has also said Argon is just the start of the series. We think Carbon and Argon share the same pretrained base model and that the nine-point gain came mainly from post-training.

Research LLM: Gemini 4 Brings Google Back into Frontier Competition as Investment Accelerates

Oct 1

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on September 30, roughly ten months after Gemini 3. On Google’s evaluations, Argon leads or ties GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 on 14 of 19 benchmarks. Artificial Analysis scores it at 53, level with Astra and below Opus 5.5 at 58 and Sonnet 5.5 at 56. Read full story

  • Since June, Anthropic has released a frontier model roughly every 35 days on average; Google took just 28 days to go from announcing Argon on September 30 to announcing Carbon. Alphabet’s third-quarter capex was US$51.4 billion, compared with about US$24 billion a year earlier. Full-year guidance remains US$195–205 billion, and CFO Anat Ashkenazi said capex will increase significantly in 2027. Pichai also said Google is developing distilled versions of Carbon for efficient deployment in products serving a billion users or more.

AI and Model Soups Are Speeding Up Model Development

  • Google attributes the faster pace to AI. Pichai said teams in Chrome, Ads and Search have used Argon over the past few weeks to write and optimize code, shipping 35% more code with 30% fewer errors. He said software engineering productivity reached a clear inflection point after Argon was deployed internally. Koray Kavukcuoglu, Google DeepMind SVP and Chief AI Architect, also wrote in the Carbon launch post that researchers and models are working together to advance model capabilities faster.- A model soup is created by directly averaging the weights of several models. A 2022 paper with Google Research co-authors found that versions fine-tuned from the same pretrained model with different hyperparameters tend to lie in the same low-loss region. Averaging their weights often yields better performance than the best individual model. For ViT-G, ImageNet accuracy rose from 90.72% for the best single model to 90.94%. The paper’s “greedy” approach adds candidate models one at a time, in descending order of validation accuracy, retaining each addition only if it improves the combined model’s validation accuracy. Merging itself requires no additional training and produces a single model with no increase in inference cost.- Model soups allow gains from multiple post-training runs to be combined in a single model. Google has extensive experience with this approach. Gemma 2 averaged the weights of several models trained with different hyperparameters. The technical papers for Gemini Embedding and Gemini Embedding 2 both explicitly describe the use of model soups. In the latter, adding specific video datasets to the fine-tuning mix lowered scores on some tasks, but after model merging, scores on all three video benchmarks exceeded the baseline. A 2024 study with Google co-authors compared several merging methods and found that merging works better as the base model becomes larger and stronger. It also found that merging eight expert models trained on different tasks often yields better generalization than multitask training.- We think model soups help shorten iteration cycles and that model soups and RSI will reinforce each other. As AI takes on more experimental and evaluation work, teams can run more post-training experiments in parallel, while model soups combine the gains from those runs. This type of merging typically combines models derived from the same base. Incompatible models can reduce overall performance, and the merged model still requires a full evaluation, including safety testing.

Implications and What to Watch

  • We expect demand for frontier training and post-training compute to keep growing. The three labs’ models are closely matched on the AA Intelligence Index. Investment in pretraining next-generation base models continues, while post-training and evaluation cycles on the same base model are measured in weeks. Alphabet has said its capex will increase significantly in 2027, and distilled versions of Carbon will add to inference demand once they are deployed in Google’s products.- What to watch: when Carbon becomes available to developers and how it performs in real-world use, especially relative to Opus 5.5 on coding agent tasks; the release cadence of OpenAI’s and Anthropic’s next flagship models; the rollout of distilled versions of Carbon in Google products; pricing after Argon’s introductory rates expire on November 30 ($4/$20 from December 1); and Alphabet’s specific 2027 capex guidance in its fourth-quarter results.
