SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a cybersecurity reseller serving enterprise customers, described a crowded market where overlapping products make it difficult for buyers to distinguish vendors. This uncertainty is delaying decisions, particularly for agentic AI tools, as some customers defer purchases while they wait for solutions to mature.

Traditional security demand remains healthy, increasingly driven by the need to secure AI adoption. Key areas include identity governance, non-human identity, data security posture management, data loss prevention, shadow AI controls, AI red teaming, and monitoring connections between organizations and external AI services.

Key Insights

AI security is creating new spending, but also a complicated buying process. Organizations are adding protection around AI investments and employee use of AI tools. Buyers often assess several separate product categories because no single vendor covers every need. CrowdStrike is gaining traction through broader platform deals. The expert highlighted larger deployments, expansion into SIEM and exposure management, and flexible purchasing that lets customers adopt capabilities over time. Microsoft poses a growing substitution risk. Its identity and network access products could pressure Okta and Zscaler, especially among mid-market and upper mid-market organizations seeking to reduce costs. Palo Alto is benefiting from identity integration and platform selling. The expert expects its newer AI and endpoint capabilities to contribute more as products become better integrated. AI product pricing remains difficult to evaluate. Vendors use inconsistent measures, making direct comparisons challenging and increasing the need for proof-of-concept testing. The expert also emphasized application security for internally developed, AI-assisted software and continuous threat exposure management as areas of rising interest.
