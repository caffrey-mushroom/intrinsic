SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed a global healthcare commercialization and communications platform following its acquisition by a private equity sponsor. The company evolved from a collection of separate businesses into a more integrated organization, with greater collaboration across medical affairs, education, patient engagement, evidence, access, and consulting.

The expert expects future value creation to center on US expansion, a more focused medical affairs offering, improved cross-selling, and deeper integration of consulting capabilities. Large pharmaceutical companies remain the most attractive clients, while oncology, immunology, and rare diseases offer the strongest therapeutic growth opportunities.

Key Insights

Medical affairs and communications are the company’s largest, highest-margin, and most strategically important services. Most historical growth was organic, supported by strong client relationships and expansion within acquired accounts. Integrated offerings improve retention and account growth, but consulting and evidence-access services remain less connected to the broader platform. One acquired creative agency integrated successfully and nearly doubled revenue for that service, while another acquisition experienced greater integration difficulty. The company commands mid-to-high pricing because clients recognize its quality and frequently include it on preferred supplier lists. Digital and artificial intelligence capabilities lag some competitors, although recent investment should improve efficiency and client offerings. The expert strongly favored US expansion, specialist talent retention, refined medical affairs positioning, and better consulting integration. Repeated restructuring has increased talent-retention risk and represents a key diligence concern for investors. Re-establishing a dedicated business-development function could support growth.
