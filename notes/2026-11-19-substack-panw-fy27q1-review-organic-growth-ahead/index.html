原标题：Review|PANW FY27Q1: A Stronger Starting Point for Faster Organic Growth in 2027

#security #software

Substack · 2026-11-19

核心摘要

  1. Palo Alto FY27Q1营收同比增长39.2%（上季34.4%），高于指引上限34%和买方34%-35%的预期；NGS ARR增长66%，FUNDA估算剔除CyberArk和Chronosphere后有机增速从28.9%加速至30.4%。
  2. 公司指引Q2营收增长36%-37%、NGS ARR增长61%，并将FY27营收和NGS ARR增速指引上调至26%-27%；FUNDA预计2027年有机增长将快于2026年。
  3. Q1净新增平台化客户约170家创纪录，超过前两年Q1之和，平台化客户占NGS ARR三分之二，净留存率超120%；一笔与全球金融机构的交易金额达8300万美元。
  4. SASE净新增ARR超1亿美元、同比增长30%；XSIAM ARR增长超65%至约8亿美元，每天处理超20PB遥测数据。
  5. Prisma AIRS推动软件防火墙ARR增长34%，上线五个季度ARR接近1.7亿美元、客户超1000家，管理层认为其支出长期可达客户token支出的2%-5%。
  6. 管理层称严重漏洞的发现数量接近去年月均的7倍，客户动用应急资金进行前沿AI防御测试，预计持续测试需求将维持12-18个月，未来两年网络安全预算将上升。
  7. PANW以45亿美元股票收购Applied Compute，用于训练自有安全模型并为企业提供开源模型的训练和托管，管理层预计到FY2029贡献超10亿美元NGS ARR。
  8. FUNDA认为AI模型厂商取代传统安全厂商的空间有限，漏洞发现与测试只占既有厂商收入的小部分，企业更倾向通过现有安全或云厂商使用模型。

I. Quarterly Results and Outlook

  1. Revenue and NGS ARR growth both accelerated in FY27Q1. Revenue grew 39.2% year over year, up from 34.4% last

quarter. That was above the high end of guidance (34%) and 4–5 percentage points ahead of the 34%–35% buy-side range we heard before earnings. NGS ARR grew 66%, up from 63% last quarter and above management’s 63% guidance. Excluding CyberArk and Chronosphere, we estimate organic NGS ARR growth accelerated to 30.4% from 28.9%.

  1. Based on our channel checks, we expect revenue and organic NGS ARR growth to pick up modestly again in Q2. Palo

Alto guided to 36%–37% year-over-year revenue growth and 61% NGS ARR growth for Q2, both below Q1 growth rates. It raised FY27 guidance for both revenue and NGS ARR growth to 26%–27%.

  1. We remain positive on the outlook for faster organic growth at Palo Alto in CY2027. We were already positive on

Q1 heading into earnings and expected growth to pick up. Even so, results came in well ahead of our expectations. Large customers placed bigger orders for new products than we expected, and more customers may have added to their security budgets than we had assumed. Both factors should continue to support growth next quarter and ease the transition to faster organic growth next year. Our channel checks continue to suggest that customers’ spending with Palo Alto will grow faster on an organic basis in CY2027 than in CY2026. As AI speeds up both vulnerability discovery and attacks, we expect demand for ongoing testing and protection to drive security budgets higher. Palo Alto should also benefit as customers consolidate spending with fewer security vendors.

  1. We see limited scope for AI model providers to displace established cybersecurity vendors. Models are taking over

some of the vulnerability discovery and testing that people and traditional scanners do today. That work accounts for a relatively small share of established vendors’ revenue. Endpoint, cloud and network security still require substantial deployment and integration work across customers’ systems. The enterprises in our channel checks also prefer to access models through an existing security or cloud provider so they can keep sensitive data segregated and protected.

II. Earnings Call Highlights

  1. Platformization
  2. Net new platformizations set a Q1 record.

“In Q1, net new platformizations hit a record of approximately 170, which was more than the last two Q1s combined.”

Platformized customers now account for two-thirds of NGS ARR, with net retention above 120%.

b. One large customer bought virtual patching and identity security in the same deal.

“In Q1, we closed an $83 million deal with a global financial institution.”

The customer consolidated its network security with Palo Alto and adopted Frontier virtual patching. It also expanded its identity security purchases, “signing the largest ever deal in CyberArk history.”

  1. Firewalls, SASE and XSIAM

a. Management expects hardware firewall demand to remain strong for at least another 12 months. It cited “people accelerating purchases because prices are going up because of component pricing” and said the company was “benefiting from the neocloud build cycle.” On consolidation, it added, “People want to start ripping out part of the old infrastructure and replacing it.”

b. SASE added over $100m in net new ARR.

“Starting with SASE, ARR grew 30% year-over-year … Demand was particularly strong for SD-WAN and secure browser.”

c. XSIAM ARR reached approximately $800m. “XSIAM ARR grew over 65% year-over-year to approximately $800 million” as customers prioritized modernizing their security operations centers in response to AI-driven threats. XSIAM now processes over 20 petabytes of telemetry a day, “leveraging AI to reduce response times from days to under 10 minutes.”

  1. New AI Products

a. Prisma AIRS drove software firewall growth.

“Software firewall ARR accelerated for the third consecutive quarter and grew 34% year-over-year. This was largely driven by Prisma AIRS.”

Within five quarters of launch, Prisma AIRS had reached nearly $170m in ARR and more than 1,000 customers.

b. Prisma AIRS now supports agent permissions. Idira’s Agentic IdP became generally available in Q1. Management described it as “integrated directly into our Prisma AIRS AI gateway, enabling real-time enforcement against agentic permissions.”

c. Prisma AIRS usage grows with enterprise token consumption.

“The more tokens you use in the enterprise, the more you have to have inspection tokens.”

Management said Prisma AIRS spending could reach 2%–5% of customers’ token spending over time.

d. Palo Alto relaunched Chronosphere as Cortex XCOR.

“ARR was up approximately four times year-over-year.”

As management noted last quarter, this included roughly “nine figures of net new ARR from a large frontier lab customer migrating to Chronosphere from an incumbent provider.”

  1. Security Threats and Customer Budgetsa. Vulnerability discovery is outpacing customers’ ability to patch.

“We’re seeing critical and severe vulnerabilities discovered nearly seven times the monthly average of last year.”

Management said that “IT teams simply cannot patch these vulnerabilities fast enough without compromising their production environments.”

b.. Customers have found funding for urgent vulnerability testing.

“I think the Frontier AI Defense budgets have just come out of emergency funds.”

Customers saw the tests as urgent.

“I don’t know where they found them, but they actually found the money to make sure they can check for vulnerabilities.”

On the longer-term budget outlook, management expects further vendor consolidation and said, “you’ll see cyber budgets rise, I’m sure, in the next two years.”

  1. Security Vendors and AI Model Providers

a. Customers are using frontier models to test their own systems. Management expects demand for ongoing testing to persist for 12–18 months. Over time, it expects continuous frontier AI defense to become “a capability that every company would have delivered to them by their platform providers, whether it’s through our XSIAM platform, through our perimeter security platform.”

b. Palo Alto plans to use Applied Compute’s expertise in adapting open-weight models both to develop its own security products and to help enterprise customers train and run their own models.

“First, we will embed AI across all of our security capabilities and are in the process of training north of 35 specialized use cases … Secondly, we will help train and serve secure sovereign implementations of open-weight models to the enterprise.”

c. Palo Alto wants to train a supervisory model alongside the customer’s business model. Management said it was hard to assess an application without knowing how it was trained to behave.

“Imagine if you can train your application and have the same team train something that supervises your application, I think that’s the future of AI security.”

III. Why Palo Alto Is Acquiring Applied Compute

  1. Palo Alto has agreed to acquire Applied Compute for $4.5b in stock. Applied Compute helps enterprises train and

run specialized models using their own data. Palo Alto plans to use this capability to train its own security models and gradually add dedicated inference capacity.

  1. The acquisition could strengthen Prisma AIRS’ runtime protection for AI agents. Palo Alto wants to train a

security model alongside the customer’s business model so it can recognize and block actions that fall outside an agent’s assigned task.

  1. Palo Alto could also sell model training and hosting to enterprises that want to retain control of sensitive

data. Management expects Applied Compute to contribute more than $1b in NGS ARR by FY2029. Our revenue forecasts currently exclude Applied Compute.
