SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed a national specialty pharmacy provider focused on IV immunoglobulin, rare-disease therapies, home infusion, and ambulatory infusion centers. The expert drew on more than 20 years of industry experience, including seven years managing specialty therapy referrals across a large regional market.

Growth was attributed primarily to profitable IV immunoglobulin demand, new rare-disease drug launches, strong manufacturer relationships, payer access, and productive physician referral relationships. Neurology practices were identified as the most valuable referral sources because patients often require higher weight-based doses.

Key Insights

The provider’s strongest advantage is its ability to offer both home infusion and infusion-center care. Many competitors provide only one setting. Early access to limited-distribution drugs and strong manufacturer contracting materially improve referral conversion. Referral relationships depend heavily on sales representative continuity, responsiveness, and trust with physician-office staff. The expert viewed centralized intake centers as the largest growth constraint because they reduce continuity and localized service. Referral acknowledgment should occur within hours, while initial benefit and eligibility decisions should generally be available within 24 hours. Delays approaching five to seven days materially increase the risk that an office sends the referral elsewhere. Missing clinical documentation, incomplete prior authorization, billing errors, and lack of written payer confirmation create substantial reimbursement risk. Nursing availability was considered manageable and less likely to constrain growth than intake execution, payer authorization, or drug access.
