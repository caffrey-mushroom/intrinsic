  1. What is the status of Huawei's latest tri-fold smartphone, Mate XT 2? What are its specific highlights?

Mate XT 2 is the second-generation tri-fold flagship smartphone officially launched on September 7, 2026, starting at RMB 19,999. Its main highlights are as follows:

Pioneering wing-spread tri-fold architecture: Featuring a G-shaped dual-inward-folding design, the left and right screens fold inward synchronously. The 10.2-inch inner screen is completely enclosed inside the body, fundamentally eliminating the durability pain point of the previous-generation tri-fold device whose exposed inner screen was prone to bumps. The folding motion intuitively mirrors opening and closing a book, requiring zero learning curve.

Extreme thinness and lightness: When fully unfolded, the thinnest point measures only 3.5mm, making it thinner than the charging ports of many regular bar-type flagships. In the folded state, thickness is around 12.3mm, and total weight is only 299g. Even during extended one-handed use, it causes no noticeable wrist drag, defying stereotypes of tri-fold phones being bulky and heavy.

Second-generation Tiangong hinge system: Utilizing a gearless dual-track linkage design, it has endured 400,000 rigorous folding tests. Opening and closing damping feels uniform and smooth, and the screen crease is virtually invisible, making it barely noticeable during everyday lit-screen usage.

Multi-dimensional upgrades were made to screen technology, including hardware-level privacy protection and a yellow-body design to safeguard wireless feedback. The inner display uses an ultra-tough folding screen solution, while the outer display adopts higher-strength glass, markedly improving overall usability and durability. Addressing user concerns over creasing, the company focused on optimizing glass layer materials and processing techniques. These enhancements aim to tackle the pain points of the prior-generation product across complex usage scenarios and elevate the user experience.

In terms of features, health monitoring capabilities such as ECG analysis and ECG acquisition were added. Regarding chipsets, the device debuts the 905 0Pro chip as an all-new iterative solution to provide performance support for the hardware.

  1. What is the target customer segment for Huawei's latest tri-fold smartphone, Mate XT 2?

The core customer base for XT2 consists of business users, primarily corporate executives and professionals, with product positioning emphasizing office productivity applications and imaging capabilities. These users frequently handle emails, review contracts, and attend meetings in public settings like high-speed trains, airports, and coffee shops. The hardware-level Armor Shield privacy screen automatically conceals display content from side angles, solving privacy leak pain points during public work sessions. Combined with the 10.2-inch large screen, HarmonyOS 7 panoramic three-way split-screen functionality enables simultaneous execution of video calls, contract browsing, and handwritten annotations. In office scenarios, the new model significantly enhances gaming performance through performance iterations, including improved ray tracing effects and high-frame-rate experiences. Meanwhile, thanks to upgraded chipset performance, the device features optimized power management and battery life compared to the previous generation.

Another customer group comprises productivity users, including photographers, short-form video creators, designers, and senior media professionals. They can directly complete detailed photo retouching, rough video edits, and graphic layouts on the 10.2-inch 3K ultra-clear display without having to carry an additional portable monitor.

  1. What is the Armor Shield privacy screen?

The Armor Shield privacy screen is Huawei's in-house developed, industry-first flexible OLED hardware-level privacy technology. Built on a proprietary underlying "dual-pixel per zone" design, it integrates two completely independent light-emitting units and driver circuits within the same pixel area on the screen:

In sharing mode, wide-angle pixel units operate normally, emitting light across all angles so visuals remain clear and transparent when multiple people view the screen together.

In privacy mode, the system switches to illuminate narrow-angle pixel units, projecting light almost vertically so that a complete image can only be viewed at a 0° direct frontal angle. Light at tilted angles beyond 30° is physically restricted: luminance at a 45° side angle drops to only 1.6% of the frontal view, and down to 0.15% at a 60° side angle, leaving the display looking virtually all black to onlookers.

In terms of privacy protection, the new model delivers significantly improved anti-peeping performance, satisfying heightened user demands for confidentiality.

The company has set a clear strategic upgrade direction for gaming performance. Previously, due to chipset performance bottlenecks, gaming was not treated as a core selling point. However, with the iterative performance gains of the 905 Pro chip, the company plans to increase investments in gaming performance. Because gaming scenarios demand extreme processing power, enhancing gaming performance is intended to further unlock hardware potential and satisfy a broader spectrum of user needs.

  1. What specific upgrades were made to the second-generation Tiangong hinge system?

MateXT2 underwent a major architectural overhaul, transitioning from the previous single-hinge layout to a dual-hinge structure. This core modification addresses durability concerns regarding inner-screen protection on the prior generation, substantially lowering the risk of screen scratching and back panel damage. Through this architectural upgrade, the device achieved breakthroughs in durability and reliability. XT2 is equipped with the second-generation hinge system, adopting a synchronized linkage design with dual hollow central shafts and dual independent flex cables. Compared to the previous generation, this system achieves a lighter weight while delivering superior crease control and suppression. This architectural iteration serves as the primary technical foundation supporting folding screen reliability.

  1. What is the specific operating mechanism behind the second-generation Tiangong hinge system?

Mate XT2 delivers outstanding ergonomics: thanks to optimized folding morphology, its center of gravity is positioned more centrally, making handling feel more natural than the previous-generation slide-out layout. In addition, the dual-hinge design offers advantages in configuration consistency, effectively optimizing overall production costs.

Mate XT2 employs a dual-hinge synchronized linkage scheme with five-arc primary shafts. Driven by the linkage of dual hollow primary shafts and dual independent flex cables, it ensures efficient inter-hinge coordination. In terms of materials, ultra-high-strength specialty steel is introduced, significantly boosting impact resistance and stability across repeated folding cycles. An internal buffer cam structure was added to achieve precise control over the folding force curve; this represents the core technical pillar for further suppressing screen creases, delivering noticeable improvements in folding tactile feel and hovering stability over the prior generation.

Through a left-and-right two-stage hinge design, Mate XT2 achieves damping consistency throughout the entire motion range, significantly improving hand feel and hovering stability at arbitrary angles. Mechanically, the new layout sharply reduces structural slack in both fully unfolded and closed states, effectively eliminating wobbling and abnormal noises. On durability, the hinge system has passed 1,000,000 opening and closing cycles, ensuring long-term operational reliability.

  1. How well does Mate XT2 adapt to HarmonyOS 7?

Mate XT2 runs on HarmonyOS 7, which prioritizes large-screen adaptability, supporting native tri-fold display and independent three-screen multitasking. In terms of professional tool adaptation, CAD, PS, and CapCut have achieved deep adaptation and perform well. However, short-form video apps (such as Douyin, Bilibili, and Kwai) currently feature limited adaptation, mainly relying on stretched scaling rather than deep adaptation. Furthermore, gaming video applications pose ongoing performance challenges on folding screens due to high CPU utilization; overall ecosystem maturity depends on close coordination with third-party app developers.

Mate XT2 deploys a 30B-parameter LLM on-device, realizing multi-agent collaboration powered by Celia Assistant. Functional capabilities span AI document processing (including document comparison and annotation) alongside multimodal comprehension (such as OCR and AI photo retouching). Management stated that these on-device AI features currently run with solid stability across the system, aiming to boost productivity and efficiency in business and office environments.

  1. What are the core on-device AI capabilities of Mate XT2 at present, and how do they compare against competitors?

Core on-device AI capabilities primarily center on several fronts: first, document processing capabilities, such as multi-document comparisons and document retrieval across various formats; second, AI photo retouching and AI video editing functions; third, various AI applications in office collaboration scenarios. In addition, they encompass specialized derivative applications driven by AI, such as a feature that evaluates anti-peeping risks based on user habits and behavioral traits, as well as "Hongfeng Imaging" technology that utilizes AI for photographic color reproduction, which shares underlying scene and image recognition capabilities with AI image generation. While the underlying HarmonyOS development framework enables faster response times and better data performance when invoking apps and low-level ports, current AI feature implementations still lag behind certain competitors (such as the Doubao phone) in terms of overall impressiveness.

  1. What stage of development are Huawei's on-device AI capabilities currently in?

Currently, on-device AI capabilities remain at an early stage. On one hand, model parameter sizes remain modest, hovering in the tens of billions (around 30B). On the other hand, while the broader industry emphasizes foundational model capabilities, vertical implementations on smartphones still face two core challenges: first, user permissions remain restricted, as executing certain actions or apps is constrained by app permissions and user authorizations; second, many application scenarios remain fragmented. Today's common standardized procedural tasks, such as ordering takeout or booking tickets, are insufficient to demonstrate the standalone value of on-device AI. Truly challenging capabilities—such as dynamic annotations or live prompts generated from real-time screen visuals and information—are not yet mature. This requires ongoing foundation model iterations and broader expansion across application scenarios; future development will likely concentrate more heavily on office productivity and personal assistant use cases.

  1. Why has the progress of mobile on-device AI in office scenarios been relatively slow?

Sluggish progress stems mainly from several factors. First, office applications demand substantial computing power, whereas on-device hardware performance faces inherent limitations; although hybrid device-cloud collaborative schemes exist (where the on-device module parses intent and queries the cloud alongside local data), on-device performance and scenario constraints remain pronounced. Second, mainstream enterprise productivity applications (such as Doubao Work, DingTalk, and Feishu) typically mandate retaining portions of data locally, introducing dual challenges for smartphones: first, built-in storage space on phones is limited; second, hosting heavy office workloads locally on mobile devices is unrealistic, restricting phones to light office tasks. Taken together, smartphones are poised to serve primarily as portals or terminals in future enterprise environments, incapable of handling heavy office computing.

  1. What is the architecture of the 9050 Pro chip?

Mate XT2 debuts the 905 0Pro chip, which adopts an innovative logic folding architecture. The chip is configured with 9 CPU cores and a Da Vinci architecture NPU, supporting dynamic module scheduling across diverse usage scenarios to achieve granular power consumption control. In terms of GPU rendering prowess, the chip significantly boosts real-time ray tracing and ultra-large video rendering performance, providing hardware support for gaming and content creation.

  1. What is the performance profile of the 9,050 chip, and where does it stand relative to industry-leading products?

The 9,050 chip delivers impressive performance under its 3-4nm process equivalent. Compared to the previous-generation chip, its CPU single-core performance gained over ~20%, multi-core performance improved by 50%, and GPU rendering performance more than doubled. Comprehensive overall device performance improved by roughly 40%, demonstrating competitive strength. While it cannot currently match Apple's A-series or Qualcomm's latest-generation flagship offerings, it benchmarks favorably against their flagship chipsets from two years ago, trailing top-tier overseas performance levels by approximately two generations overall.

  1. What subsequent improvement roadmaps are planned to address the chip performance gap relative to overseas

competitors?

Improvement plans follow two main directions. On one hand, the chip adopts an entirely new architectural scheme that retains substantial headroom for optimization. Moving forward, as manufacturing processes mature, computational performance for the CPU and GPU can be further scaled by increasing core counts. On the other hand, systematic engineering optimizations will be implemented, focusing heavily on battery life and thermal dissipation. Regarding battery endurance, alongside system-level power consumption control, substantial new customized solutions will be incorporated. Regarding heat dissipation, more advanced cooling technologies will be integrated, such as diamond vapor chambers or micro-pump liquid cooling solutions.

  1. What is the current supply status of the 9050 Pro chip, and which product lines will it primarily serve?

Supply of the 9050 Pro chip remains tight, with production capacity relatively constrained. This is largely driven by severe capacity bottlenecks across both front-end wafer tape-out and back-end packaging stages. Beyond currently deployed folding screen models, the chip will subsequently be featured in the Mate 90 series.

  1. What does the cost breakdown look like for Mate XT2, and which components represent the largest shares?

High-cost bill-of-materials components primarily span four categories. First is the display module—covering inner and outer screens, hinge, and UTG—which collectively accounts for approximately 25% to 30% of total cost. Second is the hinge mechanism itself, comprising roughly 15%. Third is the SoC, accounting for around 10%. Finally, memory components represent nearly 20%. Mate XT2 commands a relatively high gross margin.

  1. What is the current supply and pricing landscape for memory components, and what counter-measures are in place?

Memory components remain in short supply, with procurement prices surging across upstream supply chains. Counter-measures focus on two tracks: first, advancing in-house proprietary storage solutions; second, executing long-term agreements with upstream manufacturers to lock in price points and stabilize overall costs. Although the upward price trend is irreversible in the short term, over the long term, securing stable material supply will render pricing considerably easier to control.

  1. Has the overall market performance of Mate XT2 met expectations?

Market performance of Mate XT2 has essentially met expectations, primarily attributable to two factors: first, rising memory component costs drove higher retail pricing for the finished product; second, consumer replacement cycles continue to lengthen, and a segment of users maintains a wait-and-see posture toward folding devices, particularly tri-fold form factors.
