On the surface, there is a lot about this Marvell-Lumentum deal that does not make sense. But if you look just a little deeper, there are points where the reasons actually become clear.

I think NVIDIA is deeply involved in this deal. And I believe this deal is a strong signal that the real optics war is now starting.

The figure below is the NVIDIA Marvell-Lumentum scenario as I see it.

The spots marked S1 to S4 on the map show where each of the four scenarios plays out, assuming NVIDIA set up this play.

These are all my guesses, but I think this is a good chance to check, one by one, the reasons for this deal that you cannot see from the surface.

Scenario 1. NVIDIA wanted to buy Lumentum

I think NVIDIA wanted Lumentum very badly.

But in other sense, NVIDIA cannot buy it directly.

This is because SAMR had already issued a preliminary finding in September 2025 that NVIDIA violated the conditions attached to its Mellanox acquisition.

So my guess is that NVIDIA tried to reach Lumentum’s competitive edge indirectly, through Marvell.

Once this deal closes, the partner that implements NVLink in customer chips will also make the lasers itself.

NVIDIA only needs to line up chip connectivity, laser specs and production schedules with Marvell. Marvell, too, becomes a company that holds both optical interconnect design and laser volume, in a market where lasers are sold out through early 2029.

So what is in it for Lumentum?

Lumentum spreads a business concentrated in a single product, lasers, across an entire chip design business. Lasers are sold out through early 2029 for now, but Lumentum must have worried about the point when supply rises and demand turns down.

I think Lumentum judged that this structure would keep its risk as small as possible once the sell-out ends. It is also Lumentum admitting that the era of CPO as the mainstream is coming.

Scenario 2. Google TPU gets a second full-stack supplier besides Broadcom

The reason Broadcom is such a strong partner for Google is that it designs the TPU and also supplies the networking parts for the racks those chips go into.

Marvell already works with Google. Under a July agreement it develops Google’s inference accelerators, storage controllers, network interface controllers, memory interface controllers and near-memory compute chips. Lumentum supplies lasers and OCS and Google uses OCS to tie thousands of TPUs together.

So what this Marvell - Lumentum deal mean to Google? Google’s bargaining power with the companies it hands its custom chips to can change accordingly because now Google have 2 full stack ASIC vendor, Broadcom and Marvell in the end.

For NVIDIA, this is very welcome.

It checks its competitor Broadcom and puts one more company with NVIDIA money in it inside Google’s TPU supply chain.

Marvell and Lumentum can become more competitive for the same reason.

In addition, the Marvell warrant, which lets Google buy more shares as its purchases pile up, gives Google a good reason to increase Marvell’s volume. Like AMD and Meta Deal.

Scenario 3. Custom XPU scale-up may move to NVLink

One reason hyperscalers build their own AI chips is to reduce their dependence on NVIDIA GPUs.

So what is NVIDIA’s strategy against this?

It is to take the coming scale-up market.

If NVIDIA cannot replace customers’ XPUs with GPUs, it wants to claim the network that ties those XPUs together in the scale-up era.

That is the picture NVIDIA is drawing.

With Lumentum on board, Marvell can propose, from the moment a customer sets the specs for its next XPU, which sections use copper and where optics begin, and when and how many lasers it will supply.

For customers like Google that have their own fabric, it sells optics that fit ICI.

For customers that have not yet chosen how to connect, it offers an NVLink design that competes with Broadcom’s Ethernet.

In fact, AWS has decided to use NVIDIA’s NVLink Fusion in its next-generation Trainium4 chip and Marvell is already working with NVIDIA on applying NVLink Fusion to customer XPUs.

For Lumentum too, the scale-up segment moving to light is a much bigger market than scale-out.

Lumentum’s lasers are already high quality.

But with competitors such as Chinese makers steadily catching up, I think Lumentum calculated that rather than fight on unit price, it was better to bundle everything from chips to lasers as a full stack and lock in all the scale-up volume ahead.

Scenario 4. The last piece of NVIDIA’s NVHBM

The last piece of this NVIDIA scenario, as I see it, is NVHBM.

NVHBM is the custom HBM standard NVIDIA unveiled in August. HBM itself is a severe bottleneck right now, and Marvell wants to solve it with optics. NVIDIA is effectively betting on this.

Let me walk through my own speculative scenario.

If an ASIC design customer picks NVHBM, Marvell can propose everything at once, from a design that places more HBM in one package to the optical engines and lasers other accelerators need to read that data.

If this picture is completed, NVHBM can become NVIDIA’s commodity HBM in the true sense, a standard HBM that everyone uses.

Marvell and Lumentum have no reason to refuse.

With NVIDIA’s backing, Marvell can sell Celestial AI’s optical memory interconnect products, which it acquired, far more reliably. Lumentum adds a new source of demand, optical engines inside the package, on top of its existing transceiver and CPO switch markets.

The order in which the four scenarios show up

The figure above is just my estimate, and not official plan. splitting each scenario into a setup phase and the period when it shows up as actual revenue or product.

I think one of the reason for the market’s discount on Marvell is this time gap.

So we need to check this 4 watchlist first.

  1. Celestial AI revenue actually starts in the second half of 2027
  2. Google chip qualification is done by the November 2027 deadline
  3. SAMR attaches conditions
  4. Lumentum customers move laser orders to other suppliers such as Coherent before the close.

If the first two come through, the assumption that NVIDIA set up this play gains support from the numbers. If the last two go badly, half of this play will remain only a picture even after 2028.

Personally, I think NVIDIA has been drawing this picture deliberately for a long time, and I see a high probability that all of these scenarios play out. We will have to keep tracking what happens from here, but from this perspective, the current Marvell sell-off could be a real opportunity. If you believe in NVIDIA’s vision, the stock that I think can capture even more upside than NVIDIA itself is, so far, Marvell.
