SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined competition in benefits administration, with emphasis on Alight’s retention prospects, service challenges, pricing, and AI capabilities. The expert expects the 2027 request-for-proposal market to at least match 2026 activity and potentially exceed it as clients on short extensions reconsider their providers.

Service quality remains the primary reason employers switch administrators. Technology and price matter, but dependable account teams, responsive support, and consistent service-center performance generally have greater influence. Large employers also conduct extensive diligence because changing providers is costly and disruptive.

Key Insights

The greatest competitive threat is an employer deciding not to switch. However, the expert expects most organizations entering the market in 2027 to make an active selection. Deep discounts are being used to retain clients and win large accounts, but the expert considers this strategy unsustainable over the long term. Employers increasingly want to share in savings generated by AI, claims analytics, care navigation, and operational efficiencies. AI should strengthen retention by improving self-service, call avoidance, and resolution quality. It is less likely to reduce switching barriers enough to become a major customer-acquisition driver. Alight may improve retention and secure some new business in 2027, but the expert believes it could take another two to three years to regain its former competitive strength. Alight’s multiple legacy systems and reliance on third-party technology may slow the deployment of seamless AI capabilities across its portfolio. B-Swift was identified as one of the strongest AI-enabled competitors, particularly in participant support and implementation automation.
