SUMMARY

Overview

A youth-marketing expert discussed how Gen Z consumers approach holiday shopping, wellness, fashion, resale, experiences, and brand engagement. Affordability increasingly shapes purchases, although consumers still seek premium quality, self-expression, and social-media appeal. Peer-led creator content is replacing formal gift guides, while in-person activations and digital campaigns work best when integrated.

Key Insights

Gen Z holiday demand should remain resilient, supported by older members’ disposable income and younger members receiving gifts from relatives. Wellness influences beauty, food, technology, and experiences. However, stated preferences often conflict with behavior, especially regarding tanning, energy drinks, sustainability, and fast fashion. Beauty spending is shifting toward skincare, active ingredients, shorter routines, and products combining cosmetic and skincare benefits. Consumers may buy fewer items but pay more for perceived quality. Resale benefits from affordability, sustainability, natural-fiber interest, uniqueness, and the enjoyment of bargain hunting. It can coexist with off-price retail because each serves a different purpose. Athletic footwear weakness likely reflects full closets and constrained budgets more than declining interest in wellness. Gen Z increasingly favors fragmented footwear choices over dominant trends. Large incumbents should remain relevant but may lose category share to specialist brands. In-person shopping, concerts, travel, malls, and brand activations are gaining importance. Music and travel receive greater priority than costly sporting events. TikTok and YouTube remain the leading digital channels. Smaller creators are increasingly more effective than celebrities because they offer stronger engagement and credibility.
