Last week, we argued that the medium-term questions facing AI could coexist with a near-term rebound after the sharp selloff. In the first half of the week we saw a sharp rebound, which was later interrupted. We would keep the same distinction between time horizons: a recovery in share prices does little to resolve the gap between ambitious infrastructure spending and the revenue needed to support it. Jensen Huang’s departure adds another reason to look more carefully at how that spending gets financed.

As we discuss in this week’s NVDA deep dive, Huang’s role extended well beyond selling GPUs. NVIDIA’s equity investments, capacity backstops and credit support helped Neolabs and Neoclouds raise capital and commit to expansion. A more conservative approach under new leadership could make the medium-term constraints we discussed last week more visible. The first changes may show up in new investments and financing commitments over the next one or two quarters, with consequences for deployment and hardware demand emerging later. Strong near-term GPU demand can coexist with a less supportive funding environment. We think the market needs to scrutinize who is financing the next round of capacity, on what terms, and how much spending depends on continued support from NVIDIA.

This week also brought more evidence of the security and operational risks surrounding increasingly connected software. Fireworks’ security incident exposed data from some Notion workspaces, illustrating how an AI infrastructure vendor’s breach can reach customers of the applications built on it. Separately, the prolonged Microsoft 365 outage disrupted essential business services. Microsoft attributed the outage to a network upgrade issue exposed by a subsequent change, and said it was unrelated to cyberattacks or AI/agents. It does, however, underline how much business activity depends on a small number of shared services.

These developments reinforce our focus on the cost of securing and maintaining the systems that AI increasingly relies on. As AI expands software development and accelerates vulnerability discovery, enterprises face more work in remediation, identity management, third-party oversight and continuous protection. We see a firmer basis for sustained cybersecurity demand, even while the funding and returns on parts of AI infrastructure remain uncertain. The timing still matters: our latest checks point to security-spending growth among covered customers rising from 10–12% in 2026 to 13–15% in 2027, with much of the immediate response funded through existing budgets. Our PANW preview provides an early read on how that demand is reaching established security platforms.

Deep | DELL: Direct ODM Procurement and Fading Memory Benefits Pressure Margins

Our deep dive examines how large customers’ shift toward direct ODM procurement could limit Dell’s role in AI servers. We expect ISG operating margin to peak at 12.6% in FY27 before declining to 10.8% in FY28 and 10.2% in FY29 as memory benefits fade and lower-margin AI servers take a larger share of revenue.

Review | AMAT FY26Q4: DRAM Strength Supports Another Year of Expansion

Revenue of $10.41bn and non-GAAP EPS of $4.16 exceeded consensus, though both were below our preview estimates of $10.57bn and $4.22. DRAM revenue nearly doubled, and management expects 2027 WFE growth above 30%, with AMAT positioned to outgrow the market.

Preview & Review | IFX FY26Q4: Data Center Guidance Reaches the Upper End of Expectations

Revenue of €4.706bn and SRM of 23.8% were broadly in line, while FY27 data center guidance of nearly €4bn landed at the upper end of the range discussed in our preview. Orders exceed that guidance and we retain our €4.4bn forecast, with automotive growth also providing a stronger-than-expected outlook.

Column | Adobe: AI Accelerates the Development of Competing Products

Our column examines how AI is lowering the cost and time required to build alternatives to Adobe products, using ArtCraft’s expanding suite of open-source tools as a case study. Acrobat’s contribution to incremental revenue makes renewals and pricing an important test, with FY26Q4 the first quarter to watch.
