AI: Enterprise usage shifts toward cheaper open-weight systems – FT

FT reports open-weight models handled 56% of tokens on Vercel’s AI Gateway in August, up from 7% in December. AT&T now uses open models for about 40% of AI workloads and aims for 70% within a year. Tinder has begun routing some queries to open models after its annualized AI spending rose from $1M in January to $10M in July. Executives cite cost, customization and control over proprietary data as reasons for the shift.

Foundries / DB HiTek / UMC: Price increases spread as mature-node capacity tightens – The Elec / Commercial Times

The Elec reports DB HiTek raised foundry prices in March and September, with increases of 5–30%; Chinese customers faced 20–30% hikes. Revenue benefits are expected in Q4 or early 2027, while water constraints limit expansion. Commercial Times reports selective increases at UMC, Vanguard and PSMC could broaden in 2027 as AI demand lifts orders for power-management and other mature-node chips. It cites TrendForce forecasting 90% average second-half utilization for leading foundries’ 8-inch capacity.

NVDA / DELL / SMCI / Wistron: Vera Rubin deliveries build toward Q4 ramp – Commercial Times

Commercial Times reports Supermicro delivered its first Vera Rubin NVL72 rack to SpaceX AI, while Dell began deliveries to CoreWeave in June. Further Q4 shipments from both OEMs are supporting Wistron’s AI-server orders, with visibility into next year. The report says GB300 and B300 orders also continue to arrive, and Wistron plans to extend Vera Rubin production to its Texas operation, which already supplies Blackwell-based servers.

NVDA: Launches hardware-backed controls for autonomous AI agents – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Nvidia introduced its Open Agent Safety Platform, combining two open-source security tools with its hardware. OpenShell runs on Vera CPUs to enforce access rules in real time, while the new Sentry software runs on BlueField processors to monitor agents and isolate suspicious activity within milliseconds. Nvidia says the system could have prevented the Hugging Face breach and would allow advanced models to be tested safely.

AAPL: Faces $5.7B patent verdict over iPhone and Watch haptics – Reuters

Reuters reports a US jury awarded Taction more than $5.7B after finding Apple’s Taptic Engine infringed two patents covering haptic feedback in iPhones and Apple Watches. Reuters calls it the largest US patent verdict to date. Apple disputes the finding and says it will appeal.

NU / Monzo: Nubank explores £8–10B acquisition – FT

FT reports Nubank is in early talks to acquire Monzo in a cash-and-stock deal valuing the UK digital bank at £8–10B, versus its £4.5B valuation in 2024. Monzo is also considering selling up to 15% to private equity or raising venture funding. FT says Monzo has 16M customers and generated £1.7B of revenue and £87.3M of pretax profit in the year to March

NXPI / Vanguard: Singapore fab capacity sells out; second plant under review – DigiTimes / Nikkei Asia

DigiTimes reports NXP’s Singapore fab with Vanguard is its largest investment since Freescale, with volume production due in Q1 2027 and capacity targeted at 44,000 wafers per month in 2029. Nikkei Asia says the $6.7B plant’s capacity is already sold out and the partners are evaluating a second fab. The facility will produce power-management and analog chips, as well as interposers for TSMC’s advanced packaging.

TikTok: Alabama settlement replaces first state teen-harm trial – Reuters

Reuters reports TikTok and ByteDance reached their first state settlement over claims of addictive design and misleading safety assurances, ahead of the Alabama trial scheduled for September 28. Alabama will receive at least $100M within 45 days, potentially rising to $300M if specified conditions are met.

China AI: Travel approval requirements expand to executives’ families – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports China is extending overseas-travel approval requirements to spouses and children of selected AI and chip executives whose work is considered important to state security. The measures broaden restrictions previously applied to leading private-sector AI personnel, including at Alibaba and DeepSeek. Agencies have begun notifying affected families, and Beijing plans to add more individuals over time.
