SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, with extensive experience in iGaming affiliate marketing, discussed how AI-powered search is reshaping customer acquisition and assessed a focal affiliate network’s competitive position, publisher strategy, and technology platform. The conversation also covered prediction markets as a potential new referral category.

Key Insights

Affiliate marketing is shifting beyond conventional SEO toward visibility in AI-generated search. The expert warned that publishers relying mainly on older SEO tactics are losing ground, with some struggling to remain viable. High-quality traffic is increasingly scarce, strengthening publishers’ negotiating power. Affiliates often prefer upfront CPA deals over revenue share, which can be uncertain and exposed to operator account closures. Operators are increasingly prioritizing in-house marketing, which the expert said is pressuring affiliate profitability. The expert viewed the focal network’s move away from paid traffic and toward organic publishers as a sound strategic decision. The main execution risk is convincing operators to work through networks when they want clear visibility into each publisher’s traffic quality. The expert expressed confidence that the network’s in-house platform could support expansion, but had not yet evaluated it firsthand. The ability to replace revenue from paid traffic remains uncertain. Prediction markets were seen as an additional affiliate opportunity rather than a major threat to sportsbooks. Regulatory uncertainty may make affiliates favor CPA over revenue share.

GP: How are the economics of the iGaming affiliate marketing industry evolving, particularly in terms of CPA versus revenue-share arrangements, customer acquisition costs, player lifetime value, operator commission structures and competition for high-quality traffic?

EXPERT: I think there's severe competition for high-quality traffic just because it's so rare these days. If you do have an affiliate that can really produce a lot of traffic, you're going to have to pay through the nose just because of all the competition and the narrowed sources of affiliate traffic. Most of the time, they're going to require a high CPA over rev share. The CPAs seem to get higher and higher just due to the competition. Those iGaming affiliates that can adapt and learn and send a lot of traffic are going to ask for high CPAs. The other thing with rev share is a lot of affiliates have gotten burned from operators on revenue share. They've had their accounts closed when they were on lifetime revenue share, and once their accounts are closed, they no longer get that revenue share. That sucks for a lot of affiliates. Oftentimes, they just want the CPA upfront. They also want the CPA upfront, especially the PPC affiliates, because they pay out of their own pocket to send traffic to begin with. They want to account for that and can only do that really by knowing what CPA or exact commission they are going to receive before spending any of their own money and before receiving a commission for that traffic.

GP: How are operators' marketing budgets and commission terms changing as they become more selective, and what has that meant for affiliate revenue per referred player and overall affiliate profitability?

EXPERT: A lot of operators are wanting to spend money in-house on their own marketing efforts, particularly their own Google PPC budgets, mainly because they can get more ROI from their own efforts than asking affiliates for traffic that's usually very expensive. So I would say their budgets are much more based on what they can do in-house, and so that's meant affiliate revenue per referred player. I would say actually more their overall affiliate profitability has gone down just because the focus is switching more on internal marketing efforts.

GP: Following Raketech's decision to phase out its Paid Publisher Network, how sustainable is its transition towards organic publisher-led growth, and what are the key opportunities and execution challenges over the next 2-3 years?

EXPERT: I think that's a smart, calculated decision to phase out of the paid publisher network and transition to organic publisher-led growth because then you're relying on publishers that are up to date with AI, with getting traffic from the desired sources of traffic that they're looking for.
