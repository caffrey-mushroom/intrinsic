SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed DSV’s integration of DB Schenker, focusing on road-network stabilization, synergy delivery, freight- forwarding growth, IT migration and contract logistics. Road remains the principal challenge, while air and sea forwarding and contract logistics are performing more strongly. Technology, healthcare and Asian trade lanes appear better positioned for growth than European automotive freight.

Key Insights

The expert considers the expected road-volume recovery achievable but highly ambitious. Success requires customer retention, tender wins, SME and spot-volume recovery, reliable service, disciplined capacity additions and supportive market demand. Current road problems appear to represent delayed integration benefits rather than a fundamental structural failure. However, road recovery is viewed as the largest lever for 2027 earnings. Terminal consolidation must be coordinated with labor, dock and line-haul capacity. Adding capacity too late could recreate service disruption as volumes recover. DSV must integrate physical infrastructure, employees and IT systems while managing labor consultation and site closures. Execution failures in any one area could delay the broader plan. Technology and AI-related shipments are considered the clearest air and sea growth drivers. Healthcare, pharmaceuticals, intra- Asia traffic and Asia-to-Americas lanes also offer attractive demand and margins. DSV should withstand falling ocean rates better than smaller forwarders because of purchasing scale and value-added services, though it is not fully protected from lower gross profit per shipment. The hyperscaler logistics pipeline appears durable, but facility launches depend on sites, power, permits, capital spending, staffing and systems implementation.
