SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined the prospects for solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs) in data centers, particularly where grid connections are delayed. It compared Bloom Energy’s integrated manufacturing and project-delivery model with Ceres Power’s licensing approach, which relies on manufacturing partners. The expert emphasized that fuel cells could provide substantial power to some new AI campuses, but are unlikely to broadly displace grid electricity or gas turbines in the near term.

Key Insights

Bloom appears better positioned for early US deployments because of its delivery capability, service network, and major customer reference. Ceres partners could win selected projects, but will need local support, reliable delivery, warranties, and financing. The expert expects fuel cells to become a meaningful part of US data center power, while remaining a minority technology overall. At individual campuses with constrained grid access, they could supply most or all initial power. Customers are likely to prioritize power delivered on schedule, uptime, lifetime cost, and durability over carbon-capture readiness. Carbon capture could matter more for long-term gas-powered installations if transport and storage are practical. Permits and gas access can be major project schedule risks, particularly for large campuses. On-site storage, firm supply routes, and phased construction can help, but project-specific approval conditions may still affect cost and operating capacity. For high-voltage DC systems, the principal challenge is coordinating and qualifying the complete power architecture, including protection, converters, controls, and energy storage—not simply securing individual components.

Competitive Landscape

Company or group

Position and outlook

Bloom Energy

Stronger near-term US position through integrated manufacturing, installation, services, and a large customer agreement. Expansion into grid power and combined heat and power could support its European presence.

Ceres Power and licensees

Licensing can enable several manufacturers to scale, with partners developing regional production. Progress depends on factory readiness, customer references, local service, and project financing.

Doosan, Weichai, Delta, and other partners

Have announced manufacturing plans or regional routes to market. Announced capacity does not guarantee data center supply or completed projects.

Other power options

Engines and turbines may ease supply constraints, but fuel cells can offer continuous operation, black start capability, and an alternative to diesel backup.
