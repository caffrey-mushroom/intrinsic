Model capabilities race ahead of revenue, while permitting and customer budgets shape the next leg of AI spending

Nov 9

READ IN APP

After one of the most violent market selloffs in recent years, we want to take a step back and consider the AI sector across different time horizons.

Over the long term, we remain excited about AI’s potential. Frontier labs continue to make meaningful progress toward AGI, with larger pretrained models delivering substantial capability gains. As reinforcement learning scales and data accumulates, general-purpose models are also expanding into areas previously served by specialized models, including robotics and video and audio generation. In our conversations with leading researchers, optimism about reaching AGI in 2028 or beyond is higher than we have previously encountered.

The medium-term picture, through roughly 2027, is harder to assess. Much of the leading labs’ revenue still comes from AI startups and large technology companies. For startups dependent on venture funding, how sustainable is their spending if macro conditions and funding availability deteriorate? Large technology companies are already optimizing token consumption: where does their spending settle, and what would drive renewed acceleration? Meanwhile, our enterprise surveys have consistently pointed to slower adoption than the market had hoped for. Taken together, these trends raise the possibility of a meaningful gap between the spending growth investors expect and what customers ultimately deliver.

On the semiconductor and hardware side, we are hearing more reports from the Asian supply chain of slower deliveries and procurement schedules. These warrant attention, though they do not yet establish order cuts. Are they mainly the result of product transitions and deployment timing, or early signs of tighter spending discipline? And how resilient would infrastructure investment prove if financing conditions tightened further, particularly for neoclouds?

These medium-term questions can coexist with a near-term rebound. Following such a sharp selloff, even modest improvements in fourth-quarter revenue expectations could matter if share prices already discount a much weaker spending outlook. A strong model release could provide a more credible catalyst, particularly if better capabilities translate into stronger paid demand and more durable pricing. We are not making a market-direction call. We do think it is important to remain open-minded as expectations reset and new evidence emerges.

There are also new areas worth watching closely. Our latest space compute work is one bright spot: progress in thermal design, supply-chain orders and launch planning is making orbital compute a more concrete engineering proposition. We see a path to roughly 0.5 GW of on-orbit capacity by the end of 2028 in our base case, and approximately 1 GW in an optimistic scenario, with thermal validation and launch cadence still critical. Cybersecurity remains another area where we see substantial opportunity. AI is accelerating vulnerability discovery and increasing the demands on defense, remediation and governance. The need is growing, but the timing of monetization matters: more attacks will not necessarily translate into an immediate revenue uplift for listed security vendors.

Beyond the AI infrastructure trade, AppLovin and Unity are a reminder that opportunities remain elsewhere. We have followed both companies closely through several quarters, and our channel work has captured important shifts in their competitive dynamics and operating momentum. Their strong results and post-earnings rallies reinforce the value of that sustained research. We will continue looking for and tracking opportunities across our coverage, including those outside the market’s dominant AI narrative.

Premium Report Snapshot

A portion of our research is reserved for Premium subscribers and is not distributed via Substack. Below is a snapshot of what Premium subscribers received this week beyond the Substack feed. Subscribed Research | LLM: Step-Level Routing Could Pressure Frontier-Model Revenue

Our 20-task financial-research test achieved the same 95% first-pass accuracy with four-model routing as with Opus alone, at 28% lower cost. At 20–30% adoption, internal routing alone could reduce frontier-model API revenue by about 5–8% in our scenario analysis.

Deep | BTM Power: Policy Delays and Equipment Delivery Cycles

We see project deferrals as the main incremental risk from a prolonged Texas approval freeze, while keeping our 2027 forecasts for BE, INIO and FTAI unchanged. Equipment availability remains tight, with INIO slots extending into 2029–31; deliveries, capacity ramps and customer acceptance remain central to revenue recognition.

Review | LITE FY27Q1 & Marvell Acquisition: OCS and Laser Pricing Drive Growth

Lumentum reported $1.263bn of revenue and a 42.3% non-GAAP operating margin, with the next-quarter revenue guide implying 26.7% sequential growth. We see customer demand for laser capacity, joint chip-and-optics design and OCS as important drivers of the announced all-stock Marvell transaction, while approval, integration and customer procurement remain uncertain.

Preview & Review | COHR FY27Q1: PhotonLink Agreement Extends Demand Visibility

Revenue reached $2.370bn, above our preview range of $2.28–2.35bn, while the next-quarter guide of $2.65bn also exceeded our roughly $2.6bn expectation. A roughly $20bn, five-year PhotonLink supply agreement and a target for quarterly gross margin above 45% by end-2028 extend visibility, but roughly $870m of negative quarterly free cash flow highlights the cost of expansion.

Preview & Review | AMD 26Q3: Management Raises 2027 Server CPU Growth Outlook to About 100%

Revenue of $13.4bn and non-GAAP EPS of $2.09 exceeded our preview estimates of $13.29bn and $2.01, though the $17.0bn Q4 revenue guide was below our $17.36bn forecast. Management raised its 2027 server CPU growth outlook to about 100% and total revenue growth to above 80%, with MI455X and Helios shipments ramping.

Review | NET 26Q3: Platform Cross-Selling Supports Faster Growth

Revenue growth accelerated from 36% to 39%, well above the roughly 31% implied by guidance, as Workers usage drove faster pool consumption and early renewals. NET added 308 large customers and DBNRR reached 122%, with developer-platform and security cross-selling becoming more visible.

Review | FSLY 26Q3: Growth Slows Outside the Top Ten Customers

Revenue rose 20% to $190.2m, but growth outside the top ten customers slowed from 12% to 8%, increasing reliance on the largest accounts. Adjusted operating margin reached 16.4%, while we reduced our 2027 revenue estimate to about $895m; our checks point more toward customer acquisition and product breadth than broad weakness in CDN or security demand.

Review | TSEM 26Q3: Long-Term Orders Extend Through 2029

Revenue of $526m, a 34% gross margin and a $600m midpoint for Q4 revenue guidance exceeded expectations. The Japan 300 mm Track I ramp has moved forward to full production in 27Q3, with silicon-photonics orders extending through 2029, while integrated-laser adoption and longer-term competition remain key variables.

Review | Kioxia FY27Q2: Stronger Mobile Demand Meets a Softer Guide

Mobile demand exceeded expectations, but the FY27Q3 outlook implies flat sequential bit shipments and only 6% blended ASP growth amid enterprise-SSD delays and slower mix improvement. CY27 capacity is fully booked and more than half of CY28 output is covered by long-term agreements, supporting visibility beyond the near-term shipment issues.

Review | Ibiden FY27Q2: CPU Demand Drives the Beat

Revenue of ¥139.3bn and operating profit of ¥33.2bn exceeded expectations as CPU demand and substrate pricing improved. New investment of ¥151bn is focused mainly on CPU capacity and customer prepayments have risen, while unchanged FY31 guidance remains conservative in our assessment.

Preview | AMAT FY26Q4: Orders Remain Visible Into 2028

We estimate revenue of $10.57bn and EPS of $4.22, versus consensus of $10.27bn and $4.06, supported by DRAM, advanced logic and advanced packaging. Channel checks indicate order visibility into 2028, with strong 2027 DRAM equipment demand and the 4F² transition expanding the addressable market.

Preview | RDW 26Q3: Defense Supports Growth as Space Orders Await Conversion

We forecast $127m of revenue, up 23%, a 24.5% gross margin and roughly $3.5m of negative adjusted EBITDA. Defense Tech supports the quarter, while an estimated book-to-bill of about 0.9 and backlog of roughly $530m keep the focus on replacement space orders as ISS ROSA winds down.
