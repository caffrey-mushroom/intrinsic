Coming back to research, Google’s return to frontier competition gives us another reason to stay constructive on compute demand. We argued in August that Google would keep investing despite the management departures and delayed model release; Gemini 4 now supports that view. Our work on open-weight models points in the same direction: as access to full reasoning traces tightens, developers will need to spend more on their own reinforcement learning, synthetic data and training environments. The recent low-cost catch-up looks difficult to sustain, while several well-funded labs remain in the race.

Micron puts longer-term demand into a more tangible frame. More than 75% of its CY27 output is already committed through SCAs and advance purchase orders, and customer discussions are shifting toward CY28 allocation. That supports our view of a higher, longer memory-price plateau. The headline gross-margin guide looked softer than the buy-side bar, but our adjustment for incentive compensation and new-fab costs puts it above expectations. We continue to focus on how long the supply shortage lasts.

Optics stocks also responded to the prospect of a more workable FCC framework, particularly Lumentum and Coherent. There is no public draft yet: the reported 65% U.S.-company BOM threshold remains a scenario under discussion. If adopted, it could support U.S. component suppliers while allowing leading Chinese module makers to keep serving customers. Our ECOC conversations separately reinforced the NPO ramp and the search for lower-power optical links; in power generation, FTAI’s first Mod-1 deliveries and customer acceptance in Q4 are the next concrete tests of another route to faster data-center energization.

Premium Report Snapshot

A portion of our research is reserved for Premium subscribers and is not distributed via Substack. Below is a snapshot of what Premium subscribers received this week beyond the Substack feed.

Deep | LLM: Computer Use and the Scope for White-Collar Automation

Subscribed

We estimate that computer use could eventually add about $1tn of annual revenue for AI providers, with automatable work equivalent to roughly 180mn full-time jobs, or 18% of global white-collar employment. These are estimates of work hours, not a forecast of layoffs; early adoption is more likely to reallocate tasks. Reliability, compliance and the Windows experience remain constraints, with administrative and operational workflows the most immediate opportunities.

Research | Muse and Consumer Cybersecurity: Mapping the Security Value Chain

Meta’s Muse shifts consumer identity from “person + devices” to “person + personal agent + sub-agents,” but Meta’s Sentinel captures much of the internal runtime/security layer, leaving third-party upside in external authorization, governance and recovery. We identify that OKTA, SAIL and PANW/CyberArk are exposed mainly to Step 4 Authorization and Step 9 audit/revocation as agents move from read to act to transact. Directionally positive but early; monetization depends on agent volume and IAM extension into consumer-agent permissions.

Deep | Meta Muse: The Software Companies Behind Its Actions, Part 2 — Private Companies

We identify connections to 21 private software companies across finance, payments, travel, productivity, development, health and device control; ten have documented workflows and eleven are confirmed connections only. Growing Muse usage could bring more API calls and transaction activity, but a connection alone does not establish a commercial agreement, usage frequency or incremental revenue.

Research | Servers: Power and Cooling Content Keeps Rising Through 2028

Our supply-chain work points to higher power and cooling content across NVIDIA VR300, Google TPU v9 and AWS Trainium4, supporting our constructive view of Delta and Infineon. We expect VR300 shipments in 27Q3, with double-sided cold-plate ASPs around twice current levels, while TPU v9 could carry roughly $400–500 of VPD content per chip. Trainium4 may enter trial production after 27Q3 with VPD and full liquid cooling, with Infineon returning to its supply chain.

Research | Space: Suncatcher Prototype Reaches Orbit

Our note reports that Google’s first Suncatcher prototype launched on October 1, carrying four Trillium TPUs for orbital testing. Radiation and thermal-control data over the next few weeks, including cooling intervals after roughly fifteen minutes of operation, are the immediate tests, followed by a planned two-satellite laser link in 2027. Launch demand and potential Planet orders offer nearer-term business opportunities, while profitable commercial orbital computing remains a much longer-term prospect, more likely in the early 2040s.

Astra: Computer-Use Coverage and Deployment Economics

Engineer, AI research and deployment lab

The expert estimates coverage of about 70% of the top hundred enterprise applications, but only 30%–35% achieving task success above 90%, while a tested two-tier architecture cut token use from roughly 140k–150k to just over 30k.

Anthropic: Model Economics and the Computer-Use Opportunity (AMZN, GOOGL, NVDA)

Executive, Top AI Research Firm

The expert sees end-to-end agents expanding the opportunity beyond coding and Astra slightly ahead in interface-heavy tasks, while cautioning that capability does not guarantee revenue and some compute and lifetime-revenue estimates are inconsistent.

Anthropic: Model Tiers and Continued Frontier Investment (AMZN)

Executive, Global Leader in AI Systems Provider

The expert describes mid-tier models as the main monetization engine, with frontier models supporting retention, faster research and future lower-cost offerings despite weaker standalone economics.

Nuvei: AI Spending Under Compliance Constraints

Executive, Global Leader in Payment Solutions Provider

The expert estimates monthly AI spend rose from zero to $200k in six months through Copilot subscriptions and expects the next $100k of monthly spending to come from APIs, with adoption unmeasured and compliance, transaction risk and data readiness still limiting deployment.

FTAI: Mod-1 Production Ramp and Reliability (FTAI)

Specialist, Leading Aerospace Manufacturer

The expert considers annual output of one hundred units operationally feasible, with component availability and resource allocation the main constraints, while establishing a proven reliability record could take about three years.

Robotics: Data Bottlenecks and Training Economics (CRWV, NBIS)

Executive, Leading AI Solution Provider

The expert identifies real-world data collection and labeling as the main bottlenecks and expects no vendor to ship more than ten thousand humanoids in 2027, with adaptation to general environments still three to five years away.

Nittobo: Specialty Glass-Fabric Shortages and Capacity Expansion

Executive, Top chemical manufacturer

The expert expects low-dielectric and low-thermal-expansion glass shortages to persist because weaving and finishing capacity takes time to expand, with Nittobo’s product mix and Taiwan Glass qualifications shaping the supply outlook.
