SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined Coca-Cola’s North American food-service business, retail portfolio and international distribution advantages. The expert viewed the KFC contract win from Pepsi as symbolically important but potentially limited by KFC’s domestic health and relatively small outlet base.

Beyond soda, restaurant operators increasingly use coffee, energy drinks, customized beverages and protein products to attract traffic between traditional meal periods. Coca-Cola’s challenge is to translate its brand and fountain strengths into these faster- growing categories while managing declining consumption of sugar-sweetened beverages.

Key Insights

The KFC conversion required more than two years of preparation and likely reflected Coca-Cola’s brand preference data, global relationship and willingness to offer stronger commercial terms. Coca-Cola remains highly advantaged in food service through Coke, Sprite and Dr Pepper. Sprite was identified as a particularly strong Gen Z and multicultural brand with potential to surpass Pepsi. Fairlife was considered Coca-Cola’s strongest growth asset. Added production should reduce supply constraints, while broader distribution offers more upside than additional flavors. Coca-Cola lacks a consistent hydration strategy. Repeated shifts among Powerade, Vitaminwater and BodyArmor have diluted investment and retailer messaging, while Gatorade maintains clearer positioning. The expert was highly skeptical that Pibb can challenge Dr Pepper. Recent gains may reflect added shelf placement or promotions rather than durable share capture. North American affordability, weak restaurant traffic and expanding beverage choice could gradually weaken Coca-Cola’s pricing power and consumer recruitment.
