Agenda

  • Planned capacity expansion progress in Ulanqab, regional policies and environmental advantages, conversion rate from contracted capacity to grid connection and commissioning, plus power supply and electromechanical delivery bottlenecks
  • Market share erosion pace on third-party IDCs (internet data centres) driven by energy players’ entry, power cost reduction potential via green power direct supply and 10-year PPAs (power purchase agreements)
  • Capital provider structures and IRR for projects under construction, plus case studies
  • IDC order demand and construction schedules from leading internet companies and foundational model developers in the Ulanqab region

Key Insights

  • According to expert statistics, 26 enterprises have acquired land of over 20,000 mu for large-scale data centres in Ulanqab. The ratio of capacity under construction to capacity grid-connected and operational is around 3:1, indicating considerable future capacity upside. However, three major factors, namely power capacity, grid tower spacing and green power supply, are slowing the pace of implementation
  • After acquiring land, internet giants mostly adopt the build-to-suit model, having third-party IDC operators build and lease the facilities; the proportion of self-built data centres is low. Prefabricated module technology is widely used in the industry to shorten delivery cycles
  • The core advantage of new energy companies entering the IDC industry is their access to low-cost green power, enabling them to offer lower power prices and win orders from tech giants. However, their lack of experience in IDC construction and O&M, coupled with relatively high project costs, makes them inclined to sell projects after completion to cash out
  • Data centres provide stable cash flow, but with intense supply-side competition, unlevered IRR is generally below 8%. Moreover, the short local effective construction window and rising construction standards are squeezing project profit margins
