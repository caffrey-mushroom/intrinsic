GOOGL: Google Nears Release of Flagship Gemini 4 AI Model

> The Information says Google is nearing the release of Gemini 4, with DeepMind chief Koray Kavukcuoglu saying the model has entered post-training and could launch “much earlier” than year-end. Google is already seeing encouraging internal results and plans to release an early post-training version quickly, then iterate rapidly. Kavukcuoglu said he is “100% confident” Google will remain at the frontier, with Gemini 4 already being used internally for its Antigravity coding tool. He also noted DeepMind’s model roadmap is informing Google’s next two to three generations of TPUs.

Release looking more like end of October now…

P: FY28 outlook is well above Street at Investor day

P guided FY28 revenue to $7.0–7.3B, +39–45% y/y, vs. consensus ~$6.2B, putting the midpoint ~15% above Street and accelerating from ~37–38% growth expected in FY27. FY28 operating income is guided to $1.7–1.9B, implying 24–26% margins vs. 21.3% consensus (+370bps at midpoint), with management targeting a 50–70 Rule of 40 score through FY30.

Mgmt highlighted TAM expansion beyond core enterprise storage: Pure sees TAM nearly doubling from $108B in 2026 to $207B by 2030 and serviceable SAM from $34B to $69B, driven by Core/Core AI plus Hyperscale, Modern Data Software and Scale AI. The new growth vectors are expected to reach ~20% of revenue by FY30, while subscription ARR is targeted at ~20% CAGR and RPO at ~25% CAGR.

OKTA: Oktane highlights agentic identity as a potentially major new growth vector, with monetization starting to take shape

Management framed agentic identity as potentially one of the largest future categories in cybersecurity, with Okta for AI Agents able to sell independently of a customer’s existing workforce identity provider, opening the door to Microsoft Entra environments. Agent SSO is included in core SSO for free, creating a wedge to upsell paid discovery, lifecycle governance and runtime controls as adoption scales. Early customer conversations are already expanding from agent identity into governance, privileged access, workforce and customer identity. The Blueprint Alliance with AWS, CRWD, GOOGL, CRM/NOW and others aims to standardize how agents are discovered, permissioned and monitored across vendors. Execution also sounded healthier, with ~18 months of sales-model stability, high rep tenure and low attrition; key risks remain early adoption and rising competition from MSFT/CRWD.

AI Tokens / UBER: Model price cuts and efficiency gains keep customer costs in check – The Information

> The Information reports Uber kept AI-token spending broadly flat over the past three to four months despite wider use, helped by memory optimization, company-specific data and stronger open-source models. Memory: Phison sees lasting shortages; Raspberry Pi flags a possible NAND price peak – Commercial Times / Bloomberg

Taiwan’s Commercial Times reports Phison CEO Pan Jiancheng still sees severe DRAM and NAND undersupply, with shortages potentially extending through 2029 or longer if AI demand keeps growing. He argues Chinese capacity additions would help fill the deficit rather than create oversupply, as AI infrastructure and edge devices require more memory and storage. Pan also warns higher memory prices could weigh on PC and smartphone demand over the next one to two years. Bloomberg, meanwhile, reports Raspberry Pi’s CEO says it remains too early to declare the overall memory supercycle over, but NAND prices may be approaching a peak.

AAPL / OpenAI: ChatGPT integration underperformed, OpenAI says in court filings – FT

AI Infrastructure: $10.3T buildout projection highlights debt and resource constraints. Is now officially the biggest “economic bet” in the “history of the country” (“never before has the US economy been so dependent on the build-out of a single industry”) – WSJ

> The WSJ reports economist Stijn van Nieuwerburgh projects $10.3T of US data-center and AI-infrastructure investment during 2025–32, averaging 3.6% of GDP annually, while cautioning actual spending could be materially lower. FactSet estimates put five major hyperscalers’ capex at $4.2T over the four years ending 2029. The WSJ highlights a growing reliance on debt and opaque financing structures, alongside shortages of electricity, labor and equipment. The report emphasizes the economy’s increasing exposure to AI spending and the consequences if anticipated revenues fail to cover financing costs.

DeepSeek: $1B revenue run rate as $7.5B fundraising targets October close – The Information

> The Information reports DeepSeek’s annualized revenue reached $1B, up from $400M–$500M in July, after August price increases of 2.3–4.5 times that CEO Liang Wenfeng said did not reduce its customer base. CXMT / Samsung / SK Hynix: CXMT reaches 9.5% DRAM share as HBM crowds out conventional supply – DigiTimes

CXMT’s monthly capacity is estimated near 300,000 wafers and could reach 360,000–380,000 by end-2027. Its new G5 process adds at least 50% more gross dies per wafer on an 8Gb comparison.

This extends Qualcomm’s existing flagship sourcing relationship rather than representing a new foundry switch. Samsung remains a potential future partner as it ramps second-generation 2nm mobile products in the second half of 2026 and targets double-digit second-half foundry revenue growth.

AMZN / MSFT: Free Quick Plus offer adds to growing AI subscription discounts – The Information

> The Information’s Martin Peers notes Amazon is giving marketplace merchants one year of its Quick Plus AI assistant free, alongside heavier Microsoft Copilot subscription discounts and broader promotions across AI providers. Microsoft’s discounted seats still carry usage-based features, so lower subscription prices can encourage spending elsewhere. In his commentary, Peers argues customers increasingly expect AI to replace existing software costs rather than add to them, challenging suppliers’ monetization plans as infrastructure spending rises.

Healthcare AI: Blue Cross study links AI-assisted billing to $942M of added spending – Reuters

> Reuters reports a Blue Cross Blue Shield Association study links more intensive inpatient billing to $942M of additional insurer spending in 2024–25 relative to 2023, including $653M from secondary-condition claims. The association says AI record analysis and ambient documentation tools are identifying more billable diagnoses. In bowel-surgery cases, rising diagnosis complexity was not accompanied by comparable treatment increases, including blood transfusions for anemia.

OpenAI / Australia: Agent breached Medicare statistics portal; no evidence of patient-record access – Nikkei Asia

> Nikkei Asia reports an OpenAI research agent gained unauthorized access to Australia’s Medicare statistics portal on June 18, retrieving public and non-public information while researching healthcare. OpenAI discovered the incident in August and notified authorities in September, prompting government criticism of the delay. The accessed material included aggregated health statistics and internal filenames, with no evidence of patient-record access. OpenAI says the actions were unintended and is cooperating; three other Australian websites were accessed normally.
