Good afternoon. Another ugly day for the AI trade as QQQs -2.7% and semis -5.5% sold off from the open into the close and back to within 5% of post-election low, with software -2.2% dragged along. Almost everyone was invited to the selling party today — the only small pockets of green were in some app sw names.

In my conversations, AI semis sentiment is as bad as it’s been since this whole AI thing started back in ‘23. We went from one of the easiest trading environments ever seen (May/June) for bulls to one of the toughest in a span of a few months.

The AI trade was already muddied, difficult and full of concerns (Lab ARR trajectory, power supply, credit fears, ROI, etc.) and the Jensen situation added a lot of fuel to the fire in different ways: he was a driving force behind financing the buildout, he’s led deal-making with suppliers and managed a very complex supply chain, and was always pushing the leading edge with many of those same suppliers. So the read-throughs for the AI supply chain aren’t good. (We went deeper into read-throughs from Jensen leaving in our Thurs EOD Wrap here.)

Today, a couple things hit the group before the open, in addition to continued digestion of the negative ramifications of Jensen’s departure.

First, SemiAnalysis’ capex cuts were making the rounds even though they were out Friday: AI accelerator spend down 19%/28%/41%/40% for 2027-30 (2030 to $923B from $1.53T) on slower AI-lab revenue growth, tighter financing and data-center delays. In addition, they cited HBM pricing coming down and Feynman carrying a lower markup for their reasons for NVDA # cuts: SA has NVDA estimates coming down from 570B in CY27 to 390 in CY30, although the #s in their charts seem to point volume instead of price:

More info on the SA note on X here and here.

SA’s Capex numbers now more inline with where street is:

Second, BIS issued an interim final rule on remote access: a DC anywhere in the world now needs a license once it provides more than 12M TPP of aggregate compute to China-hq’d companies (indirect access included), & Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil were added to chip-export licensing.

It didn’t get better during the day as press reported NVDA board not considering bringing Jensen back despite Trump saying a return “certainly isn’t a bad idea.”

Not to be outdone by Elon’s neocloud jabs, Altman poured some more fuel on the trade with comments at Semafor when asked about his “double counting” comments last week to Samsung execs:

*“For a long time, there was basically one chip provider in the industry. And so when people were counting wafers, they could ask companies like ours what demand was going to be. And they could, I think, get a sense of where end user demand for the chips they could make would be.”

“There are now many people making chips. And many of those people think they’re going to sell most of the volume to us and Anthropic and others. And we have some limited amount of demand and data centers and everything else. And I think there are many people making chips all talking to the silicon supply chain assuming that we’re going to buy all of their chips.”

“And I think the demand, the data center build out, that stuff is solid. But we’re not all gonna be able to or need to buy every… all of our supply from every chip vendor.”

Now, is it possible that the buildout can get ahead of how quickly the economy can digest it? Yes, that could happen. I don’t see that level of recklessness happening right now.*

Investors seem to be pretty bullish on AI. Things will go up and down. Prices will go up and down. You know, there will be periods of overbuild and underbuild.

ELSEWHERE

  • TTWO -6% sold off after being up 2% at the open, with most pointing to this SensorTower tracker showing 15M units sold so far

Why the negative read-through. Assuming buyside is expecting 45-50M units in year one, about 2/3 of year one units sell in week one. (GTA V sold about half its year-one units in 5 days, Cyberpunk about 75% in 10, Elden Ring and Zelda about 60%. GTA VI should be more viral than those). That makes 35M in week one. If preorders make up 55% of week one, 35M x 55% = 20M pre orders needed by launch to hit buyside bogeys. Thanks to Anon Investor in Slack for running through this math, although he also points out he thinks the bearish read is wrong as buyers of a digital games preload 1-2 days before launch so big days should show up the next few days and the ST Tracker is on a lag. Also, ST tracker extrapolates from a panel so not completely accurate. Bulls hoping we get a PR shortly after the launch over the weekend as Strauss said this on the latest earnings call:

So as soon as we have some experience post-launch, we’re going to be very happy to describe what’s going on and how we see it. There is really no need to update numbers right now.
