SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a former enterprise sales executive at Qualtrics, discussed how AI, pricing changes, and competition are affecting customer experience management software. Qualtrics’ business spans customer experience, employee experience, and market research. The expert sees the greatest pressure on market research and customer experience, while employee experience appears more resilient. A central concern is whether customers can collect, analyze, and visualize feedback using existing software, internal AI teams, or cheaper tools.

Key Insights

Market research faces the strongest commoditization risk. Basic surveys and data collection can be handled by lower-cost tools and AI. Qualtrics may retain an advantage when customers need access to specialized or hard-to-reach research audiences. Customer experience is exposed to both new vendors and internal alternatives. The expert described CIOs and procurement teams assessing whether existing software, in-house AI, and business intelligence tools can provide an adequate substitute. Customer value depends on acting on insights. Even when Qualtrics identifies improvement opportunities, customers must implement them. If the findings do not affect business results, it is difficult to justify a substantial software expense. Pricing changes and sales responsibilities contributed to renewal pressure. Moving to interaction-based pricing made customer usage more visible and enabled procurement-led reductions. Assigning renewals to sales teams also risked diverting time from new sales and account growth. The expert expects continued industry risk, but the pace is uncertain. It depends partly on whether large platforms such as Salesforce expand their built-in capabilities. Qualtrics could grow services revenue by bringing implementation and support work in-house, but doing so may strain partner relationships and require a cost-effective delivery model.
