TSM: Stronger revenue guide and AI demand, but N2 dilution misses buyside margin bogeys

TSM delivered a modest 3Q revenue/EPS beat and guided 4Q revenue to $52.3–54.3B, implying +14% q/q/+58% y/y at the midpoint, roughly 5–6% above Street, lifting FY26 revenue growth to ~44% from slightly above 40%. The disappointment was margins: 3Q GM of 66.4% missed the 67.5% buyside bogey, while the 4Q midpoint of 66% missed ~67%, reflecting the steep N2 ramp and overseas dilution. Mgmt’s AI commentary remained strong, with no slowdown, potential upside to accelerator growth and demand broadening into CPUs/networking. FY26 capex stayed at $60–64B despite expectations for a higher midpoint, although the multi-year expansion message became more aggressive.

Key quotes:

“all I can say right now is the AI demand is very -- extremely robust. Okay? And we did not see any sign of slowdown. Whether it’s high-50 CAGR, it doesn’t matter, it probably will be higher.

“And we also talked to one of our customer who tried to build a fab, Terafab, let me be more specific. And we discussed with them, see whether that we can be utilizing that actual capacity also. So we’re still in discussion. If we have any solid conclusion, we’ll share with you. But today, and that’s it, yes, from Arizona to Texas. Yes.”

Key Takeaways

  • AI demand. Mgmt sees no signs of slowing and said its mid-to-high-50% AI accelerator revenue CAGR for 2024–29 will “probably” be higher, without formally changing the forecast. Agentic AI and open-source models are lowering adoption barriers and driving more leading-edge silicon demand. CPUs are excluded from that narrow accelerator definition, but already contribute to TSM’s overall business; they should not be counted again as entirely incremental revenue.
  • Power checks. TSM is sending its own teams to inspect US data-center construction and power readiness, with feedback “so far so good” and CSPs well prepared. Capacity planning incorporates customer funding, power and component availability, and demand still materially exceeds supply after those constraints. Mgmt would not say whether the gap narrows in 2027 because customers keep increasing requirements
  • Power checks. TSM is sending its own teams to inspect US data-center construction and power readiness, with feedback “so far so good” and CSPs well prepared. Capacity planning incorporates customer funding, power and component availability, and demand still materially exceeds supply after those constraints. Mgmt would not say whether the gap narrows in 2027 because customers keep increasing requirements.
  • Capex. The unchanged FY26 budget was not a demand signal: mgmt emphasized 2026–28 investment will be “even more significantly higher” than the prior three years. Tool, material and labor inflation contribute, but long-term demand drives most of the spending; revenue growth should still outpace capex growth if execution proceeds as intended. No annual FY27/28 budgets were provided; bullish sell-side forecasts around $85–90B/$100B remain estimates, not guidance
  • Capacity. Nearly 20 fabs are under development globally, including 13 leading-edge/packaging fabs in Taiwan and another 5–6 overseas. N3 expansion now includes four new fabs: Tainan in 1H27, an additional Tainan fab in 1H28, Arizona Fab 2 pulled forward to 3Q27, and Japan Fab 2 in 2028. N5-to-N3 conversions and cross-node optimization add further output. C.C. Wei: “We have no intention to leave food on the table for others.”
  • N2/A14. N2 annual capacity should more than double in 2027, supported by smartphone and HPC/AI demand. A14 customer activity is stronger than N2 at the comparable stage, with risk production in 2027, volume production in 2028 and expectations for a larger node than N2. High-NA adoption remains planned for 2030, based on maturity and economics rather than matching competitors’ adoption schedules
  • Pricing. Mgmt said there is “no ceiling for TSMC’s profitability,” but cautioned that a dramatic increase will not happen overnight. Pricing varies by node, customer segment and manufacturing location; no specific increases or treatment of existing versus incremental commitments were confirmed. Expectations for a 10%+ blended wafer increase next year remain analyst forecasts, not company guidance.
  • Packaging. CoWoS remains customers’ preferred solution, with 5x-reticle packages in production at excellent yields, 9x/14x under development and CoPoS targeting future cost reductions. Mgmt signaled willingness to support customers if competing packaging solutions cannot deliver, while confirming the GFS interposer partnership addresses remaining backend shortages.
  • US expansion. Texas is being evaluated for additional capacity, and Terafab discussions are ongoing, with no agreement announced. The firm requirement is that any resulting fab be operated by TSM; ownership terms remain open. Outside AI, consumer and price-sensitive markets remain challenged by component inflation and macro uncertainty, keeping mgmt prudent heading into 2027.

TECH RESEARCH/NEWS

SPOT: StoneX downgrades to Hold as limited ARPU catalysts + label minimums pressure NTM margins

StoneX sees long-term network effects intact but says NTM financial optics have worsened as premium pricing anniversaries while per-subscriber minimums with labels continue rising, cutting 4Q/2027 Premium GM by ~40bps/~30bps and implying only ~50bps y/y 2027 GM expansion vs. 100bps+ recently. Paid AI remixes could also strengthen label negotiating leverage and add compute costs before becoming a meaningful ARPU driver. Downgrades to Hold from Buy; PT removed from $650.

NFLX: Jefferies assumes coverage at Buy, sees monetization + margin + buybacks driving 20%+ EPS growth

Jefferies sees ~11% revenue CAGR over the next 3 years, with ARM +6% annually from pricing and ads, while ~190bps of EBIT margin expansion and buybacks drive ~22% annual EPS growth. NFLX still prices its standard plan ~60% below peers on a per-viewing-hour basis, leaving monetization runway, while ads are expected to grow ~35% annually; ~$50B+ of 3-year FCF could add another ~6% annual EPS if used for repurchases. Buy, PT $85 from $90.

TSLA/SPCX: Barclays says merger increasingly consensus, with SpaceX likely acquirer in all-stock deal

Barclays says the strategic rationale is AI vertical integration across TSLA’s Robotaxi/Optimus, SPCX’s Grok/Macrohard, shared Terafab compute and eventual orbital data centers, but Musk’s desire to consolidate control may be just as important. It sees SPCX as the likely acquirer via an all-stock deal given its dual-class structure; at ~$2.1T/$1.5T SPCX/TSLA market caps, Musk could own ~37% of the combined company but retain ~56% voting power. Timing could be before Tesla’s 2026 AGM, otherwise within ~6–12 months, with shareholder votes and antitrust/national-security review the main hurdles. TSLA EW/$370; SPCX OW/$254

JPM now models AWS accelerating to ~40% growth in 2027 and says that could be conservative as the OpenAI partnership scales and AMZN deploys an incremental ~2M GPUs across 2027–28. GOOGL: JPM raises Cloud estimates sharply on TPU inclusion, sees >100% growth and further upside

JPM raises Google Cloud growth to ~100% y/y in 3Q, ~105% in 4Q, ~107% in 2027 and ~63% in 2028, primarily from greater TPU sales inclusion, and says estimates could still be conservative. Reiterates OW and raises Dec-27 PT to $425 from $420 on ~25x 2028 GAAP EPS.

GOOGL: Jefferies stress-tests bear cases and still sees ~30% upside on Cloud/TPU economics + AI search durability

Jefferies says GOOGL’s main bear cases around Gemini, capex ROI, Cloud durability and agentic search don’t hold up: backlog is ~3x capex with >50% converting within 24 months, Cloud margins are ~36%, and AI server paybacks are <2 years, with TPU payback roughly half AMZN’s ~2–3 years. GCP has accelerated for 5 straight quarters and could gain ~5pts of share CY24–26, while ~90% of F100 use Gemini Enterprise. In Search, Googlebot indexes ~4.8x more of the web than MSFT and AI Overviews/AI Mode each exceed 1B MAUs, supporting ad retention as formats shift. Buy; SOTP implies ~30% upside.

MSFT: UBS raises PT to $675 as Azure/M365 bogeys drift higher

UBS says investor expectations have moved up, with Azure now carrying a ~47% 1Q growth bogey and ~46–47% guide bogey, but checks/OpenAI upside, strong AI capacity additions and GPU pricing still support potential upside. Buy/$520

Cyber: Wells sees 2027 budgets accelerating to 10–15%, with PANW/CRWD clearest AI beneficiaries

DOCN: Truist says ~15% selloff is mostly neocloud credit contagion + Character.ai bankruptcy; thesis unchanged

WDAY: RBC Rising checks reinforce Sana/headless pivot + emerging agent-identity opportunity

The bigger AI opportunity is identity/governance: WDAY’s 80M+ user system of record plus C1 extends human identity into agent identity, addressing a market where 95% of large U.S. companies use autonomous agents but only 21% have mature governance. TWLO: TD Cowen expects another beat/raise, with consumer agents creating new communications + identity tailwind

CART: Baird says price gap is narrowing while enterprise momentum + 3Q GTV improve

CSCO: MS CIO survey shows networking leadership intact, but macro/order pressure worsening

NEWS

Smartphones / AAPL / QCOM: Q3 shipments fall 22%; recovery pushed to 2028 – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports IDC estimates global smartphone shipments fell 22% in Q3, the largest decline on record, as memory costs forced price increases, specification cuts and fewer budget models. IDC sees no return to growth before 2028 and says memory costs have risen about 350% in a year. Apple and Samsung held up better than Xiaomi and Oppo thanks to their premium mix.

MU: New Taiwan plant sold out for 2027 – Reuters

Reuters reports Micron opened its new Taiwan assembly and testing facility with all of the plant’s planned 2027 capacity already sold out. COO Manish Bhatia expects HBM to outgrow the broader DRAM industry through 2028. Taiwan’s president called for good-faith negotiations over the bonus dispute after workers authorized a possible strike; unions are planning an October 19 rally.

GFS: Japan funds up to $21B for New York fabs – GlobalFoundries

GlobalFoundries announced it will provide technology and operate two new Malta, New York fabs under a Japan-funded initiative worth up to $21B. The first roughly $10B fab targets a 2029 opening; a second would receive about $10B more. Each would add approximately 900,000 wafers of annual capacity. Separately, Commerce will receive roughly 4% equity and redirect $955M of CHIPS funding toward photonics, SiGe and advanced packaging.

AAPL / BABA: China AI beta in October; December rollout planned – Bloomberg / Reuters

Bloomberg reports Apple plans to launch Apple Intelligence in mainland China in December with iOS 27.3, using Alibaba-developed guardrails and Baidu for search-related features. The rollout covers older AI features and excludes the new Gemini-powered Siri assistant. Reuters separately reports Tim Cook said Chinese approval has been secured, with a test version launching in October.

ERIC: Q3 growth and margins beat expectations – Ericsson

Ericsson reports Q3 organic sales growth of 7% and an adjusted EBIT margin of 16.4%, versus consensus of -1.2% and 13.6%, respectively. Revenue reached SEK59.5B. Mobile-network demand and cost discipline supported results; management cautioned that rising component costs will pressure 2027 gross margins and the radio-access-network market remains flat.

SK Hynix / U.S. semicap: U.S. fab plan tied to China equipment approvals – Reuters

Reuters reports SK Hynix is linking a new U.S. NAND fab to approval for additional U.S. equipment at its Dalian plant. Solidigm wants to equip unused space, with higher Chinese production helping fund the U.S. project. Dalian uses Lam, Applied Materials and KLA tools. SK Hynix says the work is being coordinated with Washington and denies seeking capacity expansion.

Samsung / MU / CXMT: Samsung completes 4F² DRAM development – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports Samsung has completed development of next-generation 4F² DRAM and is aligning commercialization with customer requirements. The report contrasts Samsung’s progress with CXMT’s targeted year-end launch.

AMAT: Packaging revenue to exceed $2.4B in 2026 – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports Applied Materials expects advanced-packaging revenue above $2.4B in 2026, up over 70% from roughly $1.4B in 2025, and more than $5B in coming years. Its tools cover about 80% of hybrid-bonding process steps. Management also expects optical-inspection and e-beam revenue to grow more than 50% this year, with new packaging-inspection products planned for the first half of 2027.

MediaTek / QCOM: ByteDance nears custom AI-chip deal – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports ByteDance is in advanced talks to buy custom data-center chips from MediaTek, potentially making it the chipmaker’s second major ASIC customer after Google. ByteDance already struck a similar Qualcomm agreement this year. MediaTek expects about $2B in AI-chip sales in 2026 and targets 15% of an estimated $80B market next year.

AI infrastructure: Gartner flags $3.5T write-down risk – Bloomberg (out yesterday)

Bloomberg reports Gartner estimates AI data-center owners need $8.5T of cumulative revenue through 2030 to cover costs and earn a 12% return on capital, versus its forecast of at most $3.6T. Gartner says more than $3.5T of infrastructure spending could face impairment and predicts over 80% of neoclouds will cease operating independently by 2030.

T / VZ / TMUS: SpaceX spectrum deal raises competitive pressure – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports SpaceX’s spectrum purchase positions it as a potential rival to AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile, but meaningful competition requires costly terrestrial infrastructure, particularly in dense markets. The carriers have announced a joint venture to expand satellite connectivity. Analysts cited by Bloomberg warn defensive spending could pressure dividends and buybacks as industry revenue growth slows.

OpenAI / Anthropic: FTC subpoenas; eight states sue OpenAI – Reuters

Reuters, citing Axios, reports the FTC sent OpenAI and Anthropic civil subpoenas seeking records on safety incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy, and plans to compel executive testimony. Separately, Reuters reports eight states sued OpenAI over alleged harms to young users, seeking product changes, monetary penalties and damages.

FIG: Prioritizes AI growth over margins – Reuters

Reuters reports Figma’s CFO said a return to historical low-90% gross margins is uncertain as AI investment pushes margins into the mid-80s. Management is willing to accept lower margins to expand its addressable market and sees eventual pricing power. Figma has introduced usage-based AI credits and expects monetization to strengthen earnings in coming quarters.

SNPS: Autonomous chip design remains out of reach – DigiTimes

DigiTimes reports CEO Sassine Ghazi says fully autonomous chip development remains out of reach. Synopsys has progressed from AI-assisted engineering to models coordinating more than 40 task agents, but has not cleared the final hurdle to autonomous design.

NVDA: Huang would cut off unsafe AI developers – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Jensen Huang said Nvidia would stop supplying a customer developing unsafe AI, answering a hypothetical question and saying discussions would come first. He also reiterated that AI infrastructure demand should exceed component supply for years and announced $250M of computing access over five years for U.K. AI-safety research.

GOOGL / SPOT: YouTube pursues audiobooks – WSJ

WSJ reports YouTube has held talks with publishers about an audiobook offering that could launch next year. It has considered bundling the product into YouTube Premium, currently starting at $15.99 a month. The initiative would extend YouTube’s subscription bundle into a market led by Spotify and Amazon’s Audible.

META / SNAP: Court permits Florida’s under-14 ban – Reuters

Reuters reports a federal appeals court allowed Florida to enforce its law barring children under 14 from accounts on social-media platforms with features such as autoplay and infinite scrolling. The court rejected the industry’s free-speech challenge to the preliminary enforcement decision and separately directed Florida’s case against Snap to proceed in state court.

AI financing / OWL: Stack seeks $6B data-center loan – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Blue Owl-owned Stack Infrastructure has appointed arrangers for an A$8.6B, roughly $6B, five-year loan to fund its Melbourne data-center project. The financing follows heightened pressure on AI credit markets, including CoreWeave’s bond selloff and postponed listings at Firmus and AirTrunk.

AI regulation: FSB strengthens human oversight of AI agents – Reuters

Reuters reports the Financial Stability Board’s final AI guidance calls for more intensive human oversight in higher-risk financial applications and clear triggers requiring agents to seek human approval. It removes earlier advice to treat AI as synthetic employees, warning that agents can interpret predefined goals unexpectedly.

HOOD: SEC clears company-metric contracts – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports the SEC approved Cboe options contracts tied to corporate performance metrics, such as Tesla deliveries and iPhone sales. Cboe plans contracts for 23 U.S.-listed companies, scheduled to debut this month through a partnership with Robinhood. The approval makes Cboe the first securities exchange authorized to list these products.

Anduril: Employee tender at $90B – Bloomberg

Bloomberg reports Anduril is offering current and former employees a share sale at roughly a $90B valuation, up from $61B in May. The tender is capped at about $1.3B at $94.80 a share and closes November 9. The defense-technology company generated $2.2B of revenue last year.
