MSFT +2: Azure above buyside bogeys accelerating to 48% and guided to 49-50% on capex delay, but the stock gave back most of its after-hours pop as investors fixated on a new “additional sources of funding” line & capex noise

Overall, very solid print for one of the most crowded longs in Tech. Azure print and guide stole the show — even despite the 3p capacity delays as mgmt talked up driving efficiencies across Azure which allowed them to beat (“each dollar of CapEx has the potential to support more revenue and higher returns over time”).

Lots of questions this morning on why stock isn’t up more. The answer comes down to Amy leaving the door open to an equity raise (every person I talked to said mistake not to keep it closed), although she also made it clear that equity is below other options and their hint at using debt despite their FCF suggests their capex might be larger than the 220B bogey, which they can pretty comfortably handle with FCF. They supposedly pushed back even more on an equity raise in the callbacks saying possibility is very low. The other bearish nit was M365 growing slower although price & upsell was still strong and the co-pilot number going undisclosed.

Outside of the equity raise commentary, the other key callback takeaway is that the 49-50% Azure guide assumes continued efficiency gains but not a repeat of F1Q’s revenue-per-token benefit. Mgmt tied that benefit to premium model launches, OpenAI’s in particular, and doesn’t expect it in Q2. It listed the H2 swing factors as supply timing, first-party prioritization, efficiency, deployment timing and tougher compares, and said at least ~$5B of capacity spend shifted into H2. Bulls will read that as a guide with cushion; bears read the swing-factor list as a setup for an H2 Azure decel.

Buyside numbers still around $25/$28+ for CY27/CY28 and bulls will say throw a 30x multiple on what is one of the cleanest Megacaps, and you get to $750+. Overall we thought this was a solid print.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

The bull thesis is that MSFT is the enterprise AI platform, and this quarter showed both engines accelerating at once at a ~$370B revenue run-rate. Azure plus Foundry is becoming the control plane where enterprises build and govern agents across any model (OpenAI, Anthropic, open-weight, MAI). M365 is moving from per-seat subscriptions to seats plus consumption via E7, Copilot credits and GitHub-style usage billing. Bulls think the moat is durable: the identity, data, security and governance layers (Entra, Work IQ and Fabric, Purview, Agent 365) sit where enterprise data already lives. That matters more as buyers move past the “shiny object stuff” to owning their weights, managing costs and reliability. Add the structural cost levers (Maia and Cobalt silicon, MAI models within 3-6 months of the frontier, rights to OpenAI’s model IP without paying rent on it, a fungible fleet), a AAA balance sheet, and capacity discipline: Satya won’t chase a few big buyers unless the pricing is right.

This quarter gave bulls their best evidence yet that the AI spend is earning a return. Azure accelerated 6pts and guided higher while third-party capacity slipped, so the growth came from getting more out of the existing fleet. Mgmt said “each dollar of CapEx has the potential to support more revenue and higher returns over time.” The rest of the print points the same way: OM near 50%, FCF of $18.6B vs. ~$8.5B Street, RPO flat when Street expected a decline, M365 commercial cloud accelerating with a ~19% guide, Copilot daily users up ~50% q/q and usage-billing customers up 75%+ q/q. The share-loss bear case is hard to make with Azure at 48% on a ~$133B run-rate.

The bear thesis is that MSFT is turning from an asset-light software compounder into a capital-intensive infrastructure business, and this quarter made the bill more visible:

  • capex incl. leases ran at ~49% of revenue, and cash PP&E at ~43% (vs. 25% a year ago);- FCF fell 27% y/y, and capex exceeded net income by ~$8.6B;- cash and short-term investments fell to $64.5B;- signed but uncommenced data center leases climbed to $364.6B;- the CFO now says MSFT will access additional sources of funding when it judges that attractive.

The returns on that capital lean on things MSFT doesn’t control. OpenAI is now ~11% of revenue vs. ~7% in FY26, and it’s a loss-making counterparty diversifying to TPUs, ORCL and AWS. Token pricing is set by frontier-model launches. Neocloud capacity just slipped. Meanwhile the old franchise is under pressure: M365 seat growth slowed to 5% with weaker E3 and standalone execution, the Copilot seat count went missing, Industry Solutions customers are taking longer on CRM and HCM decisions while they rethink workflows around AI, and Windows OEM & Devices is guided down mid-30s.

Bears say the bull case assumes too much durability. Mgmt itself said the 49-50% guide needs efficiency gains not yet found and excludes the token-pricing tailwind that helped F1Q. It also said H2 laps “a strong prior-year comparable.” The implied Azure dollar add shrinks to ~$2.8-3.0B from $3.75B. Even with implied OI above Street, the F2Q guide only gets to in-line EPS (~$4.82 vs. $4.84), because finance-lease interest pushes OI&E to roughly -$300M. That’s a cost line that grows with every lease that commences. Microsoft Cloud GM is heading to ~65%, the exit offer isn’t the margin lever some modeled, and the FY27 OM framework wasn’t repeated. M365 consumption, the bulls’ next leg, is a “slower move” by mgmt’s own account. With depreciation from $220B+ of FY27 capex weighing on gross margin, OM slips to ~45%, or ~$192B of operating income. After ~-$2.5B of OI&E (more lease and debt interest), a 20% tax rate and ~7.43B shares, CY27 EPS is ~$20-21, ~7% below Street. At 22x, closer to GOOGL’s ~20x forward and other capital-intensive peers because a ~1% FCF yield offers no support, that implies ~$450, or ~17% downside from $540.

“Lower capex could mean slower Q2/Q3 Azure” / “Q2, we have the efficiency gains, we need to find more to meet the guidance... some supply in Q2 moving out to H2” / “Is the flipside on the good token pricing that open weight adoption might hurt their growth?” / “They’ve added DC delays to their risk factors this quarter” / “New bear case is that we can’t spend capex fast enough”

NOW -18% AH: Not good. Subs in line with Street and short of the ~$4.01–4.03B bogeys, cRPO of 21% cc below the ~21.5–22% bar, and a 4Q cRPO guide of 18.5% cc that sits under both Street (19%) and the ~19.5–20% bulls wanted. EBIT margin guide worse.

Overall just an ugly print and there wasn’t much defending of the name in my conversations. There were a lot of nascent bulls in the name over the last month, although positioning shifted more even the last few days after Bernstein called out some of the cRPO headwinds in a note.

Bears got plenty of fuel : beat sizes halving vs. 2Q, organic cRPO heading to ~16.75%, and an ~18% FY27 sub growth starting point. Mgmt now calls cRPO “very nuanced,” and that having to bundle services to get AI live shows AI isn’t yet adding to total growth. GM keeps sliding toward the ~80% sub GM trough and ebit guide was worse. Bulls will say the 4Q cRPO guide is mostly optics: early renewals pulled into 3Q, ~75bps of T4C/on-prem items that still convert to revenue, and Moveworks lapping. They see the FDE carve-out as a temporary trade of ~1pt of FY27 sub growth for faster consumption that comes back at renewal.

Beat cadence. Every top-line beat roughly halved vs. 2Q:

  • cRPO ~100bps (vs. ~200bps), or ~50bps excluding early renewals- sub rev ~50bps above the high end (vs. ~150bps, half of which was an on-prem pull-forward)- OM ~150bps (vs. ~300bps)

Only ~$3M of the cc sub beat flowed to the FY guide; the $15M raise includes ~$12M of FX, and mgmt blamed the carve-out for the gap. The tone shifted too: after framing past guides as deliberately prudent, mgmt called the ~50bps underlying cRPO beat strong for NOW’s scale.

AI. The scorecard improved again:

  • AI NNACV +65% y/y- year-end AI ACV target raised a second time, to $1.7B+ (from $1B at the start of the year and $1.5B at the May analyst day)- $1M+ AI deals 4.5x; deals with 5+ AI products 8.5x (vs. 5.5x previously)- agentic consumption in production 13x YTD and accelerating each quarter- Control Tower NNACV 6x+- EmployeeWorks, the Moveworks front door, at $100M ACV in under three quarters- CRM AI NNACV tripled q/q and y/y

The catch is that most consumption still sits inside initial entitlements sold with a ~30% price uplift. So usage is running ahead of incremental revenue, and customers going over their entitlements are still a small, if fast-growing, group. Asked when AI shows up in total growth, mgmt pointed to 2027 for “the true acceleration” and reiterated AI at ~30% of ACV by 2030.

Pricing. New AI-native packaging is landing at 20-30% uplifts with no discounting, and >40% of customers migrating to the new tiers move up a tier. Mgmt pitched up to a 5x lift in revenue per user once customers are live and “token economics” kick in. It said customers are not asking for outcome-based pricing, so the hybrid seat-plus-consumption model stays.

Headless. The biggest strategic shift: NOW is openly charging for headless access. Action Fabric opens NOW workflows to third-party front ends via MCP/A2A. That now includes a ChatGPT plugin under the expanded OpenAI partnership and deeper GOOGL Gemini Enterprise integration. Every action burns Assists, and Action Fabric assists grew 500% m/m. On the callback, mgmt said:

  • assists are now fungible across platforms- consumption is moving into comp plans- the flywheel should need less new sales capacity

This is a more open stance than prior quarters’ focus on owning the front door. It is the most direct answer yet to the bear case that the AI labs’ front ends cut SaaS out.

Seats. Seat counts grew double digits y/y and accelerated vs. last year, even as customers moved to AI SKUs. Mgmt sees no seat compression, and non-seat SKUs are also growing. This is the clearest data point this quarter against AI cannibalizing seats.

Security. Security & Risk was in 16 of the top 20 deals (13 over $1M), and Security & Risk AI NNACV grew >4.5x. Mgmt’s claim that security could be bigger in a few years than all of NOW today drew skepticism on the call.

Demand. Mgmt cited widespread demand and pipelines growing “extremely well,” with no macro caution. It did not quantify 2027 IT budgets and made no comment on tariffs. Federal had an outstanding seasonal 3Q ($1M+ deals +30% y/y; Federal CRM 6x), and Federal NNACV should accelerate in 4Q, which is what drives the on-prem mix. Large deals held up: 19 deals over $5M NNACV (+135% y/y); 680 $5M+ customers (+23%, average ACV ~$15.4M); 31 new $20M+ customers; 19 of the top 20 deals with 6+ products; 98% renewal rate.

Margins. OM of 32.6% beat on opex, +14% y/y vs. Street ~+21%. Headcount fell ~5% q/q to 28,855, with cuts in sales ops, support and R&D overhead, while quota-bearing heads grew double digits. Internal AI now yields >$1B of annualized value, more than double the 2025 exit rate. Gross margin went the other way: GM of 77.3% (-360bps y/y) and sub GM of 80.2% (-300bps) missed on hyperscaler migrations, data center/AI investment and M&A. Mgmt reiterated ~80% as the trough but gave no timing on recovery. The 4Q OM guide of 32%, ~150bps below Street, matters more for estimates.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

The bull thesis is that NOW is the layer where work actually gets done in enterprise AI. Agents need context (the CMDB, Workflow Data Fabric and 20+ years of operational data), permissions, and a governed place to execute work. NOW owns that layer inside most of the Global 2000. It has a 98% renewal rate, 680 customers above $5M of ACV growing 23%, and 19 of its top 20 deals spanning six or more products. Bulls see several compounding drivers:

  • AI-native SKU upgrades priced at a 20-30% uplift- Assist consumption once customers blow through entitlements (mgmt talks about up to 5x revenue per user)- the Control Tower as the governance layer for first- and third-party agents- a security franchise that now spans asset discovery (Armis), identity (Veza) and SecOps, with the CISO as a second buyer- CRM rip-and-replace- Flow as a new down-market entry point

The model is also unusually capital-light for an AI beneficiary: capex around 3% of revenue, a 36% FCF margin, and >$1B of internal AI savings that fund growth investment while OM still expands ~100bps a year.

This quarter gave bulls more evidence on adoption: AI NNACV +65%, a second AI ACV raise to $1.7B+, consumption 13x and accelerating, Action Fabric assists +500% m/m. Double-digit, accelerating seat growth runs counter to the seat-compression thesis. Bulls will argue the 4Q cRPO guide is mostly accounting: ~75bps of T4C/on-prem items still convert to revenue, early renewals simply moved growth into 3Q, and Moveworks laps. Organic 4Q subscription growth actually improves to ~19.75% from ~18.75% despite a bigger carve-out. The FDE carve-out is a deliberate trade: supported customers adopt twice as fast with multiples higher consumption, and the carved-out value flips back into subscription at renewal. That builds in a 2028-29 tailwind just as consumption beyond entitlements scales.

The bear thesis is that AI is moving value away from seat-based workflow apps faster than NOW can charge for it. AI-native service desks, the labs’ enterprise agents and hyperscaler agent platforms can sit in front of the system of record, and internal builds get easier every model cycle. Even if NOW keeps the data and workflow layer, pricing power moves to whoever owns the user. NOW’s AI numbers are real but small: a $1.7B ACV target against a ~$16B subscription base, much of it upgrade uplift on existing seats. And total growth has slowed through the AI ramp. Organic cRPO goes from ~18.5-19% to ~16.75% in 4Q, while AI-exposed data/infra names (SNOW, DDOG) accelerated toward ~40% at $4-6B of revenue. Structurally, the model is getting more complex and lower quality:

  • sub GM has slid ~470bps since 2024 toward an ~80% “trough”- PS now runs at a gross loss- growth leans on M&A (Moveworks, Armis, Veza, Logik.ai, Sweep), which brings T4C contracts and net debt- EPS growth (~12% in 2026) trails revenue growth (~22%)

Bears think the bull case assumes the consumption flywheel arrives on time and at scale. The consumption ramp many expected in 2H26 is now “material within ~12 months,” and the “true acceleration” is pushed to 2027. Yet the callback bridge points to ~18% FY27 subscription growth, a slowdown from ~21% in 2026 and below Street ~18.7-18.9%. The ~100bps carve-out is an “early estimate” while mgmt is doubling down and building more deployment teams into its forecast, so it can get bigger. Needing FDEs to get AI live suggests the product doesn’t deploy itself. This quarter fed that view:

  • beat sizes halved, and only ~$3M of the cc beat passed through- 4Q OM was guided ~150bps below Street- GM, FCF and EPS all missed- cRPO now needs adjusting every quarter- mgmt’s claims that security and Action Fabric could each become bigger than all of NOW today strain credibility

If NOW is a high-teens grower with flat-to-down gross margins, the multiple converges toward large-cap software (10-20x FCF). Growth-oriented holders who came in since the July print would exit, and FY27 EPS keeps drifting lower on interest expense and margins.

Bears believe CY27 subscription growth lands closer to ~16% (~15.5% organic) as the carve-out headwind grows, T4C exclusions widen and seat-based expansion slows. That takes total revenue to ~$18.8B. With sub GM sliding below the ~80% “trough” and PS running at a loss, FCF margin stalls near 35%, or ~$6.6B of FCF (~7% below Street). Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bull Case

The bull thesis is that KLAC is the toll collector on rising chip complexity in what is shaping up to be a multi-year, AI-driven WFE upcycle. It holds roughly 55-60% of process control, several times its nearest competitor, and intensity rises with every driver in this cycle: larger and more valuable HPC die at 2nm and 1.4nm, HBM’s tighter specs, hybrid bonding in advanced packaging, and greenfield fabs where process control tools go in first (plus Terafab-type mega-fab optionality). When customers earn record margins and are short capacity, yield is worth more, which supports both sampling rates and KLAC’s ability to price new tools. Add a services business that is ~20% of revenue, is on track for a 17th straight year of growth and should compound at the high end of 13-15% as a fast-growing installed base comes on, plus best-in-class margins and ~90%+ of FCF returned, and bulls argue KLAC deserves a premium multiple through the cycle rather than a peak-cycle discount.

Bear Case

The bear thesis is that semicaps are late in the cycle and the multiple compresses even as numbers rise. In the last two upcycles KLAC’s P/E fell from ~25x to ~12x (2021-22) and from ~31x to ~18x (2024-25) while estimates rose ~55-60%, and the stock still trades at a premium to the market with the 10-year near 5% and the Fed leaning tighter. WFE growth is gated by cleanrooms, power and raw materials, so the ~$300B CY28 the buyside is penciling may not arrive on time; Rick said as much on the call. Hyperscaler capex printed below Street this week, and at least one large AI spender is already guiding to a capex step-down later in the decade, which feeds the peak-spend worry. KLAC-specific risks pile on top: the next leg of WFE is memory-led (DRAM greenfields), where KLAC has historically under-indexed; China (34% of Sep revenue) is heading to the low-20s%; AMAT and smaller players are pushing into process control and e-beam, threatening a ~60% share; and KLAC already under-grew WFE in CY26.

This quarter gives bears ammunition on quality. Dec’s systems growth is all memory: F/L systems are ~flat q/q, so the growth mix is exactly the one bears worried about, and ~95% of Sep’s q/q add came from China. GM is stuck at 62.5%, ~50bps below the buyside, on memory costs mgmt can’t reprice into existing backlog, with the fix pushed to mid/late CY27 into CY28 and packaging/non-semi PC growth diluting mix. Opex is ramping at ~2x the prior pace, capex steps up to $600-800M a year, and FCF missed on a $434M inventory build. The “>$200B” CY27 WFE call only matched LRCX and the $200B+ bar, below the ~$225B some buyside models already carry, so it doesn’t lift buyside numbers, and CY27 growth will be “more linear” rather than another back-half spike. CY27 segment color doesn’t come until late January, leaving the stock to trade on sector flows and hyperscaler capex headlines until then. And “customers can’t take tools because their cleanroom is late” cuts both ways: it caps CY27 upside and makes KLAC’s deliveries hostage to customers’ construction schedules.

FTNT -26%: Product peaked in 2Q and the services hand-off slipped to 2027, so beating Street on results and guidance didn’t clear the buyside bar

FTNT beat Street on billings, product revenue, OM and EPS and raised FY26 billings by $193M. But product growth (+37%) missed the ~50% buyside bogey, services slowed to +12.7% and the FY services guide was cut, and the 4Q guide implies product growth slows again to ~20%.

Sentiment/positioning skewed short heading into the print, and this print will finally make bears happy who have held on to the view that the refresh cycle has peaked: The 1H inventory cushion is gone, and pricing (worth ~10pts of billings growth) is slowing meaning more decel is coming, weakest Q3 sequential product growth since FTNT went public, product GM fell 540bps q/q to 64.5%, services missed a second time after mgmt had promised a 2H pickup, and mgmt now cites discounting and lower attach. Not a lot of bullish feedback post the print.

Key Takeaways

  • Product. Product revenue of $765M grew +36.8% y/y (2Q +51.9%).It beat Street’s $726M (+29.8%) by ~5% but fell well short of the ~50% buyside bogey (desks ranged ~47-53%, ~$820-855M), ending five straight quarters of acceleration.- Product fell ~$8M q/q vs. +$50M in 3Q25, and total revenue grew only 1.5% q/q, the weakest 3Q sequential since FTNT went public.- On a 2-yr stack, product slowed to ~55 from ~65 in 2Q, so the decel isn’t just the tougher comp (3Q25 +18% vs. 2Q25 +12.6%).- Mgmt says demand was “probably even stronger than the first half,” saw no slowdown in 3Q or October, and says customers kept moving to higher-end FortiGates (AI segmentation, data center), which lifted ASPs.

Supply. Mgmt’s explanation for the decel is new and supply-driven, not demand-driven.

  • 1H shipments drew on FTNT’s usual ~6 months of on-hand inventory, bought before memory and component prices rose. That cushion “normalized” in 3Q, and lead times on memory-heavy parts got longer.- Asked whether FTNT is supply-constrained or just paying more, Ken answered “a little bit both.” In 1H, the inventory position was pitched as a share-gain edge.- Inventory rose to $591M (from $426M at 2Q and $400M at YE25) as FTNT bought more components and raw materials.- Mgmt says standard FortiGates, switches and APs are still available; the tightness is in OT and specialized devices.

  • At the end of the call, IR corrected Ken’s “10% benefit on the top line” to billings, not revenue.- In July mgmt had assumed a “high single digits” price impact for 2H billings. The 4Q billings guide again embeds ~10pts.- Another list-price increase was announced in September, effective early November. Mgmt says increases have “slowed down” vs. 1Q/2Q and “stabilized,” and the benefit moderates in 2027.- Simple math puts ex-pricing billings growth at ~19%, ~4pts slower than 2Q. That spread is why FY27 product estimates are so far apart.

  • In July mgmt said it saw no pull-forward or excess inventory.- On callbacks, mgmt allowed for “some pull-forward” from pricing and supply worries but said there was no clear sign of a material demand pull-forward. It noted billings were more weighted to 1H this year.- Channel inventory was flat (down as a % of billings), and contract duration stayed ~30 months.

  • The FY services guide was cut $40M to $5.15-5.17B (+12.6%), implying 4Q +12.6% vs. Street +14.3%. That means no 2H pickup, after mgmt said in July services growth would “pick up in the second half.”- The main explanation is ramped activations on large new-customer and expansion enterprise agreements (some start in 2027), plus large customers folding services into enterprise agreements at lower yields. 4Q is also seasonally slow for rollouts.- Mgmt also flagged slower-moving causes:lower attach on networking products and on product-heavy sovereign deployments- more discounting on services relative to hardware as prices rise- a US tax incentive favoring depreciable hardware

The leading indicators improved:

  • service billings +26%- current service billings +18% (2Q +16%), though short of ~19-20% bogeys, with some desks in the mid-20s- short-term deferred revenue +14.5%, a five-quarter high- total deferred revenue +19%- RPO +19% to $7.98B

  • 4Q revenue: $2.155-2.245B, +15.5% at the midpoint. That beat Street’s $2.15B (+13%) but missed bulls at ~+18-19%.   • pricing adds ~10pts to 4Q billings growth and moderates next year- underlying product demand ex-pricing was roughly consistent with 2Q- some large customers bought hardware early to lock in supply and price while others deferred, so net pull-forward is hard to size- some enterprise-agreement service start dates run into 2027 (services normally start ~90 days after purchase: 60 days in the US, 120 internationally)- services growth could reach the mid-teens by 2H27 as short-term deferred revenue turns into revenue

Bull vs Bear Debate

The bull thesis is that FTNT is the volume and price-performance leader in network security just as the network becomes the main enforcement point for AI. FortiOS runs firewall, SD-WAN, SASE and AI security on one operating system and one custom ASIC roadmap. That gives FTNT the lowest cost per unit of throughput, the largest installed base (over half of global firewall units) and more direct control of its supply chain than peers. AI raises the need for inspection inside the network, from east-west agent traffic to GPU clusters to open-weight models run on-prem. That pushes customers toward higher-end FortiGates with higher ASPs. Past the current refresh, bulls see a long runway in OT/IT convergence (NIS2, and manufacturing is the most-attacked sector five years running), sovereign SASE (which mgmt sizes at 2-3x the cloud-only TAM) and the 2026-27 end-of-support refresh cohorts. Few security names can match the economics: Rule-of-55+ (~20% growth plus a ~37% OM), ~40% FCF margins, a 33.9% GAAP OM and a $2B buyback.

Bulls will argue this quarter was a beat-and-raise that the market took for a top. Billings, product revenue, OM, EPS and FCF all beat Street. FY billings were raised $193M on a $36M beat and are now $1.14B above February’s guide. The 4Q billings guide (+20%) and implied 4Q product (+20.5%) sit far above Street, and the total value of $1M+ deals grew 51%. Mgmt says demand exceeds supply, so longer lead times delay revenue rather than lose it. In the bull view the services miss is timing. Large customers bought hardware upfront and pushed service start dates to match their rollouts, but they have already committed: service billings grew +26%, current service billings sped up to +18%, short-term deferred revenue grew +14.5% and total deferred +19%.

That sets up services growth in the mid-teens by 2H27, and the math matters because services are ~63% of revenue. Moving from ~12.6% to ~15% services growth adds ~1.5pts to total growth, enough to keep FY27 revenue growth at 12%+ even if product slows to high single digits. The FortiGuard AI subscriptions and the FortiLink SASE overlay on the installed base add new attach on top. Post-print FY27 estimates moved up, so the 26% drop was entirely multiple compression, and it leaves the stock at its 5-yr average EV/FCF. Services speeding up in 1H27, or a February FY27 guide that holds product near double digits, would lift both estimates and the multiple.

The bear thesis is that FTNT is a cyclical hardware company that was priced as a durable software compounder at the top of its cycle. FY26 product growth of ~36% was boosted by four things that don’t repeat. Price added ~10pts to billings growth. A ~6-month inventory cushion bought before memory prices rose let FTNT ship while others waited for parts. Customers bought ahead of deployment to beat price increases and allocation, which mgmt now acknowledges. And frontier-model threat urgency spiked in 2Q, then faded in 3Q. FY27 laps all of it. The pricing benefit moderates, inventory has normalized and lead times are longer. Hardware already bought but not deployed, in some cases until 2027, is demand that won’t recur. The last cycle is the template: the 2021-22 refresh was followed by 2023-24 digestion, when product fell to -1% in 2024. FFIV’s repricing the day before showed how the market treats a finite refresh.

This quarter fed every leg of that thesis. Product growth fell 15pts, the 2-yr stack fell ~10pts, and the 4Q guide implies only +9% q/q vs. a +21-24% seasonal norm. Product GM fell 540bps q/q to 64.5% even with price increases, and the CFO conceded that customers’ limited budgets cap further pass-through. Services slowed after mgmt had promised a 2H pickup. The FY services cut came with explanations that look structural:

  • product-heavy networking and sovereign deals with lower attach- neocloud and service-provider customers that run their own services on FTNT gear- more discounting on services relative to hardware as prices rise- a US tax push toward hardware (likely 100% bonus depreciation)

The pillars meant to offset the firewall cycle are slowing too. SASE billings grew +22% (from +35%), SASE net new ARR halved q/q, SecOps billings grew +15% (from +25%) and OT grew ~+40% (from >55%).

The bull’s 15% FY27 services assumption is the most exposed. Current service billings at +18% missed bogeys, and services would have to speed up ~2.5pts while mix shifts toward lower-attach deals. Estimates could fall if FY27 product is flat to down, services stays at ~12-13% and the GM and opex tailwinds reverse. The multiple could also compress further toward hardware-heavy peers as the story shifts from “AI security platform” to “post-peak refresh.”
