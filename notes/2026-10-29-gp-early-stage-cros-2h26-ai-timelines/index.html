SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined the recovery in preclinical contract research, including discovery, safety testing, and regulatory toxicology. Demand began 2026 weakly but accelerated materially as biotech financing improved, pharmaceutical companies prepared for patent expirations, and sponsors advanced more selectively funded programs.

Capacity is now tightening across CROs, CDMOs, animal suppliers, and testing facilities, with some work scheduled well into 2027. The expert expects a favorable 2027, although study starts may fluctuate because of test-article availability and supply-chain constraints.

Key Insights

The expert identified biotech financing as the dominant demand catalyst. Funding typically favors mature, lower-risk assets and ultimately supports outsourced development work. Discovery demand has increased particularly strongly, while toxicology capacity and animal availability are creating backlogs. AI is improving target identification, molecule optimization, candidate selection, digital pathology, quality control, and regulatory reporting. Its impact on regulated toxicology remains limited. AI represents neither an automatic benefit nor threat to CROs. Integrated providers can retain productivity gains and offset lower revenue from commoditized services, while standalone providers face greater pricing risk. Non-animal methods are gaining adoption in screening and mechanistic toxicology but are unlikely to replace systemic animal testing soon. Chronic toxicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, repeat-dose testing, immunotoxicology, and immunogenicity remain difficult to model. The expert’s strongest conclusion was that renewed biotech activity should drive a pronounced CRO recovery in 2027.
