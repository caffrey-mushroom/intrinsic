SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a CRM implementation and integration provider, described continued weakness in consulting demand, especially for winning new customers. The discussion focused on Salesforce and HubSpot, the growing use of lower-cost software and AI-built tools, and how these shifts affect CRM vendors, implementation partners, and customers.

Smaller businesses are increasingly considering basic platforms enhanced with AI rather than paying for enterprise CRM systems and customization. Larger organizations remain more likely to value reliability, security, and compliance, but adoption of new AI features is still cautious. The expert also described declining partner-program support and uncertainty about where consulting firms should invest next.

Key Insights

New customer acquisition is the main source of weakness; existing business is comparatively stable. AI and inexpensive alternatives are creating a significant threat to traditional CRM implementation work. Customers may attempt integrations themselves, then seek help after encountering technical, cost, or maintenance problems. The expert views AI-focused consultants who can connect tools and solve problems across platforms as a likely future model, potentially displacing consultants whose value rests mainly on expertise in one CRM. Salesforce’s AI offerings have generated interest but, in the expert’s client base, adoption remains limited. Customers often prefer familiar third-party AI tools connected to their CRM. Data quality remains a prerequisite for useful automation. Customer systems often contain duplicated or poorly organized information. Salesforce and Slack integration was seen as effective, allowing users to retrieve CRM information through Slack without directly logging into the CRM. Reduced partner incentives and support are weakening consultants’ loyalty to Salesforce and HubSpot. The expert said this may encourage partners to reduce their focus or leave the programs.
