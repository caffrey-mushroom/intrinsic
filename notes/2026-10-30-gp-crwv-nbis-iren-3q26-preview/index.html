SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, an experienced data center developer and operator, discussed the market for GPU-dense colocation, especially inference sites of roughly 20–50 megawatts. Demand for available, confirmed capacity is very strong, and projects with service dates 18–24 months out can attract pre-leases. However, financing, power access, permitting, construction, and community opposition make delivery difficult. The expert expects pricing pressure to continue as these constraints persist.

Key Insights

Speed to market is the primary driver of pricing. Tenants may accept higher lease costs when capacity can be delivered quickly. Neocloud tenants are harder to underwrite than hyperscalers. Developers may require substantial deposits or credit support, and negotiations can take longer. Community resistance has become a major obstacle to approvals. The expert believes many new developers will fail to complete projects, even with industry experience and established supplier relationships. Behind-the-meter gas generation is gaining acceptance, potentially bypassing some grid connection delays. Reliability and fuel supply are critical; locally produced gas may be less dependable than pipeline supply. Projects awaiting council approval may see pre-leasing delayed. The expert expects limited new pre-leasing in the near term.

Competitive Landscape

Market participant or approach

Positioning and observations

Neocloud tenants

Can pay higher rates, but may require deposits or backstops because of credit concerns. Some developers are reluctant to lease to them.

Hyperscalers

Owner-operators

Generally offer stronger credit and lower lease rates. Some are pursuing smaller inference sites, expanding opportunities for smaller developers.

Provide facilities and power to tenants. Established operators have an advantage through proven delivery experience and trusted construction and engineering networks.

Repurposed mining sites

Existing power agreements and substations may help, but most facility infrastructure is unsuitable for high-performance computing. Adjacent greenfield construction may save time if the site already has power access.

Behind-the-meter power

Gas generation is increasingly considered as a permanent solution or bridge to grid power. Fuel cells have also been proposed, though the expert questioned their economics.
