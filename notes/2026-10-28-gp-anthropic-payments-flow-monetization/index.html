SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined how a leading AI model provider could monetize agent-driven commerce. The expert divided commerce infrastructure into three layers: consumer and merchant applications, payment processing, and card networks. The company is expected to prioritize product discovery, advertising, and outcome-based merchant services before expanding into wallets or full payment processing.

The expert viewed broader processor diversification as a natural step rather than immediate evidence of bringing all payments in- house.

Key Insights

Advertising and customer acquisition represent a substantially larger opportunity than payment-processing fees. The AI provider should therefore focus first on influencing product discovery and purchase decisions. Consumer agents are likely to remain aligned with shoppers, while merchant agents will manage product presentation, sponsored placement, and conversion. Token billing is expected to be transitional because it is unpredictable for merchants and poorly aligned with transaction value. Referral, commission, or outcome-based pricing is the preferred long-term model. Payment processing becomes strategically attractive mainly for its transaction data. That data could improve purchase-intent predictions, recommendations, advertising effectiveness, and outcome pricing. A proprietary wallet is a plausible first step toward payments. Full processing remains harder because fraud detection, disputes, regulation, and merchant acceptance require extensive data and operational capabilities. Stablecoin wallets face significant merchant adoption barriers, particularly around fraud, chargebacks, settlement, and trust. Consumer adoption may become easier if agents optimize payment costs and reduce the value of card rewards.
