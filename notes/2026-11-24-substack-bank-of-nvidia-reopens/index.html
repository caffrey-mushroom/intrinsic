TLDR

  1. I think part of why the whole AI trade ripped is that people are less worried about Nvidia pulling back on deals now

that Jensen’s back.

  1. The bank of Nvidia can get a data center built. Somebody still has to pay to use it.
  2. Trump is backing Jensen. When Cramer asked what happens if the financing goes bad, Trump said he had confidence in

Nvidia. So we have his confidence.

  1. Nvidia says its own investigation is still going and it’s still cooperating with the government inquiries.

When we think of the ramifications for the AI trade, we have to look at it through the lens of the bank of Nvidia.

Some of these companies are trying to build a lot faster than their balance sheets can handle. Nvidia has said as much. If it helps them get the money, they can build the data center and buy the chips. And anyone selling into that project has a better chance of getting an order too.

Remember what we said about Ohio. OpenAI is leasing the buildings from SB Energy, and Nvidia is helping back the site, power and buildings. The guarantees kick in as each building is ready.

You can clearly see why Jensen would do it. Get the thing built and you have somewhere to sell multiple generations of chips. But if OpenAI runs into trouble, Nvidia can still be on the hook years after it shipped the hardware.

Colette (former CFO, then interim CEO, now CFO again) was already saying on CNBC last Tuesday that they’d keep doing these kinds of deals. She was also pretty specific about what they’d look at. Is the project funded? Is the site ready? Who’s actually using the compute? And Nvidia still has to spend on its own supply and engineering. She’s still the CFO. What’s up for debate is how far Jensen will go on the next deal.

Altman (CEO OpenAI) said something recently that fits right into this. A bunch of chip companies can all be right that OpenAI and Anthropic are going to spend a ton and still be wrong about how much of that money is going to them. If three suppliers are building around the same customer’s budget, the customer only gets to spend it once.

And then there’s Washington. Oh Washington. What a mess we have here.

Bessent wanted an outside CEO on the 15th. By Sunday he was pointing out that Jensen was now on the outside. Apparently ten days off is enough to get some outside experience. Or a little bit of coercion?

Bessent was a lot clearer on what the administration actually wants. Keep US labs ahead and make it harder for China to get advanced compute, including by renting it from a data center somewhere else. That’s how you can end up with Washington cheering for Jensen and making it harder for him to sell to some customers at the same time.

Now when we think about the 200% chip tariff, Trump was talking about two years out. Which chips it would cover and who gets an exemption are still open questions. The rules already have exemptions for some US data-center uses.

And on the separate threat to penalize US companies that build abroad, we still don’t know how he’d do it. If you’re deciding where to build and what it’s going to cost, those details are the whole ballgame.

Anyway, the best question Cramer asked was who helps Nvidia if one of these deals goes sideways. Trump likes Nvidia’s chances.

Same with the board. Nvidia says government input helped it get comfortable bringing Jensen back. The company says its investigation is continuing, and so are the government inquiries. Bessent was still asking for answers about compliance on Sunday.

This is all very interesting. Becuase (at least to me) it is being made clear that 1) Nvidia is too important for the US gov to not interfere and 2) Jensen is, in a way, Nvidia.

And if you sold after the board said he wasn’t coming back, you’re probably pissed. Maybe some cases coming? A securities fraud case would hinge on what the board knew when it said that, whether it misled people with the required intent, and whether that caused their losses. The board could also have meant it at the time and changed its mind. The dates and what directors knew on those dates will be interesting.

I think we will be getting lots of headlines that can add to the volatiltiy as things get worked through.

For the AI trade, any subsequent deals will give us a lot of insight.

Who’s putting up their own money?

And how much does Nvidia owe if they stop paying?
