SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed how neoclouds and AI infrastructure providers serve customers training or deploying AI models. Providers range from raw GPU capacity sellers to inference platforms and hybrid businesses. The discussion covered customer choices, provider economics, open models, financing and data-center constraints, and competitive positioning.

Key Insights

Customers typically begin with serverless inference APIs for convenience and flexibility. As usage grows or performance requirements become more specific, they may move to dedicated endpoints or reserved GPU clusters, which offer greater control. Post-training proprietary models is becoming an important differentiator. It can improve performance on specialized tasks and make customer relationships stickier because moving the model, data, and ongoing training workflows is complex. The expert sees token-serving efficiency and software optimization as more durable sources of margin than simply buying GPU capacity cheaply and reselling it. Demand for AI infrastructure is likely to remain strong: the expert judged adoption to be at an early stage and was not especially concerned about a near-term supply glut. If US data-center construction slows, existing capacity and providers with projects already underway could become more valuable, while earlier-stage entrants may be disadvantaged. The expert favored greater vertical integration and viewed hybrid providers that combine software capability with access to infrastructure as a promising approach, partly because they may avoid the heavier debt burden associated with infrastructure- led expansion.

Competitive Landscape

Positioning and competitive factors

CoreWeave and Lambda

Focus on large-scale GPU infrastructure, particularly for model training. Moving into managed services and inference could broaden their offering, but may put them in competition with customers who rent their GPUs.

Together AI, Fireworks, and Baseten

Inference platforms with growing emphasis on open models, post-training, and model customization. The expert highlighted Together AI’s serving scale and performance, while noting that this space rewards rapid optimization.

Hyperscalers

The expert viewed major cloud providers as less focused on rapidly adopting and optimizing new open models than specialist inference firms.

Open-model providers

Chinese models are popular on inference platforms; newer US open models may reduce customer concerns about relying on Chinese models. Licensing restrictions can shape which models platforms can offer and their margins.
