Companies: VST US, SIE DE, EMR US, GE US

Joshua Winget (JW) Supply Chain Manager, Power Generation Plant at NRG Energy Inc

Anthony DeRuijter (AD) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • Vistra's (NYSE: VST) supply chain pressures for existing generation assets and impact on running costs
  • Development bottlenecks for new builds, including lead time and cost headwinds for key equipment categories
  • Brownfield vs greenfield project economics

Key Insights

  • Supply chain stress for power generation has "multiplied by five", with trends pushing data centres to new generation compounding equipment shortages across turbines, transformers and compressors
  • Tariff impacts accumulate through individual component sourcing through the value-chain, creating hidden 10-20% cost increases across assembled equipment needed by independent power producers such as Vistra
  • Expert notes generator step-up transformer lead times from Siemens have ballooned to four years from 18 months, with costs rising c15-18%. Large turbine lead times have not shortened, and vendors such as GE Vernova have increased upfront milestone payments to 20-30% from c5%
  • Computer chip shortages could eventually cascade into power generation automation systems due to older plants running for longer and requiring major upgrades to remain efficient
  • Expert views coal-to-gas conversions, such as Vistra's Coleto Creek repower, as largely uneconomical. CAPEX need rivals building an entirely new unit, given heavy capital equipment requirements
