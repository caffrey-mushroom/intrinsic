SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a software-services partner, described Salesforce demand as growing despite limited license expansion. Buyers are consolidating vendors and negotiating for lower prices, while software providers seek larger bundled deals and longer contracts. The discussion covered new customer wins, renewals, AI adoption, competitive dynamics, and the outlook through 2027.

Key Insights

The expert said Salesforce growth exceeded the practice’s conservative forecast, but growth increasingly depends on expanding existing accounts rather than winning new customers. CRM remains difficult to displace because of user familiarity and extensive customization. In larger companies, the expert sees little evidence of plans to replace Salesforce or Dynamics with custom-built systems. AI adoption is progressing gradually. Customers want to establish returns, manage costs, and develop successful internal examples before expanding deployment. Bundling and long commitments benefit both vendors and service partners: customers may receive deeper discounts, while vendors strengthen customer retention and partners gain implementation work. The expert described smaller firms as more likely to consider replacing SaaS, while analytics and reporting tools may face greater risk than core CRM platforms.

Competitive Landscape

Company or offering

Market position and observations

Salesforce

Strong in mid-market and enterprise CRM. Bundling products such as Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, Slack, and Tableau can deepen customer reliance. AI adoption is measured, and buyers are pressing for discounts.

Microsoft Dynamics

Major incumbent and competitor. Like Salesforce, it benefits from CRM switching costs and is pursuing smaller customers with more executive attention.

HubSpot

Attractive to SMB and mid-market customers; its Breeze AI product reportedly reaches a larger initial share of eligible users. It is less competitive with very large enterprises and may be used to negotiate better pricing from rivals.

Large systems integrators

Reportedly pursuing larger mid-market engagements more often as AI challenges parts of traditional consulting work.

Custom-built software

More relevant among smaller businesses. The expert sees material risks and limited displacement of core CRM among larger companies.
