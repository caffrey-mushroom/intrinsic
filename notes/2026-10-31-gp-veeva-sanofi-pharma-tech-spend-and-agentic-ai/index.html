SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, who works in commercial operations at a large pharmaceutical company, discussed software spending, AI adoption, vendor selection, and the competitive position of Veeva and Salesforce. Pharma software budgets are expected to grow modestly, with spending shifting toward AI, automation, analytics, and connected commercial workflows. Companies are also consolidating vendors and seeking to replace fragmented systems with solutions that can scale.

Key Insights

AI is moving into practical commercial tasks, including HCP targeting and sequencing, next-best-action recommendations, content development, and medical, legal, and regulatory (MLR) review. More autonomous AI is still experimental and not yet broadly mature. Data quality, system interoperability, governance, privacy, compliance, and measurable return on investment are the main barriers to wider AI deployment. Current AI use primarily automates repetitive work and supports staff; it has not led to broad headcount reductions or replaced strategic decision-making. Replacing an entrenched CRM can be highly disruptive because of integration requirements, training, parallel operations, and risks to business continuity. A competitor must demonstrate better outcomes or economics to justify a switch. Pharma buyers do not automatically favor an integrated vendor’s weaker product. They may prefer best-of-breed tools if the functionality gap is significant. The expert sees AI becoming a standard part of software purchasing decisions, alongside pressure to reduce point-solution sprawl.
