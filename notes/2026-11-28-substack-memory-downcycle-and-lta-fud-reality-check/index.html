Exaggeration and truth in the memory downcycle and LTA FUD

Damnang

Nov 28

READ IN APP

Memory company stock prices have fallen a lot. As of the November 27 close, SK hynix’s stock price was 63% lower than its 52-week high, and Samsung Electronics and Micron were also 52% and 48% lower than their own 52-week highs.

The so-called experts who were optimistic about the memory market also suddenly started to change their outlook. According to them, memory prices already hit the top, and when prices turn down, customers can break their long-term agreements (LTAs) at any time, so losses like in 2023 will come back. Their basis is that the memory industry has always repeated boom and bust.

Of course, the stock price does not always follow earnings. Because the stock price reflects expectation or worry ahead of earnings, investors must always be careful of this kind of stock price flow.

But is changing even the industry outlook only for the reason that the stock price fell really the right analysis?

Some of the people who spread the strong trend logic until a few months ago began to put forward the cycle logic as the stock price fell. I think that this kind of analysis is not the right analysis.

Having said that, it is not that I am also trying to say memory is unconditionally only good. Investors must look straight at the stories being heard from the field now. The worries that we really must look at carefully among the FUD (claims that grow fear, uncertainty and doubt) going around the market, and the talk that people make only because the stock price fell, without knowing even the basics, must be distinguished. In this article, I examined the four FUDs going around the market one by one with numbers, divided which worry is right and which worry is an exaggeration, and then summarized my outlook.

Is the 2028 Memory Downcycle Real?

Damnang

·

Sep 15 Read full story

FUD that prices are turning down

1. The claim that memory prices have turned down

On November 25, TrendForce lowered its 2027 memory price forecast, with order cuts by Chinese cloud companies as the reason. In the actual market too, the spot price of the DDR4 8Gb chip fell 7.9% in four weeks, from $47.04 on October 27 to $43.32 on November 24. The spot price is the price where demand changes appear the fastest, so after this announcement, analyses that memory prices hit the top are spreading in the market.

But the thing that must not be misunderstood is that the product whose spot price fell a lot is DDR4, an old chip. Memory companies make most of their sales at the contract price decided in advance with big customers. SK hynix decided more than 50% of its 2027 to 2028 production capacity, and Micron more than 75% of the volume to ship for one year from September 2026, by contract, and this contract price is still going up even now. TrendForce, which is lowering its forecast, also saw the size of the quarterly increase of the 2027 DRAM contract price shrinking from 5 to 10% to 0 to 5%, and it sees the decline as starting from the first quarter of 2028. This decline also came out from the assumption that the server shipment growth slows in 2028, and it did not present the size of the decline.

Damnang’s take

  • Spot price: as the price that distribution companies buy and sell between each other day by day, it moves the fastest, but the portion it takes in memory company revenue is small.- Contract price: the price that a memory company newly decides with a big customer, usually every quarter. Most of memory company revenue is sold at this price.- Long-term contract price: the price attached to an LTA that sets the minimum volume to buy over several years. It moves following the market, but it mostly moves only inside a floor and a ceiling.

Of course, in the memory industry until now, spot price declines often led to contract price declines. For example, the end of 2018 was like that. At that time, memory companies newly decided contract prices every quarter, so there was no mechanism to block customer requests to cut prices, and DRAM contract prices fell for more than one year until 2019.

But the reason I see now as different is that server DRAM demand is growing explosively. On top of this, a new variable called HBM has appeared. As HBM production increases, the wafers that can be turned to general DRAM decrease. So the forecast that DRAM supply will be short through 2027 and 2028 comes from both memory companies and brokerages. Moreover, server DRAM contract prices now mostly move only inside the floor and ceiling decided in the LTA. Even if the spot price goes down, the contract price of LTA volume does not go below the floor.

2. The claim that 2028 prices will be half and profit will fall 89%

In a scenario it released at the end of November, a Chinese brokerage saw the 2028 price of commodity DRAM (general DRAM without HBM) becoming half, from an average of $16 per GB in 2027 to $8. It even talks about $4, down 75%, in the worst case. Also, on November 20, a Korean brokerage saw SK hynix’s 2028 operating profit falling 89% compared with 2027.

What is the logic of their claim? The Korean brokerage sees that inventory is already piled up at the distribution stage now. It is the claim that as customers bought volume in advance to prepare for the supply shortage getting worse, 2026 DRAM shipments increased much more than demand. This brokerage expected that from the second to third quarter of 2027, customers will reduce purchases to use this inventory, AI infrastructure investment will also slow, and memory companies will continue expansion, so prices will fall sharply.

In other words, they see that data center investment will decrease, so memory demand will decrease, while supply will increase through fab expansion. It is the logic that the current revenue increase is a temporary phenomenon made by phantom demand, so it will go back to normal in 2028. Is this claim really right?

Let’s look at supply and demand first. After the US midterm election, a governor candidate’s pledge to stop data center construction came out, but this kind of regulation only delays the construction timing. There was also news again that the Texas state government restarted, after two months, the data center environmental permits it had stopped in September. According to the story I heard in the field, customer orders are the same even after the midterms, and memory companies are supplying only 60 to 70% of the volume customers want. Also, according to NVIDIA, its supply commitments grew from $279 billion to $390 billion in just one quarter, and it is mainly because of memory procurement over several years. Even the Chinese brokerage that released the half-price scenario sees 2028 DRAM supply being 4% short of demand even if memory companies continue expansion. In a situation like now, where demand does not decrease and supply is extremely short, it is logical to see that it is almost impossible for the price to go down by half.

Also, there are too many reasons why the logic that customer-side inventory increased is hard to accept. The report estimated that customers “seem to be accumulating” excess inventory, but if they really saw memory prices going down, wouldn’t there be no reason for customers to buy volume in advance at an expensive price like now? I see that this is likely a forced assumption to fit the 2028 memory downcycle that was put as a premise. Customers bought in advance because they saw the shortage becoming worse in 2027, and even the report saw SK hynix’s DRAM average price going up 8% more in the first quarter of 2027. If the shortage continues, inventory bought in advance does not remain and is used as it is. Phantom demand comes from duplicate orders that can be canceled, but now big customers have signed LTAs under which they pay even if they do not take the goods, and they even paid deposits. The only volume where customers can reduce purchases is the volume outside LTAs, and even that is taken by other customers who could not get all the volume they wanted. Actually, at the start of the fourth quarter, some Chinese cloud companies reduced 2027 server DRAM orders outside LTAs, but North American cloud companies took that volume right away.

On top of that, the 89% decline forecast of the Korean brokerage is a number that does not consider LTA volume at all. Also, the media is not covering it, but even if you calculate by the worst assumption of this brokerage, SK hynix’s 2028 operating profit is KRW 34.6 trillion, more than KRW 23.5 trillion in 2024. When you look at the current stock valuation, even if this number is true, the current stock price is not expensive. Even by this brokerage’s estimate, the operating profit SK hynix earns for the two years of 2027 and 2028 is KRW 348 trillion, 44% of the market cap of KRW 787 trillion on November 20. The Chinese brokerage also saw that industry gross margin is 60% even if the price goes down to $4. Looking at these extreme examples too, memory companies make a big profit in 2028, unlike in past downturns.

FUD that LTAs will break

An LTA is a long-term supply contract in which the customer decides the minimum volume to buy over several years and agrees to pay for it even if it does not take the goods. This kind of condition is called take-or-pay. Customers leave deposits or bank guarantees as collateral that they will keep the contract.

3. The claim that LTAs break when prices fall

It is the claim that if memory prices go down a lot, customers will break LTAs even if they have to pay a penalty. People who make this claim give examples such as Ford canceling a KRW 9.6 trillion battery contract with LG Energy Solution, and Microchip accepting customers’ delivery delays in a downturn. They also point to the fact that Samsung Electronics’ quarterly report says multi-year contract terms can change depending on customer demand.

For this claim to be right, the market price has to become lower than the LTA floor. Before that, the LTA price also goes down following the market price, so even if a customer breaks the contract, there is no money to save, and it only loses the deposit it left. Even if the price goes down to the floor, the profit of memory companies remains big. Micron said that even if it sells at the floor price, its gross margin would be much higher than even 61%, the highest level in the 2018 boom.

The claim that customers break LTAs even though prices did not crash is the same as saying data centers do not need HBM anymore. But this is only a premise made without any understanding of AI computing. HBM is not a perfect memory structure, but most AI accelerators used in data centers now do not run without HBM. Actually, at its October earnings release, SK hynix said its DRAM and HBM volume is practically sold out until 2029. According to the stories heard from the field, Micron and Samsung Electronics are not very different either.

A customer who breaks the LTA and wants to newly buy memory at a cheap price must give up HBM volume. HBM is developed with the memory company from when the accelerator is designed, and it can be used only after passing qualification. Is there any reason for a memory company to give HBM volume again to a customer who broke the contract at will and tries to buy again later? People who make this claim do not understand the memory business that has changed now at all, and they see the market only as the old memory business that sold commodities you could buy anywhere. It is easy if you think about the relationship between NVIDIA and SK hynix. In June, the two companies signed a multi-year contract to develop and supply next-generation memory together. After that, there was even an analysis that the Rubin volume ramp would be delayed to 2027 because of HBM4 yield problems, but NVIDIA has no choice but to go with SK hynix until the end. It is because SK hynix took 50% of the HBM market in the second quarter of 2026, and newly qualifying HBM from another company takes more than one year. Even NVIDIA, the biggest HBM customer, cannot change the supplier easily, so for a customer that broke an LTA, getting HBM again is more difficult.

By what I heard from industry officials, some hyperscalers are paying unusually big advance payments and are trying to take the memory volume that was allocated to NVIDIA first until now. In the field, there is also a story that as the Rubin volume ramp gets delayed, part of the 2027 HBM that was set as NVIDIA’s share is moving to ASICs. Samsung Electronics, which is pushing the industry’s fastest speed, supplies HBM4 to both NVIDIA and Google, and it can choose better conditions. Now, when customers fight each other over HBM volume, a customer who breaks the LTA only hands that volume over to another customer.

4. The claim that LTAs do not show on the books

Memory companies announced that they signed dozens of LTAs with customers. But if you look at the company books, the money received in advance from customers did not increase that much. The people who make this claim raise this number and see that because the LTA is a weak contract in which the customer paid almost no money, it can be broken anytime.

In my thinking, this is nothing more than an excessive worry. On the company books, the whole LTA contract amount is not written. In the contract liabilities item of the books, only the cash received in advance from the customer before handing over the goods is written. If a customer contracted several years’ worth of volume and paid only part of the price in advance, only the money paid in advance is caught on the books. The contract amount of the volume not handed over yet is written separately, with the name of remaining performance obligations, in the notes that are attached at the back of the financial statements.

If you look at the numbers of Micron, which disclosed its LTAs like this, the difference is big. Micron’s remaining performance obligations are $134 billion, but the contract liabilities on the books are $12.9 billion. If you look at only the books, the LTA looks like it is not even one tenth of its real size. This $134 billion is also a number that Micron set conservatively by multiplying the committed volume by the minimum price.

The collateral that customers entrusted is also not all caught on the books. If a customer submits a letter of credit instead of cash, this collateral does not enter the books. A report also came out that SK hynix entrusts customer deposits to a third party, so this money is not caught on the books.

Nevertheless, the money recorded on the books is increasing fast. Samsung Electronics’ contract liabilities increased by more than KRW 10 trillion, from KRW 14.0 trillion at the end of 2025 to KRW 24.1 trillion at the end of September 2026. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix will write remaining performance obligations for the first time in the notes of their annual reports in March 2027. At that time, we can see the LTA size of the two companies in numbers for the first time.

My thoughts

I also do not see memory prices as going up blindly. Cloud and PC companies can reduce purchases while they use the inventory piled up this year, and as consumer device demand becomes weak, the price of old chips like DDR4 is already going down. From the middle of 2027, the new fabs of Micron, SK hynix and Samsung Electronics also start to put out wafers in order. So I see that memory prices will have difficulty going up steeply like now after the second half of 2027.

But even if this kind of pressure comes, it is difficult for prices to drop sharply at once like before. By what I heard in the field, memory companies have set 50 to 70% of their sales volume as LTAs with a price floor, and the HBM price is decided only once a year. New fabs also take time to build the cleanroom and fill it with equipment, so meaningful volume comes out only in 2028.

As the center of AI computation moves to agents that work for several hours each, demand has also become different from before. Memory bandwidth and capacity came to decide performance, and DRAM for CPU servers that run agents is also needed more. On top of this, Physical AI, which is not even caught in demand forecasts yet, is added. In a podcast in November, the CEO of Figure, a humanoid robot company, said memory companies turned all their production capacity to smartphones and servers, so there is no volume to give to robot companies. He said even smartphone companies cannot get memory on time, and humanoids are not in the demand forecasts of the memory fabs being built now either. If robot demand is also added, the memory shortage can last longer. If DRAM sales volume increases 20 to 30% in 2028, revenue is similar to 2027 even if prices go down 20%.

So I see that memory companies remain as companies that steadily make high profit, unlike the previous cycles. In the structure where the customer pays money in advance and contracts several years’ worth of volume, even if the price increase stops, it is difficult for the profit to drop sharply or to turn to a loss like the past downturns. And I am seeing that the structure that makes this possible is exactly the LTA structure based on customer-customized design, which continues to HBM and further to custom memory.

Current stock prices already reflect the forecast that profit will decrease a lot. SK hynix’s market cap of KRW 787 trillion on November 20 is 2.4 times KRW 333 trillion, its third quarter operating profit extended to one year. A few months ago, when stock prices were the highest, the market was full of expectations that prices would go up endlessly, and now the market is full of worries about half price and an 89% decrease. Meanwhile, DRAM contract prices kept going up and customers put in more deposits. For anything, you should be careful when the market is full of euphoria, and you should look for opportunity when it is full of fear. I see that memory stocks are on the fear side now.

SK hynix returns about KRW 100 trillion, 13% of the market cap on November 20, to shareholders this year through dividends and buybacks. On November 27, saying that the stock price became much lower than the company value, it doubled the daily buyback order from about 350,000 shares to about 700,000 shares. Based on the closing price of KRW 1.11 million that day, it is up to KRW 777 billion worth per day, and even after this buyback ends, it will continue buying after a board resolution. The company that knows the LTA terms with customers and the 2027 to 2028 orders the best is buying its stock like this with its own money. If the company saw 2028 profit decreasing 89%, would it buy stock like this?

In the January and April 2027 earnings releases, we can check whether contract prices keep going up. If even the LTA size of Samsung Electronics and SK hynix is confirmed in the March annual reports, the basis of the claim that LTAs break and of the 2028 crash forecast becomes weak. I see that a chance for memory stocks to rebound again can come in early to mid 2027, when this FUD disappears.

On the other hand, if delivery delays of major customers, contract breaks of customers with weak credit, 2028 HBM prices returning to the 2026 level, or expansion continuing even while prices go down come out, I will change my judgment. The LTA renegotiation that I picked as a condition of a downcycle in “Is the 2028 Memory Downcycle Real?“ on September 15 has not happened yet either.
