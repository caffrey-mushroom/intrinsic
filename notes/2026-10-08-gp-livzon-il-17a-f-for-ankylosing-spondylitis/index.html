SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed a Phase 3 trial of a domestically developed dual IL-17A/F inhibitor for active ankylosing spondylitis after inadequate response to conventional therapy. It covered patient representativeness, efficacy, safety, dosing convenience, and competitive positioning against established IL-17A and IL-17A/F biologics.

Current treatment generally begins with NSAIDs, followed by biologics when response is inadequate. IL-17A inhibitors are frequently selected as first-line biologic therapy. The trial population broadly resembled routine moderate-to-severe patients, although prior exposure to TNF inhibitors was unclear.

Key Insights

The expert ranked efficacy in the upper-middle range among approved biologics but cautioned that cross-trial comparisons are not definitive. ASAS40 is now the key efficacy benchmark, while ASAS20 represents a less demanding response threshold. The increase in response through week 52 was considered typical for biologics and indicative of durable efficacy. Responses commonly plateau around this point. Dual IL-17A/F inhibition is theoretically preferable to IL-17A inhibition alone, but meaningful clinical superiority remains unproven. Pricing and reimbursement will likely matter more than mechanistic differentiation. At price parity, the dual inhibitor should have an opportunity; a substantial premium could erase its practical advantage. Injection-site reactions may negatively affect patient experience, depending on severity and need for treatment. The absence of new inflammatory bowel disease was encouraging but insufficient to establish long-term gastrointestinal safety.
