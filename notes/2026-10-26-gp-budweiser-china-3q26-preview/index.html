SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed a leading international brewer’s China business, particularly its dominant South China operation. Recovery remained uneven: eastern Guangdong and southern Fujian improved as customer stability, staffing, inventory freshness, and sell- through normalized, while the Pearl River Delta continued to struggle with distributor complexity, management turnover, channel conflict, and excess capacity.

The expert expects the brewer’s performance to remain stagnant in upcoming quarters because improvements outside its core market cannot offset weakness in Guangzhou, Foshan, and surrounding areas.

Key Insights

South China contributes nearly 70% of the brewer’s annual revenue, making regional disruption especially damaging. The Pearl River Delta remains the central problem. Its distributor relationships and internal organizational issues are deeply entrenched and difficult to resolve without sacrificing near-term sales targets. The brewer retains a commanding position above RMB 10 per bottle but is losing the strategically important RMB 8–10 segment to domestic competitors. Shifting incentives from immediate invoice discounts toward reimbursement after verified market activity should reduce cross- region dumping and improve price discipline. However, slower reimbursement may strain smaller distributors. Production profitability depends more on capacity utilization than modest changes in raw-material costs. Large factories face pressure as sales volumes decline. Nightlife remains the brewer’s strongest channel, while restaurants, e-commerce, and instant retail are weaker. The company entered digital channels later than major domestic peers. Distributor inventory turnover was described as among the industry’s weakest.
