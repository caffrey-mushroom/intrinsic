SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a mission-critical facilities engineer with more than 20 years in the sector, discussed data center development, permitting, backup power, and potential regulatory effects following an election. The view was that demand for data centers remains strong, but new projects may face greater scrutiny over emissions, water use, grid capacity, noise, and community concerns.

Projects with permits, approvals, and equipment orders are expected to face little disruption. Early-stage projects are more exposed, as they may need revised designs or encounter longer approval timelines. The expert expects any meaningful regulatory effects to take time to emerge.

Key Insights

The expert characterized the data center expansion as a durable trend, arguing that demand for digital infrastructure makes a broad slowdown unlikely. Projects that secure electrical, air-quality, and water permits are comparatively well protected. Developers are therefore trying to advance approvals, while unpermitted projects face greater uncertainty. Regulatory changes could affect generator design, emissions controls, fuel use, operating limits, and noise mitigation. Local rules vary, so impacts may differ by site. Public concerns about electricity bills, water availability, and environmental effects could influence approvals. The expert believes better community understanding and credible assurances about utility impacts are important. Battery systems may help with short-duration load shifting and reliability, but are not expected to replace diesel generators as the primary backup source. Batteries add cost and permitting requirements. The moderator explored possible order declines and impacts on suppliers. The expert saw no immediate evidence of a downturn and said a substantial regulatory shock would be needed to shift purchasing leverage.

Company or technology

Position and observations

Caterpillar, Cummins, and Rolls- Royce

Identified by the expert as the main generator suppliers encountered in the market. The expert did not expect tighter standards to favor or disadvantage one over the others.

Diesel generators

Expected to remain central to backup power, despite potential emissions, fuel, noise, and operating restrictions.

Battery energy storage

Positioned as a supplementary reliability and load-management option. High costs and limited duration constrain its role as a replacement for generators.

Gas turbines and reciprocating engines

Alternative on-site generation options, but permitting, service availability, and the shortage of skilled maintenance workers may limit adoption.

Modular nuclear and fuel cells

Discussed as possible technologies, but the expert had little direct experience with them.
