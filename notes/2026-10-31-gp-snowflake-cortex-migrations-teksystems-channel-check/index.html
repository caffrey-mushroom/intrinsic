SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, a systems-integration and cloud-alliance leader, discussed enterprise demand for Snowflake, AI adoption, workload migrations, and competition from Databricks and hyperscaler platforms. Traditional migrations from legacy databases remain the largest source of work, while Snowflake’s Cortex tools are adding new AI projects. The expert expects most large organizations to use several data and cloud platforms, choosing based on the workload.

Key Insights

Faster, cheaper migrations are expanding the range of legacy workloads customers consider moving to Snowflake. AI-assisted code conversion is improving project economics. Cortex adoption is progressing beyond pilots, especially among customers that have standardized on Snowflake. Natural- language analytics, document processing, support-ticket analysis, and AI agents are recurring use cases. Microsoft Fabric is a competitive risk when customers are less invested in Snowflake and can benefit from Microsoft bundling or contract discounts. Established Snowflake customers are generally less inclined to replace a working platform. The expert sees open-weight models as important for scaling enterprise AI affordably, while retaining access to leading models for more demanding tasks. Databricks and Snowflake often serve different strengths: Databricks in data and AI engineering, and Snowflake in governed enterprise data and analytics. The moderator pursued further detail on their overlap and on Fabric’s competitive effect. Iceberg can reduce consumption through Snowflake, but the expert views its flexibility as a strategic way to retain and expand customer relationships.
