SUMMARY

Overview

An executive at a major European omnichannel sports retailer discussed footwear demand, pricing, inventory, promotions, and forward orders. Q3 sales grew solidly, supported heavily by football-related demand and higher-priced running products. Underlying volumes were nearly flat, largely because of continued Nike weakness.

Performance footwear outpaced lifestyle, while premium products performed substantially better than entry-level and mid-priced footwear. Men’s products grew strongly, whereas women’s declined.

Key Insights

Nike remains the retailer’s greatest concern. Excess supply, weak franchise management, and limited product innovation are depressing full-price sales. Salomon offers the largest perceived upside, with consumer demand exceeding available supply. ASICS and New Balance are strongly positioned through premium running products, authenticity, and further geographic expansion potential. The expert became more positive on On after reviewing its upcoming pipeline, although expansion into football was viewed as costly, low-margin, and risky. Adidas remains the largest brand, but slowing running growth, product-quality concerns, and uncertainty around terrace-style durability warrant caution. Inventory and promotional health were considered acceptable overall because orders were reduced early.
