Agenda

  • Drivers and risks behind Iqony's regulated returns in district heating and flexible power
  • Execution risks behind converting existing power sites into flexible energy hubs
  • Competitive differentiators across business segments
  • M&A strategy and integration process

Key Insights

  • District heating prices in Germany vary wildly, ranging from EUR 0.09 to EUR 0.35 per kilowatt-hour, due to the absence of direct regulatory price controls. New German law, which is expected by year-end 2026, will likely cap margins at 6-8% above costs, potentially reducing profitability for well-positioned Iqony grids
  • 40% government CAPEX cash subsidy for green heating infrastructure is at 50% risk of disappearing, as fiscal pressures mount and green energy ranks low in voter priorities
  • Warmer winters are quietly eroding revenues. Customers are reducing contracted heating capacity purchased years ago under colder climate assumptions, shrinking Iqony's base fee income
  • Legacy contracts using outdated pricing formulas represent real portfolio risks. Mid-contract formula changes are nearly impossible, making short 1-3-year contract cycles critical going forwards
  • On European court ruling, unlike Germany's standard three-year claims window, suppliers now face unlimited retrospective liability if pricing formulas are found non-compliant
