CSP earnings test the returns on AI spending

Nov 2

READ IN APP

Over the past few weeks, we have been highlighting a market that is becoming more demanding about capex and free cash flow. This quarter’s CSP results reinforced that shift: promises of incremental capex are receiving less credit, and increasingly raise concerns about when the spending will generate returns. Strong cloud growth alone is no longer enough to settle the debate. Investors want a clearer path from capacity commitments to utilization, earnings and cash generation. Oracle’s guidance for peak capex in FY27–FY28, followed by a decline in FY29, added to infrastructure investors’ anxiety about a spending peak, even as the prospect of better cash flow offered reassurance to Oracle’s own investors.

Anthropic’s S-1 is especially relevant in that context. Roughly $132b of cash provides substantial funding, but about $800b of compute commitments makes the timing of revenue and costs central to the story. Our first financial model works through those obligations, margins and customer concentration. For CSPs, the same commitments can support growth while increasing dependence on a small number of large AI customers; the scale of a contract does not by itself establish its eventual cash return.

The operating picture remains uneven. Microsoft reported 48% Azure growth and accelerating Copilot revenue, while Google Cloud grew 102.6% alongside greater infrastructure spending pressure. ServiceNow’s smaller-than-expected beat also showed how much work can sit between an AI order and production revenue. We underestimated the deployment support required. Across these businesses, we are watching how quickly demand converts into profitable revenue, and how much additional spending is needed along the way.

Cybersecurity could become a much bigger focus from here. The roughly $6.3b theft from Binance on October 29, reported as the largest crypto theft on record, makes the scale of the security risk tangible, although AI’s role in this particular attack has not been established. AI is lowering the barrier to attacks and accelerating vulnerability discovery, creating a growing need for remediation and continuous protection. But a sharp increase in AI-enabled attacks may not translate quickly into revenue for listed security vendors. Many products are sold through subscriptions rather than consumption-based pricing, and customers still need to approve budgets and make purchasing decisions. Some of the incremental spending may also accrue to AI labs. We see a potentially significant longer-term demand shift, while looking for evidence in new budgets, contracts and guidance before assuming a near-term revenue acceleration.

Subscribed A portion of our research is reserved for Premium subscribers and is not distributed via Substack. Below is a snapshot of what Premium subscribers received this week beyond the Substack feed.

Preview & Review | GOOG 26Q3: Search Slows as Cloud Growth Accelerates

Search revenue grew 12%, consistent with our preview’s caution on advertising, while Cloud grew 102.6%. Strong cloud demand provides support, but weaker AI Mode monetization, competing ad budgets and higher infrastructure spending complicate the earnings and cash-flow outlook.

Review | SNDK FY27Q1: Pricing Momentum Slows

Revenue of $11.0b and EPS of $49.51 exceeded guidance, but the next-quarter revenue guide of $12.2–12.6b fell short of expectations. We lowered near-term ASP growth assumptions as price increases moderate, while supply expansion and capital spending remain key uncertainties.

Review | INTC 26Q3: Server Strength Meets PC Weakness

Revenue of $16.5b and non-GAAP gross margin of 44.1% exceeded expectations, supported by server demand and better yields. The weaker 4Q outlook reflects memory shortages affecting PCs and the Fab 28 stoppage, with much of the additional server capacity arriving only in 27H2.

Review | FTAI 26Q3: Aerospace Margins Recover and Power Visibility Improves

Adjusted EBITDA of $348.3m beat consensus by 9.2%, driven by aerospace product margins recovering to roughly 31%. More than 100 contracted Mod-1 units and $234m of prepayments support our increase in the 2027 delivery forecast from 80 to 90 units, with acceptance and execution still critical.

Preview & Review | ATI 26Q3: Engine Demand and Pricing Drive Another Raise

Adjusted EBITDA of $327.9m exceeded our $316m preview estimate, validating the expected contribution from engine demand and contract price increases. ATI raised full-year EBITDA guidance for the third time to $1.19–1.20b, while the investor day should clarify 2027 pricing and capacity.

Review | Delta 26Q3: Inventory Charges and Component Constraints Weigh on Margins

Gross margin of 34.1% and EPS of NT$9.04 fell short of expectations as inventory charges, input costs and mix weighed on results. We continue to see power-discrete availability as a constraint on deliveries, with pricing and HVDC shipments important to the 4Q recovery.

Preview | TSEM 26Q3: Expansion Supports Shipments, Competition Remains a Longer-Term Test

Faster equipment installation and qualification could support near-term revenue as silicon-photonics utilization stays high. Long-term agreements cover more than 70% of capacity through 2028, but largely fixed pricing makes capacity delivery central to growth.

Review | EMC 26Q3: Strong Margins Extend Capacity Planning

EMC reported revenue of NT$60.89b and gross margin of 37.7%, with high-end mix and tight materials supporting further CCL price increases. Customer agreements extend capacity planning through 2030, while we raise the 4Q gross margin forecast to 40.7%.

Preview | NET & FSLY: AI Traffic Supports CDN and Security Demand

AI and agent traffic are supporting usage, WAF, bot management and edge-compute demand. We forecast 3Q revenue growth of 36% for NET and 19.5% for FSLY, while watching gross margins, RPO quality and the durability of pricing.

Research | LLM: Kimi K3.1 Pro Faces a Wider Gap on Updated Tests

The revised AA index gives greater weight to private and held-out tests, placing Kimi K3.1 Pro at 46 versus Opus 5.5 at 58. Its cost per task also exceeds that of an equally scoring GPT-6.1 Sol configuration, highlighting the importance of capability and cost together.

Deep | Unimicron: Tight ABF Supply and Higher-End Mix

Our initiation examines demand from TPU and CPU substrates, larger packages and EMIB-T as material supply remains constrained. We forecast EPS of NT$25.21, NT$49.48 and NT$89.03 for 2026–2028, with capacity and product mix central to delivery.

Research | PCB: TPCA Points to Material Shortages and Higher Specifications

AI servers require larger, higher-layer-count substrates and faster CCL, while glass cloth, HVLP copper foil and equipment supply lag. Supplier estimates put key-material shortages at 5%–30% in 2027, with qualification and production ramps determining when new specifications reach volume.
