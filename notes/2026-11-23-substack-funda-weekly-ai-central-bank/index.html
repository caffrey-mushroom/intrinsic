We have spent the past few weeks highlighting the medium-term uncertainties facing AI: the gap between infrastructure spending and customer revenue, the durability of demand, and the financing needed to sustain expansion. Even so, we did not anticipate the speed or magnitude of this week’s selloff. Identifying a risk does not mean knowing when it will hit, or how far markets will move. Jensen Huang’s sudden departure appears to have set off a chain reaction across an industry whose investment plans depended more heavily on him than many had appreciated.

NVIDIA is often described as AI’s ‘central bank,’ and Jensen personally was central to that role. Our NVDA deep dive explains why: equity investments, capacity backstops, credit guarantees and commitments to suppliers helped turn ambitious expansion plans into financeable projects. His willingness to take risk supported investment well beyond NVIDIA’s own balance sheet. A change in that willingness can affect the terms on which the entire ecosystem raises capital. That is why we focused on financing and capital allocation immediately after his departure. This week’s earnings review reinforces the distinction: NVIDIA raised its FY2028 revenue growth outlook to roughly 75%, but strong current demand does not resolve the uncertainty around future ecosystem support, product development and supply-chain coordination.

Deep|NVDA: The AI Supply Chain in the Post Jensen Huang Era

Nov 13

On November 12, 2026, Nvidia announced that its founder, Jensen Huang, had resigned from his positions as President, CEO, and a member of the Board of Directors, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress was appointed Interim CEO. While the market initially focused on potential market share shifts between GPUs and ASICs, we believe the more important implications lie in three areas: 1) a possible decline in risk appetite in corporate governance; 2) a potential contraction in financing for the AI ecosystem; and 3) a possible reshaping of value distribution across the supply chain.

Read full story

Our new institutional Neolab report follows that financing dependence further downstream. It examines how NVIDIA’s support affects smaller model developers’ ability to secure compute, and how their contracts feed into Neocloud investment and financing. Tighter credit support could raise both funding and compute costs for these labs, weaken contract fulfillment and renewals, and leave their infrastructure providers facing a less certain revenue base. The consequences could extend well beyond the labs themselves, affecting lenders’ confidence, new capacity commitments and GPU rental pricing. We think this deserves much closer attention when assessing how much of the industry’s planned investment will actually be funded and deployed. Against that backdrop, we also published our Anthropic (ANT) deep dive at the time of its IPO listing. We believe it may be the most comprehensive and in-depth research on the company currently available, drawing on extensive customer research to connect enterprise adoption, token budgeting, pricing, model switching and new applications with its revenue outlook. The near-term spending constraints are real, but the competitive picture is also changing. Our analysis suggests that anti-distillation measures and tighter access to advanced compute could make it harder for open-weight developers to close the capability gap, reducing substitution pressure on leading closed-model providers such as Anthropic. These restrictions could strengthen their position even as customers become more disciplined about spending. The report examines both sides in detail, including where new enterprise applications could support the next phase of demand.

Review|NVDA FY27Q3: FY2028 Growth Guidance Raised; Jensen Huang’s Potential Return Remains Key

Nov 18

Since our Preview report, NVIDIA has seen several important developments. Jensen Huang resigned, and the Board stated at the start of the earnings call that it does not intend to reinstate him as CEO. The Board has begun a formal CEO search covering internal and external candidates and established a National Security and Export Control Committee. Near-term results exceeded expectations: FY27Q3 revenue was $115.8b (+20.4% QoQ), and FY27Q4 revenue guidance was $130b ± 3%. FY2028 revenue growth guidance was also raised from 70% to approximately 75%. Kress took the unusual step of reassuring employees during the call and acknowledged internal disagreements, highlighting the challenge of retaining key talent. We continue to believe that whether Jensen Huang returns is an important factor in NVIDIA’s future competitiveness. Without Founder Mode, NVIDIA may find it harder over the long term to sustain the competitiveness of its product roadmap and its supply-chain ecosystem integration advantages.
