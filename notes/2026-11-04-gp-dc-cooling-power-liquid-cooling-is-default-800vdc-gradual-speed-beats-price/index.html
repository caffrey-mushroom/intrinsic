SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, who works on data center power and cooling design, discussed how rising AI and high-performance computing demand is changing facilities from the rack outward. Key topics included liquid cooling, heat rejection, power architectures, modular infrastructure, suppliers, and project execution.

Liquid cooling is increasingly the primary design choice for new projects, while air cooling remains necessary for some workloads. Larger facilities and denser racks are driving bigger cooling equipment and changes in where systems are located. The expert emphasized that equipment availability, climate, water access, and reliability requirements all shape design decisions.

Key Insights

Single-phase, direct-to-chip cooling is established and has substantial room for further improvement. The expert views pumped two-phase cooling as costly and insufficiently proven for large-scale deployment. Chillers remain important despite the growth of dry cooling. Hot climates, water constraints, residual air-cooling needs, and backup requirements support continued demand. 800 VDC is expected to gain traction as rack power rises, but adoption will be gradual. Hyperscalers are likely to lead, with other operators following after systems are validated. Modular electrical and white-space infrastructure is gaining favor because it can reduce on-site labor, coordination effort, commissioning time, and time to operation. Modular thermal systems have less consistent appeal. Speed and equipment availability often outweigh price. Supply constraints are encouraging developers to consider a wider range of suppliers. Rapid production expansion is contributing to more quality problems. The expert expects improvement if demand becomes more predictable, but sees limits to how much quality risk buyers will tolerate.

Competitive Landscape

Market area

Positioning and competitive dynamics

Liquid cooling

Single-phase direct-to-chip leads current designs. Cold-plate and system improvements may extend its advantage; two-phase remains less proven at scale.

Cooling equipment

Dry coolers are gaining share, but chillers remain important for hot conditions, air-cooling loops, and backup capacity. Manufacturers are adapting chillers for higher fluid temperatures.

Modular power

Vertiv, Schneider Electric, Eaton, ABB, Siemens, and Regal Rexnord were cited among suppliers. More vertically integrated firms may be credible but have fuller order books; newer assemblers can sometimes deliver sooner.

Supply chain

Compressors, fans, stainless-steel piping, and related components are constrained. Higher demand is supporting price increases and longer lead times, though the expert did not report widespread extreme price jumps.
