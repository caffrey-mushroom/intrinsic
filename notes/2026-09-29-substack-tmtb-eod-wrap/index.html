#markets

Substack · 2026-09-29

Good afternoon. QQQs +19bps in a choppy session: +55bps at the open, flat by late morning with the 30Y pushing through 5.6%, and modestly green into the close. Semis +1.2% led the way higher, though well off the +2.4% highs from just before 11am, with semicap (AMAT +5.2%; KLAC +3.9%; ASML +3.6%; LRCX +3%) bid on the TSM Texas report and optical (LITE +5.7%; GLW +4.7%; COHR +3.5%) bouncing off Monday’s ECOC hangover. Software -20bps lagged for a second day as app software (MNDY -3.8%; HUBS -2.5%; ADSK -1.9%; TEAM -1.4%) made its lows mid-keynote at an OpenAI Dev Day where the main focus was selling agents into the enterprise, while consumer internet names hit on Monday bounced (DUOL +6.2%; CVNA +5.2%; DASH +4.9%).

Macro didn’t help. The Conference Board’s September confidence print was ugly (81.9 vs. ~89 expected, the lowest since April 2014, with respondents citing fuel costs and jobs), and the curve bear-steepened: 2Y -3bps to 4.89% on the data while the long end kept selling off, with the 10Y +2bps to 5.26% (+30bps in a week) and the 30Y +3bps to 5.59% for the first time since 2002. NY Fed’s Williams said at 2pm that “one further upward adjustment of the federal funds target range may be appropriate late this year,” with no urgency after the September hike; the market still has ~70% odds of a 25bp hike on 10/28, ~40-50bps of hikes for the rest of the year and ~100bps cumulative through next September. Crude -3.6% to ~$89 as Saudi Red Sea exports recovered, Gulf flows got back above 90% of pre-war levels and DOE offered a 40M-barrel SPR exchange. BTC flat at ~$83.5K.

Then, Axios reported around 10:15am that OpenAI’s annualized revenue run rate is up more than 70% since the start of 3Q to almost $70B, with B2B revenue more than doubling over the same period and more consumer revenue added in 3Q than in all of 2025. Compare that with the last number given to the media ($40B on 8/13) and Yipit’s ~$43B (9/17). RI and PHC dug into the math for us in TMTB Slack:

Open AI related names ripped on the news: ORCL +4% went from +1% to +8.4% in about 30 minutes, BE +10.8% touched +15% (although was helped by a bullish MS note as well), and the neoclouds and semis peaked right after.

Second, Dev Day. Less ‘AGI has arrived,’ more ‘please contact your enterprise sales rep.’ The headliner was Dots: always-on agents with their own cloud computer and browser and 4,000+ app connectors, pitched squarely at work and bundled with paid plans (plus a new $500/month tier with 25x the usage capacity of the standard ChatGPT Plus plan and Ultrafast tier up to ~8x faster at ~6x the price). Also: ChatGPT Space, where people and agents co-edit docs, sheets and decks and assign each other tasks; GPT-6.1 Sol at a fifth of Astra’s price; an Ultrafast tier and an enterprise Marketplace where customers can spend their OpenAI commitments with 32 partners (NOW, CRM, HUBS, PANW, CRWD, ADBE, FIG among them). No consumer agent to take on Muse as it seems OAI has ceded the space to META +3.3%, which bottomed at 1:05pm as the keynote started & rallied into the close.

Third, at 1:40pm Bloomberg reported OpenAI is targeting at least $30B at a ~$1.4T valuation after pushing back its IPO, a bridge round in place of an IPO as it focuses on AI-safety concerns; Altman told Bloomberg investors are patient on the IPO timeline.

Outside of that, big Tech dinner at the White House today where Trump said “whoever wins superintelligence wins” and floated renaming AI “Super Intelligence.”

  • Some good debate in TMTB Slack herearound FUNDA’s morning piece saying distillation will get harder. FUNDA argued the recent narrowing between open-weight and frontier proprietary models is unlikely to last as OpenAI/Anthropic/Google increasingly hide raw reasoning traces, making cheap distillation materially harder. Open-weight labs will need more in-house RL, synthetic data and training environments, pushing them toward greater compute spend; FUNDA expects the model gap to widen back to roughly 6–12 months. In TMTB Slack, we officially reached the part of the AI cycle where HF PMs argue about RLVR over lunch. ALC fundamentally disagreed, arguing "China's biggest limitation is compute, not distillation." In ALC's view, the game is now post-training RL, where there's no clear path to distilling a bigger model, and China's large and growing labeled-data ecosystem better explains the recent catch-up. - atl42 countered that the compute gap is exactly why the China labs distill in the first place. Frontier labs are getting very good at hiding chain-of-thought, and those traces are what made RL affordable. Without them, China labs start RL from a weaker base and need more rollouts and compute they don't have, and final-answer distillation alone won't cut it for reasoning and enterprise work. - Arun Dhar landed in between: hidden traces make distillation roughly 10x more expensive, but off a negligible base. He added that RLVR is cheap, reconstruction attacks have gotten much better and the frontier labs distill each other anyway, so "distillation is a bigger problem for investors than it is in SV." atl42 and Arun ended up roughly agreeing the effect is marginal (fewer gradients, a lower RL starting point). atl42 argues those margins compound into a wider lead, especially with enterprises barely using the China labs as is, while ALC says time will tell whether the frontier really pulls away.
