SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, an experienced data center facilities developer, discussed how election outcomes and tighter financing could affect US data center construction. Key challenges include possible state and local restrictions, access to electricity, higher capital costs, and community concerns. The expert expects strong demand to continue, but believes these factors may raise costs and delay projects, especially those not yet funded or approved.

Key Insights

Power access must be addressed early. A site without a credible power arrangement may have little value for data center development. Projects with permits and construction underway are harder to halt than those still seeking zoning changes or approvals. However, legal disputes over power and development agreements may be resolved case by case. Utility infrastructure and data center construction often proceed in parallel. Large transformers can take years to procure, making early coordination essential. The expert sees on-site generation as one response to grid constraints, but permitting and construction can take several years. Fusion could eventually ease power shortages if it becomes affordable and scalable, but it is not yet available at the scale needed. Local opposition commonly centers on electricity and water use, continuous noise, and changes to rural communities. Closed- loop cooling can limit water consumption; persistent noise is harder to mitigate.
