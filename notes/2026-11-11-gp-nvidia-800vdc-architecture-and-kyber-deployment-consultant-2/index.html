SUMMARY

Overview

The expert assesses power-system readiness for NVIDIA’s high-density AI racks, including Kyber shipment timing, 800VDC adoption, rack-level conversion, sidecars, solid-state transformers (SSTs), and supplier capabilities. The discussion emphasizes that architecture choices depend on rack power needs, facility design, safety certification, and serviceability.

Key Insights

Kyber is expected to ship around its planned mid-to-late-2027 window, with no more than an estimated six-month delay. The largest risks are independent certification and availability of 800VDC circuit protection, followed by dense midplanes and power-management electronics. Unipolar 800VDC is favored over ±400V bipolar distribution. Although bipolar systems may simplify protection and use lower- voltage components, the expert believes high-voltage DC service skills and technician comfort make bipolar deployment more complicated. Adoption is tied to rack power density: once racks exceed roughly 200–250 kW, 48V systems may no longer suffice. After protection devices are certified, adoption could ramp sharply if several suppliers qualify in close succession. The expert considers direct 800V-to-12V conversion more likely to prevail than an intermediate 48V stage, while emphasizing vertical power delivery near the chips. Estimated total-cost savings from removing an intermediate stage range from a few percentage points to low double digits. SSTs remain uncertain. The expert questions their role if facilities deliver native 800VDC, and considers end-of-2028 readiness ambitious without more public evidence of field trials, reliability work, and third-party certification. Reliability and safety validation are greater near-term concerns than supply availability. Facility design matters more than operator type: greenfield sites can incorporate new distribution more readily, while brownfield sites may need extensive infrastructure replacement. Sidecars remain important when AC enters the data hall; native DC delivery could make them unnecessary.
