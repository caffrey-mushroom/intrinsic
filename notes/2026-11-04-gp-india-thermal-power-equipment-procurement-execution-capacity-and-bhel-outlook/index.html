SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed India’s planned thermal build-out, procurement models, equipment supply constraints, and the position of a major domestic power-equipment manufacturer. New 800 MW units are being pursued by central and state utilities and private developers. Some projects are extensions at existing sites; others use long-term, tariff-based arrangements that require bidders to secure land and arrange project financing.

The expert expects thermal capacity to remain important even as solar and battery storage expand. However, integrating renewable power with thermal generation remains unresolved, and future plants may need improved operating flexibility. The discussion also covered the manufacturer’s backlog, outsourcing, nuclear opportunities, and aftermarket services.

Key Insights

Announced capacity plans are not all equally bankable, but the expert estimated that a substantial majority could reach binding awards. Established central buyers tend to award contracts more efficiently due to standardized tender documents. Other buyers face greater complexity when dividing projects among many suppliers. The biggest execution concern is limited vendor capacity across equipment, civil works, transformers, and material handling. Overbooking and supply delays can disrupt project schedules. The expert views fabrication outsourcing and supplier development as the fastest way to increase throughput, provided vendor capability and quality controls are thoroughly assessed. Renewable integration has not yet led to material changes in thermal equipment specifications. Flexible operation remains a future design challenge. Nuclear equipment could offer a significant opportunity, but suppliers will need dedicated teams, technical partnerships, and nuclear-specific certification and manufacturing capability.
