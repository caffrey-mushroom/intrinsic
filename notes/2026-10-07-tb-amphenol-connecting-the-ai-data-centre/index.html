Colin Duncan (CD) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • AI data centre demand and Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) and Broadcom (NASDAQ: AVGO) exposure
  • Optical interconnects, compatible pluggables and structured cabling
  • Amphenol's CommScope integration and acquisition-led expansion
  • Decentralised operating model, cross-selling and growth outlook

Key Insights

  • Amphenol's AI datacom strength remains heavily tied to Nvidia-led reference architectures, which pull through its interconnect, cabling and optics content. Greater adoption of Broadcom or custom silicon, or a faster shift towards optical architectures, could weaken that advantage
  • AI clusters can require c10x the physical cabling volume of traditional data centres, materially increasing CommScope's dollar content per deployment, even if its percentage share of the networking bill of materials stays similar
  • CommScope acquisition gives Amphenol direct hyperscaler relationships that historically sat outside its OEM or original design manufacturer-led component model, moving the company closer to the end buyer and broadening its exposure across the AI data-centre stack
  • Halo is benefiting from long Mellanox transceiver lead times, giving operators a compatible alternative that can accelerate GPU-cluster go-lives and avoid costly SLA delays. However, Halo's growth appears more dependent on supply constraints and channel positioning than on a durable hyperscaler design-in advantage
