Good afternoon. QQQs -70bps never saw green on a low volume holiday , hiding another degross out of anything with AI-capex or AI-financing beta while megacaps held in (META +3.4%; AAPL +1.3%; MSFT +1%).

Let’s start with the good news first before talking about the sea of red that was semis today. The day’s big launch landed at 12:55pm when META released Muse Wonder (aka Watermelon), reaching the frontier and only behind Claude Opus 5.5 on AA’s Intelligence Index.

Muse Wonder claims 57.5% on SWE-Marathon vs 46.3% for Opus 5.5, and it’s priced at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens, similar to Opus 5.5. Zuck told the FT META is “already working on the next step up” and will sell the best versions of Wonder directly to businesses while also signaling more spending and rejecting slowdown calls. Wang and Zuck were on the podcast circuit today. We covered them in TMTB Podcast Intel brief from today - the cheeky pint pod with Wang and Zuck worth a full listen if you have the time. FT also had an interview with Zuck. Some key quotes from both:

Part of our philosophy on this is that you can kind of measure the compute strength of a lab by how big its largest single computer is, supercomputer, for being able to do these large training runs… So, I mean, we built this Prometheus cluster, we call it, in Ohio…That's scaling to larger than a gigawatt. And now we're building this Hyperion cluster in Louisiana that's going to scale to 5 gigawatts. Well, I guess the Louisiana one will come online over a couple years.

And that's also a financial advantage for us building out the data centers as opposed to a bunch of the other companies that are paying clouds, whereas they're paying the annual rate, but then at some point the GPUs will be fully depreciated, but we're probably still going to be using them for a while […] the value of even like an H100 at this point is higher than it was last year, right? Muse Wonder will also be “closed”, with its best versions sold directly to businesses.

“I’m much closer to the belief that AI is a technology that can be engineered with normal guardrails and built responsibly than the anthropomorphic vision of AI, which is that it is just going to go do its own thing,”

“A company like Meta is going to do way more things than it does today, but each thing may potentially be able to be done with somewhat fewer people,” Zuckerberg said. “I would guess that in the future, way more people are going to have the job of working with technology to make things than [they] do today.”

The other thing is that we use the models to accelerate our core business, right? So I mean, you have this core business that’s hundreds of billions of dollars and still growing extremely quickly. Ok, back to semis -2.7% (many names down 3%+), which took the brunt of the beating today as we saw more follow-through on the rotation out of semis that started mid-week last year and was turbo-charged by the Thursday’s “Great OpenAI Gross-to-Net Panic of 2026.” While we don’t think the $70B → $50B gross-to-net realization materially changed the narrative (see our Thursday EOD wrap for the glass-half-full vs. glass-half-empty takes), it did add doubt at the margin and reminded investors how much of the AI trade currently rests on the ARR trajectories of two private labs whose financials remain largely opaque. This has become more evident today as investors struggle to figure out what expectations are for Anthropic’s ARR amid worries we might get another sell-everything in reaction to a headline miss, especially when the market is placing so much weight on numbers for which it has so little underlying visibility.

While Zuck said they weren’t slowing spending, BTJ explained the bearish readthrough from Muse Wonder in TMTB Slack here: the frontier was “an effective duopoly that required infinite external capital raising”; if it’s now an oligopoly with META (and potentially GOOGL and xAI), “what is the revised value proposition for investors of investing in the frontier labs?” And VK highlighted another point: “As the hyperscalers launch increasingly capable models, they have less need to pay OpenAI and Anthropic. Therefore, models like Muse Wonder are a double-whammy negative for Anthropic: more competition and less revenue from a huge customer.”

On top of the already tee’d up rotation (not surprising to see more follow through on that front), several other things being passed around further hitting AI sentiment. Just one of those days where every piece of news flow is seemingly negative and attributed to the weakness, with most highlighting Hock Tan’s comments saying capital, not memory is AI’s biggest hurdle and likening some AI investors to “casino gamblers” w/o understanding the tech although he also had bullish comments in same interview saying compute demand exceeds supply into 2030. Steve Eisman comments were also being passed around flagging weak chatbot loyalty/pricing power and investors’ limited diversification, estimating >65% of the S&P is AI-linked.

Other reasons for the sell-off we heard today: NVDA’s reported Reflection AI acquisition talks raising more competition concerns. Nadella’s call for an AI “emergency brake” although investors seem increasingly desensitized to safety headlines. Elsewhere: Memory/HDD pressure following density comments from CXMT’s CEO, with Hynix/Samsung down ~4.5% overnight.

Positioning still skews long semis which has made this a frustrating market for many. Here’s how we framed investor sentiment in our weekly yesterday:

The trade was about as easy as it gets back in May/June. Since then, it’s felt like a knife fight despite many names being up significantly from the Leopold lows: false starts and failed breakouts, mixed reactions to positive news, and lots of chop. As we wrote last week: some debates have shifted in the bulls’ favor, but they haven’t been resolved. That’s been evident in our conversations this week. Our conversations late in the week remained broadly positive on fundamentals, but we heard more frustration about the lack of sustained breakouts & price action. We still feel fine about the fundamentals driving the semi trade, but we’re less convinced about when, or what, will drive a more durable inflection in the vibe/debate.

With semis charts needing repair and sentiment mixed to put it lightly, we're not in the mood to take any ST/MT swings unless charts improve, something changes the prevailing narratives, or we get juicy r/r entries.
