Agenda

  • Capacity growth outlook for new CCGT (combined-cycle gas turbine) builds, including vulnerability of active development to lower-than-expected demand
  • CAPEX expectations and potential cost overrun triggers
  • Supply chain pressures and key equipment lead-time elongation risk

Key Insights

  • CCGT CAPEX has risen c50% over 5-6 years to cUSD 3,000 per kilowatt, with all CAPEX components, including key capital equipment and EPC, poised to inflate
  • EPC-related labour may be the most significant CAPEX inflation driver moving forwards, with a tight labour market driving up costs in real time. Concurrent data centre developments compete for the same pool, exacerbating the issue. By comparison, power island CAPEX is less likely to be a CAPEX shock, given costs are typically locked in once contracts are signed
  • Expert thinks contractors will not accept cost overruns in today's market, leaving utilities and IPPs bearing CAPEX blowout risk. This could pressure CCGT new-builds, given c50%-plus of project CAPEX is committed within the first two years for permits, long-lead equipment, foundations and vertical construction
  • Regarding coal-to-gas conversion complexity, boiler systems were not designed for gas combustion, making conversions capital-intensive and technically demanding, rather than a simple burner swap
