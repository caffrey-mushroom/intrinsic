SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed Miter and competing construction-focused payroll, HR, timekeeping, and accounting platforms. Construction payroll is unusually complex because taxation, union rules, prevailing wages, job costing, and compliance vary by employee location and project. Many small and midsize contractors still depend on paper, spreadsheets, email, bookkeepers, and disconnected software.

Miter is positioned as an early purpose-built, end-to-end platform for commercial and industrial contractors. Its strongest opportunity is among contractors with 50–200 employees, where enterprise systems are often too costly and complicated.

Key Insights

Miter was characterized as the first vendor to effectively address this midmarket need, supported by a modern interface, strong onboarding, suitable pricing, and construction-specific workflows. The market remains in early adoption because most contractors use only isolated digital tools rather than a complete employee- lifecycle platform. Consultant networks around construction ERP systems are critical distribution channels because they reduce implementation concerns and encourage adoption during broader system changes. Miter’s main risk is losing focus by moving too far toward either small businesses or enterprises. Generational ownership transitions could become the largest long-term adoption catalyst as technology-oriented operators replace manual processes. Potential acquirers include construction software platforms, ERP vendors, industrial technology groups, and vertical-software consolidators.
