SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion reviewed treatment sequencing for unresectable or metastatic intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, including frontline chemoimmunotherapy, molecular testing, targeted therapies, resistance testing, and later-line chemotherapy. The expert emphasized that biomarker-driven treatments are highly important after frontline progression and may increasingly move into frontline care.

Key Insights

About 80%–90% of patients in an academic setting can receive frontline gemcitabine, cisplatin, and durvalumab or pembrolizumab. Renal dysfunction, advanced age, and autoimmune disease are common limitations. Durvalumab is preferred largely because it was adopted earlier and embedded into institutional workflows; the expert considers pembrolizumab similarly active. Comprehensive molecular profiling is performed broadly, but results frequently arrive after treatment begins because testing takes approximately three weeks and rarely changes current frontline management. For FGFR2-altered disease, futibatinib is preferred after frontline progression due to its irreversible binding and perceived activity against some resistance mutations. Clinical trials are strongly favored over FOLFOX. The expert considers FOLFOX weakly active, particularly for rapidly progressive or symptomatic disease. If pemigatinib gained a covered frontline indication, the expert would use it for essentially all eligible FGFR2-rearranged patients, prioritizing response, oral administration, and quality of life. Targeted therapy is preferred in second line for IDH1, HER2, BRAF V600E, and other actionable alterations.
