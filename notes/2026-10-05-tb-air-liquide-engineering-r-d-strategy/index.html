Georgia Beattie (GB) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • Comparison of Air Liquide's (EPA: AI) R&D capabilities and IP portfolio against key competitors
  • Impact of in-house engineering and construction on long-term plant economics and execution costs
  • Greenfield development challenges in Europe vs Asia, focusing on resource constraints and project delays
  • Air Liquide's global manufacturing footprint, utilisation and divestment outlook

Key Insights

  • Linde-Praxair merger created outsized margin gains by combining German engineering discipline with Praxair's lean headcount model, generating cEUR 1bn in one-time cost savings. Reaching Linde's 30% profit margin is unlikely, but c25% is achievable via SG&A consolidation, plant digitalisation and expanding the successful Americas pricing model to Europe and Asia
  • Air Liquide excels in electronics – driven by AI and semiconductor demand, with a EUR 5bn-6bn backlog and recent EUR 0.5bn wins – and resilient healthcare, outperforming competitors such as Linde in these niches
  • Air Liquide is culturally risk-averse, avoiding massive single-project bets, such as EUR 1bn-plus mega-projects taken on by Air Products and Chemicals or Linde, and favouring in-house engineering – c75% – to control quality and cost
  • Clean hydrogen costs at EUR 12-14 per kilogram far exceed commercial viability targets of EUR 6-7 per kilogram. Profitability relies strictly on long-term power purchase agreements, EU mandates and subsidies, making start-up delays a key margin risk
