Good afternoon. QQQs +40bps after a choppy session (+60bps at the open, -60bps at the lows.) Semis +30bps gave back most of its +1.5% open and 4% post-NVDA pop as NVDA -3% faded as well. The mood is despondent for semis investors, to put it lightly, as today was just the latest day where any excitement for a rally deflated rapidly. Many of the non-long duration investors I talk to - including us - continue to adopt the posture Druck laid out yesterday: wait and see until things clear up, with many confused about what “clear up’ actually entails although Frontier Lab acceleration is near the top of bulls wish list. Yes, it’s as muddied as narratives get for semis investors with confidence nowhere to be found, among retail or institutional investors.

Positioning and sentiment is pretty washed out according to GS, who said HFs are dumping global Tech at the fastest 6-week pace in a decade (-4.4 z-score). The long/short ratio is down to 1.79 from a 2.9 YTD peak, near 10-year lows. Some other charts from GS via X:

BofA: The SOX has fallen about 38% in 136 trading days. The past four downturns averaged a 32% decline over 253 days. SOX trades at 13.7x forward earnings vs. 17.7x for the S&P 500, and cycle troughs have historically come near 12x.

Consensus has gone from explosive multi-generational cycle at the end of June to “we’re speed-running the semis downcycle” in a matter of 4 months. Quite unprecedented.

Anthropic IPO

The other news is Anthropic pricing tonight with bloomberg putting the # at $83/share upsized to $77B. CNBC reported ~$400B of institutional demand (books closed a day early) plus ~$100B of retail demand. The Hyperliquid perps point to an ANT open of about $96–99.

I went into some positive and negative feedback from investors in our weekly Sunday; almost everyone thinks the IPO will be received well on the first day given ANT is in a narrative sweet spot at the moment and pitches a compelling LT case. Lots of LO love, lots of love from long-duration HFs, but many on the buyside still express plenty of skepticism around ‘27/’28 numbers and next big use case post-coding.

The other interesting topic we’re hearing lots debate around on the buyside is “can ANT do well if the AI trade feels dead?” The main answer is that its a different trade:

To put it succinctly: AI semis are really bets on capex and the logic is that labs can grow revenue on fewer chip dollars per revenue dollar, so ANT can do great while semis keep sliding.

Then there’s the open question where all the $’s flowing to ANT come from, with no great consensus — we’ll find out in the coming weeks.
