Muse Scares Amazon, Doosan’s Largest Bet, ASE’s Test Buildout, TPU Ahead of GB200, Opus 5.5 Release Timothy Chang, Nick Doyle | Friday, September 25, 2026

Executive Summary

  • Positive: META, ASE & Test Equipment, Doosan, TPU Ecosystem, QCOM, NBIS
  • Neutral: CRWV, Nscale
  • Negative: AMZN, CUDA Moat

Muse Scales Personalized Agents and Scares Amazon With nearly 400k GitHub stars, OpenClaw’s success shows strong interest in personalized agents. Meta simply built on it. Nat Friedman acknowledged that Muse was “heavily inspired as a product by OpenClaw”. As we discussed in our September 11 weekly, Meta put security and ease of use at the center of the pitch. That could help explain why Muse passed 2.5 million downloads within 13 days. More broadly, Meta’s competitive advantage is its ability to improve consumer experience. Rather than being first, it makes existing ideas easier to use and brings them to a wider audience. We believe much of Muse’s upside will come from Meta’s existing platforms, which reached 3.6 billion daily users in June.

However, Amazon blocked Muse from purchases on September 20 and cited privacy and security concerns. We think this is a tacit acknowledgment of Muse’s capabilities and its potential to take control of the online shopping experience. Amazon previously restricted ChatGPT’s web access to its site in November 2025. Our September 17 Muse flash note already showed that Muse sent users straight to payment links. If the online shopping experience begins to run through agents, billions of dollars in advertising could move with it. Amazon would also have fewer opportunities to upsell shoppers or charge brands for sponsored placements. Shopify chose to partner with Muse and enable Shop Pay checkout across its stores. We believe the key indicators now are whether other large online retailers follow Amazon or Shopify, and whether Meta and Amazon reach a deal.

ASE’s Test Buildout Leads the OSAT Equipment Surge ASE Technology Holding disclosed equipment purchases on September 18th and 22nd totaling $260M, equivalent to nearly half ASE’s avg. quarterly CapEx from 2020-24:

  • $133M of Advantest tools for SPIL
  • $48M of Teradyne tools for ASE (Singapore)
  • $37M of Murata Machinery equipment for SPIL
  • $42M of Mirle Automation for ASE

The $181M committed to Advantest and Teradyne points to a substantial test-capacity build ahead of 2027. ASE said earlier this year that most incremental leading-edge advanced packaging (LEAP) machinery would go to wafer sort, with equipment deployed in 4Q26 supporting a ’27 ramp. By July, ASE said both wafer sort and final test were near full. Our August note found that Taiwanese OSAT equipment orders had already exceeded 3x full-year 2025 and test-heavy OSATs were seeing overflow demand. The second image shows Taiwan OSAT revenue following equipment orders; the 3MMA rose to nearly $3B in August, well above prior peaks. The Murata and Mirle orders are consistent with factory automation vs. test tools. Filings name suppliers and amounts, but do not identify specific tools. China’s aggregate equipment data obscures back-end strength. Total semiconductor equipment imports were – 5% YTD, while August OSAT equipment imports jumped 200% Y/Y. We see Chinese stockpiling as a possibility. Note these customs series include equipment categories outside pure WFE and record import timing, so we would not equate their growth rates with installed fab capacity. We remain positive on ASE/SPIL and test-exposed equipment suppliers as more advanced packages require greater test capacity.

Doosan Makes Its Largest-Ever Bet on AI CCL In High Density Interconnect, High Demand Incoming, we showed how rising layer counts and the shift to HDI increase material and manufacturing requirements. Doosan’s existing CCL capacity is already stretched, with its Jeungpyeong and Gimcheon plants reporting utilization of 109.8% and 102.7%, respectively. Doosan is responding with 970 billion won ($700 million), the largest investment in its history. The larger piece, about 550 billion won, builds a new plant in China for AI accelerator and network switch CCL. Doosan chose China because that is where the global PCB makers are concentrated. Being close to them speeds up customer response and lets the plant go up faster. The other 420 billion won expand its Korean lines for optical-module CCL. Our checks with Doosan show Nvidia makes up about 60% of its AI server CCL. Its DS-7409 DQN laminates are used by both Nvidia and AMD’s MI450, and TPU and Trainium are testing them through PCB vendors. DS Investment expects VGT and Unimicron to be major customers, and industry chatter says VGT may co-invest in the China plant. EMC is still the main rival. In Nvidia’s CCL Supply Chain Shake Up, we showed EMC overtaking Doosan at Nvidia with Rubin, though we expect Doosan to keep the Strata compute board. Doosan’s new lines don’t start until 2H28, shortage in 2027 remains the worst. Even with aggressive expansion from Doosan, EMC, and others, we still see shortages on high-end CCL in 2028.

Popular Inference Engine Puts TPU Ahead of GB200 Following up on last week’s note, the maintainers of a widely used open-source inference engine released open-source TPU v7 megakernels. On Kimi K3, the popular 2.8T-parameter model, 16 TPU v7 chips deliver 1.4x to 2x the decode throughput of 16 GB200s, with the biggest lead at small batch sizes. On Google’s internal cost basis, we estimate TPU is up to 3.5x better per dollar.

Source: Inferact

The more important signal is who built it. The people who maintain open-source inference software have long written for CUDA first, and NVIDIA benefits from that work largely without paying for it. That free labor is a large part of why CUDA is hard to displace. These maintainers are now putting real engineering time into TPU and want to do more. One of the megakernel’s authors answered the launch with “Can we buy more TPUs plz.” As more of that work goes to TPU, NVIDIA’s software lead gets harder to defend.

Source: X, Inferact, Xuting Liu

Opus 5.5: Cheaper Per Token, Not Per Task This Tuesday, Anthropic launched Claude Opus 5.5 at $4/$20 per million input/output tokens, 20% below Opus 5 and Opus 4.8 ($5/$25), and 60% below Astra ($10/$50). Opus 5.5 also cut cached input from $0.50 to $0.20 (Astra at $1.00). On Anthropic’s launch table, Opus 5.5 beats Fable 5.1 (priced at $10/$50) on all nine benchmarks. On benchmarks where they overlap, Opus 5.5 leads GPT-6 Astra on four of the six benchmarks, trailing on AutomationBench (where Astra took the lead earlier this month), and on Terminal-Bench-Science. Artificial Analysis (AA) ranks it first on its Intelligence Index at 58, versus 53 for both Astra and Fable 5.1.

Source: Tokenomics Dashboard

Cheaper tokens do not guarantee cheaper tasks. At max effort, AA measures ~119K output tokens per Intelligence Index task for Opus 5.5 versus ~27K for Astra, so output cost per task comes to ~$2.38 versus ~$1.35, roughly 1.8x more despite a 60% lower list price. We continue to work on internal benchmarks. We read the cut as a response to OpenAI’s competitive pressure, while open models such as Kimi K3 ($3/$15) keep pressing from below. Pricing across providers is live on the Tokenomics Dashboard. Sonnet 5.5, expected in the coming weeks, will show whether the cut extends down the lineup. Inside SemiAnalysis, Opus 5.5 is gaining traction, reaching 30.7% of our daily reported tokens on September 23, its first full UTC day after launch. However, the accompanying chart provides the broader context: OpenAI’s share of reported costs climbed sharply in September, reaching 52.6% in the seven-day window ending September 23. Astra won the crowd, but Opus 5.5 has us looking back at ol’ reliable.

Source: SemiAnalysis

Quick Hits:

  • Apple-Qualcomm License Renewal: Qualcomm renewed its global patent license agreement with Apple,

effective April 1, 2027. The 2019 agreement was due to expire in March 2025 and included a two-year option, which Apple exercised in 2024 to extend the existing license through March 2027. The latest deal is a fresh renewal beyond that option. In Qualcomm Quits Apple, Keeps the Royalties note, we argued that Apple’s shift to its own modems would phase out Qualcomm’s chip sales while preserving its patent royalties. We estimated roughly $11 per Apple cellular device, or about $3 billion annually across a 280 million-device base, regardless of whose modem Apple uses. The renewal supports that call, though Qualcomm disclosed neither the new duration nor the royalty rate.

  • ClusterMAX 3.0: Our latest testing puts Nebius alongside CoreWeave in Platinum, while CRWV remains

the technical benchmark. We favor NBIS for its combination of managed-cluster quality, premium rental pricing, and substantial customer prepayments, which should help monetize new capacity as GPU supply remains tight. Across 77 reviewed (323 tracked) providers, only 19 earned a medal: reliable, secure, usable compute remains scarce. Next, we plan to apply hands-on testing to bare metal, inference endpoints, post-training systems, sandboxes, models, and harnesses.

  • Nscale’s IPO: Nscale has filed for a US listing with $103.4bn of active and contracted TCV, highlighting the

scale of its planned expansion. But we believe it is misleading investors by advertising managed clusters and inference endpoints it does not actually provide, as discussed in our ClusterMAX 3.0 note. We are also seeing permitting and construction risks from our datacenter team, that could delay turning contracts into revenue. However, we still believe Nscale’s access to land and power, construction experience and strong demand for affordable bare metal give it substantial growth potential. Our upcoming TCO and neocloud coverage will examine whether that growth can generate attractive returns after financing and infrastructure costs.

  • Murata MLCC Expansion: Management is considering another MLCC expansion beyond March 2028, on

top of ¥80 billion of FY26–FY27 equipment spending targeting more than 20% added capacity. We see the proposed expansion as validation of AI-server MLCC demand, with little relief for the 2027 supply

squeeze via our May thesis. The new plan is undecided, and we estimate new lines will take 18–24 months.
