SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed a Phase III trial of a personalized mRNA neoantigen vaccine combined with pembrolizumab for resected stage 2B through stage 4 melanoma. The expert considered the recurrence and metastasis findings unexpectedly strong and potentially practice-changing.

Current adjuvant care commonly uses pembrolizumab or nivolumab, while clinically node-positive disease is increasingly treated with neoadjuvant immunotherapy. Adoption of the vaccine combination will depend on subgroup outcomes, payer coverage, manufacturing capacity, and tissue-handling logistics.

Key Insights

The expert expects the combination to become a preferred adjuvant option if benefits remain consistent across disease stages. Favorable results could strengthen treatment recommendations for stage 2B patients, whose decisions are currently more preference-sensitive. Mature overall survival data are not considered necessary for initial prescribing because existing PD-1 adjuvant therapies were adopted based on recurrence and metastasis benefits. Clinically node-positive patients may remain better suited to short-course neoadjuvant therapy, particularly when a strong pathological response can eliminate further treatment. The most important follow-up is stage-specific analysis, especially for stages 2B, 2C, 3A, recurrent 3B, and clinically node- positive disease. The expert also wants recurrence-site data, particularly for liver and central nervous system metastases. Strong adjuvant efficacy could reduce the future population eligible for metastatic and post-PD-1 therapies.
