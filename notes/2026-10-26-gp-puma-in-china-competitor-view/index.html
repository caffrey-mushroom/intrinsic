SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed Puma’s China sales, product positioning, discounting, distributor confidence, HYROX strategy and the potential impact of Anta’s involvement. The expert described Puma as a franchise-led sports-fashion brand that has lost relevance as consumer demand shifts toward professional running and outdoor products.

Recent performance was characterized as slightly declining overall. Online and outlet activity provided some support, but could not offset weakness in wholesale and full-price stores. Distributor caution has intensified amid high inventory, weak store economics and uncertainty over whether Anta may shift Puma toward direct-to-consumer operations.

Key Insights

Puma’s fashion positioning left it poorly aligned with the market’s return to professional sports. Low-profile, motorsport-inspired and German Army Trainer-style footwear remain its strongest areas. Apparel was viewed as structurally weak and largely supplementary to footwear. Running offers long-term potential but starts from a small base. Puma must invest consistently because its running credibility, visibility and value proposition trail leading competitors. HYROX is more valuable for rebuilding Puma’s professional-sports image than generating rapid sales. Conversion is constrained by its niche audience, competitor usage and expensive Puma products. The expert strongly expects Anta to influence Puma’s China operations. Product support could be beneficial, while aggressive DTC conversion could destabilize distributors and materially hurt near-term sales and profit. Local product-development authority was identified as the most important requirement for a turnaround. Channel localization alone would be insufficient.
