SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined semiconductor automated test equipment (ATE), especially Teradyne and Advantest, as demand grows for AI accelerators, GPUs, and memory. The expert drew on experience in semiconductor test research and services, emphasizing that customer investment decisions depend on product requirements, installed equipment, and expected utilization.

Key Insights

The AI cycle resembles earlier equipment cycles, but data-center assets last longer and involve larger business investments than consumer-device cycles. Capacity buying may be followed by a digestion period. The expert considered the market’s view that Teradyne’s recent quarter could mark a near-term peak reasonable, based on typical short equipment-demand cycles. Qualification is not the same as a production ramp: a product can pass testing and still not reach scaled production. Moving from qualification to capacity can take 6–18 months. Teradyne’s rising inventory may reflect both supply-chain protection and softer demand. Flattening guidance and customer capacity digestion support the possibility of slower purchases. ATE share is sticky because customers reuse equipment, retain staff and test-program expertise, and seek to keep testing costs manageable. However, dual sourcing can provide bargaining leverage and reduce operational risk. The expert suggested comparing wafer output with tester sales and service activity to assess whether test intensity is changing.
