Analyst: What is a use case for inspection? What do you mean by NDT?

Expert: Non-destructive testing is just simply a way of getting an accurate understanding of the integrity of a material without taking it apart or destroying it. You would essentially run a probe across the surface of material and then be able to gain a clear picture of what's happening underneath the surface. You are looking for fatigue cracking, fatigue-based damage or volumetric defects from manufacturing process.

The use case for North Sea oil and gas inspection would be driven partly by regulation. Obviously, if you have got systems that are containing hydrocarbons or, particularly, if they are under pressure, there's a mandatory inspection regime. That's what would be the main driver for hiring an inspection business to meet regulatory requirements. That would be the same for Trac and any other. Also have drivers like decommissioning and how stable an asset is to take apart and what work is needed to support it. To a lesser extent, you might have best practice-type scheduled inspections, ie, to preserve the life of the asset. That's not as common in the North Sea now, but it's obviously very common in renewables. That would be the main thing that would drive work there.

Analyst: Is the hardware used for inspection proprietary to Trac or does the company licence the hardware off the shelf from OEMs?

Expert: A normal model would be for that equipment to be supplied with a technician and it would be charged. It would have a separate chargeable value. Typically, they would own the equipment. That's a pretty standard model, I think.

Analyst: What is a use case for repairs and maintenance?

Expert: In simple terms, this would be fixing things that inspection throws up. Damage that's comes to light under inspection regime or during something like a marine classification survey on a vessel where they have like a five-years survey and they realise there's damage to a hull or whatever. That would create a use case for repair and maintenance services. For companies that are 17020-accredited, which they are, they need to be able to demonstrate that they are not actually using their inspection as a wedge for their repair and maintenance activity. That's why it's split out in a different service line.

Analyst: What is a use case for renewables?

Expert: A use case for renewables, I can tell you exactly, generally, is either scheduled inspection driven by a manufacturer or warranty or best practice. These tend to be newer assets and they want them to last a long time or it would be statutory inspection driven by a regulatory need to keep cranes in service, for example, to keep lifts in service, to keep ladders in service, that kind of thing. There would also be periodic support to construction phase activity, which could be much broader or interventions like a vessel collision and so on. Usually, they fall into an annually recurring regime that is a mixture of best practice integrity inspections and statutory must do inspections.
