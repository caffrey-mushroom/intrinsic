A quick thesis + financial model

If you invest in the AI infra buildout, Tower Semi has to be on your radar.

The setup over the past few quarters has improved materially and I believe this is one of the companies that has a really durable role to play.

They are a specialty semiconductor foundry. Other companies design chips, then use Tower to manufacture them. The part of the business I care about most is silicon photonics (SiPh), where they manufacture the photonic chips used inside optical transceivers and eventually NPO/CPO.

Q2 revenue reached a record $460M, up 24% YoY. Gross profit grew 72%, operating profit more than doubled and net income grew 95%. Q3 guidance calls for $520M of revenue, which would be another 13% sequential increase.

Silicon photonics is driving a huge portion of that acceleration. SiPho revenue grew more than 270% YoY in Q2 and reached a >$680M annualized run rate. Management expects that run rate to reach roughly $1B by Q4 2026.

The customer commitments give us unusually strong visibility into what comes next. They already have $1.3B of contractual SiPh revenue commitments for 2027, backed by roughly $290M of customer prepayments. Management has said its actual 2027 SiPho expectations are substantially higher than that contractual minimum. Customer capacity commitments for 2028 are larger again.

And they are meaningfully expanding production to meet those commitments. Its current SiPh and silicon-germanium (SiGe) capacity expansion is already being absorbed by customers, while the new Japan build adds another large wave of capacity for 2028 and beyond.

Today, most of the optical demand comes from 800G and 1.6T connections. Tower expects NPO to become a meaningful portion of SiPho shipments in 2027. Tower and NewPhotonics are already shipping laser-integrated 800G and 1.6T products, with 6.4T NPO products planned for the first half of 2027.

SiGe gives Tower another way to participate in the same growth. SiPho handles the optical side of the connection, while SiGe is used in the high-speed electrical components around it. More ports and faster links increase demand for both. Think TIAs, drivers etc.

Basically the thesis has moved from future optics demand to contracted demand, capacity expansion and rapidly growing earnings.

Management recently raised its 2028 financial model from roughly $2.84B of revenue and $750M of net income to $3.6B of revenue and $1.2B of net income.

That is roughly 27% more revenue and 60% more net income than management was targeting earlier this year.

Tower’s current 2028 target calls for roughly $3.6B of revenue, 45% gross margin, 38% operating margin and $1.2B of net income.

My base case stays close to that revenue target while leaving some room below management’s profitability target.

My base case stays very close to management’s $3.6B revenue target while assuming slightly lower net income than the company’s ~$1.2B target.

The contractual demand I mentioned earlier gives me more confidence using that revenue assumption than I would normally have with a 2028 management target.

At roughly $10.10 of 2028 EPS, a 29× multiple gives me approximately $290/share.

Around $195 is where I become much more interested in starting a position.

It’s a confluence of multiple supports. July lows aren’t too far off, theres’s been a consistent pattern of bouncing off that level, the 200EMA and 200D are both within a %. I like when multiple signs all converge, and the valuation can back it up for my desired return.
