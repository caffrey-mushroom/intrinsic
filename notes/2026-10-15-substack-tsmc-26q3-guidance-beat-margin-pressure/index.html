Substack · 2026-10-15

TSMC expressed confidence in advanced-node and long-term AI demand during its earnings call. Management said that, alongside discussions with direct customers and their downstream customers, its teams had checked US data center construction and power availability, with encouraging feedback so far. Agentic AI and open-source models are also increasing demand for computing chips. TSMC is responding by adding N3 fabs, departing from its usual practice of stopping capacity additions once a node reaches its target.

Revenue Guidance Beats Expectations; N2 Ramp Weighs on Margins

TSMC guided 26Q4 US-dollar revenue to $52.3–54.3b, implying 14.1% QoQ growth at the midpoint, above our 11.7% forecast and in line with the more optimistic buy-side expectations we track. The gross margin guidance midpoint of 66.0% was below the 67%–68% buy-side range we use as a reference. Near-term dilution reflects the N2 capacity ramp and volume production at fabs outside Taiwan. Management expects these fabs to dilute 2026 gross margin by close to 2 percentage points. As more US fab phases enter volume production, dilution is expected to reach 3–4 percentage points in the latter half of the company’s previously outlined five-year planning period. The 2026 capital expenditure budget remains $60–64b.

AI Demand Remains Strong; Consumer Markets Warrant Caution

Subscribed

End-market demand remains mixed. Rising component costs and macroeconomic uncertainty are weighing on consumer markets, prompting caution in TSMC’s 2027 business planning. AI continues to drive demand for advanced nodes. Management said the adoption of agentic AI is increasing compute requirements, while open-source models are lowering adoption barriers. Alongside AI accelerators, CPUs used for agentic AI and networking chips are adding to silicon demand. Demand remains well above available capacity, and customers continue to raise their requirements as TSMC works to narrow the gap.

TSMC previously projected a mid-to-high-50s percentage CAGR in AI accelerator revenue from 2024 to 2029. Management sees no sign of demand slowing and said growth could be higher. This forecast uses a narrow definition of AI accelerators and excludes server CPU demand.

N3 Expansion Continues as N2 and A14 Advance

Management expects annual N2-family capacity in 2027 to more than double from 2026. A14 remains on track for risk production in 2027 and volume production in 2028. The company has previously described strong customer engagement and expects A14 and its derivatives to become a larger node than N2.

Wafer Allocations and Mask Licensing

Asked about customers transferring wafer allocations, TSMC said it does not permit customers to sublet or auction capacity. Customers may cooperate through mask licensing agreements in limited cases, but this is not standard practice and involves very small volumes.

Financial Forecasts

A higher mix of N2 and production outside Taiwan explains the gross margin guidance shortfall against buy-side expectations. TSMC remains confident in demand and plans to continue expanding N3 capacity. As discussed in our earnings preview, we believe demand remains supportive and revenue growth depends primarily on the pace of capacity expansion. Based on our understanding, TSMC is already discussing demand beyond 2030 with customers. We forecast 40.6% growth in 2027 NT-dollar revenue and diluted EPS of NT$153.65.
