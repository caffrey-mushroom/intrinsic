核心摘要

  1. FUNDA在PANW FY27Q1财报前访谈了六家渠道，多数渠道反映客户支出增速持平或略有上升，预计有机收入增速在FY27Q1小幅提升、FY27Q2进一步加速。
  2. 北美渠道A预计FY27Q1客户支出增长34%–35%、FY27Q2达36%–38%；欧洲渠道A当前客户群增速由FY26Q4约23%升至FY27Q1约25%，并预计FY27Q2约27%。
  3. 北美渠道B估计CyberArk与Chronosphere两项收购为季度增速贡献约0.8–1个百分点，澳洲渠道有机增速约18%。
  4. 四家渠道估计CY27网络安全预算增速平均比CY26快约2–3个百分点，PANW预算份额有望再提升低个位数百分点；北美渠道A称大企业中PANW份额由CY25约26%–30%升至CY26的30%–35%。
  5. 客户对PANW的支出中与用量挂钩的比例已由约5%–8%升至10%–15%，主要来自XSIAM数据摄入、云安全和AI安全产品。
  6. 产品端软件防火墙增长最快，北美渠道A增速约42%；SASE增长约20%–40%，XSIAM因替代Splunk、QRadar等传统SIEM在多家渠道增长30%–100%。
  7. 一家覆盖大型银行的渠道称银行安全支出占IT预算由约12%升至约20%，预计到2027年底安全支出将接近翻倍，大型三年合同价值可能从1,800–2,000万美元升至3,800–4,000万美元。
  8. CyberArk与Chronosphere的整合仍需时间，交叉销售尚未转化为大额订单，Chronosphere预计2027年二季度才完全整合；Prisma AIRS仍处试点阶段，占客户PANW支出仅约1%–2%。
  9. 渠道认为企业更倾向在统一安全平台上按任务选择底层模型，一项漏洞扫描对比中Mythos识别约55%–60%漏洞、Astra约35%–40%。

Before earnings, we spoke with six PANW channel contacts and gathered updates on the following topics:

  1. Customer spending growth and outlook
  2. Customer security budgets and platformization
  3. Key product developments
  4. New product progress and acquisition integration
  5. How enterprises use AI models in security operations

Customer Security Budgets and Platformization

Subscribed

We also asked some channels about customers’ early plans for CY27, including how much cybersecurity budgets could grow and what share might go to PANW.

Source: FUNDA channel checks

Source: FUNDA channel checks

We used the midpoints of the security budget growth and PANW budget share ranges to estimate organic spending growth in CY26 and CY27. For most channels, we deducted a single-digit number of percentage points from CY26 growth to account for acquisitions. European Channel A cited CyberArk as the largest contributor to PANW’s share gains that year, so we adjusted its CY26 PANW budget share before calculating growth.

Source: FUNDA channel checks, FUNDA estimates

  1. North American Channel A
  2. The channel estimates that customer cybersecurity budgets grew 8%–12% year over year in CY25. In CY26, budgets

grew about 12% at large enterprises and about 5% at small and midsize businesses. The channel expects large-enterprise budget growth of 12%–17% in CY27, about 2–3 percentage points faster than in CY26, with little acceleration at midsize companies. As more AI workloads move into production, customers need to spend more on privileged access and machine identity management. Some of that funding comes from existing budgets for AI and strategic projects.

  1. Large customers’ vendor consolidation is the main driver of PANW’s share gains. The contact estimates that PANW’s

share of large enterprises’ security budgets rose from about 26%–30% in CY25 to 30%–35% in CY26. Over that period, large-enterprise security budgets grew about 12%, and spending on PANW grew about 30%–35%. The contact initially estimated a further gain of about 5–7 percentage points in CY27 and said PANW’s share could approach 40%–42%. The channel has seen customers merge previously separate network and security operations centers and centralize monitoring.

“AI productionization increasing and AI concerns continuing to increase is, let’s say, 60% of the overall concern within the enterprise. Then 40% is, ‘Hey, I have way too many vendors and products in my ecosystem. It’s not helping me. I’m spending more on licensing and people costs.’ By consolidating with Palo Alto, which has all these capabilities under one shell, there are operational efficiencies. Even if consolidation represents a large deal in dollar terms, implementation and migration take time. Even with agreement at the executive level, the teams doing the work may need longer to replace old products and commit their licensing spend to Palo Alto.”

  1. Some customers spent more on AI than expected and still have room in their budgets for year-end renewals. The channel

said customers are setting funds aside for renewals while renegotiating other IT services contracts.

“When they sign up for multiyear renewals and move to platformization and things like that, it’s not like they are paying all of it in Q4. … Some customers have spent more, their AI budgets have caused sticker shock or surprises. … In my view, customers have allocated budgets for upcoming renewals of most enterprise products. … Calendar Q4 is where most vendors are open to making deals, too. … Many customers are proactively going for bids for the IT managed services. … They might have three-year or five-year managed services. … They are making the vendors reduce the budget, saying that you can take advantage of AI capabilities and do more for less. … While the spend has been higher, customers are also saving in some areas.”

  1. The share of customer spending on PANW tied to usage rose from about 5%–8% last year to 10%–15% now. The contact

cited XSIAM data ingestion and usage of some firewall and AI security products.

  1. North American Channel B
  2. Customer cybersecurity budget growth rose from 9.9% in CY25 to about 11.5% in CY26. The channel expects

11.5%–14.25% growth in CY27, with incremental spending on identity, data, network and endpoint security. PANW’s share rose from 10%–14% in CY25 to 12%–18% in CY26 and is expected to reach 17%–26% in CY27, driven mainly by demand for SASE and SOC automation.

  1. Platformization is furthest along in SOC automation. The channel sees customers using security operations tools

together that they previously bought separately.

“Overall, platformization is accelerating, and I would lean toward saying the acceleration is meaningful. I wouldn’t start from the assumption that everything in cybersecurity will be consolidated under Palo Alto. … We are seeing the maximum benefit from platformization in the autonomous SOC. … It’s an agglomeration of XSIAM, XSOAR, exposure management and other things. … That is where we are seeing one of the best examples of platformization. Second is Prisma Cloud, and then third is, to some extent, AI security.”

  1. About 13%–20% of customer spending on PANW is tied to usage, mainly in cloud security, cloud firewalls and the volume

of data processed by XSIAM. The contact also estimated the share of total security budgets spent on AI tokens.

“For a typical large enterprise, which means revenue upwards of $3b annually, about 1.2% to 1.8% of the cyber budget is going toward AI tokens. … If you are an AI-native or digital-native firm making serious investments in these areas, then it will be upwards of 4%. It can go as high as even 11%, but then that is more because you are approaching it from the business perspective. … For the typical large enterprise, I expect that to rise to 1.4%–2.2% over the next one to two quarters.”

  1. European Channel A
  2. Customer cybersecurity budget growth rose from about 7%–8% in CY25 to 10%–11% in CY26 and is expected to reach

11%–13% in CY27. Customers’ total IT budgets typically grow about 5%–7%. The contact said CY25 budgets were tight, with many renewals coming in flat. Customers also used savings from vendor consolidation to fund AI projects. AI-related security needs started to show up more clearly in budgets in early CY26. Most incremental spending goes to AI security, identity, fraud prevention and compliance. PANW’s share of customer security budgets rose from about 14%–15% in CY25 to 17%–18% in CY26. The contact sees CyberArk as the biggest contributor to the gain because identity security spending previously counted separately now goes to PANW. Including CyberArk, PANW’s share is expected to rise by about 1–2 percentage points to 19%–20% in CY27. Excluding CyberArk, the contact still expects a gain of about 0.5–1 percentage point.

  1. The share of large orders spanning multiple product areas rose from about 20% a year ago to about 40%, partly due

to acquisitions. The contact also estimates that about 40%–50% of the large enterprises covered will buy across multiple product areas over the next year.

“Procurement teams tend to like further consolidation because they can get better terms from suppliers and simplify their IT environments. Palo Alto offers bundle discounts and cross-selling incentives. Some customers are still loyal to best-of-breed products, such as CrowdStrike for endpoint security and Okta for identity. We often see customers wait until they have to renegotiate with their existing suppliers before they consider Palo Alto. If they have two years left on an Okta contract, they’re not going to migrate everything today. Platformization will continue as existing contracts come up for renewal. Customers like having a single platform, but it leaves them more dependent on one provider for technology and pricing and adds switching costs. Some companies will pick one provider, and others will keep several platforms. … Moving to one platform costs money in its own right. It takes staff and time, and sometimes you need an outside systems integrator.”

  1. Customers are reallocating routine IT spending and asking for new funding after security incidents. The channel sees

some budgets previously earmarked for keeping infrastructure running shifting to AI and cybersecurity.

“Companies are increasing their cybersecurity spend, sometimes by reallocating resources and sometimes by using these events, like the PHP event, to gain new resources by going to the CFO … and asking for it. So there is a net increase in spending. I wouldn’t say that the shift is mainly from hardware, but it’s still a good example. … You take the money from keeping-the-lights-on-type IT spend … and then you funnel it to AI and to cybersecurity.”

The channel also said a recent security incident in Sweden had made local companies pay more attention to cybersecurity and increased related spending. It saw no clear step-up in spending in the fourth calendar quarter.

“Just this week, we had an incident in Sweden. Hackers broke into the freezers of several large grocery chains, and some goods had to be thrown away. … It made people more aware of the need to strengthen cybersecurity. I wouldn’t call it a step-change in Q4 spending, but it has intensified those discussions, and cybersecurity spending has increased as a result.”

  1. The share of customer spending on PANW tied to usage rose from about 5%–8% a year ago to 10%–15%. The contact

attributes most of the increase to the growing share of SecOps and cloud security in the purchasing mix.

  1. European Channel B
  2. The channel expects security spending to rise from about 12% of total IT budgets to 18%–20%. The contact expects

identity security and acquired products to drive more PANW purchases. Some projects are likely to close when new budgets become available in the first calendar quarter.

  1. Platformization is progressing as expected. The channel is reviewing customers’ long-term contracts and current

suppliers to see which products they can move to PANW.

“Palo Alto lacks something similar to CrowdStrike’s Flex Consumption. Customers have less flexibility to move to Palo Alto while they’re negotiating their way out of existing contracts. … For about 80% of the deals we do now, it’s a consolidation of products. It goes beyond firewalls.”

The channel sees SentinelOne, Fortinet and CrowdStrike doing better among midsize customers. The contact said these customers are more price-sensitive and that most PANW sales in the channel still go to large enterprises.

  1. AI Projects Keep Hardware Spending High as Customers Cut Other Equipment Costs In our previous conversation, European

Channel B said higher AI spending and rising hardware and software prices had squeezed IT budgets. Hardware spending on AI projects remains high this quarter. The channel sees some customers starting to cut nonessential equipment spending to protect cybersecurity investments, particularly in identity security and compliance.

“They’ve standardized specs for most hardware. … There’s less focus on things like AI laptops. Most customers still aren’t going to buy AI laptops. … They’ve specced down in many cases on laptops. … They’re doing longer contracts for hardware, but actually fewer vendors in the environment. That helps drive cost efficiencies. Overall, they’re still spending a lot on hardware because of AI initiatives, but they’re finding ways to reduce costs and avoid some of the spend that is perhaps not critical. … They’re prioritizing cybersecurity, identity in particular, and compliance. … They’re working on a lot of governance and compliance.”

  1. About 15%–18% of customer spending on PANW is tied to usage, primarily across AI and cloud-delivered security

products.

  1. Australian Channel
  2. Customer cybersecurity budget growth rose from 6%–8% in CY25 to 8%–12% in CY26 and is expected to reach 10%–15% in

CY27. The contact sees customers shifting some dedicated AI budget toward AI security, identity protection and data governance. Cloud security expansion, compliance and AI agent security are also driving incremental spending. Among existing PANW customers, PANW’s budget share rose from 12%–15% in CY25 to 15%–20% in CY26 and is expected to reach 17%–22% in CY27, mainly through cross-selling.

  1. Platformization Picks Up Modestly as Customer Expansion Begins to Contribute to Revenue

“I still see net revenue retention among platformized customers around 110% to 120%. The main driver is existing customers consolidating network security, SASE, cloud security, and SecOps onto a few strategic vendors. However, large customers typically require 6 to 18 months to migrate platforms due to existing contracts, integration complexity, and procurement cycles. Therefore, I expect the platformization strategy to contribute more meaningfully to growth in FY27Q2 and FY27Q3 as customer expansions convert into recurring revenue.”

  1. Security incidents mainly bring existing projects forward. The contact distinguished customers’ immediate response

from when it shows up in spending.

“In my view, these incidents raise board-level awareness and create urgency. … Most customers already have approved annual security budgets, so their immediate response is to reassess vulnerabilities, accelerate existing projects, and reprioritize spending toward identity protection, endpoint detection, cloud security, and incident response. … The financial impact usually becomes more visible over two to four quarters as customers complete their risk assessments and approvals, and begin expanding existing security contracts.”

  1. The share of customer spending on PANW tied to usage rose to 15%–20% from about 12%–15% a few quarters ago. Most of

the increase came from Cortex data ingestion, cloud security workloads and cloud-delivered security services. Most firewall spending is still billed through subscriptions or contracts.

  1. Additional Channel Feedback We have also been researching enterprise AI spending and security budgets. A separate

channel contact who covers large banks said Palo Alto could capture a larger share of new security spending. We included the contact’s feedback as a supplement to the PANW channel checks.

  1. The channel is very positive about security budget growth among its bank customers. It has seen security spending

rise from about 12% to about 20% of total IT budgets. Before October, bank IT budgets were tight. Banks prioritized servers and storage, and most security purchases were one-year renewals. Some customers started reviewing contracts and purchasing needs in early October. By late October, inquiries and multiyear projects had picked up noticeably, and some large projects were already underway. The contact expects a few projects to be signed from late November through December, with more moving ahead in early 2027. The contact’s preliminary estimate is that annual spending on security products and services could nearly double by the end of 2027 among the banks covered. AI deployment is the main driver the contact sees, and banks are pursuing security projects alongside changes to their data and network infrastructure.

“Before October, we didn’t see many multiyear contracts because IT budgets were tight at most customers. Banks were mainly spending on servers and storage. … Most security purchases were one-year renewals. … I think the real turning point was the past two weeks. In early October, customers had already started discussing what they needed and reviewing their existing contracts and usage. … Then things changed two weeks ago. … Suddenly, there were many more inquiries, deal registrations and opportunities. … I think some projects will be signed from late November through December, but the bulk of them will probably get started in January. December is a short working month, and approvals take time.”

“Security used to account for about 12% of total IT budgets, and it’s about 20% now. … The security projects we see are linked to many other bank projects, including data and networking. AI is the main driver from our perspective. Banks want to be prepared because past security incidents have already cost them a lot. So we estimate that security budgets will rise by about 100% by the end of 2027, including products and services.”

  1. The contact expects PANW to capture a greater share of incremental bank security budgets and puts CY27 growth for the

channel’s PANW business at about 30% or more on a preliminary basis. Security platforms already deployed at banks are also better positioned to serve new AI security needs.

“I think Palo Alto Networks will take more share. Its cloud security, SASE and firewall products are a good fit for these customers. … Banks already trust Palo Alto and CrowdStrike. It’s relatively easy to adopt their AI capabilities on the platforms they already use.”

  1. The channel has about 14–15 opportunities for PANW enterprise software agreements. The contact estimates that a

typical three-year PANW contract at a large bank previously had a total value of about $18–20m, while upcoming large deals could reach $38–40m in total, depending on the customer. The channel has already seen some contracts extend to five or six years this year.

“If customers are planning to stay with Palo Alto anyway, they can get a bigger discount by signing for five or six years. … We’ve already seen five- and six-year contracts this year. Customers plan to stay with Palo Alto, and contract values are increasing.”

“It will last longer.   1. Australian Channel
  2. In our previous check, the channel estimated FY26Q4 spending growth at 12%–15%, driven mainly by equipment

refreshes, and expected demand to hold up for at least another six months.

  1. Spending again grew 12%–15% this quarter. The channel expects growth in the same range next quarter.
  2. About a quarter later, the channel still expects strong hardware firewall growth for another two to three

quarters, followed by a gradual slowdown.

Source: FUNDA channel checks

  1. North American Channel A
  2. Spending grew about 42% YoY this quarter, up from about 40% last quarter. Software firewalls were the

fastest-growing part of the channel’s cybersecurity business.

  1. The channel expects this growth cycle to last another two to three years.

“The software firewalls will continue to grow for another two to three years in my view. … As AI rollouts, agentic rollouts [increase, and there is] increased adoption of multiple hyperscalers within the environment, Palo Alto is benefiting a lot.   1. North American Channel B
  2. The channel estimates spending grew about 21.4% this quarter, up from 20.6% last quarter and above its prior

  1. The channel expects software firewall growth to last longer than hardware firewall growth.

“I do expect that for software firewalls, the momentum will last longer. … Even my [Fortinet] practice is benefiting from this. … The effect is going to last longer because of … more and more requirements for microsegmentation and as customers diversify their cloud ecosystem, … reduce dependency on one provider, [and] have this concept of … primary, secondary, tertiary providers. So that will obviously drive the need for more software firewalls.”

  1. European Channel A
  2. Among the customers the expert now covers, software firewall spending growth rose from about 20% last quarter to

about 25% this quarter. The channel sees existing customers gradually shifting spending toward software and virtual firewalls.

  1. The channel expects software firewall growth to last three to four years, possibly longer.

“For software firewalls, that is a longer trend. … You have [software firewalls] for cloud and AI data center workloads, which is … a growing trend in itself. So there we’re talking … three, four years, I would say, as a growth cycle, maybe even [longer].”

  1. European Channel B
  2. More customers are moving to software firewalls as they deploy SASE. The channel tracks software project

conversions and estimates growth of about 15%–18% in 2026 and about 25%–27% in 2027.

  1. The channel expects software to keep growing faster than hardware, particularly in industries with many branch

locations where customers still have room to consolidate network security spending.

“A lot of customers are gradually moving into software. … Hardware is not moving away for sure. … But we’re definitely seeing a gradual process of focus into software. … In certain industries … where we’ve got branches, that’s going to continue to evolve. Palo Alto is going to continue to … consolidate a lot of those customers, even customers that it doesn’t have.”

  1. Australian Channel
  2. In our previous check, the channel estimated FY26Q4 growth at 20%–25%, driven mainly by growth in cloud workloads,

virtualization, and hybrid cloud deployments.

  1. Spending again grew 20%–25% this quarter. The channel expects growth in the same range next quarter.
  2. The channel expects the current growth cycle to last 12–18 months or longer.

“Software firewalls have a longer growth runway, potentially 12 to 18 months or even longer, driven by cloud migration, hybrid infrastructure, and the adoption of virtualized security.”

Follow-Up: How Enterprises Use AI Models in Security Operations

In earlier research, we looked at whether enterprises might build some security operations capabilities in-house using frontier models and what that could mean for demand for existing security products. This time, we followed up on how enterprises choose security platforms as model providers launch their own security products, and where enterprises procure the model usage for those tasks.

  1. We discussed with European Channel A whether enterprises would adopt security products directly from OpenAI or

Anthropic or keep using model capabilities through platforms such as Palo Alto and Microsoft. The expert sees room for model providers in some specialized use cases. For day-to-day security operations, enterprises tend to keep a unified security platform and choose the underlying model by task.

“We’re seeing more customers treat the underlying models as something they choose on the fly. You could choose OpenAI, Anthropic or someone else, and then you want your cybersecurity to sit a layer above that. … I think the hyperscalers are better positioned to take a big share of cybersecurity, especially Microsoft. But most enterprise customers have a multicloud strategy, so they may want cybersecurity on a layer above the hyperscalers as well.”

  1. We also asked North American Channel B how enterprises procure tokens for security tasks. The channel said that when

companies buy model usage separately, they generally go through existing cloud providers such as AWS and Azure. They can draw on cloud spending commitments and avoid onboarding another vendor. When they use AI features built into a security product, the spending goes through the security vendor.

“If a customer invests in Palo Alto’s AI Runtime Security or the AI features of XSIAM, that spending goes through Palo Alto. … It’s similar with CrowdStrike and Charlotte. The security vendors might also offer some customization.”

  1. We heard from another source that in one vulnerability scanning comparison, Mythos identified about 55%–60% of

vulnerabilities and Astra identified about 35%–40%, with only partial overlap. A security platform using multiple models can combine their findings, remove duplicates and screen for false positives, potentially surfacing issues that a single model missed. Based on our channel checks, we think enterprises with established security platforms are more likely to use model capabilities within those platforms.
