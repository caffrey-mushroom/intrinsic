SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion assessed third- and fourth-quarter 2026 conditions in electrical distribution, with emphasis on data centers, commercial construction, utilities, industrial projects, and Hubbell. The expert described exceptionally strong summer demand, led by data center expansion, modular construction, and favorable construction weather. Activity in October remained robust.

Data center growth is increasingly concentrated in expansions of established multi-building campuses rather than new locations. Utilities are benefiting from the associated power requirements. Commercial, manufacturing, semiconductor, and oil and gas markets also remain healthy, although labor and product availability constrain growth.

Key Insights

Third-quarter market performance was significantly stronger than expected. Data center construction accelerated, with modular systems gaining importance because they reduce on-site skilled-labor requirements. The expert considers modular construction a major long-term shift but warned that OEM participation could divert sales from distributors. Strong demand is not translating into stronger distributor margins. Customers are requesting bids from more distributors, creating significant pricing pressure. Hyperscalers increasingly purchase high-value power equipment directly from OEMs, bypassing traditional distribution. Domestic equipment remains preferred, but prolonged lead times and capacity constraints may encourage greater adoption of imported transformers, switchgear, and cable. Distributor inventory values and quantities are rising to support demand, making inventory management a major concern.
