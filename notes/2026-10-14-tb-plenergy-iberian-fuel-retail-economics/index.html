原标题：Plenergy – Scale, Unit Economics & Growth Trajectory in Iberian Fuel Retailing

#energy #consumer

Third Bridge · 2026-10-14

Analyst-led • 8 Oct 2026

Companies: REP ES

Jose Maria Vidaurrazaga (JMV) Portfolio Development Manager, Mobility & Convenience, Europe at BP plc

Orwa Mohamad (OM) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • Plenergy's site-level unit economics and margin structure, benchmarking its automated forecourt build costs, throughput and per-litre gross margins against full-service incumbents and hypermarket forecourts
  • Network roll-out trajectory and saturation risk, assessing expansion pace
  • Regulatory risk around unmanned fuel retailing, focusing on any pending Spanish or Portuguese staffing, safety or environmental mandates
  • EV charging and ancillary revenue diversification, including CAPEX requirements and revenue potential

Key Insights

  • While Plenergy undercuts Repsol's pump price by EUR 0.20, Repsol neutralises this advantage by offering massive EUR 0.45 per-litre discounts to customers bundling all utility contracts
  • Competitor Ballenoil possesses structural supply advantages in southern Spain hinterlands through Moeve refinery integration, forcing Plenergy to diversify suppliers to mitigate heavy historical reliance on Repsol
  • Expert estimates Plenergy's realistic Spanish network ceiling to be 500-600 sites, representing roughly 9-11% volume market share, beyond which cannibalisation severely limits further profitable expansion
  • Plenergy's EV pivot requires a 15% minimum utilisation rate to break even, but its fundamentally undersized urban plots cannot accommodate necessary multi-charger infrastructure hubs
  • Given that fully unmanned fuel retailing remains structurally uncommon in Portugal, Plenergy must uniquely introduce manned services at select new locations, fundamentally compromising its core zero-labour economic model
