SUMMARY

Overview

The expert, with extensive experience in Microsoft communications and customer implementations, described a market shifting from traditional cloud communications deployments toward AI-enabled services. Demand for conventional unified communications and contact center migrations was around average to somewhat below expectations in the recent quarter. The expert expects AI agents, agent assistance, and automation to become increasingly important sources of growth.

A major Microsoft service disruption affecting email, Teams, and Copilot brought business continuity and reliance on a small number of cloud providers into focus. The expert expects customers to review emergency communication plans, though many may resist paying for a second full platform.

Key Insights

AI features are becoming a central driver of industry demand, while traditional migrations continue at a slower pace. Customers may favor a simple backup system that can restore basic calling during an outage, rather than duplicating their full communications setup. The expert views Microsoft’s broad integration across productivity, security, and communications as a major advantage. Copilot’s access to workplace information across email, chat, and meetings may distinguish it from tools focused mainly on meeting summaries. Microsoft’s Dynamics contact center offering has limited traction in the expert’s experience; third-party contact centers remain common in Microsoft environments. Pricing for AI services is still evolving. The expert expects models combining user fees with usage-based charges, as providers manage variable computing costs. AI-native companies could disrupt established software providers by quickly producing tailored tools, though enterprise adoption must also address security and compliance.
