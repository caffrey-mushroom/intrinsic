Key Takeaways

AWS. AWS revenue of $46.0B grew +39.3% (last q +36.7%), the sixth straight quarter of acceleration, and beat Street’s $45.45B (+37.7%) by 1.2%, the smallest AWS beat since 2Q25 (2Q: +4.3%). It missed the ~43% buyside bar, with desks ranging 41-45% and a widely followed third-party cloud panel at ~44%. The q/q add slowed to $3.75B from a record $4.6B in 2Q; asked directly, Jassy said only that “the amount that we add every quarter is not equal” and pointed to capacity planned over the next eight to 10 quarters, with no 4Q number. The 2-yr stack still improved to 59.5 from 54.2, and the run rate is $184B (2Q $169B). Mgmt said core compute and storage grew at their fastest rates in several years.

Outlook. The incremental piece is the first multi-year AWS guide: “north of 40%” growth in each of 2027 and 2028, and a $1T revenue business “in the next five to 10 years” (July: “very possibly ... in time”). On ~$176B of FY26 AWS revenue (9M actual plus post-print Street 4Q), 40% means ~$247B in FY27 and ~$345B in FY28, which takes ~$4.5-4.8B of q/q adds every quarter in 2027 and ~$7B a quarter in 2028, vs. $3.75B this quarter. Pre-print Street had FY27 AWS at ~$236B (+35%) and FY28 at ~$308B (+31%), so the sell-side moves up, and early post-print Street is already ~$251B (+42%) and ~$351B (+40%). The problem is the buyside: FY27 AWS bars sat in the mid-40s to low-50s, so “north of 40%” lowers those curves even as Street numbers rise.

Backlog. Backlog of $578B rose +$82B q/q and ~2.9x y/y, with a 6.1-year weighted-average remaining life (2Q 6.4 years). The add slowed from +$132B in 2Q and +$120B in 1Q, but those quarters carried disclosed mega-deals (OpenAI’s +$100B expansion in 1Q, Anthropic’s >$100B in 2Q), while 3Q had none and mgmt called the backlog “well-diversified.” The release listed new AWS agreements with META, NVDA, CRM, ORCL and others. Jassy said customers reserve blocks of hundreds of megawatts up to a gigawatt well in advance, giving “very good visibility into 2027 and 2028 and some of 2029 too.” Dropped from the call vs. July: the “double the power capacity by the end of 2027” line, any GW figures, the “not enough capacity to meet all the demand” line, and the “>$25 billion” chips and AI run rates, now described only as growing triple digits.

AWS margins. AWS OI of $17.8B (38.7% OM) beat Street’s 37.3% by ~140bps but missed the ~40% bogey, and reported OM fell ~70bps q/q from 39.4%. The q/q decline is optical: 3Q carried a $91M energy-derivative gain vs. $551M in 2Q, so ex both, OM rose ~40bps to ~38.5% from ~38.1%. CFO said he is “not guiding at all” on AWS margin, citing AI mix and component pricing as headwinds. Jassy said AI margins are “well ahead” of where core was at the same stage (July: “a little ahead”), but they are still below core. Contracts under energy-derivative accounting rose to ~690M MWh from ~270M, so future mark-to-market noise in AWS OI can get bigger.

Chips. External Trainium sales are now a plan: “We intend to do so late in 2027,” and Jassy expects “a very significant business,” vs. “a real chance” in July. Graviton and Trainium revenue commitments each grew more than 20x y/y, most of Bedrock runs on Trainium, and Bedrock is “the fastest growing multi-billion dollar service in AWS’s history” with Jassy saying it could get as large as EC2. Inference is now “well over half” of AWS’s AI revenue. On useful lives, prior-generation Graviton backlog more than doubled q/q after the Graviton5 launch, and short-term prior-generation Trainium capacity sells at a premium to 1H. Jassy also said neocloud capacity runs ~3,500bps worse on OM than owned or long-leased capacity, which is most of AWS’s.

Margins. EBIT of $28.1B (13.9% OM, a record) beat Street’s $26.2B and the $22.5-26.5B guide, and was at the top of the ~$27.5-28B bogeys. The beat over the guide high end shrank to 5.9% from 14.4% in 2Q. NA OM of 7.7% was ~90bps above Street’s 6.8%, while International’s 3.7% was ~40bps below 4.1%. The +61% y/y is flattered by 3Q25’s $2.5B FTC settlement and $1.8B of severance; ex those, OI grew +29%. Shipping costs rose +11% (+8% ex fuel and line-haul) vs. +12% units, and cost to serve per unit fell for a fourth straight year.

Ads. Ads of $19.6B grew +10.9% (+12% ex-FX; last q +26.2%) vs. Street $20.9B (+18%); 2Q+3Q combined was +18.1% vs. Street’s 21.9%. Mgmt blamed three timing items: Prime Day (“850 basis points sequential impact to Q3 year-over-year growth”), NBA games in 2Q but not 3Q (they return in 4Q), and lapping a 3Q25 search-engine-marketing change; the 850bps is a 2Q-to-3Q swing in the growth rate, not something to add back to 12%. 4Q ads are guided to “the high teens” vs. Street +18.7%. On agents, Jassy answered the question of whether outside horizontal shopping agents are meaningfully hurting Amazon Ads with “The answer is no,” and positioned Alexa for Shopping as the primary agent for shopping Amazon while saying AMZN anticipates “working with horizontal agents.”

Capex. Cash capex net of proceeds was $75.9B (+122% y/y; $77.3B gross) vs. Street $58.2B and $53.1B in 2Q. Part of the jump was payment timing: the build in unpaid P&E fell to $0.9B from $10.7B, so accrual net P&E additions grew a smaller 22% q/q to $78.3B. The ~$220B FY26 guide was reiterated, which implies ~$47.8B in 4Q vs. pre-print Street $64.8B. For 2027 mgmt said “meaningfully more” with no number (pre-print Street $287B, bogeys ~$325-350B), and early post-print Street has moved to ~$312B. P&E purchase commitments are now broken out at $159.6B, $63.9B of it due in 2027.

FCF. FCF was -$33.5B vs. Street +$1.3B, and TTM FCF fell to -$42.4B from -$7.6B in 2Q. 3Q was funded with $48.1B of long-term debt ($25.0B of July notes, ~$5.6B of sterling notes and a $17.5B term loan), taking LT debt to $173.1B from $65.6B at YE25 against $114.2B of cash and securities. New this quarter is an ~$80B secured supplier loan facility for infrastructure purchases, drawable 1Q27-3Q28. Interest expense tripled to $1.69B from $0.54B. Mgmt’s only funding comment was “We can fund this plan,” with nothing on equity, and most of the $24.0B “acquisitions and non-marketable investments” outflow was the final $21.3B OpenAI Series C payment.

Retail. Revenue of $201.9B (+12.1%, +13% ex-FX) came in $56M under the $202.0B guide high end, missing Street by 0.2%, the first Street miss since 2Q24. The Prime Day shift into 2Q cost ~400bps, and 2Q+3Q combined growth of +15.7% was in line with Street’s 15.8%. NA revenue of $112.9B (+6.2%; last q +16.1%) missed the $113.8B Street mean but was in line with the ~6-6.5% bogey and the $113.2B median; 2Q+3Q combined NA was +11.0%. International was $43.1B (+5.4%, +8% ex-FX). Units grew +12% (2Q +17%), and Jassy said “some folks are sleeping a bit” on retail.

Guide. The 4Q revenue guide of $240-247B (+12-16%, ~60bps FX headwind; midpoint $243.5B, +14.1%) sits under Street’s $244.4B at the midpoint, with the high end at the bottom of the ~$247-248B bogeys. The OI guide of $28.5-33.5B (midpoint $31.0B, +24%) is under Street’s $32.9B and ~$1B below the ~$34-35B high-end bogeys. Both ranges widened ($7B / $5B vs. $5B / $4B for 3Q). Leo is the main OI swing: certain costs now start being capitalized in 1Q27, “later than I’ve been predicting,” so most Leo costs stay expensed in 4Q, and fuel is “higher in Q4 than it was in Q3.” For reference, over the last 12 quarters AMZN topped the OI guide high end 10 times

Callback. On callbacks, mgmt said it deliberately chose “north of 40%” when told the buyside sat at high-40s to low-50s for FY27 AWS, and said it was surprised expectations had run ahead on third-party data points. Asked several ways about the smaller q/q add, mgmt gave no reason, insisted it has the capacity, and redirected to the 40% outlook. Core AWS margins are ~40%+ and the AI business is in the mid-20s, with mgmt not forecasting a drop to 35% over the next couple of quarters but saying the full D&A impact hasn’t hit yet; training carries lower margins than inference, and Bedrock on Trainium is the favorable piece. Mgmt did reaffirm doubling power from end-2025 to end-2027, with the August NVDA chip commitment inside that doubling rather than on top of it. Other points: 2027 capex is only a “meaningful increase” for now; AMZN will not let agents “blindly access” its site but expects to work with horizontal agents; NA retail margin is up 10+pts over the past couple of years with robotics in only ~6 buildings this year; and mgmt hasn’t decided how it will report external chip sales, which could bring a TPU-style optics debate next year.

Bull vs. Bear Debate

The bull thesis is that AMZN owns two businesses that can each reach $1T of revenue, and that the market still values it like a retailer with a cloud attached. AWS is the largest cloud, with a $578B backlog running more than 3x its revenue run rate, and AI adds a second growth engine on top of a core where ~85% of IT spend is still on-prem. As inference becomes most of AI demand (already “well over half” of AWS AI revenue), AWS’s enterprise base, Bedrock’s multi-model approach and Trainium’s cost advantage matter more than raw frontier-training share. Owned or long-leased capacity (~3,500bps better OM than neocloud capacity, per mgmt) and in-house silicon should keep AWS margins structurally above the AI-infra peer set. On the retail side, regionalization, same-day perishables and robotics have added ~10pts to NA margins in a couple of years with a long runway left, ads compound at high margins, and Prime Video is now profitable. Leo, Trainium sold outside AWS, and ~$289B of Anthropic and OpenAI stakes (~$26 a share before tax) are all optionality beyond core numbers.

This quarter gave bulls the multi-year AWS visibility they had asked for: “north of 40%” in both 2027 and 2028, reserved gigawatt-scale blocks into 2029, a $82B backlog add with no mega-deal behind it, and early post-print Street FY27 AWS already up ~6% to +42%. Ex energy-derivative gains, AWS OM rose q/q despite D&A at 20% of revenue, and prior-generation chips are pricing at a premium, which answers the useful-life worry. The consolidated OM hit a record 13.9%, NA OM beat by ~90bps, and capex timing (an implied ~$47.8B 4Q) means the FCF hole partly reverses next quarter. Bulls will also look through the ads miss (Prime Day, NBA and SEM timing, with 4Q guided back to high teens) and the 4Q OI guide (Leo expensed one more quarter, with no Leo drag in 2027 once capitalization starts). Estimate drivers from here are 4Q AWS adds rebounding as capacity lands, GPU repricing flowing into revenue per chip, external Trainium sales in late 2027, and re:Invent in early December as the next catalyst.

Bear Case

The bear thesis is that AMZN is turning into a capital-intensive utility for AI labs at a time when its retail profit pool faces its first structural threat in years. Capex goes from ~$116B in the year to 3Q25 to ~$220B in 2026 and “meaningfully more” in 2027 (Street ~$312B and rising), FCF stays negative through 2027 (Street FY27 ~-$49B), and the funding has shifted to debt: $181B of face value vs. $69B at YE25, plus an ~$80B secured supplier facility and $160B of P&E purchase commitments. AI margins in the mid-20s vs. core AWS at ~40%+ mean AWS OM drifts lower as AI becomes the mix, and AWS D&A (+63% y/y) is already running ahead of revenue (+39%). The backlog is large but concentrated: OpenAI’s ~$138B and Anthropic’s >$100B of commitments are a big share of it, both labs are cash burners, and Trainium commitments are tied to their success. In retail, agentic shopping threatens the discovery funnel that feeds ads, the FTC’s ad-auction suit is pending, and a TPU-style debate over lumpy, low-margin chip sales arrives in 2027.

This quarter supports the bears on the AWS bar and on cash. AWS missed every buyside number on growth and margin, the q/q add fell ~20% with no explanation on the call or the callbacks, and “north of 40%” lowers buyside curves; 4Q AWS bars dropped to the low-40s from 48-49% pre-call. Mgmt stopped repeating the power-doubling and capacity-shortage lines on the call and dropped the $25B AI and chips run-rate figures, which reads as less transparency right when the bar was highest. Cash capex hit $75.9B, FCF -$33.5B, interest expense tripled, and the only funding answer was “We can fund this plan.” The 4Q guide sits under Street at both midpoints with wider ranges, Leo slipped a second time, ads slowed to +11% reported, and NA missed the Street mean. Bears will argue estimates fall where it matters: buyside FY27 EPS moves to ~$11-12 from $12-13, and the multiple compresses if 2027 capex lands at the top of the bogeys while AWS adds stay near $4B.

INTC -25%: Big miss driven by PC inventory flush (CCPG guided down >20% q/q) and the Israel fab outage put the Dec guide ~9% below Street, and 2027 is now a back-half, no-FCF year

Just an ugly print for a stock that was still a crowded long in semis land - focus on the Q3/Q4 Revs and GM miss. Didn’t hear much defending yesterday despite better DCAI. 3Q revenueof $16.51B in line with Street ~$16.5B but missed bogeys of ~$17.2B with GM (44.1%) and EPS ($0.52) ahead, but the Dec guide of $15.0-16.0B / 38.0% GM / $0.25 EPS came in ~$1.5B, ~450bps and $0.18 below Street and well under the ~$17.7-18B / ~44% bogeys.

DCAI was the standout at $7.32B (+78% y/y, +17% q/q) vs. Street ~$6.6-6.7B and is guided up another mid-teens % to ~$8.4B (Street ~$7.4-7.7B) even with ~$300M of lost Israel output, while CCPG missed by ~$0.8B at $8.10B (-9% q/q) and is guided down >20% q/q as OEMs cut inventory into a memory-driven PC slump (TAM -12% in ‘26 and -10% in ‘27, with the correction running through 1Q and maybe into 2Q).

The 38% GM guide (~40% ex ~200bps of Israel scrap) a step lower than July’s “comfortably into the 40%s” goal, 2026 capex was trimmed to $20B from “>$20B,” 2027 capex is “up strongly,” and the CFO doesn’t expect positive FCF in 2027.

Bulls are focused on DCAI: the CFO called the Dec-Q run rate (mid-$30Bs annualized) “a good starting point” for 2027 and raised industry server CPU unit growth to >30% in both ‘26 and ‘27; bulls will say the client hit is a 2-3 quarter inventory event, not share loss, and that packaging customer announcements are due within two quarters. Bears will of course focus on the big PC miss, point to a supply-gated 1H27 while AMD and ARM take share, a CFO who is “not optimistic” on memory through 2027, dilutive mix (purpose-built silicon, 18A underloading), the foundry capex talk up given no FCF after a $23B raise. Buyside CY27 EPS #s coming down 10-15%.

For AMD (-9%), assuming -25% notebook and desktop units would strip out about ~5% of AMD’s revs for Dec Q.

Key Takeaways

  • Guide. The Dec-Q guide of $15.0-16.0B ($15.5B midpoint, -6.1% q/q, +13.4% y/y) is ~$1.5B (~9%) below Street ~$17.0B and ~$2.2-2.5B below the ~$17.7-18B bogeys; even the high end is ~$1B short. The bridge is client: CCPG is guided down >20% q/q (- PC outlook. INTC now sees the 2026 PC unit TAM down ~12% (2H units down >20%) and is planning for another ~10% decline in 2027, weighted to 1H, with possible improvement in 2H against easier compares; in July it called 2026 “down low double-digits” and gave nothing on 2027. Mgmt doesn’t expect a sustained recovery until memory availability and pricing normalize, and the CFO was blunt that “we are not optimistic about next year in terms of memory”; the 10-Q now expects client trends “to worsen in Q4 2026 and persist in 2027,” after the 2Q 10-Q said constraints would “ease over the second half of 2026.” The product side is better: Wildcat Lake units rose ~70% q/q and are sold out, AI PC revenue grew 35% y/y, and 18A is now nearly a third of client CPU revenue (- Client. CCPG revenue of $8.10B (-5% y/y, -9% q/q; last q +13% y/y) missed Street ~$8.9B by ~$0.8B, against a July outlook of “up a bit.” Mgmt blamed memory prices and availability plus “limited IT budgets,” with OEMs cutting inventory aggressively over the last two months of the quarter; customers only began changing build plans with INTC in late August/early September. For 4Q, CCPG is guided down >20% q/q as customers “reduce weeks of inventory” to match their 2027 consumption plans, and the CFO expects a similar correction in 1Q27 on top of normal seasonality, “maybe stretching a little bit into Q2,” before INTC ships in line with sell-through. The correction hurts INTC more than the TAM decline itself: CPUs had been running ahead of PC units for several quarters, and third-party trackers had 3Q PC units down ~9-14% q/q, among the worst third quarters in 20+ years. CCPG OM still rose to 28.2% (2Q 26.4%) on lower inventory charges. The 10-Q shows notebook plus desktop revenue of $6.9B, down $424M y/y on volume -14%, partly offset by ASPs +9% from pricing actions to offset higher input costs plus richer mix; in 2Q the ASP gain was mix, so INTC is now raising client prices into a weakening market.- DCAI. DCAI revenue of $7.32B (+78% y/y, +17% q/q; last q +59% y/y) beat Street ~$6.6-6.7B by ~$0.6-0.7B, the segment’s strongest growth in 15 years, with OM up ~200bps q/q to 41.5% ($3.0B of operating profit). Growth was broad-based across hyperscale, enterprise and neocloud, purpose-built silicon grew >50% q/q and more than doubled y/y, and Granite Rapids (Intel 3) is now the largest server family and accretive to GM. Per the 10-Q, server revenue rose ~$2.5B y/y on ASPs +54%, mostly premium mix with demand-based pricing a smaller factor, while server volume grew only 10%; other DCAI (mainly purpose-built silicon) was $1.4B, up $743M y/y. The 4Q guide calls for another mid-teens % q/q (~$8.4B vs. Street ~$7.4-7.7B) with purpose-built silicon growing faster than the segment, and that’s after the ~$300M Israel hit; ex-Israel, DCAI would be up ~19-20% q/q. The CFO said DCAI is on track to finish 2026 nearly $10B above INTC’s own plan entering the year, and mgmt raised industry server CPU unit growth to >30% in both 2026 and 2027 (July: “strong double-digit”), adding that growth would be “higher in both years if additional industry supply were available.” Clearwater Forest (18A) is shipping with “significant” 2027 demand, Diamond Rapids (18A-P) is sampling for a 2027 ramp, ORCL signed a multiyear Xeon collaboration for OCI, and LBT said multiple customers want long-term supply agreements.- Supply. Three things cap DCAI near term: Intel 3 wafer capacity (Granite Rapids is the only Intel 3 product), an industry-wide shortage of the advanced organic substrates used in EMIB packaging, and the Israel outage. The fixes mostly land in 2H27: ~40% more substrate capacity, much of which “does not hit us until the back half of the year,” 18A tools in Oregon (freed up by weaker client demand) converted to Intel 3 with output in 2H27, more Intel 3 tool installs in Ireland, and 18A ramping in Oregon and Arizona (Fab 62). The CFO said supply will be “more challenging” in 1H27 than in 2H and declined to guide 1Q DCAI. He also said 2027 server share will be “a function of supply,” and with INTC’s 3Q server volume up only 10% y/y against industry units growing >30% (and AMD guiding ~70% revenue growth, as an analyst noted on the call), INTC is already ceding unit share and likely keeps doing so through 2027.- Israel. An accident earlier this month shut Fab 28 for about two weeks, and the CFO expects production to restart by the end of this week. On an early assessment, the guide includes ~$300M of revenue and ~200bps of GM in 4Q, almost all 10nm server, with most of the wafer scrap taken in 4Q. INTC is assuming a similar ~$300M revenue hit in 1Q27 but little additional scrap, so mgmt expects the GM hit to be mostly a 4Q event. The 10-Q is more cautious than the call: it expects scrap, remediation and excess-capacity charges to hit costs and gross profit in 4Q “and through the first half of 2027,” says INTC can’t yet estimate the total impact or insurance recoveries, and notes that a significant portion of revenue comes from Intel 7 products made in Israel.- Margins. Non-GAAP GM of 44.1% beat Street (~42%) and the 42% guide by ~210bps (+410bps y/y, +230bps q/q off a 2Q that carried a stranded-inventory write-down), on lower Intel 3 and 18A costs (18A down ~40% since 1Q, Intel 3 ~15%) and better mix. The 4Q guide of 38.0% is ~450bps below Street (~42.5%) and down 610bps q/q, split roughly evenly (~200bps each) among lower revenue on the fixed cost base as client wafer starts are cut, the Israel scrap, and mix, mainly fast-growing purpose-built silicon with below-average GM. Ex-Israel that’s ~40%, short of the “comfortably into the 40%s in every quarter” goal mgmt set in July. Opex helped the 3Q beat ($4.05B vs. Street ~$4.3B; OM 19.6% vs. ~16%), and FY26 opex is now ~$16.3B vs. “roughly $16.5 billion” in July, though 4Q steps up to ~$4.4B.- GM path. For 2027, the CFO reiterated the 40-60% fall-through rule and said a growth year “should see gross margin expansion,” though “it may not work out every quarter that way.” Tailwinds are Granite Rapids, more Intel 3/18A yield and cycle-time gains, and the Israel scrap not recurring; headwinds are 18A underloading (partly fixed by the Oregon conversion), Panther Lake still below the corporate average, and purpose-built silicon, packaging and external wafers, which all run below the corporate average. The new emphasis is gross profit dollars over GM %: “We’re just going to grow the gross profit,” which “may put a little pressure on the operating margin percentage through next year.” Rising depreciation from the capex ramp was raised on the call but not addressed directly.- Capex. 3Q gross capex was $4.2B (~$11.8B YTD). 2026 gross capex is now “on track to be $20 billion” vs. “more than $20 billion” in July, which still implies ~$8.2B in 4Q, nearly double 3Q. For 2027, gross capex will be “up strongly” (July: “significantly above the 2026 levels”), with the number set around year-end; Street was ~$25B before the print, and an analyst on the call said many are thinking ~$30B. Firm equipment POs rose ~40% q/q, nearly all spend outside Ohio is now equipment, and INTC is evaluating a “unique and highly differentiated opportunity” to accelerate Ohio. The 10-Q shows capex commitments of $19.6B and other purchase obligations of $9.8B (vs. $12.8B and $6.7B at year-end), and after the quarter INTC signed two substrate supply agreements requiring ~$5.3-6.1B of funding, ~$3.3B of it by the end of 2027. The small 2026 trim in the middle of a shortage reads like tool lead times and install capacity rather than a choice to spend less.- FCF. OCF of $5.1B missed Street ~$6.2B (2Q $7.0B), and adjusted FCF was $1.6B including $0.8B of partner contributions. Wins included the first eight-figure Conversations deal (a health insurer on Conversation Relay plus real-time transcription) and, per the deck, an eight-figure Flex consolidation at a fintech/payments company, but the AI-native proof point (a $6M+ run-rate customer growing 65%) was the same customer, with the same figures, that mgmt showed in August.- Consumer agents. The COO said the faster pull is on the other side: businesses standing up virtual agents to field inbound consumer-agent traffic. TWLO put an identity product into private beta to verify whom an agent represents, with GA next year, usage-based pricing and “software margin-like” GM, and mgmt called agent-to-agent protocols “all tailwind.” No revenue was attributed to any of it.- Gross profit. Non-GAAP GP of $767.8M grew 17.7% vs. Street $730.6M (+12%) but missed the ~$775-785M bogeys, ending a run of five straight quarters of accelerating GP growth (2Q +18.1%). Bull vs. Bear Debate

Bull Case

Bulls see TWLO as the neutral communications layer for the AI era. Every AI application that has to reach a consumer on a phone still needs numbers, carrier connectivity, deliverability, compliance and authentication, and mgmt says virtually every app-layer voice AI player runs on TWLO’s rails, so it wins whichever model or agent platform wins. Voice AI is ~1% of voice interactions, self-serve voice (where AI natives start) is growing ~55%, and those customers land on voice and then add messaging, WhatsApp and software add-ons (Branded Calling, Conversation Intelligence, Relay, Verify) that carry software-like margins. Consumer agents and the identity layer (GA in 2027, usage-priced, software-like GM) add a second leg that isn’t in any numbers yet. Around that sits a much better P&L: FY26 organic growth of 15% vs. the 7-8% Investor Day framework, OI of $1.19-1.20B vs. a $1.04-1.06B initial FY26 guide, FCF converting ~1:1 with OI, SBC under 10% of revenue and steady buybacks, from a mgmt team that has guided low two years running.

This quarter backs up the parts of that case that matter long term. Voice held 20%+ on a ~5pt tougher comp, self-serve held 30%+, software add-ons held 25%+, the first eight-figure Conversations deal landed, and GP still outgrew organic revenue by ~2pts with ex-fee GM up 80bps y/y. Profit was the cleanest part (OI +39%, record $370M of FCF), and the 2027 OI and FCF floors are already at or above Street. Bulls will say the messaging decel is a comp concentrated in a few ISVs and that the guide is set the same way as before: 3Q’s 11-12% guide became 15.7%, and a normal 2-4% beat puts 4Q at ~14-16%. If 2027 repeats FY25 and FY26 (a 5-7pt gap between the initial guide and the actual), organic lands in the mid-teens, Street’s ~11% moves up, and FCF heads toward ~$1.5B. The multiple can re-rate on proof of consumer-agent volume, identity pricing at the April Investor Day, regulated-industry voice POCs reaching production, and a starting point of ~22x CY27 FCF vs. ~32x for infrastructure software peers.

Bulls believe TWLO can grow organic revenue ~14% in CY27 (vs. the 10%+ floor) with GP growing about as fast, producing ~$1.48B of FCF, and deserves ~30x EV/FCF, implying ~$285 per share.

Bear Case

Bears see a ~60%-messaging, usage-based business with ~49% GM whose 2026 acceleration leaned on easy comps, ISV volume and a voice AI wave that is still ~1% of minutes, and they doubt TWLO keeps the value as voice moves up the stack. Frontier labs and voice AI platforms (OpenAI’s Presence, ElevenLabs and others) own the customer relationship and the intelligence layer, TWLO supplies connectivity and compliance plumbing they can multi-source as they scale, and CPaaS price competition hasn’t gone away. Part of this year’s acceleration also came from earlier pricing changes that are now being lapped, and carrier fees (~$245M of pass-through this year) raise costs for the small-business messaging base and push some senders toward other formats. Email is growing below the company average against newer developer-first rivals like Resend, and AI-native revenue is concentrated in a handful of fast-growing but volatile customers. The mix shift to software hasn’t moved GM much either: mgmt itself says messaging’s weight sets the margin rate. In this view, 2026 is the peak growth year, and TWLO drifts back toward the low-double-digit grower the Investor Day framework described.

This quarter gave bears their first data point fuel in several quarters. Organic decelerated for the first time this year, GP growth ended a five-quarter acceleration streak, ISV growth fell to 20%+ from 25%+, ex-fee DBNE slipped ~1pt to ~110%, and the beat shrank to 3.3% with mgmt calling 2-4% normal. The bull assumption most at risk is GP leverage: mgmt guided 4Q GP growth to ~11-12% (below 3Q’s dollars) and 2027 GP only in line with organic, because the email migration tailwind is used up and holiday hosting costs return. OI was flattered by ~$11M of one-time credits, and 4Q OI steps down ~$21M. If 2027 lands at ~10-12% organic (the floor plus a small beat) with GP in line, TWLO is a low-teens grower, and with consumer agents explicitly “going to take some time,” there’s no near-term catalyst to re-rate it while a crowded long unwinds. Estimates won’t fall much, but the multiple can compress toward the ~20x forward FCF the stock carried at the start of 2026, before the voice AI re-rating.

Bears believe TWLO grows organic revenue ~10% in CY27 (the floor), with GP up ~9% to ~$3.23B and FCF at the ~$1.35B floor, and deserves ~20x EV/FCF, implying ~$175
