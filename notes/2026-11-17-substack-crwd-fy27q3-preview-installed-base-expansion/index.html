  1. FY27Q3 is likely to beat, with existing-customer expansion the main growth driver. Channel checks show no sign of

sequential deceleration, while Falcon Flex allows existing customers to add modules without restarting procurement and Re-Flex enables contracted customers to expand commitments. NG-SIEM, cloud security and identity are the key areas for upsell. We expect this growth path to remain intact in 2027.

Industry Demand: Security Budgets Rise, with Incremental Spend Shifting to Platform Vendors

Customer security budgets have accelerated this year, and most channel partners expect further increases in 2027, although the pace varies across customers and industries. Incremental spending is increasingly directed to platform vendors as customers reduce the number of suppliers and consolidate purchases that were previously spread across multiple vendors. Some large end customers are also beginning to pursue larger-scale purchases.

Large Banks and Energy Companies Increase Security Budgets and Shift to Multi-Year Contracts

Over the past two weeks, channel partners have seen some large banks and oil and gas companies increase security spending following AI-driven attacks and vulnerability incidents. Some customers have made substantial budget increases, while contract terms have extended from the typical three years to five or six years.

Incremental spending is mainly flowing to core security platforms that customers have already deployed. PANW, CrowdStrike and SailPoint are viewed as the main beneficiaries, while demand for external security services has also increased. Most of these contracts are expected to be signed between late November and January, limiting their contribution to the current quarter. So far, only one channel partner has provided relatively specific data, and the scale still needs to be validated with a broader sample.

Channel Checks: Acceleration Emerging in Q3, with Installed-Base Expansion Supporting Growth

FY27Q3 Channel Performance: Momentum Remains Steady, with Re-Flex Gaining Traction

Channel partners with CrowdStrike business data have not observed sequential deceleration, and overall growth is broadly in line with the company’s ARR growth. Expansion from existing customers is contributing more to growth; we estimate NRR at 115%–120%, while new-customer acquisition remains strong.

Source: FUNDA

Against a steady demand backdrop, Flex lowers the procurement hurdle for existing customers adding modules. Customers can include point products they plan to replace over the next few years in their commitments, which can create a one-time increase in ARR when they move from a standard contract to Flex. As new customers increasingly adopt Flex from the outset, the pool of existing customers that can convert from standard contracts is shrinking, and future expansion will rely more on Re-Flex.

The number of Re-Flex customers is growing quickly, although the Flex customer base is also expanding. Re-Flex penetration currently stands at around 20%–25%, leaving room for further growth.

Customers can Re-Flex after using their committed credits ahead of schedule. The first Re-Flex typically increases contract value by about 25%, while the second delivers an average increase of about 22%, indicating continued expansion in platform spending among some customers.

Flex contracts are priced on total committed value, with larger commitments generally receiving greater discounts, giving customers an incentive to include more modules. Shorter intervals between Re-Flex transactions suggest that customers who have completed a Re-Flex are consuming credits quickly. However, around three-quarters of Flex customers have not yet completed a Re-Flex. Flex was rolled out at scale in 2H 2024; given that contract terms are typically three years or longer, these contracts will enter renewal periods in 2027–2028, making renewal retention and NRR important metrics to watch.

US Channel Partner 8 (11/4/2026): “More than 40% of customers have moved from standard subscriptions to Flex, and customers that completed their first Re-Flex increased contract value by more than 20% versus baseline.” “Typically I see committed utilization between 70% and 80%, so overall adoption is high, but there is a lag when you look at individual customers. Likewise, under-utilization leads to smaller renewals.” “The biggest drivers are deployment delays, integration complexity, and overestimating how quickly add-on modules will be adopted.” “In terms of timing, the first Re-Flex happens within six to eight months and delivers an average uplift of more than 25%.”

European Channel Partner 1 (11/6/2026): “This quarter we saw an uplift in value of about 15%–18%.”

North American Channel Partner 2 (11/6/2026): “Overall, about 20% of customers have completed a Re-Flex.”

Channel Transcript 1 (10/23/2026): “Existing-customer net ACV grew about 25% y/y, driven mainly by Falcon Flex adoption, additional Falcon modules, identity protection, cloud security and SIEM, and large customers consolidating multiple security capabilities onto the platform.”

Business Trends This Quarter: NG-SIEM, Cloud Security and Identity Drive Cross-Selling

Endpoint remains CrowdStrike’s core business. Through FY27Q2, the segment had accelerated for four consecutive quarters and is expected to remain steady in the near term. However, an increasing share of incremental growth is coming from modules beyond endpoint security.

Source: FUNDA

Endpoint

Growth in endpoint security comes mainly from replacing legacy antivirus software and earlier-generation EDR, as well as from existing customers extending protection to servers, cloud workloads and additional endpoints.

Management noted that around 50% of endpoints in the market still use legacy EDR, leaving room for replacement. However, these customers are concentrated in the mid-market, public sector and price-sensitive segments, where Microsoft Defender is also strong. CrowdStrike’s response is to bundle endpoint security with NG-SIEM, identity and cloud security, rather than compete with Microsoft on endpoint pricing alone.

  • The increase in value per endpoint is in the low-single-digit range, mainly reflecting customer upgrades to premium capabilities such as MDR and AI automation.

Channel Transcript 1 (10/23/2026): “The endpoint security business showed good, clear acceleration this quarter, continuing last quarter’s momentum. Paid endpoint licenses grew about 15%–20% y/y, closer to 15%; part of the revenue growth came from premium features and higher contract values rather than more endpoints.”

European Channel Partner 1 (11/6/2026): “Endpoint security is absolutely their core product, and very few competitors can beat them in that space.”

US Channel Partner 1 (10/9/2026): “So we have seen many times the engineers have the preference to go with CrowdStrike for that reason.”

NG-SIEM

Both CrowdStrike and PANW benefit from customers migrating security operations off Splunk, but differences in their products mean they win different customers and deals. CrowdStrike is lightweight to deploy, fast to go live and lower in data cost, and mainly wins Splunk replacements within its existing Falcon base; its weakness is the orchestration ecosystem, where out-of-the-box connectors trail XSIAM. PANW XSIAM is better suited to large SOC transformation deals and customers already on Cortex, at the cost of complex onboarding that demands strong data engineering capabilities from customers.

Industry Expert 4 (10/20/2026): “First is LogScale (i.e., next-gen SIEM). Its core driver is that it is becoming a very effective alternative for existing Splunk customers.”

US Channel Partner 5 (11/3/2026): “Concerns that LogScale historically lacked out-of-the-box orchestration connectors are valid.”

US Channel Partner 5 (11/3/2026): “The trade-off is higher onboarding complexity, especially when legacy logs must be mapped to Palo Alto Networks’ structured schema, which requires strong data engineering capabilities on the customer side.”

US Channel Partner 8 (11/4/2026): “I expect CrowdStrike to have the strongest conversion opportunity within its existing Falcon customer base, while Palo Alto will be more competitive in larger SOC transformation deals.”

Cloud Security and Exposure Management

Wiz and Microsoft lead on market share. CrowdStrike differentiates on runtime protection and attack response, and its growth comes mainly from cross-selling into Falcon customers; it is growing faster than customers’ cloud security budgets, but share gains are limited. Exposure management is a direct beneficiary of the vulnerability incidents, but few customers added budget within the quarter, and most new purchases will land next quarter. Remediation is not a CrowdStrike strength.

Industry Expert 3 (10/19/2026): “For our channel, cloud security is the fastest-growing segment; we also see some growth in identity security, but from a small base.”

US Channel Partner 5 (11/3/2026): “CrowdStrike’s Falcon cloud security product is also frequently adopted. A faulty CrowdStrike update a few years ago may have shifted some future contracts to Palo Alto Networks.”

US Channel Partner 1 (10/9/2026): “We are not seeing any momentum, I would say, specifically with CrowdStrike for patching.”

Identity Security

CrowdStrike’s identity business targets compromised and misused identities; it does not issue identities or handle access approvals, and is largely complementary to Okta and SailPoint. Following the SGNL acquisition, it has begun extending into real-time authorization, covering both humans and AI agents.

US Channel Partner 1 (10/9/2026): “But the identity solutions, what cyber CrowdStrike has is nowhere near to what Okta does, just so you know that.”

AI Security

AI security is growing rapidly from a low base. AIDR governs prompts and data leakage when employees use AI, while Falcon Guardian governs autonomous agents; Falcon Guardian became GA in September 2026 and is priced on subscription plus tokens. AI Gateway is still in development, and AI Kill Switch launched in November. Management has not yet disclosed token usage or AI ARR. The pending acquisitions in agent behavior observability (Fiddler AI) and red teaming (Dreadnode) will not close until FY27Q4 and have no impact on this quarter.

CrowdStrike management callback (10/13/2026): “It’s too early to talk about token use or ARR at this point.” “AI Gateway is actually still in development, but it’ll be part of Falcon Guardian.”

CrowdStrike CEO interview (10/29/2026): “Handling a routine remediation task costs about $10 with a top frontier cybersecurity model, versus just 3 cents with CrowdStrike’s model.”
