SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined Westinghouse’s operating services, AP1000 reactor pipeline, US nuclear construction prospects, and eVinci microreactor. The expert views the installed-base services business as durable because utilities depend on the original equipment manufacturer for proprietary reactor systems, regulatory support, and rapid outage resolution.

The outlook for new reactors is less certain. AP1000 economics could improve using completed designs and construction lessons from recent projects, but labor inflation, supply-chain constraints, financing, and competition from gas generation remain major obstacles.

Key Insights

Approximately 80% of operating services was characterized as predictable plant support and refueling work, with about 20% linked to upgrades and capital improvements. Westinghouse’s intellectual property creates substantial customer retention. Alternative contractors may offer savings, but delays or technical failures can erase those benefits quickly. Existing-fleet service profits should broadly rise with inflation. Meaningful long-term growth requires new reactor construction accompanied by maintenance agreements. Future AP1000 projects must materially improve productivity and use completed design information and actual construction experience from recent units. The expert strongly favored dividing work among competing specialist contractors rather than transferring an entire project to one engineering and construction provider. US nuclear demand may be overestimated. Gas generation currently offers lower costs and faster delivery, while long-term data- center power demand remains uncertain. The expert believes eVinci leads the microreactor field because it appears more deployable and technically mature. The AP300 was viewed as Westinghouse’s most vulnerable product because it competes with the proven AP1000 while carrying first-of-a- kind risk.
