Substack · 2026-10-19

DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash-1017 on October 17, an update to the V4.1 Flash model launched on September 10. The API model name is unchanged. Pricing remains $0.30 per million input tokens and $1.20 per million output tokens, with a 50% off-peak discount. The weights are available under the MIT license. The new version outperforms its predecessor on all 11 benchmarks reported by DeepSeek: its Terminal-Bench 4.0 score increased from 31.2 to 42.3 and its DeepSWE score from 74.2 to 77.0. In our view, it now offers the best balance of capability and cost among open-weight models for coding and agent tasks.

Since the release, some articles have claimed that 1017 beats GPT-6, mainly on the strength of its DeepSWE score and cost per task. Our preliminary view is that 1017 has joined Kimi K3 and Xiaomi’s MiMo-V2.6 Pro in the top tier of open-weight models. DeepSeek has made clear progress, but this update has not brought the open-weight frontier any closer to the leading proprietary models.

Our channel checks indicate that data from the previous generation of proprietary models remains accessible. Distilling from newer models, particularly Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5, has become more difficult. Opus 5.5 retains a clear lead, and 1017 has not narrowed the gap. OpenAI also has an even stronger internal model, Bel.

Joins the top open-weight tier, but trails proprietary leaders overall

DeepSeek’s model card compares 1017 with Kimi K3, GLM-5.3, GPT-5.6 Sol and Opus 5. It does not include Opus 5.5, GPT-6, Fable 5.1 or Gemini 4.

DeepSeek’s published benchmark comparison for V4.1-Flash-1017

According to DeepSeek’s results, 1017 outperforms Kimi K3 and GLM-5.3 on almost every benchmark and beats GPT-5.6 Sol on 9 of 11, trailing only on HLE and ZeroBench. It leads Opus 5 on four benchmarks and trails on seven. On HLE and Terminal-Bench 4.0, it trails by 13.7 and 9.5 percentage points, respectively.

The claim that 1017 beats GPT-6 rests mainly on DeepSWE. DeepSeek reports 77.0 for 1017, while Datacurve’s official leaderboard shows 74.1 for GPT-6 Astra. The two scores come from different sources and do not establish a lead. In Mercor’s independent testing, the September version, Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and Opus 5.5 all scored around 72, with very little between them.

Public Terminal-Bench 4.0 results suggest 1017 is still well behind the leading proprietary models. DeepSeek reports 42.3 for 1017, while Google’s launch page lists 58.2 for Astra and 66.4 for Opus 5.5. Artificial Analysis (AA) measured 59% for Astra and 60% for Opus 5.5 in independent testing but has not yet tested 1017, so the size of the gap still needs to be confirmed in a like-for-like test.

As of October 19, AA had not published an Intelligence Index score for 1017; the September version scored 39. The leading open-weight models are clustered between 44 and 46, with MiMo-V2.6 Pro at 46, GLM-5.3 at 45 and Kimi K3 at 44. Our preliminary view is that 1017 has moved into this group, pending independent testing. For comparison, Opus 5.5 scores 58, five points above GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 4 Argon and Fable 5.1 and 12 points above the best open-weight score to date.

Haiku 5.5, Anthropic’s small model released in October, offers another useful comparison, with an AA Intelligence Index score of 43. V4.1 Flash scored 69% on AutomationBench-AA versus 35% for Haiku 5.5. Haiku 5.5 performed better on Terminal-Bench 4.0, HLE and factual accuracy. This comparison uses the September release; 1017 has yet to be tested independently.

Compared with proprietary flagships, 1017’s main advantage is cost. DeepSeek’s DeepSWE cost chart puts the new version at under $0.20 per task at its highest reasoning setting. Separately, AA measured a weighted average cost of $0.27 per Intelligence Index task for the September version. In the same AA tests, Astra cost roughly 12 times as much, and Opus 5.5 at its highest reasoning setting roughly 22 times as much. The DeepSWE and AA figures are not directly comparable because the tasks and cost calculations differ. Enterprises also need to factor in retries and human review: a low cost per attempt does not necessarily translate into a low cost per successfully completed task.

Distillation data and evaluation harnesses

Subscribed

Our industry contacts indicate that models from the Opus 5 generation can still provide useful distillation data. Even when raw chains of thought are unavailable, answers, summaries and tool-call traces have training value. The large pool of previously collected Fable data also remains in use.

DeepSeek says the update uses reinforcement learning with self-feedback, producing shorter outputs and completing tasks faster at a lower cost per task.

Scores also depend on the agent harness. 1017 has been adapted for use with DeepSeek Harness and Tencent’s CodeBuddy and WorkBuddy. DeepSeek evaluated it on Terminal-Bench 4.0 using Claude Code. When the September release was evaluated on DeepSWE across eight harnesses, scores ranged from 65.5 to 74.2, a spread of 8.7 percentage points. The September version’s Terminal-Bench 4.0 results also vary by source: DeepSeek reported 31.2, AA measured 26.8 and Vals measured 19.7.

On Terminal-Bench 4.0, 1017 scores 11.1 percentage points higher than the September release, which came out just five weeks earlier. Independent testing is needed to establish how much of that gain carries over to other harnesses and unseen tasks. Current benchmarks have fairly clear requirements and acceptance criteria. They offer limited coverage of real-world engineering, where requirements evolve and codebases require ongoing maintenance.

Early user feedback

Early tests suggest that 1017 is fast and inexpensive. DeepSeek’s official API delivers about 150 output tokens per second. API reseller OmniaKey tested five tasks and found that 1017 produced the same answers as V4 Pro at 1.3 to 14 times the speed and about one-sixth of the cost. Another reseller, Kingy, tested seven simple tasks, and 1017, GPT-6.1 Sol and Sonnet 5.5 passed every one; 1017 cost about a quarter as much as Sol. Both resellers sell API access to these models, and the samples are small, so the results should be treated as preliminary.

Early results on prototyping tasks have been encouraging. One blogger used 1017 in Claude Code to build a rotatable 3D model of the Forbidden City’s Hall of Supreme Harmony in 23 minutes for about RMB 1.4 (roughly $0.20). Some developers have reported problems when modifying existing projects: in one case, 1017 removed existing interactive features, and each fix broke something else. Others report that it gets stuck on difficult bugs that a stronger model then resolves on the first attempt.

Some developers use a stronger model for planning and review, then assign specific tasks to 1017 to reduce costs. These reports illustrate potential uses, but they are not enough to assess its overall performance on complex existing codebases.

Competition among open-weight models intensifies as further catch-up becomes harder

Price competition among open-weight models is intensifying. Released just five weeks after its predecessor, 1017 retains the same pricing and 50% off-peak discount. The open weights allow third parties to host the model, and some providers already offer prices of $0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens, below DeepSeek’s own rates. MiMo-V2.6 Pro costs just $0.13 per task in AA’s tests. With little separating the leading open-weight models on capability, cost is becoming the main differentiator.

Proprietary labs are also cutting prices for entry-level models. Haiku 5.5 costs $0.10/$0.50 per million input/output tokens for prompts of up to 100,000 tokens, below DeepSeek’s rates. For longer prompts, its rates rise to $0.50/$2.50. AA measured Haiku 5.5’s cost per task at $0.21, close to the $0.27 for the September version of V4.1 Flash. For short prompts, the price advantage of open-weight models is already being squeezed.

Access to fresh distillation data is becoming a greater constraint on further catch-up. Distilling from the latest generation of frontier models, particularly Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5, has become harder. The Fable API does not return raw chains of thought, and Fable 5.1 introduced further restrictions on the use of thinking blocks.

Our channel checks indicate that a distillation experiment of the same scale now costs 10 to 50 times as much as it did in the first half of the year. As returns from existing data diminish, open-weight developers will need to invest more in their own R&D and training compute to keep closing the gap.

Opus 5.5’s lead has not narrowed, and OpenAI has an even stronger internal model. In September, OpenAI said an internal model significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra had solved the Navier–Stokes problem, one of the Millennium Prize Problems. In early October, the company published more than 700 mathematics papers. We understand that Bel was the model behind both.

We therefore maintain the view set out in our September note: rapid catch-up based on previously collected data may prove difficult to sustain. We expect the leading open-weight models to fall roughly six to twelve months behind the top proprietary models again. Low-cost open-weight models will nevertheless continue to influence pricing and how tasks are allocated across models.

Business implications and what to watch

We expect low-cost open-weight models such as 1017 to handle more high-volume, well-defined agent tasks, putting downward pressure on pricing for models in the GPT-6.1 Sol class. Complex engineering, knowledge work and enterprise tasks requiring high reliability will continue to rely primarily on frontier models such as Opus 5.5.

We expect demand for inference compute to keep growing, as increases in task volume and API usage more than offset the decline in compute per task.

We will watch for independent evaluations by Artificial Analysis and others, particularly whether the gains hold up with other harnesses and on complex existing codebases. We will also track when DeepSeek releases a larger model based on its new architecture.
