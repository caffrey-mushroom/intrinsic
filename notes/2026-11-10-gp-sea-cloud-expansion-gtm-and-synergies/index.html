SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined Sea Limited’s move from relying on leased data-center capacity and external cloud services toward offering cloud services to outside customers. The expert viewed the initiative as consistent with Sea’s broader approach of building capabilities that support its e-commerce, gaming, and financial-services businesses, while potentially creating a regional business of its own.

The expert expects an asset-light approach, combining colocation with build-to-suit facilities developed alongside local partners. Indonesia was identified as the likely largest market, followed by Thailand, Malaysia, and Singapore. Malaysia already has a build-to- suit arrangement; operations in Indonesia, Thailand, and Singapore were described as primarily colocation-based, with a possible Thai partnership under consideration.

Key Insights

The expert expects Sea Cloud to face low margins initially because competition is intense, particularly from aggressively priced Chinese providers. Profitability would depend on building scale and improving margins over time. Gaming was viewed as the clearest external customer opportunity, given Sea’s experience operating gaming infrastructure. The expert also saw potential among local small and medium businesses, though demand for AI computing in Indonesia remains less developed than in Western markets. Sea’s regional presence, local-market knowledge, and Singaporean identity may appeal to customers seeking an alternative to US- or China-based providers. However, the expert was unsure whether these advantages can overcome Chinese competitors’ lower costs. Existing businesses could provide early demand, but the expert expects internal usage to ramp gradually. Shopee may be easier to migrate than Garena, which has an existing cloud relationship. The initiative may weigh on Sea’s near-term financial results. The expert expects management to contain losses by securing early customers and prioritizing investment in established businesses.
