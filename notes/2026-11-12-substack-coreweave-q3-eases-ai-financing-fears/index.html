Today we have to talk about CoreWeave.

The earnings yesterday were a great catalyst for the AI trade, and if we look at this morning’s price action, they were clearly well received by the market.

CoreWeave has been at the center of financing concerns and has often been a reason for selloffs across the industry.

So it’s nice to see it finally be a reason that stocks caught a bid.

We can have plenty of demand for computing and still have a problem if the money isn’t there to get it built.

Are the equipment orders we’re counting on getting closer to delivery, or further away?

There are a handful of things that stuck out to me in this earnings release and call because they speak to whether CoreWeave can keep that buildout moving.

  • Management says lenders are still willing to finance specific customer contracts, even with unsecured bonds under pressure.- Customers are agreeing to pay earlier, giving CoreWeave another way to fund the equipment.- Some existing infrastructure is being rented out again after it has already been paid off.- CoreWeave raised the bottom of its capex range because it expects to deliver infrastructure sooner than planned.- Management outlined a way to grow from its committed backlog with 2027 capex around this year’s level.

I want to break down what those developments mean for the rest of the AI trade, what’s encouraging for the suppliers and where we could still run into trouble.

We also have NBIS earnings report tomorrow to see how much of this holds up beyond CoreWeave.

Financing

The concern here has primarily been that CoreWeave could have customers waiting for computing and still struggle to get the money to deliver it. If that starts pushing out projects, it can also push out orders for the chips, networking and other equipment going into them.

That is why, even as someone not invested in CRWV, their financing situation impacts the entire trade.

On the call, management said they would not issue more unsecured debt at current yields. They expect to fund most of the remaining buildout for their backlog with loans tied to specific customer contracts.

They also said their existing secured loans had continued trading around par. And they expect to launch another facility in the coming weeks, with a yield around 7% to 7.5% at current benchmark rates, financing more than 90% of the associated capex. Alongside the weakness in unsecured debt, that is a very different signal from the lenders financing particular contracts.

They can look at who has agreed to pay for the computing, the cash that contract should bring in, and the hardware securing their loan. Management said facilities tied to investment-grade customers have priced more cheaply than those tied to AI labs.

So who is paying for the computing affects what CoreWeave pays to finance it.

The credit market is making those distinctions even when the stocks are getting sold together.

CoreWeave expects about $9B in customer prepayments and deposits over the next three months. That is a substantial amount of money for a company that has been at the center of this financing scare.

If the customer pays over time, CoreWeave has to find more of the money upfront. If the customer agrees to pay earlier, it may want a better rental price. So CoreWeave is negotiating on both sides of this.

How much future rent is it worth giving up to avoid the borrowing costs available today?

Management described weighing exactly that on the call. A project can still go ahead while the cost of financing changes who gets how much of the return.

The payments are expected over the coming months, and CoreWeave will still owe the customer the computing. It gets help with the hardware bill now, with less of that same contract left to collect later.

IREN has signed a five-year deal in which the customer agreed to a prepayment covering 90% of the associated GPU capex. Most of the GPU bill, before the service has even been delivered.

CoreWeave still reported $846M of interest expense in Q3 and a $709M net loss. That is a lot of interest to cover.

So a supplier can get an order, deliver the equipment and get paid while the cloud company is still reporting a net loss.

We can get a better outlook for equipment orders without having to get comfortable with all the risks of owning the cloud company.

More equipment reaching customers

Their Q3 revenue reached $3.7B, up 168% year over year, and they raised the bottom of its 2026 capex range from $35B to $37B. The top stays at $39B. Management expects to get infrastructure to customers sooner than planned.

In the October selloff update, I was watching for financing pressure to start pushing out deployments and equipment orders.

So getting deliveries sooner is a welcome change.

After all the concern about whether this company could fund the work, it is raising the bottom of its capex range because it expects to deliver more of it this year.

IREN had the customer and the financing arranged for the capacity supporting its more than $4B annualized revenue target, subject to the financing conditions. It still moved that target from year-end to January, possibly February. A customer-requested design change and supplier timing meant the work needed longer.

If you were counting on all that capacity earning revenue at year-end, you now have a longer wait. If a lender has pulled back, the next project may be in trouble too.

CoreWeave says that if it focused only on delivering its committed backlog, 2027 capex could stay around this year’s level while revenue and EBITDA grew significantly.

Flat capex at this level would still mean a huge year of deployments. And the plan still needs additional secured loans and customer payments. CoreWeave is describing how it could fund and deliver the backlog, with more of the earlier buildout contributing for a full year.

Older GPUs

More than $3B of the expected customer receipts is tied to existing infrastructure being rented out again. Management says that particular equipment is already paid off, with no associated debt service or new capex required for those contracts.

If customers move on before you’ve paid for the GPUs, you still have lenders to deal with and less rent coming in.

CoreWeave still has electricity, rent, maintenance and people to pay, but on this particular equipment the purchase and associated debt are behind it. It needs another customer willing to pay enough to make running it worthwhile.

IREN recently contracted H100 capacity at double the price of its previous H100 contract at Prince George. That is an encouraging answer to the concern about how well older GPUs hold their value.

If a customer needs computing now, waiting for a newer cluster to be finished may be a poor option.

The owner of an older cluster that is already running has something useful to offer. Keep delaying the new sites and the existing capacity can stay scarce for longer, helping its rental price. Once the sites open, customers have more choices again.

So the delay that worries the chip supplier could actually help the owner of an older cluster get a better rental. It is awkward to fit both of those into one bullish or bearish view of “AI”.

The customer has a bill too

Ask an AI lab to pay for computing earlier and it needs the cash earlier too.

If it is buying capacity ahead of its own revenue, the customer has more of that funding gap to cover.

In October, I wanted to see Anthropic’s financial statements to get a better look at how its growth was turning into cash. The updated filing shows positive operating cash flow over the first nine months of the year and about $132B in cash and short-term investments at September 30, before the IPO.

The nearly $17B of Q3 revenue, up about 47% from Q2, broadly confirms the growth we had already discussed.

About $70.7B of its contractual obligations is expected to come due in the twelve months following September.

So there is a large existing cash balance, and a very large set of bills coming. Anthropic says cash use could rise as it pays for infrastructure ahead of availability. It also has a planned $26B private placement from chip and cloud partners, contingent on the IPO and other conditions.

Nebius reports tomorrow

Nebius reports before the market opens on Thursday.

CoreWeave finally gave the trade a reason to feel better about the financing, and the earlier deployments make that more useful than another big capacity target on its own. More contracted demand from Nebius, with funding on workable terms and delivery dates intact, would help support the orders suppliers are counting on.

If demand is strong but the money or the construction is falling behind, we’ll have to look at which deliveries could move. I want to hear how much of the demand can actually get built, and how soon the companies supplying it can get paid.

Shall be a fun morning!
