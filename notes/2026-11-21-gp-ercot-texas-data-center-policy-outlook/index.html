SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed Texas policy changes affecting data centers, electricity demand, and grid investment. The conversation focused on environmental permitting, ERCOT’s large-load process, expected legislative action, and the balance between economic development, consumer costs, and local authority.

Key Insights

The expert expects a temporary moratorium on new data center projects, followed by a bipartisan legislative package. Existing projects in the baseload category are viewed as more likely to proceed, while studied and later-stage requests may face delays. The expert expects added costs and regulatory requirements to slow some projects, but believes most viable developments can adapt rather than leave Texas. Local control, grid-cost allocation, and water use are likely to be central negotiating issues. The expert anticipates stronger city authority and some expanded county involvement. Transmission expansion is seen as necessary for rising demand and new generation. The expert expects less political resistance to major transmission projects under the incoming administration. The expert expects limited near-term change at the PUC and ERCOT, with potentially larger changes at TCEQ, including tighter reporting and enforcement.
