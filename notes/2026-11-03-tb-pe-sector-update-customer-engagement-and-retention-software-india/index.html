原标题：PE Sector Update: Customer Engagement & Retention Software

#software

Third Bridge · 2026-11-03

核心摘要

  1. 受访者是前CleverTap产品增长与战略总监，讨论印度客户互动平台软件。
  2. 专家预计印度客户互动平台市场未来五年年增20–25%，驱动来自二线及以下城市企业的数字化。
  3. 数字原生企业的渗透率已达70–80%，传统企业只有20–30%，增长空间大。
  4. 客户互动平台可将客户终身价值提升10–20%，但传统归因模型可能高估营销活动的增量，因为部分购买本会自然发生。
  5. 专家认为AI决策能力可带来约30%的价格溢价，可能抵消商品化消息服务10–15%的降价压力。
  6. 纳入数据云、获客和忠诚度平台等板块后总市场规模还可扩大。

Companies: CRM US, MMYT US, ADBE US, ETERNAL IN, 500285 IN, SWIGGY IN

Mohit Minhas (MM) VP, Product & Go-to-market Strategy at Adjetter Media Network Pvt Ltd (Kapture CX)

Devanshi Shah (DS) — Third Bridge Sector Analyst

Agenda

  • Market structure analysis, including consumer adoption and impact of AI
  • Pricing model, NRR (net revenue retention) and logo churn rate drivers
  • Competitive landscape, positioning of key players and long-term scalability of the sector

Key Insights

  • India's customer engagement platform software market is expected to grow at 20-25% annually over the next five years, driven by digital adoption among businesses in tier 2 and smaller cities. Inclusion of segments such as data clouds, customer acquisition and loyalty platforms could expand the total market size
  • Digital-native companies are 70-80% captured by customer engagement platforms, while traditional enterprises undergoing digital transformation remain only 20-30% penetrated, representing significant growth runway
  • Customer engagement platforms can increase customer lifetime value by 10-20%. However, actual incremental impact of marketing campaigns may be lower than reported by conventional attribution models, as some purchases would have occurred organically. The extent of this uplift varies by sector and transaction value
  • Expert believes customer engagement platforms can command a c30% pricing premium for AI-driven decisioning capabilities, potentially offsetting 10-15% pricing pressure from commoditised messaging services

00:10:31] How do businesses measure the return on loyalty and retention programmes in practice?

MM [00:10:40]: It's an interesting question because typically, when it is measured only in the number of orders that can be attributed to purchases that had linked loyalty or discounting or any of those sorts of things, they lead to skewed metrics because purchase behaviour, excluding discounting or loyalty and any of these things, is ignored. Typically, what I would say is that as a general industry-wide spread, 2x incremental reporting happens when you only try to measure based on the volume of conversions, but the way that brands look to measure this more accurately is either via an A/B test happening on customers that are included in promotions and the ones that are not and comparing the outcomes across the two buckets to see how much the incremental growth is between those two or time period-wise, so the same quarter last year as compared to this year or something like that. Lots of brands also measure this in terms of the increment or decrease perhaps in the lifetime value associated with any specific customer rather than more rudimentary metrics like orders or purchases happening.

In a nutshell, there is a concerted effort made to measure it either via time or user exclusion via A/B test, comparison to the ones that are included in promotions vs the ones that are not and then seeing the outcome rather than attributing everything to someone who is going through a campaign. If you attribute everyone to a campaign, the metrics get inflated. Swiggy today at lunchtime sends me a coupon code to make a purchase and I make a purchase. It can be attributed to the coupon being sent, but that is ignoring the fact that maybe I would have made the purchase anyway during lunchtime, even if I did not receive the coupon. This is actually the user intent already being there towards a purchase that marketers and organisations try to exclude via methods like A/B testing or comparison against similar time windows or measuring the overall customer lifetime value to ensure that the true effect of a marketing campaign is measured, not just you send a campaign to someone who is already going to make a purchase and then saying, "Oh, they purchased because I sent the campaign."
