  2. 企业环境中无人工监督的后台自主Agent任务成功率仅约20%–30%，因此企业普遍排斥不可控的后台试错，仍首选步骤固化的有向无环图（DAG）或沉淀日常工作流的Skills Markdown，并配合LangSmith等工具进行人工与AI双重审计。

SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examines DeepSeek’s open-source agent framework and the broader shift in enterprise AI from improving models alone toward building systems that can reliably complete work. The framework combines an orchestrator with task-specific agents and uses efficiency and safety mechanisms. The expert views this approach as useful in some settings, but says it does not resolve core enterprise concerns around reliability, oversight, security, and cost.

Key Insights

The expert considers agent orchestration an established approach, not a unique DeepSeek breakthrough. Scaffolding can compensate for weaker models, while leading models may handle some multistep work with less external support. Enterprise adoption remains bounded by trust: the expert estimates unsupervised task completion succeeds only about 20–30% of the time. Businesses therefore tend to favor defined workflows, reusable task skills, and human review over autonomous agents. Cross-department agents are uncommon. Organizational structure, permissions, and accountability often present greater obstacles than the technical design. The expert expects product experience, workflow integration, and ecosystem reach to matter more than model capability alone, particularly as models become less differentiated. The expert says always-on agents could increase demand for inference, but raise expectations for latency, reliability, and service quality. This may pressure cloud providers to balance performance with profitability. Traditional SaaS providers risk losing ground if their AI features remain weak and customers can build tools around their own processes. The expert sees this as a growing threat over the next year or two.
