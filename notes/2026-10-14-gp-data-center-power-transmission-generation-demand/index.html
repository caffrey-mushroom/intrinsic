SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion covered transmission, distribution, and power-generation construction, with emphasis on the southwestern United States. Electricity demand from data centers and other large loads is driving investment, but equipment shortages, permitting, limited gas infrastructure, and scarce engineering and construction labor are restricting execution.

Distribution demand is broad and steady, while southwestern transmission work is focused mainly on regional reliability, congestion relief, and reconductoring. Other US regions are experiencing much larger transmission programs. Generation demand is exceptionally strong, particularly for gas-fired plants, battery storage, and upgrades to existing assets.

Key Insights

The expert expects a prolonged western transmission expansion as the grid adapts to more dispersed generation and load locations. Thermal-generation inquiries have risen sharply, but manufacturing capacity now largely determines how quickly projects can proceed. Gas turbines, reciprocating engines, and other generation equipment are heavily committed. Developers are considering refurbished assets and unconventional equipment sources. Engineering expertise and construction capacity are major constraints. Experienced specialists are scarce, while contractors can selectively pursue the most attractive projects. Large interstate transmission projects remain difficult in the Southwest because routes often cross federal or tribal lands. Proposed federal permitting reform could revive delayed projects. Suitable sites combining land, water, gas access, and grid connectivity are becoming increasingly scarce. Higher interest rates are not currently viewed as a material obstacle because large-load developers appear willing to spend heavily for dependable power.

Engineering firms

Demand exceeds available experienced staff, forcing selective bidding and accelerated training.

Large contractors

Expanding design capabilities, but face the same shortage of experienced specialists. Strong backlogs support direct awards and reduced bidding competition.

Equipment manufacturers

Hold substantial leverage because turbine and engine capacity is committed years ahead.
