SUMMARY

Overview

An expert from the pipeline engineering, procurement, and construction sector discussed demand, costs, permitting, competition, and consolidation. Market activity is being supported by data-center power needs, renewable natural gas projects, pipeline integrity requirements, and replacement of aging distribution infrastructure.

Transmission demand is broadly stable, while renewable natural gas activity is accelerating. Data centers represent a major long- term growth opportunity but require unusually fast execution and may face local opposition, land constraints, and permitting delays.

Key Insights

Renewable natural gas is the clearest near-term growth area, with substantially more requests for proposals and continued expansion expected. Data-center pipeline demand could grow rapidly because existing power shortages are being compounded by new computing loads. Fully integrated EPC providers with in-house construction have a meaningful scheduling advantage. This is especially important for time-sensitive data-center projects. Higher material, fuel, and financing costs have not materially slowed projects because renewable gas receives incentives, utility work can be funded through rate cases, and data centers prioritize speed. Permitting remains a major bottleneck. Staffing shortages and environmental reviews may limit the near-term benefit of federal reforms. Contractors remain aggressive on pricing. Low initial bids can later become more expensive through change orders. Acquisitions are expanding contractors into engineering, environmental services, land acquisition, inspection, and integrity management.
