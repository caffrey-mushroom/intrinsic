Key Takeaways

  • AI demand. Super Intelligence revenue reached $7.56B, +830% y/y (last q +248%) and +195% q/q, vs. Street $6.47B. Higher-margin cloud agreements drove adjusted EBITDA to $4.95B vs. $3.97B, at a 65.5% margin. The acceleration extended beyond the acquisition contribution: solutions and infrastructure revenue grew approximately 192% q/q excluding Cursor’s $608M contribution. Mgmt described demand from hyperscalers and smaller customers as several times available capacity.
  • Compute ramp. Nameplate compute ended Q3 at 1.7GW vs. Street 1.75GW, but mgmt expects approximately 2.2GW by October-end and 2.5–3.0GW by year-end, up from the prior >2GW target. For 2027, mgmt expressed high confidence in adding approximately 10GW, reaching roughly 13GW total vs. Street ~6.1GW. The separate ambition to add 20GW of power and cooling infrastructure provides capacity ahead of GPU deployment; it should not be treated as 20GW of operational compute.
  • Power advantage. Mgmt sees power, cooling and electrical infrastructure as the principal deployment constraints. Applying rocket-engine casting capabilities to turbine blades and vanes could shorten power-delivery timelines by 12–18 months. Deployment is running toward approximately 200,000–220,000 GB300s monthly through year-end, with the pace expected to continue or accelerate in 2027.
  • Pricing. New compute pricing is slightly above $50/W in annualized revenue, exceeding the high end of the previous $30–50/W range. Short-duration contracts are renewing at higher prices, and mgmt cited older H100s renting above their original rates. New deployments are generating paybacks below one year; six-month paybacks in 2027 were presented as a possibility. T
  • Revenue visibility. The $33–37B Q1 guide implies approximately $140B of annualized revenue at the midpoint, already above pre-results FY27 Street of roughly $117B before further growth through the year. Backlog increased to $63B from $47.5B, including $17.5B of deferred revenue, with approximately $42B expected to convert within 12 months. Mgmt reiterated the $100B December annualized-revenue target and outlined a $500B–$1T exit-2027 ambition.
  • Internal monetization. Mgmt’s intended compute allocation is approximately 50% internal and 50% external, with flexibility to shift monthly. Illustratively, 13GW with half rented at $50/W produces $325B of annualized rental revenue before utilization adjustments. Reaching the broader $500B–$1T ambition therefore requires substantial monetization of internally consumed compute, contributions from other businesses, higher pricing or additional capacity. Applying external rental economics to the entire fleet overstates visibility.
  • Applications. Grok Bot reached approximately 800,000 paid weekly users in early October, more than doubling over the preceding month, with B2B the fastest-growing category. Enterprise customers above $1M in annualized revenue increased approximately 50% q/q on a pro forma basis including Cursor. The combined Grok/Grok Bot/Cursor/X Premium subscription supports cross-selling, with wider enterprise deployment expected within weeks. A 440,000-system Vera Rubin training cluster is targeted by end-Q2’27 to improve Grok’s competitiveness.
  • Capital requirements. Q3 operating cash flow of $9.63B funded approximately half of $19.59B in CapEx, leaving FCF at negative $9.96B. Q4 CapEx rises to $35–40B, approximately 77% above Street at the midpoint and 91% above Q3. Mgmt pointed to operating cash flow, debt markets and vendor/customer partnerships while emphasizing preservation of its investment-grade rating.
  • Margin quality. Q3’s 60.4% adjusted EBITDA margin vs. Street 53.0% benefited from favorable customer mix and one-time items. Connectivity gross margin included approximately 300bps of one-time benefit, primarily tariff refunds; excluding one-offs, segment adjusted EBITDA margin expanded approximately 200bps y/y. Mgmt expects Q4 consolidated gross margin to move closer to Q2’s 55.3%, compared with Q3’s 62.4% and pre-results Street near 63%.
  • Starlink. Connectivity revenue grew 69.5% y/y to $5.25B (last q +65.8%) vs. Street $4.92B, with adjusted EBITDA of $3.50B vs. $2.94B. Consumer subscribers reached 13.6M vs. Street 14.3M, with 1.6M net adds. ARPU of $69 vs. Street $67, up 5% q/q but down from $83 a year ago, offset the subscriber shortfall through a more favorable geographic mix. Increased V5 kit production and holiday channel inventory should support Q4 activations.
  • Enterprise connectivity. Enterprise and government revenue grew approximately 91% y/y to $2.1B, following 108% growth last quarter, supported by Starshield and aviation. Aviation contracts now cover roughly 12,000 aircraft, approximately one-quarter of the addressable fleet. Production capacity has quadrupled since the start of the year and is expected to double again by year-end, supporting faster installations in 2027. Mgmt continues to expect enterprise and government revenue eventually to exceed consumer connectivity revenue.
  • Starship. Space revenue of $1.77B, +6% y/y (last q +29%), beat Street $1.46B on favorable mission mix; adjusted EBITDA of $357M beat $115M. Flight 14 deployed operational V3 satellites with bandwidth equivalent to approximately 10 Falcon 9 launches despite being only partially loaded. Flight 15 in November will attempt catches of both stages, followed by second-stage reflight and orbital refueling milestones in early 2027. Weekly launches are targeted during 1H27 and at least twice weekly by year-end.
  • Mobile expansion. Starlink Mobile surpassed 22M monthly users, +37% q/q. Mobile V2 deployment begins in 2027, with U.S. service targeted for early 2028. The architecture combines satellites, Wi-Fi, mid-band spectrum, 800MHz spectrum for building penetration and terrestrial radios using existing installations. A fuller strategy presentation could clarify network costs and competitive positioning. Separately, broadband users may begin seeing meaningful V3 benefits with a few hundred satellites by mid-2027, versus the previous framing of approximately 1,000.
  • Terafab. The first functional test chip is targeted for May/June 2027, with volume production around Q3’28 on an explicitly aggressive timetable. Supplier equipment deliveries are progressing, but EUV orders were not explicitly confirmed.
  • TSLA combination. Both companies have formed special committees, with Musk recused. No transaction terms have been agreed. Approval would require support from at least 50% of all TSLA shares, including shares that do not vote.

The #s:

Q3 revenue of $12.87B, +11.8% y/y (last q +13.4%), met Street at $12.87B and EPS beat at $0.87 vs. $0.82, but Q4 revenue/EPS guidance of $13.44B/$0.67 missed Street at ~$13.52B/$0.73 and bogeys which were a little higher. UCAN was ok as 3p had pointed to, but ROW was main drag on #s.

The main disappointment was the FY26 advertising reset to ~$2.7B, +80%+, from ~$3B previously and vs. Street at ~$3.07B, reflecting delayed product launches and partner integrations. Stronger subscriptions cushioned the impact, with FY26 revenue now ~$51.1B vs. Street at ~$51.2B and the prior $51.0–51.4B range; the 31.5% operating-margin target held. Q3’s profit beat benefited from expense timing, while Q4’s 26.0% margin guide includes ~120bps of restructuring costs, implying ~27.2% excluding those costs vs. Street at 27.6%. Improved churn and healthy pricing was one thing bulls could hold on to, but bulls need better US content execution and delivery against the ad roadmap to restore confidence in durable dd growth.

Key Takeaways

  • Growth. Q4 revenue guidance implies +11.5% y/y vs. Street at ~12.1–12.2% and buyside bogey of +12.6%. FX-neutral growth is expected to hold around 11%, following 11% in Q3 and 12% in Q2. The subscription business remains healthy, but the guide offers little evidence of an imminent acceleration.
  • Advertising. The ~$300M reduction in FY26 ads is the clearest incremental negative. Mgmt attributed the shortfall to delayed capabilities and partner integrations, while describing advertiser demand as strong and the advertiser base as expanding. Preserving NFLX’s first-party data advantage is contributing to integration complexity.
  • 2027 ads. Street estimates around $5.2–5.3B now require roughly 93–96% growth from the lowered ~$2.7B base. That is a demanding hurdle after this year’s execution miss. A ~$4B outcome would still represent roughly 48% growth, yet leave advertising approximately $1.2–1.3B below those expectations.
  • Ad roadmap. US upfront commitments nearly doubled, providing evidence of advertiser interest. Near-term execution milestones include TTD audience targeting in November, broader programmatic buying, expanded measurement, and a new NFLX Pixel and upgraded Conversion API supporting more than 20 lower-funnel conversion events. Partnerships with MELI in LATAM and Adform in EMEA broaden access to demand. These capabilities should improve targeting, attribution and conversion measurement as they become available.
  • Monetization. Combined subscription-plus-ad revenue per member remains below the standard ad-free tier, although the gap is narrowing. Improving fill rates, measurement and targeting provides a multiyear opportunity; continued ad-tier membership growth simultaneously adds inventory that must be monetized. Expansion into 15 additional countries in 2027 adds another growth avenue, with the first group launching in March. Mgmt provided neither the current ARM gap nor a date for reaching parity.
  • Pricing. Global and US churn improved y/y and was better than forecast despite recent price increases. Member responses remained consistent with previous pricing cycles. This is the strongest evidence against an immediate deterioration in the franchise: customers continue to accept higher prices even while viewing growth is under scrutiny.
  • Regional mix. UCAN growth accelerated to 11% from 10%, benefiting from the first full quarter of the March US price increase. International FX-neutral growth slowed: EMEA to 9% from 11%, LATAM to 12% from 16%, and APAC to 15% from 18%. India was the counterexample, with viewing hours up 24%, viewing per member also increasing, and revenue growing nearly 20%. Local content can still generate meaningful growth, although that does not establish the timing of a US recovery.
  • Content. Peter Rice’s appointment as Chief Content Officer acknowledges the need for better US execution and stronger talent relationships. Mgmt wants more breakout US hits that travel globally, while retaining the existing strategy; roughly 95% of spending remains focused on the core offering. Rice can influence projects already underway, but a sustained improvement in the broader slate will take time to demonstrate. Q4 franchises and live events, followed by titles such as Narnia, Bridgerton and Wednesday in 2027 are next
  • Margins. Q3’s $4.46B operating profit vs. Street at ~$4.27B benefited from spending shifting into Q4, including less than $200M of content and marketing expenses. The Q4 guide implies approximately $3.66B of operating profit excluding restructuring, still about 2% below Street at ~$3.73B. The full-year margin held at 31.5%.
  • Restructuring. The headcount charge represents approximately 120bps of Q4 margin and 30bps for FY26. Mgmt acknowledged overinvestment in some areas and framed the reductions as a way to improve prioritization and redirect resources. Annual savings, the reinvestment share and the FY27 margin benefit were not quantified.
  • Buybacks. NFLX repurchased $4.8B of shares in Q3, with $22.3B remaining under its authorization. Diluted shares fell 3.3% y/y, helping per-share earnings growth exceed operating-profit growth
  • Partnerships. TF1 viewing is increasing monthly, and early testing indicates the engagement is incremental to NFLX. Mgmt sees potential to replicate the model and is open to third-party subscription add-ons, with nothing announced. Disney and Globo licensing agreements broaden the content offering, while selective 45-day theatrical windows and box-office reporting expand distribution options for certain films.
