SUMMARY

Overview

The expert discussed enterprise storage, its role in supporting business applications and backup, and the shift from hardware purchases toward subscription and service-based models. The conversation focused on how AI is increasing the importance of clean, accessible, secure data, while rising component costs and fixed budgets shape purchasing decisions.

Storage demand includes conventional block and file systems as well as object storage for unstructured information such as images and video. The expert expects businesses to prioritize modernizing and organizing data alongside expanding capacity, rather than treating storage as a simple infrastructure purchase.

Key Insights

The expert believes storage demand will remain important even if some AI projects struggle to justify their investment, because businesses still need infrastructure for their applications and data. However, constrained budgets may reduce the amount of capacity purchased. In the expert’s view, storage vendors will increasingly compete on data management, AI access, cybersecurity resilience, and environmental efficiency—not just capacity. AI pilots often fail to reach production because data may be duplicated, poorly organized, or unsuitable for secure use. The expert estimated that only a minority of pilots make that transition. Component price increases do not necessarily pass fully to customers; vendors and partners may absorb some cost through lower margins or discounts. The moderator repeatedly explored whether AI investment concerns could weaken storage demand. The expert maintained that AI-related data preparation and storage remain complementary needs, while acknowledging budget constraints.
