SUMMARY

Overview

A communications systems integrator discussed the impact of a major outage affecting Microsoft services and what it means for unified communications as a service (UCaaS) and contact center as a service (CCaaS). The expert’s experience spans UK enterprise, mid-market, and public-sector deployments, including cloud and on-premise systems.

The outage exposed the risks of relying on one provider for email, collaboration, and telephony. The expert expects resilience to become a standard part of communications planning, particularly for organizations where customers depend on telephone access or where on-site operations cannot stop.

Key Insights

Basic phone access remains business-critical, even when collaboration tools are widely adopted. Customers care that they can reach a business, not which communications app carries the call. Resilient design means considering the entire call path: carriers and phone numbers, network infrastructure, cloud platforms, and fallback options. Using different vendors does not ensure resilience if they depend on the same underlying cloud. Hybrid approaches are gaining relevance. Cloud tools suit mobile and office workers, while on-site systems may be necessary for facilities such as hospitals, factories, and logistics operations. The expert expects cloud communications spending to remain active, but basic UCaaS features are increasingly commoditized and prices are under pressure. Contact center and AI offerings are important ways for vendors to expand revenue. The moderator pursued the expert’s views on contact center competition and future budgets, prompting discussion of cloud dependencies, CRM platforms, and AI.

Competitive Landscape

Positioning and considerations

Microsoft Teams

Dominant workplace platform with broad adoption, but a single-provider dependency can disrupt voice and collaboration.

Mitel

The expert described it as the leading global enterprise option for on-premise voice, supported by a long history in critical communications.

AudioCodes and Ribbon

Their session border controller products can support carrier connections and potentially provide fallback voice capabilities.

8x8 and other UCaaS providers

Can offer an alternative client or platform when the primary collaboration environment is unavailable; integration quality varies.

NICE and Five9

Seen as established next-generation contact center providers, with AI capabilities and resilience offerings. Their standalone focus may mean separate systems and troubleshooting from UC platforms.

Salesforce and Microsoft Dynamics

CRM providers are expanding into contact center and treating voice as one of several customer interaction channels.

Hyperscalers

Contact center vendors may rely on Azure, AWS, or Google Cloud, so a separate vendor may not provide true infrastructure diversity.

Quantitative Insights

Metric

Expert’s assessment

UCaaS price, historically

Roughly £10 per user per month for larger opportunities, as a broad estimate.

UCaaS price trend

Larger deals are now well below that level; the expert expects prices may approach £1–£2 per user per month over time.

Contact center seat revenue

Typically at least 10 times the revenue of a voice seat.

Potential fallback window

A separate client or routing arrangement could enable service to resume in about an hour during an outage lasting several hours.

DDI resilience example

An organization with 1,000 numbers could split them between two carriers, such as 500/500 or 900/100, depending on risk tolerance.
