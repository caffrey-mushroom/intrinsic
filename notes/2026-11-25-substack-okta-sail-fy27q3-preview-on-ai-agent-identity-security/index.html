Executive Summary

  1. AI agents expand identity security beyond employees to agents and machine identities, shifting the main risk from employee logins to machine credentials. Our 26Q3 channel checks show that customers rank identity security among their highest-priority security spending areas for 2026/27, although incremental budgets are unevenly allocated. Identity governance (IGA), privileged access management (PAM) and non-human identity (NHI) are seeing the earliest spend.
  2. SailPoint is likely to monetize AI-related demand ahead of Okta. It can add machine identity and agent governance modules to cloud migration projects within its installed base before agents are widely deployed. Okta’s agent products are more dependent on customers moving from pilots to large-scale production.
  3. Okta is broadly tracking channel expectations.   4. SailPoint is tracking ahead of guidance in channel checks. Large customers continue to prioritize separation of duties and hybrid-environment governance. How AI Agents Affect Identity Security

AI agents affect identity security in two ways. First, they expand the number of identities to manage: every agent needs an account and credentials. In current deployments, many still rely on long-lived credentials such as service accounts and API keys, often without a clearly assigned human owner or regular review. Second, the risk shifts from employee logins to machine credentials. Once a credential is exposed, an attacker can use its existing permissions to access resources directly, bypassing the protection that employee-login MFA provides.

Recent cases of internal misuse and external attacks illustrate these risks: Subscribed In a customer case disclosed by NordLayer November 18 customer case, a QA engineer asked a coding agent to review a merge request. To access a GitLab token, the agent loaded the entire .env file, exposing other credentials in the session output, including production passwords and an HR-system API token. Similar incidents occurred in 10 sessions over three weeks. In another case, a product manager created a personal API token after an MCP connection became unavailable, allowing the agent to read Confluence through the REST API.

Microsoft’s November 5 report describes attackers using an agentic AI framework to search GitHub for exposed credentials and then access cloud resources with the application credentials they obtained. In one case observed by Microsoft, the agent went from scanning GitHub to stealing data from Azure Key Vault in just five minutes.

Enterprises therefore need to inventory agents and assign clear owners, issue credentials with least privilege and defined expiration periods, require human approval before high-risk actions, and revoke credentials immediately when suspicious activity is detected. Okta and SailPoint both offer relevant products, but their integration models and coverage differ.

AI Monetization: SailPoint Likely Ahead of Okta

The main difference between the two companies is the timing and route to monetization. Okta’s agent-specific revenue depends more on customers moving applications from pilots into large-scale production. SailPoint can include machine identity and agent governance in existing cloud migration and governance projects. As a result, AI is likely to have a limited near-term revenue impact on Okta, while SailPoint may see related demand appear earlier in bookings and ARR, with a somewhat larger contribution.

A higher agent count does not automatically translate into Okta workforce identity seat revenue. Customers need to purchase an enterprise agent-management product or integrate Auth0 authorization into their applications, then complete integration, permission configuration and production deployment before the demand becomes billable. Few projects are paid today and most remain at the POC stage. Near-term performance therefore still depends mainly on renewals, pricing and attach rates for existing modules.

SailPoint customers may pay for machine-account inventory, owner assignment and audit processes before agents are widely deployed. These activities can be included in existing governance budgets and purchased alongside ISC migration projects. Package- or connector-based pricing also allows customers to pay for governance coverage before usage volumes scale. For customers already planning a migration, adding governance modules may involve a shorter sales cycle.

Budgets and Procurement: Governance, Privileged Access and NHI Benefit First

Channel feedback indicates that some identity-security demand has moved into budgeting and procurement. Customers rank identity among their higher-priority security spending areas for 2026/27, and some budgets are shifting from traditional firewall products to identity security.

Machine identities now outnumber employee identities, increasing the work required to inventory them, assign owners, enforce least-privilege access and conduct periodic certification. Pricing is also expanding beyond per-employee models to per-agent or per-connector fees. Procurement has begun, although most customers remain in the inventory and pilot stages.

Growth differs meaningfully across identity products. Identity governance (IGA) and privileged access management (PAM) address agent discovery, authorization and certification, so incremental spending is appearing first in these categories. Workforce identity faces competition from Entra bundled with E5. Customer identity and access management (CIAM) has lower switching costs and more alternatives, making it one of the less sticky layers of the identity stack, according to channel feedback.

For SailPoint, longer contract terms increase total contract value and are showing up mainly in RPO and term-license revenue near term. Incremental ARR still depends on customers adding modules.

Channel Feedback: Where Incremental Growth Comes From

Okta: Installed-Base Expansion Supports Low-Double-Digit Growth

Okta’s performance this quarter was broadly in line with channel expectations, and channel partners expect results to remain stable over the next several quarters. Growth is mainly coming from installed-base expansion; a meaningful contribution from agent products has not yet emerged.

Figure: Okta Revenue Guidance and Channel Expectations

Source: FUNDA

Workforce Identity seat counts remain stable, with growth driven mainly by pricing and phased deployments. Renewals have not shown broad seat reductions, and concerns about seat contraction have not yet appeared in contracts. Large customers typically add seats gradually over several years after signing, limiting the contribution in any single year. Competitive pressure comes from customers with E5 moving authentication back to Entra, which constrains Okta’s pricing power.

North American Channel Partner 17 (11/6/2026):

“It’s not at the level the market is worried about. Frankly, whether in cybersecurity or enterprise software, we have not seen broad seat reductions…”

European Channel Partner 4 (11/6/2026):

“Price increases did drive growth; although list prices went up about 10%–12%, most customers don’t absorb the full increase, so overall the effective contract price increase is around 7%–8%.”

Industry Expert 3 (10/19/2026):

“Okta usually wins in non-Microsoft or multi-cloud environments…”

Customer Identity is broadly flat, with neutral-to-weak channel feedback. It is easier to replace than workforce identity, and customers often revisit CIAM vendors during identity-system consolidation to compare pricing. Cloud providers offer lower-cost alternatives. Management said customer identity ACV growth accelerated in Q2, but channel partners have not observed the same improvement.

North American Channel Partner 17 (11/6/2026):

“We have seen the customer identity business trend gradually lower over the past few quarters. So this quarter it is 13.7%, versus about 14.6% last quarter (Q2).”

European Channel Partner 4 (11/6/2026):

“The two are performing about the same. Overall, both are growing at around 16%–18%.”

Module upsell remains the main growth driver, while channel views on new-logo acquisition are mixed. Limited pricing power makes Okta more reliant on fine-grained authorization, advanced MFA and OIG upsell. Evidence points more clearly to installed-base expansion than to broad new-customer acceleration. OIG is easier to sell to companies with fewer than 2,500 employees; larger customers often need separation-of-duties functionality and deep connectors, where SailPoint is more mature. FGA, MFA and OIG also serve traditional security needs, so demand is not driven solely by AI.

North American Channel Partner 17 (11/6/2026):

“Okta’s pricing power isn’t very strong. Growth comes mainly from attach rates on new modules, which in my view contribute at least 55% to 60%, even up to 65%, of total revenue.”

North American Channel Partner 18 (11/3/2026):

“It is still the benchmark for standalone workforce identity and customer identity and access management, but right now it is mainly maintaining existing customers rather than winning many net new ones.”

Industry Expert 15 (10/29/2026):

“Okta is clearly less mature in IGA. Its core strength is access management, especially single sign-on (SSO).”

Agent products: paid customers and production deployments remain limited. Auth0 for AI Agents became generally available on November 19, 2025, and Okta for AI Agents on April 30, 2026. Channel feedback indicates that most customers remain at the POC stage, and management has said these products are not expected to make a material contribution to FY27 revenue. Incremental usage-based revenue will depend on further growth in customer agent deployments.

European Channel Partner 4 (11/6/2026):

Industry Expert 4 (10/20/2026):

“Over the past three months Okta has launched products for AI agents, which is a welcome development, but it has not yet produced a meaningful breakthrough in large-customer deals.”

SailPoint: Growth Ahead of Guidance, Driven by Deep Governance and Cloud Migration

SailPoint’s channel performance is ahead of company guidance. Large customers continue to show solid demand for separation-of-duties functionality and governance across hybrid environments, while SaaS migration creates opportunities to sell additional machine identity and agent governance modules.

Figure: SailPoint ARR Guidance and Channel Expectations

Source: FUNDA

Cloud migration: discounts help secure contracts, while module upsell increases initial contract value. SailPoint uses migration programs to move customers to three-year SaaS contracts, often at substantial discounts, with pricing increases at renewal. Channel partners describe migration activity as steady, with some seeing an acceleration. Higher initial contract values mainly reflect the addition of AI and machine identity modules; moving an existing deployment to the cloud alone adds limited ARR in the near term.

Machine identity and Agentic Fabric: signs of contract expansion, while usage-based pricing is still developing. New agent capabilities are built mainly on ISC. Customers using on-premise IIQ who want agent governance may therefore have an incentive to accelerate migration to ISC. Agentic Fabric is currently priced mainly by connector.

North American Channel Partner 17 (11/6/2026):

Customer retention remains high, but competition for new opportunities is increasing. SailPoint’s retention among large, highly regulated customers is close to 97%. In new IGA projects, Microsoft is winning a share comparable to SailPoint’s. In agent governance, Saviynt is ahead in agent-creation capabilities, while AI-native IGA vendors such as C1 and Lumos are also beginning to win new customers. Whether new opportunities can replace the migration-driven contribution as that tailwind fades will shape the sustainability of growth.
