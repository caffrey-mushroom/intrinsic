SUMMARY

Overview

The discussion examined Amadeus’s recent results and its outlook for travel distribution and airline software. Bookings improved as cancellation levels normalized, but weaker demand and airline capacity constraints remain concerns. The expert sees potential growth from low-cost carriers joining global distribution systems (GDSs), a gradual return of some NDC bookings to intermediaries, airline IT product sales, and AI-related services. However, newer booking channels may bring lower revenue per transaction and higher search costs.

Key Insights

The expert expects baseline travel growth to broadly track economic growth, with additional potential from newly distributed low-cost carrier content. This could support growth beyond organic levels, though adoption will determine the scale. Low-cost carrier deals are a significant market change. They may shift some bookings from aggregators while also enabling airlines to reach corporate customers they could not previously access. NDC re-intermediation is likely to be gradual. Large online travel agencies may retain direct connections to their biggest airline partners, while smaller agencies and long-tail content are more likely to return to GDSs. The expert believes Amadeus has a stronger distribution technology proposition than competitors, but expects NDC and low- cost content to generate less contribution per booking than traditional full-service content. AI travel agents are expected to change how consumers search and plan trips, but the expert does not expect them to quickly displace intermediaries. Existing distribution models can extend to these channels. The expert warned that AI-driven searches could sharply increase search costs relative to completed bookings, with cloud providers capturing some of the resulting expense. Amadeus’s ability to use multiple cloud providers was viewed as a cost- management advantage. AI partnerships were characterized as less important than applying AI products to the company’s existing customer base.
